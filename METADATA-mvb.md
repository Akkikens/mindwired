Only one man in World Cup history has done what Jude Bellingham just did — score twice in back-to-back World Cup knockout games at his age — and his name is Pelé, 1958. In four days, that 23-year-old walks into the Estadio Azteca to face Lionel Messi in the 2026 World Cup semifinal: Argentina vs England, the defending champions against the tournament's form team.

This is the complete pre-match breakdown of football's ultimate generational duel. Messi at 39, playing his fifth and final World Cup, top of the Golden Boot race with 8 goals — leading Argentina the way he always has: in silence, deciding games in ninety-second bursts. Bellingham at 23 — four knockout goals, every one scored with England behind or level, 14 kilometres a game — leading by fire, dragging England through two straight extra-time wars. Two opposite theories of leadership collide for one place in the World Cup final.

We break down how each captain leads his dressing room, the full tournament numbers behind both players, the tactical battle — England hunting the space Messi refuses to press, Argentina targeting English legs carrying 240 minutes of extra-time football in eight days — and the one number that already tells you who wins this semifinal.
