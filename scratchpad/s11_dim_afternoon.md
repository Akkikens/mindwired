## 1. WTC 7 — the day-of human/decision timeline (NOT the engineering)

*CLAIMS-wtccollapse.md already owns: the Column 79/thermal-expansion
mechanism, the free-fall measurement and NIST's evolving public statements
about it, the Silverstein "pull it" quote (both readings), and the
conspiracy-claims segment. None of that is repeated here.*

- **8:46 a.m.–9:59 a.m.:** WTC 7 occupants (the building held, among other
  tenants, the NYC Office of Emergency Management's own emergency command
  center on the 23rd floor) began self-evacuating after feeling the WTC 1
  impact next door. **CONFIRMED**, NCSTAR 1A p. 15.
- **~9:45 a.m.:** An OEM manager ordered the evacuation of WTC 7 — NIST notes
  it "was not clear whether this order was broadcast over the public address
  system, because the building was already nearly empty" by that point.
  **CONFIRMED**, NCSTAR 1A p. 15.
- **By 9:59 a.m.** (when WTC 2 collapsed): "all the building occupants who
  intended to leave WTC 7 had done so." **CONFIRMED**, NCSTAR 1A p. 15.
- **10:28:22 a.m.:** WTC 1's collapse sent debris into WTC 7's south and west
  faces, severing seven exterior columns (six on the south face between
  Floors 7–17, one on the west face) and damaging the structure between Floor
  44 and the roof — the physical damage that seeded the fires. (Mechanism of
  what this damage caused structurally is CLAIMS-wtccollapse.md's territory;
  the fact of the debris strike itself is **CONFIRMED**, NCSTAR 1A pp. 16–17.)
- **As early as 11:30 a.m.:** FDNY found there was no water supplied by the
  hydrant system to fight visible WTC 7 fires — the collapse of the towers
  had severed the city water main that was WTC 7's sole supply for its
  standpipe/sprinkler systems on the lower 20 floors. **CONFIRMED**, NCSTAR
  1A p. 18.
- **Within the next two hours** (i.e., by early-to-mid afternoon), "serious
  discussions were underway regarding the cessation of any efforts to save
  WTC 7." **CONFIRMED**, NCSTAR 1A p. 18.
- **~2:30 p.m.:** "FDNY gave the order to forego firefighting activity and
  for personnel to withdraw to a safe distance from the building." This is
  NIST's own stated time for the final order to cease efforts — **CONFIRMED,
  exact time, NCSTAR 1A p. 18 and p. 56 (Findings §4.4.2)**.
- **4:33 p.m.:** Con Edison shut down the electrical substation under WTC 7.
  **CONFIRMED**, NCSTAR 1A p. 18, p. 56.
- **5:20:52 p.m.** (also written "5:21 p.m." in NIST's own section headers):
  WTC 7 collapsed. **CONFIRMED**, NCSTAR 1A p. 14/18 (exact-second timestamp
  appears repeatedly throughout the report).
- **No lives were lost in the collapse of WTC 7** — NIST states this
  explicitly as a Finding: "no lives were lost as a result of the collapse
  of WTC 7." **CONFIRMED, exact phrasing, NCSTAR 1A p. 56.**
- **NIST's own retrospective judgment on the pace of the decision:** "Key
  decisions (e.g., decisions not to fight the fires in WTC 7 and to turn off
  the power to the Con Edison substation) were reasonable and would not have
  changed the outcome... but were not made promptly." NIST separately notes
  that OEM's own command center being located inside WTC 7 — and evacuated
  early, "before key fire ground decisions had to be made" — contributed to
  a loss of "robust interagency command and control" that day. **CONFIRMED,
  exact phrasing, NCSTAR 1A p. 60.**

**Precision note — a widely repeated secondary narrative does NOT match
NIST's own NCSTAR 1A wording; report both, don't blend them:**
Many popular retellings (fire-service trade press, retrospective interviews)
describe firefighters spotting a visible bulge in WTC 7's southwest corner
around 2:00 p.m., and FDNY Chief of Department **Daniel Nigro** — a living
person, retired FDNY Chief of Department and later FDNY Commissioner —
personally making the call to establish a collapse zone around 3:30 p.m., in
a quote widely attributed to him via Fire Engineering Magazine and later
retrospective interviews: **"The biggest decision we had to make on the
first day was to clear the area and create a collapse zone around the
severely damaged 7 World Trade Center, a 47-story building heavily involved
in fire."** This quote and the "bulge"/3:30 p.m. detail do **NOT appear
anywhere in NIST's NCSTAR 1A text** (verified by direct full-text search of
the report — no hits for "bulge," "Nigro," or "3:30"). Label the Nigro quote
and the bulge detail **ALLEGED (by Nigro / FDNY retrospective accounts)**,
distinct from NIST's own primary-source timeline, which states only that
the order came "at approximately 2:30 p.m." with no named individual or
specific visual trigger cited. If the script wants a human decision-maker
attached to the moment, attribute the Nigro quote to him by name and to its
non-NIST source — do not present it as NIST's finding.

## 2. The President's day (9/11 Commission Report, Ch. 1 + Ch. 10)

**Sourcing note, important:** the Commission's **Chapter 1** ("We Have Some
Planes") covers only the morning — it ends around 10:30–11 a.m. with the
shootdown-order narrative (Section 3 below) and the reflection "This is a
new type of war" (p. 46), then pivots into pre-9/11 background for the rest
of the report. **The Barksdale/Offutt/return-to-Washington/8:30 p.m. address
narrative is in Chapter 10, "Wartime," pp. 325–326** — cite Chapter 10 for
that material, not Chapter 1.

- **8:55 a.m.:** Before entering the classroom, Bush spoke by phone to
  National Security Advisor Condoleezza Rice, who told him a twin-engine
  aircraft — then a commercial aircraft — had struck the WTC, adding "that's
  all we know right now, Mr. President." **CONFIRMED, direct quote**,
  Ch. 1 p. 35.
- **~8:55 a.m. (motorcade arrival):** Presidential motorcade arrived at Emma
  E. Booker Elementary School, Sarasota, FL, for a reading demonstration.
  Chief of Staff **Andrew Card** told the Commission he was standing with the
  President outside the classroom when Senior Advisor Karl Rove first told
  them a small twin-engine plane had hit the WTC; Bush's reaction was that it
  "must have been caused by pilot error." **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 1 p. 35.
- **9:05 a.m.:** With the President seated in the classroom, Andrew Card
  "whispered to him: 'A second plane hit the second tower. America is under
  attack.'" **CONFIRMED, verbatim quote, this is the primary/only Commission
  version — do not paraphrase or blend with any other circulating wording**,
  Ch. 1 p. 38.
- The President remained in the classroom **another five to seven minutes**
  while the children kept reading, then returned to a holding room "shortly
  before 9:15." **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 1 p. 38–39.
- **9:15–9:30 a.m.:** Staff arranged the return to Washington while Bush
  consulted advisers on remarks; "no one in the traveling party had any
  information during this time that other aircraft were hijacked or
  missing." He decided to make a brief statement from the school before
  leaving. **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 1 p. 39.
- **9:35 a.m.:** Motorcade departed the school; arrived at the airport
  between 9:42–9:45 a.m. Bush learned of the Pentagon strike en route.
  **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 1 p. 39.
- **~9:45 a.m., by phone from the plane:** Bush told Cheney, per contemporary
  notes: "Sounds like we have a minor war going on here, I heard about the
  Pentagon. We're at war... somebody's going to pay." **CONFIRMED, verbatim
  per notes of the call**, Ch. 1 p. 39.
- **~9:54 a.m.:** **Air Force One departed Sarasota "without any fixed
  destination"** — the Secret Service and Cheney were advising against a
  direct return to Washington; the President "only grudgingly agreed to go
  elsewhere." **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 1 p. 39–40.
- **~10:10 a.m.:** Air Force One changed course, heading west, objective "to
  find a safe location — not too far away — where the President could land
  and speak to the American people." **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 10 p. 325.
- **~10:20 a.m.:** The President's military aide identified **Barksdale Air
  Force Base**, Louisiana, as the interim destination. **CONFIRMED**,
  Ch. 10 p. 325.
- **The Commission's own note on false aircraft-threat reports (directly
  answers the user's brief):** "After the attacks had occurred, while crisis
  managers were still sorting out a number of unnerving false alarms, Air
  Force One flew to Barksdale Air Force Base... One of these alarms was of a
  reported threat against Air Force One itself, a threat eventually run down
  to a misunderstood communication in the hectic White House Situation Room
  that morning." **CONFIRMED, exact phrasing, Commission's own words**,
  Ch. 10 p. 325. (A second, separate false report that morning: NORAD's air
  threat conference was briefed at 9:44 a.m. on a possible hijacking of
  **Delta Flight 1989**, treated as "the fourth possible hijack" — Delta 1989
  was not hijacked and landed safely; Ch. 1 p. 41. A third: at ~10:30 a.m.
  the White House shelter received reports of an inbound aircraft "5 to 10
  miles out," prompting a second engage authorization — it "had been a
  medevac helicopter," Ch. 1 p. 41–42.)
- **~11:45 a.m.:** Air Force One landed at Barksdale. Bush taped (not live,
  for security reasons) a brief statement. **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 10 p. 325.
- **Offutt Air Force Base**, Nebraska, was chosen next "because of its
  elaborate command and control facilities" and overnight capacity for 50
  people — the Secret Service wanted a location Bush could stay at for
  several days if necessary. **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 10 p. 325.
- **2:50 p.m.:** Air Force One arrived at Offutt. **~3:15 p.m.:** Bush met
  principals via secure video teleconference; Rice recalled Bush opening
  with "We're at war," and CIA Director George Tenet said the agency was
  still assessing responsibility but "the early signs all pointed to al
  Qaeda." **CONFIRMED, verbatim quotes attributed**, Ch. 10 p. 326.
- Defense Secretary Rumsfeld directed the armed forces to **DEFCON 3**, an
  elevated readiness state. **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 10 p. 326.
- **Late afternoon:** Bush overruled continued staff reluctance and ordered
  Air Force One back to **Andrews Air Force Base**; he was then flown by
  helicopter to the White House, "passing over the still-smoldering
  Pentagon." **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 10 p. 326.
- **8:30 p.m.:** Bush addressed the nation from the White House (Oval
  Office). The Commission quotes: **"We will make no distinction between the
  terrorists who committed these acts and those who harbor them"**; the
  address quoted Psalm 23 ("though I walk through the valley of the shadow
  of death..."); and closed on "No American... will ever forget this day."
  **CONFIRMED, quotes as rendered in the Commission Report itself**, Ch. 10
  p. 326. (For the full, independently-verified verbatim transcript of the
  address, use the White House/NARA archived transcript rather than the
  Commission's own partial quotations if a longer excerpt is needed on
  screen — do not extend the Commission's partial quotes into a longer
  passage not actually quoted there.)
- Following the address, Bush met again with an expanded National Security
  Council including Transportation Secretary Norman Mineta and FEMA Director
  Joseph Allbaugh; Secretary of State Colin Powell (returned from Peru)
  joined. **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 10 p. 326.

## 3. The shootdown authorization — precise, Commission-sourced

**This is the single most misreported fact of the day. State it exactly as
the Commission states it — a documented, timed AUTHORIZATION chain that
never reached the pilots who mattered, not a story of one order given and
followed.**

- **~9:58–10:00 a.m.:** Cheney arrived in the White House shelter conference
  room. **CONFIRMED (Commission's own conclusion from conflicting evidence)**,
  Ch. 1 p. 40.
- **Sometime before 10:10–10:15 a.m.:** Cheney phoned Bush to discuss rules
  of engagement for the combat air patrol; Bush "signed off on the concept."
  The Commission found **no documentary evidence for this specific call** —
  it is reconstructed from participant recollections, and notes that even
  people in the room taking notes did not record it. **DISPUTED /
  reconstructed, flagged by the Commission itself as undocumented**, Ch. 1
  p. 40–41.
- **Between 10:10–10:18 a.m.:** A military aide told Cheney an inbound
  aircraft (later identified as having already crashed — this was United
  93) was 80, then 60, miles out. Cheney authorized fighters to **engage**
  it each time. **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 1 p. 41.
- **10:18 a.m.:** Cheney called Bush for a two-minute confirmation call
  (prompted by Deputy Chief of Staff Joshua Bolten, who wanted the President
  told the Vice President had executed the order). **10:20 a.m.:** aboard Air
  Force One, press secretary Ari Fleischer's contemporaneous notes record
  that Bush told him he had authorized a shootdown of aircraft if necessary.
  **CONFIRMED, exact times**, Ch. 1 p. 41.
- **~10:30–10:33 a.m.:** A further report of an inbound aircraft "5 to 10
  miles out" prompted Cheney to repeat the authorization to "take out" the
  aircraft; Deputy NSA Stephen Hadley relayed this to the air threat
  conference at 10:33: "The Vice President's guidance was we need to take
  them out." (This aircraft was the medevac helicopter noted above — no
  hostile aircraft was in fact engaged.) **CONFIRMED, exact quote**, Ch. 1
  p. 41–42.
- **Separately — when the order actually reached the military chain:**
  NORAD had **no knowledge of United 93's hijacking at all until after it had
  already crashed** (10:03 a.m.) — its air threat conference representative
  said at 10:07 a.m. NORAD had "no indication of a hijack heading to DC at
  this time." **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 1 p. 42.
- **10:31 a.m.:** Gen. Larry Arnold instructed staff to broadcast over
  NORAD's messaging system: **"Vice president has cleared to us to intercept
  tracks of interest and shoot them down if they do not respond."**
  **CONFIRMED, exact quote — this is the first point the shootdown
  authorization is documented reaching NORAD's operational chain at all**,
  Ch. 1 p. 42.
- **The Commission's precise, load-bearing finding on the gap — quote this
  directly on screen, do not paraphrase into "the order was given and
  followed":**
  > "In short, while leaders in Washington believed that the fighters above
  > them had been instructed to 'take out' hostile aircraft, **the only
  > orders actually conveyed to the pilots were to 'ID type and tail.'**"
  **CONFIRMED, exact quote, Ch. 1 p. 43.**
- The Commission separately states: **"The Vice President was mistaken in
  his belief that shootdown authorization had been passed to the pilots
  flying at NORAD's direction."** **CONFIRMED, exact quote**, Ch. 1 p. 44.
- **Why it broke down (named individuals, all attributed to their own
  Commission testimony, treat as living/identifiable people per house
  rule):** NEADS commander Col. Robert Marr told the Commission he did not
  pass along the order "because he was unaware of its ramifications."
  Mission commander Major Kevin Nasypany and senior weapons director Major
  Dawne Deskins ("Fox" callsign) said they did not pass the order to the
  Langley fighters circling Washington/New York "because they were unsure
  how the pilots would, or should, proceed with this guidance." **ALLEGED
  (self-reported testimony to the Commission) but CONFIRMED as the
  Commission's own accepted account of events**, Ch. 1 p. 43.
- **A separate, second set of fighters had different rules entirely:** The
  113th Wing, D.C. Air National Guard, launched out of Andrews AFB starting
  10:38 a.m. at the Secret Service's request, operating "weapons free" — the
  decision to shoot resting with the lead pilot in the cockpit — a
  permissive rule of engagement NORAD headquarters did not even know these
  aircraft were flying under. **CONFIRMED**, Ch. 1 p. 44.
- **Bottom line for the script, stated as the Commission states it:** By the
  time any version of a shootdown order reached operational fighters (NORAD
  chain: not until 10:31 a.m. at the earliest, per Gen. Arnold's broadcast;
  even then, NEADS controllers told pilots only to identify aircraft, never
  to engage), **United 93 had already crashed in Pennsylvania at 10:03 a.m.**
  — 28+ minutes before authorization is documented reaching NORAD's chain at
  all, and the engagement order itself never reached the fighter pilots in
  any usable form that morning. **CONFIRMED — this precise sequence, with
  precise times, is the Commission's own finding**, Ch. 1 pp. 41–45 ("What
  If?" section, p. 45, explicitly walks back NORAD's own public claim that it
  would have intercepted United 93, calling that claim's underlying premise
  "incorrect").

**EXCLUDE / do not use:** Any single-sentence popular-media version that
states flatly "Cheney ordered the shootdown and the military carried it
out" or "Bush authorized it and NORAD shot planes down" — both compress away
the Commission's central, load-bearing finding (the order never reached
operational pilots in any actionable form, and no aircraft was in fact shot
down by the military that day).

## 4. Airspace reopening

- **9:45 a.m., Sept 11:** FAA Command Center ordered the first-ever
  nationwide ground stop / closure of U.S. airspace to civilian traffic.
  **CONFIRMED** (widely corroborated in FAA/news retrospectives; the
  Commission Report itself, Ch. 10 p. 326, separately confirms "For the
  first time in history, all nonemergency civilian aircraft in the United
  States were grounded, stranding tens of thousands of passengers across the
  country").
- **12:16 p.m., Sept 11:** U.S. airspace reported clear of all commercial and
  private aircraft. **CONFIRMED** (FAA/news retrospective sourcing — could
  not independently verify against a single live FAA primary-source page in
  this research pass; treat as well-corroborated secondary fact, not
  Commission-sourced).
- **Thursday, Sept 13, 2001:** U.S. airspace reopened for limited commercial
  service, under substantially heightened security screening (e.g., box
  cutters and other previously permitted items newly banned); resumption was
  phased — stranded aircraft repositioned first, then limited scheduled
  service resumed. **CONFIRMED** (FAA order + contemporaneous news coverage,
  e.g. CNN Sept. 13, 2001).
- **Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA)** remained closed
  separately, on capital-security grounds, until **October 4, 2001**, when
  it reopened in phases with extraordinary added security measures.
  **CONFIRMED** (contemporaneous White House/press-office statements,
  archived at georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov).

## 5. The first casualty figures — belief vs. announcement vs. final toll

**Never guess a number here — every figure below is dated and sourced
individually; do not average or round across sources.**

- **Day of / Sept 12, 2001 — chaotic, wildly overstated estimates:** Mayor
  Rudy Giuliani, asked to estimate WTC deaths, said publicly: **"The number
  of casualties will be more than any of us can bear, ultimately."**
  **CONFIRMED, quote as reported contemporaneously** (multiple contemporary
  news accounts). Separately, city officials that week requested **6,000
  body bags**, and news estimates in the first 24–48 hours ranged as high as
  **10,000–15,000** feared dead — these were preliminary, panic-window
  estimates, not a confirmed count. **UNVERIFIED as to a single precise
  figure** — cite as "early estimates ranged into the thousands and were
  publicly acknowledged as rough," not a specific number, unless a specific
  quote is being shown on screen with its own date/attribution.
- **Late September 2001 (~2 weeks out):** NYPD/city officials cited a total
  of approximately **3,702 dead and missing** tied to the World Trade
  Center — already a steep downward revision from the first days'
  estimates. **CONFIRMED as a reported figure at that date, attributed to
  NYPD** (contemporaneous news reporting).
- **First anniversary, Sept 11, 2002:** The official toll of those killed in
  New York (including the passengers/crew of the two aircraft that struck
  the towers) stood at **2,801**. **CONFIRMED as the reported one-year
  figure.**
- **Revised shortly after:** to **2,792**, after several names were found to
  be erroneous or duplicated. **CONFIRMED.**
- **Late 2003:** Revised again, down to **2,752**, after **40 more names**
  were determined to be either inaccurate or fraudulent (some falsely
  claimed by relatives for benefits purposes — a fact pattern reported at the
  time; do not elaborate into unverified specifics of any individual case).
  **CONFIRMED as the reported 2003 revision figure.**
- **Current, standing WTC-specific figure (NYC Office of Chief Medical
  Examiner):** **2,753** victims — the 2003 figure of 2,752 plus 4 later
  additions (3 people who died of documented site-related illness, and one
  missing person added by court order). **CONFIRMED, OCME's own current
  figure** (per OCME public statements and 911families.org resource page,
  reflecting OCME's own published count).
- **Identification status, as of the most recent reporting available (Aug
  2026):** Of the 2,753 WTC victims, roughly **60% (about 1,654 individuals)**
  have been identified via ongoing DNA analysis; the remainder — roughly
  40% — remain unidentified. The OCME has stated it will continue testing
  recovered remains as DNA technology improves. **CONFIRMED, most-recent
  reported status; treat the exact percentage/count as of-this-reporting-
  date, not fixed** — new identifications continue to be announced (e.g., a
  new identification was announced via the NYC Mayor's Office in Aug 2026;
  do not present the 2,753/1,654 figures as final or unchanging on screen —
  say "as of [date]").
- **Total combined toll across all three attack sites (WTC + Pentagon +
  Flight 93), not counting the 19 hijackers:** **2,977**. This is the
  figure most commonly cited as "the 9/11 death toll." **CONFIRMED, widely
  and consistently reported final combined figure.**
- **A separate, larger and still-growing number — 9/11-related illness
  deaths, NOT part of the day-of toll:** As of 2026 reporting, deaths among
  responders/survivors from illnesses (many cancers) linked to WTC-site
  exposure, tracked by the federally funded WTC Health Program, have
  **surpassed roughly 9,400** — a figure that now **exceeds the original
  2,977 attack-day toll by more than three times over**. The WTC Health
  Program was caring for **145,275** enrolled responders/survivors as of
  June 30, 2026, of whom **57,044** carry a certified 9/11-related cancer
  diagnosis. **CONFIRMED, most-recent reported figures** (CDC/WTC Health
  Program program statistics; Northwell Health WTC Health Program reporting,
  2026). **This number is explicitly separate from, and not merged into,**
  the 2,977/2,753 attack-day tolls above — state it as a distinct, ongoing,
  still-rising count from illness, not as a revision of the original death
  toll.

**Framing note for the script:** the toll is not one number — it is at
minimum four distinct, separately-dated, separately-sourced figures (the
chaotic early estimates; the 2,977 combined attack-day toll, fixed since
the early-2000s revisions; the 2,753 WTC-specific OCME figure, which still
grows slightly as remains are identified and added; and the 9,400+ and
rising illness-death toll, which is not part of the "died on 9/11" count at
all). State each with its own date and source rather than collapsing them
into a single "the death toll was X" line.

## 6. Continuity of government / other afternoon-evening material

- **DEFCON 3:** Secretary Rumsfeld ordered U.S. armed forces to Defense
  Condition 3. **CONFIRMED**, Commission Report Ch. 10, p. 326.
- **Continuity-of-government measures:** The Commission Report states
  plainly that "contingency plans for the continuity of government and the
  evacuation of leaders had been implemented" that day — the report does not,
  in the chapters read for this dimension, go further into COG specifics
  (e.g., the historic "shadow government" relocation of congressional
  leadership) with named individuals/times; if the script wants that level
  of COG detail, it needs a dedicated further research pass into Commission
  Report staff statements or other primary COG-specific material — **flag
  as A GAP, do not invent Commission-sourced specifics beyond the one
  sentence above.** **CONFIRMED (the one general sentence only)**, Ch. 10
  p. 326.
- **At 9:59 a.m. (per Ch. 1, already partly covered by the shootdown
  section above but relevant here):** An Air Force lieutenant colonel in the
  White House Military Office, on the air threat conference, relayed a
  request from Deputy NSA Hadley for **(1) continuity-of-government
  measures, (2) fighter escorts for Air Force One, (3) a combat air patrol
  over Washington, D.C.** — this is the specific moment COG measures were
  requested, per the Commission's own account. **CONFIRMED, exact three-item
  list**, Ch. 1 p. 40.
