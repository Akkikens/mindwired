## 1. AA77: boarding, departure, hijacking, turn, transponder loss, reacquisition, final approach, impact

Primary source: 9/11 Commission Report, Chapter 1, "The Hijacking of American 77" (pp. 8–9 of the chapter; footnotes 52–61). Text below is pulled directly from the Commission's own HTML publication of Chapter 1, not paraphrased.

**CONFIRMED** (9/11 Commission Report, Ch.1, fn. 52–61):

- AA77 was a Boeing 757, scheduled Washington Dulles → Los Angeles, scheduled departure **8:10**.
- Piloted by **Captain Charles F. Burlingame** and **First Officer David Charlebois**; four flight attendants; **58 passengers** on 9/11 (this figure includes the five hijackers, per the Commission's later passenger/crew accounting).
- **8:09** — pushback from the gate.
- **8:20** — takeoff from Dulles.
- **8:46** — reached assigned cruising altitude of 35,000 ft.
- **8:51** — last routine radio communication from AA77.
- Hijacking began **between 8:51 and 8:54**. Hijackers used knives (per one passenger) and box cutters (per another passenger); passengers (and possibly crew) were moved to the rear of the aircraft; a passenger reported a cockpit "announcement" that the plane had been hijacked.
- **8:54** — aircraft deviated from its assigned course, turning south.
- **8:56** — transponder turned off; primary radar contact also lost at this point. Indianapolis ARTCC and American Airlines dispatch both tried and failed to raise the aircraft.
- **9:00** — American Airlines EVP Gerard Arpey learns communication with AA77 is lost; grounds Northeast AA departures.
- Shortly before **9:10** — American HQ, suspecting AA77 hijacked, considers it may have been the second aircraft to hit the WTC; ground stop extended nationwide after learning United was also missing a plane.
- **9:12** — flight attendant Renee May calls her mother, reports the flight hijacked "by six individuals" who moved passengers to the rear (note: official 9/11 Commission finding is **five** hijackers on AA77 — see discrepancy note below).
- **Between 9:16 and 9:26** — Barbara Olson places two phone calls to her husband, Solicitor General Theodore Olson, reporting the hijacking, hijackers armed with knives and box cutters, and (in the second call) that the pilot had announced the hijacking over the intercom; the second call was cut off.
- **9:29** — autopilot disengaged; aircraft at 7,000 ft, ~38 miles west of the Pentagon.
- **9:32** — Dulles Terminal Radar Approach Control (TRACON) "observed a primary radar target tracking eastbound at a high rate of speed" — later determined to have been AA77. (This is the Commission Report's own account of the reacquisition; see note below on the "lost interval.")
- **9:34** — Reagan National advises Secret Service of an unknown aircraft headed toward the White House; AA77 is 5 miles west-southwest of the Pentagon and begins a 330-degree turn; at the end of the turn it is descending through 2,200 ft, pointed at the Pentagon and downtown Washington; throttles advanced to maximum power in a dive.
- **9:37:46** — AA77 crashes into the Pentagon, traveling at approximately 530 mph.
- "All on board, as well as many civilian and military personnel in the building, were killed." (Commission Report, Ch.1, direct language.)

**Source note on "reacquisition"**: Some secondary retellings online cite a specific "9:05" or "9:10" radar-reacquisition timestamp for AA77. I could not confirm any such timestamp in the 9/11 Commission Report's own narrative — the Report's account moves from the 8:56 transponder/primary-radar loss directly to the 9:29 autopilot-disengage position fix and the 9:32 Dulles TRACON primary-radar sighting. Any more granular "reacquired at X" claim not in that text is **UNVERIFIED** here and excluded rather than guessed.

**DISPUTED / worth flagging**: Renee May's call reported "six individuals" as hijackers; the Commission's official finding, based on manifest and video/investigative work, is that AA77 carried **five** hijackers — Hani Hanjour (trained pilot), Khalid al-Mihdhar, Majed Moqed, and brothers Nawaf and Salem al-Hazmi (Ch.1, describing boarding: Mihdhar/Moqed through security at 7:18, Hanjour at 7:35, the Hazmi brothers shortly after; boarding at 7:50–shortly after). This is a normal eyewitness-vs-official-record variance under stress, not a conspiracy claim — noted here only as a factual discrepancy between a passenger's live phone report and the Commission's post-investigation count.

**Quote handling (hard rule compliance)**: I did not find a single, stable "primary" wording of what Barbara Olson said to her husband — the Commission Report itself does not quote her verbatim; it paraphrases ("reported that the flight had been hijacked," "asked her husband what she should tell the captain to do"). Numerous media accounts (published in the years after) attribute more specific verbatim quotes to her, but I could not trace any of those verbatim wordings to a primary transcript or recording. **Per the hard rule, any verbatim Barbara Olson quote beyond the Commission's own paraphrase should be marked EXCLUDE** unless a specific primary transcript can be produced — I did not locate one in this pass.

## 2. The Pentagon structure, the Wedge 1 renovation, the collapse, the fire

Primary source: **Pentagon Building Performance Report** (ASCE, Jan. 2003), full text extracted directly from the NIST-hosted PDF (https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=860488).

**CONFIRMED** (ASCE Pentagon Building Performance Report, Executive Summary and §1.4/§1.5/§8):

- The Pentagon (6.6 million sq ft) was built September 1941–January 1943. A major renovation of the entire building began in 1999, phased in five "wedges," each centered on a building vertex; full program was scheduled for completion in 2010 (as of the report's writing).
- **"The renovation of Wedge 1 began in 1999 and was essentially complete at the time of the crash."** (ASCE report, §1.4, direct quote.)
- AA77 struck the Pentagon's first story. Per the ASCE report: **"At 9:38 a.m. on September 11 an airliner was flown into the first story of the Pentagon."** — note this is the ASCE report's own stated time, which differs by ~14 seconds from the Commission Report's 9:37:46; both are effectively the same moment and both are primary-sourced, but I flag the **minor discrepancy (9:37:46 vs. "9:38")** between the two primary documents rather than silently picking one.
- The impact occurred in the renovated portion of the building, approximately 140 ft south of the boundary with the next section still scheduled for renovation; the aircraft continued into the not-yet-renovated section.
- **Wedge 1 renovation features actually documented by the ASCE team**: new steel framing and reinforced concrete added where original capacity was insufficient; an **upgraded, blast-resistant window system** installed in the reinforced steel frames. Direct quote: *"The team observed that the upgraded window system was generally still in place within the reinforced frames... Windows that had not been upgraded generally were broken for several hundred feet to the north of the impact point."* And from the Executive Summary: *"Very few upgraded windows installed during the renovation broke during the impact and deflagration of aircraft fuel."*
- The ASCE report explicitly credits the renovation's structural measures — **"continuity, redundancy, and energy-absorbing capacity"** of the reinforced, spirally-wound concrete columns — with mitigating collapse and casualties, and recommends these features be adopted more broadly in construction where progressive-collapse resistance matters.
- **CONFIRMED, columns**: the direct impact "destroyed the load capacity of about 30 first-floor columns and significantly impaired that of about 20 others" along a damage swath ~75 ft wide by 230 ft long through the first floor; impact "may also have destroyed the load capacity of about six second-floor columns" near the exterior wall. (Total commonly summarized elsewhere as "~50 columns" — the ASCE Executive Summary itself says: *"the impact of the aircraft destroyed or significantly impaired approximately 50 structural columns."*)
- **CONFIRMED, collapse timing**: *"An area covering approximately 50 by 60 ft of the upper floors above the point of impact did collapse approximately 20 minutes after the impact."* Given impact at 9:37:46–9:38, this places the partial collapse at **roughly 9:57–9:58**, not 10:10. This matches the Commission Report's independently sourced figure below.
- The 9/11 Commission Report (Ch. 9, direct text) states: *"At 9:55, the incident commander ordered an evacuation of the Pentagon impact area because a partial collapse was imminent; it occurred at 9:57, and no first responder was injured."*
- **Correction to the brief**: the collapse is documented in two independent primary sources (ASCE report's own "~20 minutes after impact" figure, and the Commission's **9:57** figure sourced to the Arlington County AAR) as occurring at **approximately 9:57, not 10:10**. I could not find a primary source supporting a 10:10 collapse time; the closest documented "10:xx" event is the Commission's account of a **10:15** order for a *full evacuation of the command post* (a separate event — a precautionary evacuation prompted by an FBI warning of another possible inbound hijacked aircraft, not the structural collapse itself). **Flagging this as a correction rather than silently substituting a number**: treat 9:57 as the sourced collapse time; 10:15 is the sourced command-post evacuation time; there is no primary support located here for "10:10" specifically.
- **Fire**: ASCE report states the fire, fed by aircraft fuel, aircraft contents, and building contents, caused damage "throughout a very large area of the first story, a significant area of the second, a small part of the third, and only in the stairwells above." Approximately 2,200 kg of jet fuel is estimated to have been involved in the prompt fire (ASCE report, fuel-load analysis section). I did not locate a precise, sourced "fire burned for N hours" figure in the excerpts reviewed — **UNVERIFIED**, not stated as a round number here.
- **"Kevlar panels" claim**: Widely repeated in secondary reporting (e.g., trade press, retrospectives) that Kevlar fabric/cloth was installed between Wedge 1 windows to catch fragmentation. **I did not find this claim in the ASCE Pentagon Building Performance Report itself** — the primary engineering document discusses upgraded blast-resistant windows and reinforced steel framing but does not use the word "Kevlar" anywhere in the text extracted. Treat the Kevlar detail as **UNVERIFIED against the primary engineering source** — sourced only to secondary press accounts (e.g., National Defense Magazine, Sandboxx News), not confirmed here against ASCE/NIST or Commission primary text.

## 3. Casualties

**CONFIRMED, DoD/Commission-sourced total**: **184 people killed** (this is the figure used for the Pentagon Memorial's 184 illuminated benches, and matches DoD's account for the 2008 memorial dedication) — but note this 184 figure **excludes the five hijackers**, while the 9/11 Commission Report and the ASCE report both give figures that **include** the hijackers in the aircraft total. Reconciling the two counting conventions:

- 9/11 Commission Report, Ch. 9 (direct quote): *"All 64 people aboard the airliner were killed, as were 125 people inside the Pentagon (70 civilians and 55 military service members). One hundred six people were seriously injured and transported to area hospitals."*
- ASCE Pentagon Building Performance Report, Executive Summary (direct quote): *"One hundred eighty-nine persons were killed"* (64 aboard the aircraft + 125 in the building = 189 — this figure **includes** the 5 hijackers).
- Pentagon Memorial FAQ / DoD 2008 dedication figure: **184 total** (125 in the building + 59 non-hijacker victims on the plane) — this figure **excludes** the 5 hijackers, consistent with 64 aboard minus 5 hijackers = 59.

**So: 189 (Commission/ASCE, hijackers included) and 184 (DoD Memorial figure, hijackers excluded) are not in conflict — they are the same underlying death toll counted two different ways.** Both are CONFIRMED from primary/DoD-adjacent sources; state which convention is being used whenever the number appears on screen.

- Aboard AA77: 58 passengers + 6 crew (4 flight attendants + 2 pilots) = 64 total aboard, of whom 5 were hijackers → 59 non-hijacker deaths on the aircraft.
- In the Pentagon building: **125 deaths** — 70 civilians, 55 military service members (9/11 Commission Report, Ch. 9, direct figures).
- **106 people seriously injured** and transported to area hospitals (9/11 Commission Report, Ch. 9, direct figure).
- No first responders were killed or injured in the partial structural collapse itself (Commission Report, Ch. 9: "it occurred at 9:57, and no first responder was injured").

## 4. Rescue and response: Arlington County Fire Department, incident command, and the After-Action Report

Primary source: 9/11 Commission Report, Ch. 9, §9.3 "Emergency Response at the Pentagon" (text pulled directly from the Commission's own HTML page), which in turn quotes the Arlington County After-Action Report directly.

**CONFIRMED**:

- Because the incident was "a plane crash, fire, and partial building collapse," the **Arlington County Fire Department served as incident commander** — even though the Pentagon is a facility under the control of the Secretary of Defense, and the Department of Justice (with authority delegated to the FBI) was the lead federal agency for the terrorism investigation.
- A formalized **Incident Command System (ICS)** was already in place across the National Capital Region on 9/11, which the Commission Report credits as a key reason the response worked across jurisdictions.
- Responding agencies included: Arlington County fire, police, and sheriff's departments; the Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority; Reagan National Airport Fire Department; Fort Myer Fire Department; Virginia State Police; Virginia Dept. of Emergency Management; FBI; FEMA; a National Medical Response Team; the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms; and military personnel of the Military District of Washington.
- **Command was established at 9:41.** Simultaneously, Arlington County's Emergency Communications Center requested mutual aid from Fairfax County, Alexandria, and the District of Columbia fire departments.
- **9:55** — incident commander orders evacuation of the impact area, partial collapse imminent.
- **9:57** — partial collapse occurs; no first responder injured.
- **10:15** — incident commander orders a full evacuation of the command post itself, after an FBI warning of another possible approaching hijacked aircraft — the first of three such evacuations triggered that morning by reports of inbound aircraft.
- The Commission Report explicitly attributes the relative success of the Pentagon response to three factors, quoted directly: *"first, the strong professional relationships and trust established among emergency responders; second, the adoption of the Incident Command System; and third, the pursuit of a regional approach to response."* It also notes many responding agencies had recently trained together in preparation for the IMF/World Bank annual meetings scheduled later that same month in Washington.
- It also notes structural/situational factors that made the Pentagon response more tractable than New York's: a single incident, not 1,000 feet in the air, an easily secured site with no collateral damage beyond the building itself.

**On why the Arlington County After-Action Report is regarded as the best-documented response of the day**: The Commission Report cites the Arlington County AAR directly and by name as its source for the response's remaining problems, describing the AAR's own findings on **self-dispatching** and **communications breakdowns**. Direct quotes the Commission pulls from the AAR:

> "Organizations, response units, and individuals proceeding on their own initiative directly to an incident site, without the knowledge and permission of the host jurisdiction and the Incident Commander, complicate the exercise of command, increase the risks faced by bonafide responders, and exacerbate the challenge of accountability."

> "Almost all aspects of communications continue to be problematic, from initial notification to tactical operations. Cellular telephones were of little value.... Radio channels were initially oversaturated.... Pagers seemed to be the most reliable means of notification when available and used, but most firefighters are not issued pagers."

The AAR itself (per its Library of Congress/DOJ catalog description, and its own table of contents) was produced by Titan Systems Corporation for Arlington County under a Department of Justice/Office for Domestic Preparedness grant, and its project team **conducted 92 separate debriefing sessions and interviewed approximately 475 participants** — a scale of structured after-incident interviewing that is the specific, citable basis for its reputation as the most thoroughly documented emergency response record of 9/11. **CONFIRMED** (catalog/OJP description of the AAR's methodology) — though I was not able to fully text-extract the AAR PDF itself in this pass (see §0 above); the "92 debriefings / 475 interviews" figure comes from the document's own published abstract via the OJP/NCJRS library record, not from a full independent read, so treat it as CONFIRMED-by-abstract rather than CONFIRMED-by-direct-page-citation.

## 5. Engineering findings: what the ASCE Pentagon Building Performance Report actually concluded

Direct quotes from the Executive Summary of the ASCE report (text extracted directly from the NIST-hosted PDF):

> "Impact analyses revealed that the spirally reinforced columns could withstand substantial dynamic lateral loads and deflections. Static analyses indicated that the floor system was capable of significant load redistribution without collapse when several adjacent supporting columns were removed or severely damaged by an extreme action. Thermal analyses showed that the ensuing fire could have sufficiently weakened some damaged frame members to result in collapse within an hour of initiation."

> "The BPS team concluded that the impact of the aircraft destroyed or significantly impaired approximately 50 structural columns. The ensuing fire weakened a number of other structural elements. However, only a very small segment of the affected structure collapsed, approximately 20 minutes after impact. The collapse, fatalities, and damage were mitigated by the Pentagon's resilient structural system. Very few upgraded windows installed during the renovation broke during the impact and deflagration of aircraft fuel."

> "The BPS team recommends that the features of the Pentagon's design that contributed to its resiliency in the crash — that is, continuity, redundancy, and energy-absorbing capacity — be incorporated in the future into the designs of buildings and other structures in which resistance to progressive collapse is deemed important."

In the body of the report (§8, Findings), the team attributes the *limited* extent of collapse specifically to:
- redundant/alternative load paths in the beam-and-girder framing;
- short spans between columns;
- substantial continuity of bottom reinforcement through the supports;
- the original 1940s design load of 150 psf (warehouse-grade live load), well in excess of the actual service load, giving the structure reserve capacity;
- significant residual load capacity retained by damaged spirally-reinforced columns even after their concrete cover was destroyed;
- the ability of the exterior walls to act as transfer girders, redistributing load around missing columns.

The report's overall finding, in plain terms: the building did not "hold up because it's the Pentagon" in some generic sense — the ASCE team traces the outcome to specific, named structural design choices (continuity of reinforcement, redundancy, the columns' spiral reinforcement, and the fresh Wedge 1 blast-resistant window/framing upgrade), and recommends those specific features for wider adoption in progressive-collapse-resistant design.
