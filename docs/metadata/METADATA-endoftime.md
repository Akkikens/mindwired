## Description (English first, then Hindi)
Every star you have ever seen will die. This video does not stop until the
very last one is gone.

Travel from today to the final moment of the universe, with the speed of time
doubling every few seconds. Watch the sun swallow the Earth, the Milky Way
collide with Andromeda, the last star flicker out, and — 10^100 years from now
— the final black hole in existence evaporate into nothing. Then see why some
scientists think the story might begin again.

This is the far future as our best current science understands it — updated for
2026. Unlike older films, this journey reflects what we have learned since:
that the Andromeda collision is now only a coin-flip (Nature Astronomy, 2025),
that proton decay remains unproven, and that the DESI survey has reopened the
question of how the universe actually ends. The ending was rewritten while you
were alive.

Almost every image and clip in this video is real — genuine footage and
visualizations from NASA, the Hubble and James Webb space telescopes, the Solar
Dynamics Observatory, and NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio (public domain;
full credits below). No dramatization of the science; only the far-future shots
that no telescope could ever capture are cinematic recreations.

Subscribe to mindwired for a new deep-dive space documentary every week.
