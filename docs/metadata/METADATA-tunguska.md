## Description (English ONLY)
On June 30, 1908, at roughly 7:14 in the morning, the sky over Siberia split open.
A trader 65 kilometers away was knocked backward off his porch by the heat. The blast
flattened 80 million trees across 2,000 square kilometers — an area bigger than
Tokyo — with the energy of 10 to 15 megatons of TNT. It's still the largest impact
event in recorded history. And when the first scientific expedition finally reached
the site in 1927 — nineteen years later — they found no crater. No fragments. No
proof of what actually hit.

This is the real story, told through the actual expedition photographs of Leonid
Kulik, the Soviet scientist who spent decades trying to find physical proof of an
impact that, by every modern calculation, should have left one. We walk through the
real science — why an airburst several kilometers up explains the missing crater —
and the fringe theories that came after (a mini black hole, antimatter, even a crashed
spacecraft), each one clearly labeled as speculation and weighed against the evidence,
never presented as fact. We also connect it to the one modern event that gave
scientists a real, filmed analog: the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor.

Every fringe theory in this documentary is presented as exactly that — attributed,
never asserted — before the real science answers it. Real archival footage: the actual
1927-1930 Kulik expedition photographs (public domain), real 2013 Chelyabinsk
dashcam/CCTV footage, and real NASA planetary-defense mission footage.

If a 1946 science-fiction story was enough to convince people aliens were behind
Tunguska, wait until you hear the pilot who swore, on the record, that what he saw
was not from this world — watch "The Pilot Who Said 'It Was Not From This World'"
next on this channel.
