File: `The Astronaut Who Nearly Drowned in Space (Short).mp4` · Comp `ShortAlmostDied2`

### TITLE
He almost became the first person to drown… in space #space #astronaut #shorts

### DESCRIPTION
In 2013, astronaut Luca Parmitano nearly became the first human being to drown — in space.
44 minutes into a spacewalk outside the International Space Station, water from a clogged
cooling filter began pooling inside his sealed helmet. In zero gravity it had nowhere to
go: it crawled over his ears until his radio went muffled, then over his eyes. Then the
station crossed into orbital night and the sun went out. Blind, half-deaf, with water
creeping toward his nose, Parmitano found his way back to the airlock by feeling his safety
tether hand-over-hand. Nearly 1.5 litres of water came out of his helmet. It's the reason
every spacesuit that goes outside today carries an absorbent pad and a snorkel.

## SHORT 3 — "Armstrong Was Spinning to Death" (Gemini 8, 1966)
File: `Armstrong Was Spinning to Death 3 Years Before the Moon (Short).mp4` · Comp `ShortAlmostDied3`

### TITLE
3 years before the Moon, Neil Armstrong nearly died in orbit #space #nasa #shorts

### DESCRIPTION
Three years before he became the first person to walk on the Moon, Neil Armstrong was
spinning to death in orbit. In March 1966, Armstrong and David Scott pulled off the first
docking of two spacecraft in history — then, out of radio contact with the ground, their
Gemini capsule began to roll. A thruster was stuck firing. The little spacecraft spun up to
296 degrees per second — nearly a full rotation every second — and the astronauts' vision
began to blur and gray out at the edges. After the blur comes blackout; after blackout,
nobody flies the ship. Half-blind, Armstrong reached overhead and found the reentry-control
thrusters by feel — the switches you're never supposed to touch in orbit — and stopped the
spin. It worked. Three years later, he stepped onto the Moon.

File: `The First Spacewalker Couldnt Get Back Inside (Short).mp4` · Comp `ShortAlmostDied4`

### TITLE
The first man to walk in space couldn't get back inside #space #shorts #history

### DESCRIPTION
The first human ever to walk in space almost didn't survive it. In March 1965, cosmonaut
Alexei Leonov floated out of his Voskhod 2 capsule and made history with a 12-minute
spacewalk. Then he tried to come back in — and found his spacesuit had ballooned stiff in
the vacuum. His fingers pulled out of the gloves, his feet left the boots, and he could no
longer bend enough to fit through the airlock. Locked out, 200 km straight up, Leonov did
the only thing left: he opened a valve and bled his own suit pressure down — risking the
bends — until the suit was soft enough to bend, sweat sloshing to his knees. He squeezed in
head-first, against every rule. And that was only the beginning: the automatic reentry
failed, and the crew landed in a Siberian forest full of wolves, in −30°C, and had to ski
out to be rescued.
