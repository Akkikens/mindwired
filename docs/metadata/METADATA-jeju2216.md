## Description
On the 29th of December 2024, Jeju Air Flight 2216 belly-landed at Muan International
Airport in South Korea, slid off the end of runway 19 and hit a grassy embankment
carrying the runway's localiser aerials. Inside that mound was concrete. 179 of the
181 people on board died. The only survivors were two cabin crew in the tail.

This is not a film about pilot error. It is about a structure an international
standard said had to break on impact — and didn't. We go through the primary record
line by line: the ARAIB preliminary report (AAR2404), the FAA's own definitions of
"frangible" and the "runway safety area", the four minutes and seven seconds both
black boxes are missing, the interim finding that was withdrawn after the families
protested, Korea's Anti-Corruption and Civil Rights Commission ruling that the
embankment violated frangibility requirements, the state audit that found 14
non-compliant localiser installations at 8 airports certified for up to 22 years,
and the government-commissioned simulation that concluded everyone on board could
have survived.

The investigation is still open and the final report has not been published. Every
causal claim here is attributed to its source; contested findings are presented as
contested. When the final report lands, we will go through it here the same way.
