## DESCRIPTION (paste as one unit)

Something on the world's most famous corpse is still alive. In June 2026, researchers at Eurac Research announced they had cultured four species of cold-adapted yeast — alive and reproducing — from the skin, internal meltwater, and stomach contents of the Iceman, the 5,300-year-old mummy found melting out of an Alpine glacier in 1991. Three of the four can digest the very disinfectant sprayed on him to protect him. One of them multiplied inside his sealed sub-zero museum cell between 2010 and 2019. Nothing of the man himself survives — his own cells died on that ridge — but the ice sent him into the future with passengers.

And the man they live on is the world's oldest identified murder victim. This is the full case file: the German couple who found him on September 19, 1991 with one frame of film left in their camera; the pneumatic drill that tore into his hip; the border survey that moved him 92.56 meters into Italy; the flint arrowhead that ten years of science missed; the 2007 verdict that he bled out in minutes; the deep defensive cut on his hand from a fight days before he died; the Munich murder detective who profiled the killing in 2017; and the two papers — May and November 2025 — that overturned the answer. The new reconstruction of the wound says he did not die in minutes. He likely sat on that ice, wounded, for hours.

Every visual in this video is real: Wikimedia Commons archival photographs, the actual excavated artifacts, and open-access figures from the two 2025-2026 scientific papers (CC BY 4.0).
