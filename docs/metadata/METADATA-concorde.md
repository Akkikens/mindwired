## Description (English ONLY — mindwired)
For twenty-seven years, ordinary people could look up and see the future flying
overhead — a British-and-French supersonic airliner that crossed the Atlantic in
about three and a half hours, twice the speed of sound, at the edge of space.
Concorde was never meant to work: two governments signed a treaty in 1962 specifically
so the program couldn't be cancelled, and it survived a Soviet rival, a cancelled
American competitor, and a price tag many times its original estimate to become the
most glamorous object ever to fly. Then, on 25 July 2000, a strip of metal on a
runway outside Paris set off a chain of events that brought down Air France Flight
4590 in ninety seconds, killing 113 people. Concorde flew again — Kevlar-lined tanks,
burst-resistant tyres — but the world it returned to, after September 11, wasn't the
same one, and in 2003 the airlines grounded it forever.

This is the full story: the two-nation gamble that built it, the ogival delta wing
and afterburning engines that let it fly, the Concorde crash's real causal chain per
the official French investigation (shown on screen), the criminal case against a
Continental Airlines mechanic that was overturned on appeal, and the quiet economic
collapse — 9/11, an aging fleet, Airbus withdrawing support — that ended the
supersonic era. We are, to this day, the only civilization in history to have made
commercial travel measurably slower than it used to be.

A note on fairness: the 2000 crash was investigated by France's BEA and its findings
are presented as reported; the 2010 criminal conviction against Continental Airlines
and mechanic John Taylor was overturned on appeal in 2012, and this film states that
clearly — no criminal guilt was ultimately upheld. Cost, price, and record figures
are stated with the ranges and caveats the historical record actually supports.
Everything here is grounded in the official investigation record and reputable
reporting — real archival photography, no AI recreation of the real crash or the
people involved.
