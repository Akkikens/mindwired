## DESCRIPTION (paste as one unit)

On November 24, 1971, a calm man calling himself Dan Cooper hijacked Northwest Orient Flight 305, demanded $200,000 and four parachutes, and jumped from the plane's rear stairs somewhere over the forests of southwest Washington state. He was never seen again. Of every hijacking in American commercial aviation history, this is the only one that has never been solved.

This is the real case file: the note passed to a flight attendant, the jump into the night, the only piece of the ransom ever found — buried in a riverbank nine years later by an 8-year-old boy — and the seven men the FBI has investigated and cleared over five decades. On July 8, 2016, the Bureau made its position official: after tens of thousands of leads, it redirected its active investigation elsewhere. It has never reopened that finding. Any video telling you this case has been solved is telling you something the FBI itself has not confirmed.

Sources: FBI.gov official case page and FBI Vault FOIA releases, HistoryLink.org, Seattle Times archives, NPR, CNN, and multiple named-suspect retrospectives (Newsweek, KING5, Cowboy State Daily) — full citation list in the production notes.
