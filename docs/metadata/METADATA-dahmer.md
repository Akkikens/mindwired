## Description (English only)
```
In May 1991, three women found a 14-year-old boy in a Milwaukee street and called the police. Two officers arrived. The man with him said the boy was 19 and that this was a domestic argument. The officers released the boy back into his care. That man was Jeffrey Dahmer, and he was at that moment on probation for sexually assaulting the boy's 13-year-old brother three years earlier.

This film is not a retelling of the killings. It's the record of what happened afterward — read from a published Wisconsin Court of Appeals opinion, in the court's own words.

The chief discharged the officers. The Fire and Police Commission upheld it. A circuit court called the discipline unreasonable. The discharges were vacated, the officers were reinstated with back pay, and then nine more years of litigation followed — over whether the statutory phrase "60 days" meant 60 working days or 60 calendar days.

Nineteen pages of statutory construction, filed in the year 2000, about a night in 1991. Nowhere in them does anyone argue about whether handing that boy back was wrong.

Every document on screen is a real public court record. Where we could not obtain a clean copy of a filing — the family's federal civil suit — we say so on screen and show nothing in its place.
```
