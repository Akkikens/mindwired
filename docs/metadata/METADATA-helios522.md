## Description (English ONLY — Black Box is not a Hindi channel)
August 14, 2005. 1:25 in the morning, Larnaca, Cyprus. A Boeing 737 lands
from London, and the cabin crew log a problem before anyone even leaves the
aircraft: a door seal that freezes, and hard bangs during the flight. A
ground engineer runs a manual pressure check by hand, signs the aircraft
back into service at 3:15 AM — "nil defects, no leaks, no abnormal noises" —
and leaves for the night. He never resets one switch back to automatic.
Nobody notices. The plane is cleared to fly again in a few hours.

Six hours later, that same aircraft takes off for Athens carrying 121
people. Twelve minutes in, a warning horn sounds — the exact same tone as a
completely different, harmless warning that only ever matters on the ground.
The crew misreads it. By the time the aircraft levels off at 34,000 feet,
everyone aboard is losing consciousness to oxygen deprivation, and the
autopilot — programmed, patient, doing exactly what it was told — keeps
flying the aircraft on its own. For nearly three hours. Alone. Two F-16
fighter jets are scrambled just to look through the cockpit windows and
report back what they see. What they find becomes some of the most quoted
testimony in aviation history.

We rebuild this flight using the actual Greek accident investigation report,
the real cockpit voice recorder transcript, and the F-16 pilots' own on-record
radio descriptions — including the one flight attendant who happened to hold
a real commercial pilot's license, and made it into the cockpit before the
end. We also correct a detail almost every retelling of this crash gets
wrong: the famous "F-16 intercepting the airliner" photo used in nearly every
documentary about this crash is a CGI illustration, not a real photograph.

The investigation's technical findings and the criminal courts' verdicts are
two different things, decided years apart in two different countries, with
two very different outcomes — and the one man most documentaries blame by
name is the only person fully cleared on appeal. Living individuals named in
the criminal proceedings are referenced only via their own court records and
public statements, never invented quotes or assigned guilt beyond what an
actual court found.
