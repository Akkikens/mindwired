## Description (English ONLY — mindwired)
In 2012, a Dutch startup called Mars One promised something no government ever had:
a permanent human colony on Mars, funded by turning the whole thing into the biggest
reality show in history. It opened applications to the entire planet — and told the
world that more than 200,000 people had signed up for a one-way ticket, a trip with
no way home. Seven years later, it was declared bankrupt by a court in Basel,
Switzerland, with about a million euros in debt and, by one account, less than
$25,000 left in the bank. The world found out weeks later, from a post on Reddit.

This is the full story of how it happened — and why so many brilliant people believed
it. We follow the receipts: the founder who sold his wind-energy company to fund the
dream; the one-way logic that made Mars sound suddenly possible; the "200,000
applicants" that independent reporting traced back to roughly 2,700 completed
applications; the tiered application fee that turned hopefuls into paying customers;
finalist Dr. Joseph Roche, the astrophysicist who went on the record about a
points-and-donations leaderboard and a ten-minute Skype "interview"; and the 2014 MIT
engineering study that concluded the first colonist would die in about 68 days — not
from too little oxygen, but from too much. We show you the actual study pages on
screen. Then the collapse: the reality-TV deal that fell through without a single
frame shot, the reverse merger into a dormant Swiss shell, the launch dates that slid
from 2023 to 2031, and the quiet bankruptcy.

A note on fairness: Mars One's failure was resolved through bankruptcy, not a criminal
court. No fraud was ever proven and founder Bas Lansdorp was never charged; where this
film uses the word "scam," it is quoting named critics, and Lansdorp's on-record
denials are included throughout. Everything here is grounded in reputable reporting and
the primary MIT study — real NASA public-domain Mars imagery, no AI recreations of real
people or events.
