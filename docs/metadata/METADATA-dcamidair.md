## DESCRIPTION

On 29 January 2025, an American Eagle regional jet and a US Army Black Hawk
collided over the Potomac River on approach to Washington National. Sixty-seven
people died — the deadliest air disaster in the United States since 2001.

This episode is built entirely from the investigation's own evidence file: the
real DCA tower recordings, the Black Hawk's cockpit voice recorder transcript,
the real STARS radar replay showing the conflict alert naming both aircraft, and
NTSB Aviation Investigation Report AIR-26-02, released January 2026.

The controller told the helicopter to pass behind the CRJ. The crew never heard
it — their own microphone keyed for eight tenths of a second and cut the
instruction out of the air. But that is not why this happened. The route they
were flying had no charted width. The screen the controller watched rounds every
altitude to the nearest hundred feet. The altimeter in that helicopter reads low
by design. And in the three years before the crash, an airliner and a helicopter
came dangerously close at this one airport 15,214 times.

Nobody had ever added them up.

## PINNED COMMENT

The instruction was "pass behind the CRJ." The words "pass behind the" never
arrived — the helicopter keyed its own mic for 0.8 seconds and cut them out.
But the NTSB found that even that wasn't the root cause. In the three years
before this crash there were 15,214 close-proximity events between airliners
and helicopters at this one airport, 85 of them inside 1,500 feet laterally and
200 feet vertically. Every one was recorded. Nobody was counting.

## HONESTY / SENSITIVITY NOTES FOR THE UPLOAD
- The NTSB named the helicopter crew's ineffective visual separation as **one**
  cause among several. The episode states this (chapter 8) in the report's own
  order and does not reduce the accident to crew error — nor does it omit the
  finding. Do not let any Short or comment reply drift either way.
- Active civil litigation is ongoing; the "wholly avoidable tragedy" phrasing
  belongs to family filings and is not stated as the channel's conclusion.
- The helicopter's ADS-B Out was off **legally**, under a standing DoD
  exemption, and the NTSB found it did not contribute. Correct anyone who
  claims otherwise in the comments.
