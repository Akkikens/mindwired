## Description (paste as ONE unit)

---

On January 28, 1986, Space Shuttle Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after liftoff. Seven people died, including a schoolteacher chosen from 11,000 applicants to be the first ordinary citizen in space. Six hours before launch, a Morton Thiokol engineer named Bob Ebeling told his daughter "the Challenger's going to blow up" and spent the night before trying to stop it. He was right. The warning he and his colleagues gave — in writing, six months earlier, and again the night before — never reached the one NASA official whose job it was to give the final go.

Inside: the real July 1985 memo warning of "a catastrophe of the highest order," the actual January 27th teleconference where a cold-weather launch recommendation was reversed after Thiokol management was told to "take off your engineering hat," the 8°F joint-temperature reading nobody scrubbed the mission over, Richard Feynman's live-television ice-water demonstration to the Rogers Commission, the "normalization of deviance" pattern that let the danger look routine until it wasn't, why no one was ever criminally charged, and what changed at NASA because of it — including a 32-month grounding and a redesigned joint that a later NASA administrator pointed back to directly when a different agency crew was stranded in orbit decades later.

Every claim in this video traces to the 1986 Rogers Commission Report and named, on-the-record testimony — Roger Boisjoly's and Allan McDonald's own words to the commission, NASA's own medical findings on the crew, and family statements from NASA's own 2026 Day of Remembrance coverage. The cause is the Rogers Commission's settled, uncontested finding. This video does not dramatize the crew's final two minutes and forty-five seconds beyond what NASA's own investigators stated on the record, and does not platform the "scream tape" hoax or any crew-survived conspiracy theory — both are addressed directly and dismissed.
