## Description (English ONLY)
On July 2, 1937, a US Coast Guard radio operator aboard USS Itasca is copying down
Amelia Earhart's transmissions, word for word, onto a government log sheet. Her voice
gets more strained with every call. Then — nothing. That log sheet still exists. It's
a real US National Archives record. And it still doesn't tell us what happened.

This is the full story of the world's most famous aviation disappearance, told through
the actual documents: the real Itasca radio log (National Archives Catalog ID
6210268), the massive US Navy/Coast Guard search that followed and found nothing, and
the decades of forensic work that came after — including thirteen bone fragments
found on a remote Pacific atoll, examined once in 1940 and re-examined by modern
forensic anthropologists decades later. We separate what the primary documents
actually confirm from what's theory, myth, and — in at least one widely-repeated
case — outright media fabrication.

Every spoken radio exchange in this documentary is clearly labeled a RECREATION (no
audio recording survives from 1937 — only the written log); every claim about
Nikumaroro artifacts or theories is attributed to the researchers who made it, never
asserted as settled fact. Real archival footage: British Movietone and Pathé Gazette
1930s newsreels (public domain), Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery photographs,
and the actual NARA radio-log exhibit pages.
