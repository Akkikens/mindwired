## Description (English only)
An astronaut is floating outside the International Space Station when his
helmet starts filling with water. Not a little — enough to cover his eyes,
then his ears, then reach his nose and mouth. He can't see. He can't hear
his crewmate or mission control. He has to find his way back to the airlock
from memory alone. This isn't a movie. It's Luca Parmitano, July 16, 2013,
and it's the first of twelve real, documented moments when a real astronaut
or cosmonaut came the closest they ever have to not making it home.

We built this from the primary sources, not secondhand retellings: mission
transcripts, NASA investigation boards, published memoirs, and direct
interviews. Neil Armstrong and David Scott's spacecraft spinning toward a
full rotation per second on Gemini 8. Gene Cernan's near-disastrous
spacewalk, which he titled "The Spacewalk From Hell" himself. Fred Haise —
the last living member of the Apollo 13 crew — on the cold that nearly
finished what the explosion started. A live, fully energized solar array
that could have electrocuted Scott Parazynski or ignited the oxygen in his
suit. A Soviet cosmonaut's reentry gone so wrong the story was suppressed
for over twenty years, with no photograph or footage of it ever taken —
the one moment in this video we had to illustrate, honestly labeled as a
reconstruction, because nothing else exists.

Every quote is real and attributed. Every technical detail is checked
against the primary record — including a few corrections most retellings of
these stories get wrong. Living astronauts and cosmonauts are quoted only
from their own on-record words, never invented.
