## Description

The deadliest plane crash in aviation history killed 583 people — and neither
aircraft ever left the ground. On March 27, 1977, two Boeing 747s met in fog
on a runway they were never supposed to share, on an island neither was
supposed to visit, because of a bomb that killed no one. This is the complete
story of the Tenerife airport disaster — and every myth about it, tested.

The KLM captain whose face ran in the airline's own punctuality ads. The
criminally enforceable duty clock that made him refuel — 55,500 litres that
became the fire. The Pan Am jet that flew the first 747 flight in history and
was once inspected by Fidel Castro on a Havana runway. The route clearance
that wasn't a takeoff clearance. The two radio calls that collided into 3.74
seconds of electronic squeal — erasing the warning that would have saved 583
lives. The flight engineer's last question, and the two words that ended the
conversation. The 61 survivors who all saved themselves. And the myths: the
football match (unproven), the "missed exit" (false), the rogue captain
(wrong — and why the truth is scarier).

All radio and cockpit voice segments in this video are clearly labeled
recreations based on the official CVR/ATC transcripts — no genuine Tenerife
audio has ever been publicly released. Archival photographs: Dutch Nationaal
Archief (CC0), Wikimedia Commons contributors, FAA/NASA (public domain).
