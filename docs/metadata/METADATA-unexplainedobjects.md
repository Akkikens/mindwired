## Description (English)
In 1977, an unpaid volunteer reviewing a stack of computer printouts circled
six characters in red pen and wrote one word in the margin: "Wow!" Whatever
his radio telescope heard that night has never been detected again — not
once, across more than fifty years and over a hundred dedicated attempts to
find it.

That's not the strangest thing on this list. There's a star that appeared to
be building something, and a visitor from outside our solar system that broke
rules we thought applied to every rock in the sky. All three are real. All
three were measured by real telescopes, published in real peer-reviewed
papers, and are still being argued about by real scientists today.

We rank all three from the one that's mostly been figured out, to the one
that hasn't been figured out at all: the true story of KIC 8462852 ("Tabby's
Star") and why the "alien megastructure" headlines were wrong about what the
actual scientists claimed; 'Oumuamua, the first confirmed interstellar object
ever observed, and the real, measured anomaly that still doesn't have an
agreed explanation; and the Wow! signal itself — the strongest unexplained
signal SETI has ever recorded, using the actual 1977 printout as our primary
source.

None of this is proof of aliens. Every one of these, when scientists actually
checked, pointed toward something natural, something genuinely strange, or
something we simply don't have an answer for yet — which, honestly, is the
more interesting finding. Every claim here is sourced to the actual
peer-reviewed papers and the scientists' own on-record statements, not
secondhand retellings — several of which get key details backwards.
