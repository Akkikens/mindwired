March 8, 2023. Nine years to the day after MH370 vanished, a streaming
service drops three documentary episodes at once — each built around one
theory, one on-camera champion. Episode one: the pilot did it. Episode two:
Russian hijackers did. Both theories were argued, on camera, by the same man.
Neither has held up. By 2026, even he doesn't fully believe them anymore —
and the documentary's own director has said, on the record, that she thinks
the plane is sitting in the one place her own series barely shows.

This is our third MH370 investigation, and it is not a rehash. Our first
video covered the disappearance minute by minute; our second ran every
theory — hypoxia, fire, hijacking, the pilot, shot-down, Diego Garcia, the
fringe file — against the evidence. This one does something neither did: it
fact-checks Netflix's "MH370: The Plane That Disappeared" episode by episode

math — the actual burst-frequency-offset analysis, the descent-rate

in full.

Every theory here is attributed to the person who actually argues it, tested
against Malaysia's 2018 ICAO Annex 13 safety investigation report and Ian
Holland's 2017 technical analysis of the final satellite handshakes — both
used as on-screen exhibits, not paraphrased secondhand. Captain Zaharie Ahmad
Shah: the official investigation's background check found nothing supporting
