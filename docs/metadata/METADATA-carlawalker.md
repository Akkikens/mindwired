## DESCRIPTION (paste as one unit)

In March 1974, three weeks after 17-year-old Carla Walker was abducted and murdered in Fort Worth, Texas, detectives sat down across from a 27-year-old man named Glen Samuel McCurley. They had a real reason to suspect him — a gun magazine dropped at the scene matched one he owned. He gave them an explanation, agreed to a polygraph test, and passed it. Detectives crossed his name off the list. The case went cold.

It would stay cold for 46 years and 7 months. In 2019, Fort Worth police sent decades-old DNA evidence to Othram, a Texas forensic genetic genealogy lab, which built a family tree from the degraded sample and traced it back to McCurley's own family. In September 2020, at 77 years old, he was arrested and charged with capital murder. On the third day of his 2021 trial, he pleaded guilty. He died in prison in July 2023.

This is the real record of that case — the interview, the polygraph, the DNA match, and the plea — built entirely from court filings, appellate records, and contemporaneous news coverage. Sources: CBS News Texas, NBC 5 DFW, WFAA, Dallas Morning News, Fox 4, KWTX, Othram Inc., and the Second Court of Appeals opinion in McCurley v. State, No. 02-21-00122-CR.
