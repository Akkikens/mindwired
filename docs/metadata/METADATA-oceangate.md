## Description (English ONLY)
At 9:31 AM on June 18, 2023, a submersible the size of a minivan slipped
beneath the North Atlantic carrying five people toward the Titanic. An hour and
forty-five minutes later, it stopped checking in. Four days later, the world
learned it had imploded — most likely within hours of losing contact, while a
secret U.S. Navy listening system already knew and the search went on anyway.

This is not a story about bad luck. For five years before the implosion,
engineers, employees, and the deep-sea industry itself warned OceanGate's CEO
Stockton Rush, in writing, that his carbon-fiber submersible could kill
someone. His own Director of Marine Operations was fired weeks after filing a
report warning the hull had never been properly tested. More than twenty of
the world's leading submersible experts signed a letter calling his approach
potentially catastrophic. He called every one of them a "baseless cry."

We tell the full story with the real receipts: the whistleblower's actual
court filing, the real 2018 warning letter quoted from the U.S. Coast Guard's
own investigation, and the Coast Guard and NTSB's final reports — released in
2025 — which concluded the disaster was preventable, and that OceanGate's own
"toxic workplace culture" was a primary cause. No dramatization for the exhibit
documents — every page shown is a real government or court record.

the edge of what's possible, and what it costs the people who trust them.
