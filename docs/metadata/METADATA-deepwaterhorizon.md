## Description

April 20th, 2010. An hour before the Deepwater Horizon oil rig explodes, senior toolpusher Jason Anderson calls a colleague to say the well just passed its final pressure test. A good test, he says. It wasn't. Less than an hour later, two explosions kill eleven men and start the largest oil spill in American history.

Two years later, BP stood in a federal courtroom and pleaded guilty. Eleven counts of manslaughter — one for every man who died — plus obstructing Congress and violating clean water law. Four billion dollars in criminal penalties. Then, in 2016, a separate 20.8 billion dollar civil settlement, the largest ever paid by a single company in U.S. history.

This is the real story, built from the actual BOEMRE/Coast Guard joint investigation report: the cheaper well design, the six centralizers used instead of the recommended twenty one, the cement test that got canceled to save time, the "who cares, it's done" email, and the eleven names most people never hear. Sixteen years later, BP is still cutting checks — the last scheduled restoration payments don't land until the 2030s.

Primary source: "Report Regarding the Causes of the April 20, 2010 Macondo Well Blowout," Bureau of Ocean Energy Management, Regulation and Enforcement, September 14, 2011.
