## Description

December 24th, 1999. An Indian Airlines flight from Kathmandu to Delhi is hijacked forty minutes after takeoff. It will spend the next seven days on the ground — in Amritsar, Lahore, Dubai, and finally Kandahar, Afghanistan, under Taliban control — before India makes a decision it is still paying for twenty-five years later.

To end it, the Indian government released three imprisoned terrorists, including a man named Masood Azhar. Weeks later, Azhar founded Jaish-e-Mohammed — a terror group later linked to the 2001 Indian Parliament attack, the 2016 Pathankot attack, and the 2019 Pulwama attack. He is still alive today.

This is the real minute-by-minute reconstruction: the loophole that let a hijacker board with a gun, the passenger murdered in the first hours, the bluff that talked their way into Pakistan, the seven-day standoff at Kandahar, the decision India made to bring 176 hostages home — and the 2024 Netflix series that reopened the argument over what that decision actually cost.

Primary source: Statement by the Minister of External Affairs to the Lok Sabha on the hijacking of IC-814, 13 March 2000 (Indian Parliament record).
