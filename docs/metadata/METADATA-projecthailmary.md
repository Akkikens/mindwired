## Description (English only)
A single sunspot the size of twelve Earths once crossed the face of our sun and
visibly dimmed it. A decade later, one of the best-selling science fiction
novels in years — and now a major 2026 film — imagined something far stranger
doing that on purpose: a living microorganism that feeds on stars and starves
them of light.

This is the real science behind Project Hail Mary. We take the book's three
biggest scientific claims — a microbe that can dim a star, a ship that reaches
94% of the speed of light, and an alien built from a different chemistry than
our own — and check every one of them against real astrobiology, real physics,
and the novel's own author, Andy Weir, who has spoken publicly and extensively
about exactly where his research ends and his one deliberate act of fiction
begins.

Sourced entirely from real science journalism (Scientific American, The New
York Times, Northeastern University, Inverse, Physics World), published
physics and astrobiology research, and Andy Weir's own interviews — never from
the novel's text, and with zero footage from the film itself.
