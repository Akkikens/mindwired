## Description (paste as ONE unit)

---

On September 2, 1998, Swissair Flight 111 was 53 minutes from Geneva when the crew smelled smoke. Seventeen minutes later, all 229 people aboard were dead — including one of the world's leading Aids researchers, on his way to a conference meant to save lives. Investigators spent four and a half years and $57 million taking apart 2 million pieces of wreckage to answer one question: how does a fire nobody could see bring down a jet that experts called "the Flying Bank"? The answer rewired how every airliner on Earth is wired today — and this month, the first dramatized feature film about that night has its world premiere in the same city where the wreckage came ashore.

Inside: the real TSB Canada investigation (Aviation Investigation Report A98H0003) and its actual finding — arcing wiring for a newly installed in-flight entertainment system ignited flammable insulation blanket material used throughout the cabin — the real 17-minute cascade from first odour to loss of control, the real ATC recordings from Moncton and Halifax Terminal, the in-flight gambling system almost nobody remembers was on board, the fuel-dump myth that persisted for years after investigators disproved it, the arc fault circuit breaker technology that could have stopped this and TWA Flight 800 both — and still isn't required on most aircraft flying today.

Every claim in this video traces to the public record: TSB Canada's Aviation Investigation Report A98H0003, TSB Safety Recommendations A99-01 through A03-08, U.S. Navy recovery records, and on-the-record statements from named sources including Halifax Terminal air traffic controller Bill Pickrell. The cause of the fire is TSB's settled, uncontested finding; no theory in this video contradicts it. The dramatized film referenced is a real, confirmed festival premiere — not a general theatrical release — and no claim here goes beyond what its own director has said on the record.
