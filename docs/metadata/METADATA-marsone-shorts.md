## SHORT 1 — "200,000 → 2,782" (the number)
File: `200,000 Signed Up to Die on Mars (Short).mp4` · Comp `ShortMarsOne1`

### TITLE
200,000 people signed up to die on Mars. Almost none actually did. #mars #space #shorts

### DESCRIPTION
Mars One told the world that more than 200,000 people had applied for a one-way ticket to
Mars — a trip with no way home. It became one of the most repeated numbers in modern space
history. There was just one problem: it wasn't true. Those 200,000 were mostly people who
left an email address on a website. A real application meant paying a fee and submitting a
video — and by NBC News' count, only about 2,782 people actually did. The most famous number
in the whole story was off by roughly 99%. The mission never left the ground, and in 2019
the company quietly went bankrupt.

## SHORT 2 — "68 Days" (the MIT study)
File: `MIT Said the First Colonist Dies in 68 Days (Short).mp4` · Comp `ShortMarsOne2`

### TITLE
MIT said the first Mars One colonist would die in 68 days #mars #space #shorts

### DESCRIPTION
In 2014, a team of MIT engineers ran Mars One's own colony plan through a life-support
simulation — and concluded the first colonist would die in about 68 days. The twist: not
from running out of oxygen, but from too much of it. To grow food, the crew would farm crops
inside the sealed habitat, and those plants would flood the cabin with oxygen until it became
a fire hazard. With no technology in the plan to remove the excess, the only fix drains the
nitrogen, the pressure collapses, and the crew suffocates — on schedule. MIT also found the
mission would need far more rockets and money than Mars One claimed. The founder's response?
He called the study "a confirmation" of his plan.

## SHORT 3 — "Bankrupt for the price of a car"
File: `Bankrupt for Less Than the Price of a Car (Short).mp4` · Comp `ShortMarsOne3`

### TITLE
This Mars colony went bankrupt for less than the price of a car #mars #space #shorts

### DESCRIPTION
Mars One promised to put humans on Mars for around $6 billion, funded by turning the mission
into the biggest reality TV show ever made. Then the TV deal collapsed without a single frame
filmed, the launch dates slid from 2023 all the way to 2031, and the company reverse-merged
into a dormant Swiss shell to look like a real business. In January 2019, a court in Basel
declared it bankrupt — with debts of about a million euros and, by one account, less than
$25,000 left in the bank. A project that once claimed 200,000 applicants ended for less than
the price of a used car. And almost nobody noticed: the world found out weeks later, from a
post on Reddit.

## SHORT 4 — "The whistleblower" (Roche) — attributed + founder's denial
File: `A Finalist Blew the Whistle on Mars One (Short).mp4` · Comp `ShortMarsOne4`

### TITLE
A finalist chosen to die on Mars blew the whistle #mars #space #shorts

### DESCRIPTION
Joseph Roche is an astrophysicist at Trinity College Dublin — and one of the 100 finalists
Mars One picked for its one-way mission. Then he went public. By his account, his entire
vetting to leave Earth forever was a questionnaire, a video, and a single 10-minute Skype
call. He also alleged that candidates climbed Mars One's public leaderboard by buying
merchandise and donating money — so the front-runners looked like the biggest donors, not
the best qualified. Mars One's founder, Bas Lansdorp, flatly denied it, calling the claim
"simply a lie." So it remains an allegation — but the venture collapsed into bankruptcy in
2019, and no one ever flew.
