## Description
Seven days after the towers fell, the head of the Environmental Protection Agency
said the air over Lower Manhattan was safe to breathe.

Two years later, the agency's own Inspector General published a 165-page report
showing the sampling behind that sentence had not been finished when it was said —
and printed, in its own Table 2-4, the draft of an earlier press release beside the
version that actually went out. A warning about workers returning to Water Street was
deleted and never replaced. Asbestos measured at two to three times the trigger level
was published as "slightly above" it. And one instruction in the margin was not a
sentence at all. It was a stage direction.

This month, after a court found the city's handling of public records requests
arbitrary and capricious, New York opened a portal with the first 170,000 pages of
its own long-sealed files on the same subject.

This is a story about what officials said in the days after — what their own watchdog
later found they did not know when they said it, and what the people who believed
them are still paying. It is not a story about how the towers fell.

Everything here traces to EPA's own published investigation and to real public-domain
FEMA footage. Sources are named on screen as they appear.
