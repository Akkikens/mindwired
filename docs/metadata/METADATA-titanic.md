## Description (English ONLY)
Two governments investigated the Titanic disaster in 1912. They read the same
testimony, looked at the same night, and reached different verdicts — and on at
least one point, they still don't agree with each other, even today.

This isn't another shipwreck-forensics breakdown of how the hull failed. This is
the actual British Wreck Commissioner's Inquiry and the actual US Senate Inquiry —
two real, public, still-readable government documents — used as the evidence. We
show the real report pages on screen: the British verdict that Captain Smith
wasn't negligent (he was following the accepted practice of the era) but that the
practice itself should be abandoned; the harsher American verdict that his
"indifference to danger" directly contributed to the disaster; and the still-
disputed finding that a nearby ship, the Californian, might have reached the
lifeboats in time and didn't.

Along the way: the real regulatory flaw that mattered more than any iceberg —
outdated lifeboat law that Titanic legally exceeded and still wasn't enough; the
real ice warning left on a wireless-room table that never reached the bridge; the
real rescue by the Carpathia; and the direct regulatory legacy — the first
international treaty on safety at sea, created because of this ship.

Everything here traces to the two 1912 inquiry reports (both public domain,
linked in the pinned comment) plus real archival photography and footage — no
shipwreck CGI, no dramatization of the collision itself (nobody photographed it;
the one beat that needs a reconstruction is clearly labeled as one, on screen).
No living survivors exist to be affected by this account (the last, Millvina Dean,
died in 2009) — the one still-live historical dispute, over the Californian, is
presented as "the inquiry found X, researchers still argue Y," never asserted
either way as settled fact.
