This exact ship had 45 minutes left afloat — and almost everyone aboard had no idea. On the night of January 13, 2012, Captain Francesco Schettino sailed the Costa Concordia within 150 meters of a charted rock off the Italian island of Giglio, as an unofficial salute to his own former mentor. An officer tried to turn the ship away. Schettino overrode him. Minutes later, the hull tore open across 53 meters and five watertight compartments.

This is the real story behind the world's most famous cruise ship disaster — and the part Netflix's brand-new documentary, "Shipwrecked: Nightmare at Sea" (July 2026), left out entirely.

It took Italian authorities 65 minutes to order the ship abandoned. It took almost 7 hours to finish the evacuation. Thirty-two people died. One of them, Indian crew member Russel Rebello, wasn't found until November 3, 2014 — almost three years later, during the ship's dismantling in Genoa. Netflix's new retelling, built from real black-box audio and new survivor interviews, doesn't mention his name once.

We also feature the real, famous radio exchange between Schettino and Italian Coast Guard Commander Gregorio De Falco — the actual recorded call, ordering Schettino back onto his own sinking ship. Captain Schettino was convicted on 32 counts of manslaughter and sentenced to 16 years. As of this year, he remains incarcerated in Rome's Rebibbia Prison, with a scheduled release around 2033. Five other officers took plea deals and never served a single day. The disaster also triggered the largest maritime salvage operation in history by weight, and real, lasting changes to international cruise-ship safety rules.
