## Description (English ONLY — mindwired)
In 1980, a broke, recently divorced salesman named Dennis Hope looked up at the Moon and
decided he owned it. His argument: the 1967 Outer Space Treaty says nations can't claim
celestial bodies — but it never says the word "individual." So he filed a declaration of
ownership, mailed notice to the United Nations, the U.S. and the USSR, and waited for
someone to say no. Nobody ever did. Forty-five years later, Dennis Hope says he has sold
more than 611 million acres of the Moon — about 6-7% of its entire surface — to more than
six million customers in over 180 countries, for around twenty dollars an acre.

This is the full story: the real 1967 treaty text (shown on screen), what space-law
scholars actually say about his "loophole" (most reject it — one called it invalidated
outright; a minority genuinely disagrees), the one time this business actually went to
court (Beijing, 2005 — and lost), the popular myth about a US court ruling that never
happened, who really buys a deed to the Moon (mostly gifts, not investments), and the
honest, unresolved question at the center of it all: is Dennis Hope a sincere believer,
or a showman who's been selling the same piece of paper for 45 years?

A note on fairness: no court has ever convicted Dennis Hope of fraud, and he has never
been criminally charged. Where this film uses words like "scam," it is quoting named
critics — not asserting a fact. Everything here is grounded in the real Outer Space
Treaty text, on-record legal scholars, and reputable reporting. No AI recreations of
real people or events — real NASA public-domain Moon imagery throughout.
