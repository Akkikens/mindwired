## Description
```
Before any human being flew to space, we sent animals up first — to find out if it would kill us. For many of them, it did. This is the complete story of every animal we launched into the sky, in order, from the very first passengers to the two tiny creatures that beat every human to the Moon.

THE ANIMALS IN THIS DOCUMENTARY:

• FRUIT FLIES (1947) — the first animals in space, launched on a captured V-2 rocket and recovered alive.
• ALBERT II (1949) — the first mammal in space; his parachute failed and he was killed on impact.
• LAIKA (1957) — first to orbit Earth, first to die in space; a Moscow stray on a one-way flight.

• ABLE & BAKER (1959) — the first American animals brought home alive; Able died days later on an operating table, Baker lived another 25 years.

• FÉLICETTE (1963) — the only cat ever flown to space; she survived the flight, then was euthanized for study.
• THE ZOND 5 TORTOISES (1968) — two tortoises who became the first Earthlings to circle the Moon, three months before Apollo 8.
```

```
Every space program sent dogs or monkeys. Only one country ever sent a cat — and almost no one remembers her. In October 1963, France chose a former Paris street cat from fourteen candidates, implanted electrodes in her skull to read her brain, and launched her past 150 kilometers on a rocket. For decades she was forgotten, even misremembered as a male cat named Félix. Only recently did she finally get a statue.
```
