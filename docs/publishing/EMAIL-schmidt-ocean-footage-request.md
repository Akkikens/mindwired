**To:** imagerequests@schmidtocean.org

**Why this is needed:** SOI releases the 2025 colossal squid footage under

**Expected turnaround:** ~4-6 weeks.

**Subject:** Permission request — 9 March 2025 colossal squid footage, educational documentary

Hello,

I'm writing to request permission to use Schmidt Ocean Institute footage in an
educational documentary, under terms outside CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

**The footage:** the first confirmed observation of a live colossal squid
(*Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni*) in its natural habitat — the ~30 cm juvenile filmed by
ROV SuBastian from R/V *Falkor (too)* at 600 m near the South Sandwich Islands on
9 March 2025, during the Ocean Census GoSouth expedition, announced 15 April 2025.

**The use:** a 35-minute documentary titled "Nobody Has Ever Seen a Full-Grown
Colossal Squid," on my YouTube channel Mindwired (youtube.com/@mindwiredd), which
makes long-form science and ocean documentaries. The film covers the species from
Robson's 1925 description through the Te Papa specimens to the 2025 sighting and the
still-open search for an adult. I would use a short excerpt — under 60 seconds — in
the section covering the 2025 observation.

**Why I'm asking rather than relying on the CC licence:** the channel is monetised
through the YouTube Partner Programme, which I understand to be commercial use and
therefore outside the NC term. I would rather ask than assume.

**Attribution:** on-screen credit to Schmidt Ocean Institute at the point of use, plus
a written credit in the video description. I'm happy to use any specific wording you
require, and to name the Ocean Census / GoSouth partners alongside it.

**How the footage is treated:** the episode is built entirely from public-domain and
commercially-licensed archival material (NOAA Ocean Exploration, Ifremer under CC BY
4.0, Wikimedia Commons, and public-domain 1920s South Georgia whaling photographs).
The 2025 observation is presented accurately — as a confirmed sighting independently
verified by Dr Kat Bolstad and Dr Aaron Evans on the diagnostic mid-arm hooks, and
explicitly not as peer-reviewed published research. The film also states plainly that
the animal filmed was a juvenile and that no adult has ever been observed alive.

As it stands the episode describes the 2025 sighting without showing it, and uses NOAA
public-domain footage of a related glass squid as a stand-in, which I disclose. Your
permission would let the film show the actual moment it is about.

If a licence fee or a formal agreement applies, I'm glad to discuss it. And if there's
a better contact for this, I'd be grateful for a pointer.

Thank you for making this work public — the expedition and the footage are the reason
this episode exists.
