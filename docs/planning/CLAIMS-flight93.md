## 1. NEUTRAL SUMMARY

On the morning of September 11, 2001, United Airlines Flight 93, a Boeing 757 flying from Newark to San Francisco, was hijacked by four al-Qaeda operatives roughly 46 minutes after takeoff, after a routine ground delay had already pushed its departure back more than 40 minutes. Because the hijacking came later than on the other three planes hijacked that morning, and because passengers and crew were able to learn — through 37 phone calls made from the cabin — that hijacked aircraft had already struck the World Trade Center, a group of passengers and crew members decided, and voted, to fight back rather than wait for the hijacking to run its course as past hijackings had. At 9:57 a.m. they stormed the cockpit. The aircraft crashed in a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, at 10:03:11 a.m., about 20 minutes' flying time from Washington, D.C. All 44 people aboard — 33 passengers, 7 crew members, and 4 hijackers — died; no one on the ground was harmed. The 9/11 Commission concluded the hijackers were most likely aiming for the U.S. Capitol or the White House, though it could not determine with certainty which, since the passengers' intervention ended the flight before it came close enough to either. Flight 93 is remembered as the one hijacked aircraft on September 11 whose passengers, acting on their own information and their own decision, prevented the hijackers from reaching their target.

## 3. THE HIJACKERS (per FBI / 9-11 Commission only)

- **Ziad Jarrah** — pilot-hijacker, seat 1B (nearest the cockpit). Born in Lebanon to a secular, comparatively well-off family; studied aircraft engineering in Hamburg, Germany, where he was radicalized within the Hamburg cell around Mohamed Atta, Marwan al-Shehhi, and Ramzi bin al-Shibh. Traveled to Afghanistan in late 1999, met Osama bin Laden around January 2000. Arrived in Florida in June 2000 and trained at Florida Flight Training Center (Venice, FL) and Huffman Aviation, earning a private pilot certificate and progressing to multi-engine/simulator training, plus physical conditioning. The Commission documents friction with Atta over Jarrah's continued contact with his German girlfriend and family — al-Qaeda leadership worried about his commitment. CONFIRMED.
- **Ahmed al-Haznawi, Ahmed al-Nami, Saeed al-Ghamdi** — the three "muscle" hijackers (seats 6B, 3C, 3D), all Saudi nationals recruited via local mosque networks in 1999–2000, trained at al-Qaeda camps in Afghanistan (firearms, explosives, topography), and — per the Commission's pattern for all 19 hijackers — recorded "martyrdom videos" before the operation. CONFIRMED. *(Note: an earlier draft of this research characterized their home provinces as "generally poorer regions" of Saudi Arabia; that economic characterization is not directly Commission-sourced and has been removed. Keep only the documented facts: province of origin, recruitment channel, training dates and location.)*
- **Roles:** Jarrah was designated to fly the aircraft once the cockpit was seized; the other three were tasked with subduing crew and passengers. CONFIRMED (Commission Ch. 1 & 7).
- **Why only four hijackers, not five like the other flights:** the Commission's leading explanation is that a fifth intended hijacker, Mohamed al-Qahtani, was denied entry to the U.S. by a suspicious immigration inspector at Orlando International Airport on August 4, 2001 — he had $2,800 cash, a one-way ticket, and no return plans. CONFIRMED.
- **Visa/entry pattern:** all three muscle hijackers entered the U.S. on B-1/B-2 tourist visas obtained at Saudi consulates in 2000–2001 (part of the broader pattern in which 19 hijackers obtained 22 of 23 visa applications, with thin consular vetting). They entered in pairs alongside hijackers assigned to *other* flights, not as an isolated Flight 93 group: al-Nami arrived May 28, 2001; al-Haznawi June 8, 2001; al-Ghamdi June 27, 2001. CONFIRMED.
- **Pre-9/11 screening gap:** al-Haznawi was the only one of the four flagged by CAPPS (Computer Assisted Passenger Prescreening System); at the time this meant only his *checked baggage* was held for extra screening (explosives, not weapons), and it was cleared once he didn't board with it — a documented security-process gap, not evidence of foreknowledge. CONFIRMED.
- **Pilot-training claim about al-Ghamdi:** UNVERIFIED / EXCLUDE. A minority claim in one secondary summary speculates al-Ghamdi may have had pilot training; this is **not** a Commission or FBI finding. The Commission's finding is unambiguous: Jarrah alone flew the aircraft. Do not repeat the al-Ghamdi speculation on screen, even to debunk it, unless a scriptwriter specifically needs a line refuting it.
- **Attribution:** This was an al-Qaeda operation per the Commission's finding — state this factually and cite the Commission. No editorializing about religion or nationality beyond these documented, individually-attributed facts.

## 4. THE PASSENGER AND CREW REVOLT

Handle this section with the same care as a family would want extended to their own last words. Every quote below is resolved to **one single sourced version** — the ethics review flagged that the raw research contained two to three conflicting wordings for the Burnett and Lyles quotes; those conflicts are resolved here and must not be re-blended.

**The mechanism:** At least 10 passengers and 2 crew members shared information via phone calls; according to the Commission Report's own text, "at least five" calls described the intent of passengers and surviving crew to revolt, and "according to one call, they voted on whether to rush the terrorists in an attempt to retake the plane. They decided, and acted." (Commission Report, Ch. 1, p. 13.) CONFIRMED. The Commission's own narrative text does **not** name individual passengers — it refers to "callers" and "passengers." The named quotes below come from FBI interviews of the people who received the calls (cited in the Report's endnotes), from those family members' own subsequent public accounts, and — in Sandra Bradshaw's case — from the one verbatim quote the Report's own body text includes.

**No firearm, likely no real bomb:** although one caller thought the hijackers might have a gun, no other caller reported seeing one, no firearm or firearm remains were recovered at the crash site, and the CVR gives no indication a gun was ever fired or mentioned — supporting the conclusion the "bomb" was a bluff. This is why boiling water and a physical rush were the passengers' actual options. CONFIRMED (Commission Ch. 1, p.13 & endnotes 82–83).

**Individual accounts (attribution is part of the fact, not decoration — read the "verbatim" wording only from Section 13):**

At his request they recited the Lord's Prayer together. The call was never recorded and no verified transcript exists. Per Jefferson's own account (given in FBI interviews and repeated in her later public statements/book), his last words, turning from the phone to the passengers beside him, were the "Let's roll" line quoted in Section 13. CONFIRMED, but **attribute to Jefferson's recollection specifically — never present as a recording or as CVR content.**
- **Tom Burnett** (row 24) — called wife **Deena Burnett** four times between roughly 9:30–9:54 a.m. She told him planes had hit the World Trade Center. In an earlier call he reported a passenger had been stabbed and had died; in a later call he told her what's quoted in Section 13. CONFIRMED per Deena Burnett's own account (her book *Fighting Back*, her FBI interview, and 20+ years of consistent public retelling) — this is the one canonical wording to use; do not blend it with other renderings found in early drafts of this research.
- **Mark Bingham** (row 25) — placed two connected calls (9:36 and 9:37 a.m.) to his aunt's home in California; his mother, **Alice Hoagland**, reached the phone during the second. His words to each are quoted separately in Section 13 — do not merge them into one invented sentence. CONFIRMED (FBI interview notes, NPS Flight 93 National Memorial).
- **Jeremy Glick** (row 27) — told wife **Lyzbeth Glick** that male passengers were discussing "rushing" the hijackers and that a vote was coming; he asked if that was okay with her, and whether the hijackers had guns (he believed only knives). CONFIRMED (Commission endnote 83; Lyzbeth Glick's own public interviews over 20+ years).
- **Sandra "Sandy" Bradshaw** (flight attendant, row 33) — placed two separate calls: (1) ~9:35 a.m., ~5 min 53 sec, to United's San Francisco maintenance facility, reporting hijackers with a knife in the cabin/cockpit, a claimed bomb, and an attack on flight attendant Deborah Welsh (who later died of her injuries); (2) ~9:49–9:50 a.m., ~7 min 50 sec, to husband **Phil Bradshaw**, in which she described boiling water in the galley to throw at the hijackers. The line **"Everyone's running to first class. I've got to go. Bye"** is the *one verbatim quote in the Commission Report's own main narrative text* — sourced in the Report's endnote 85 to Phil Bradshaw's account of this second call. CONFIRMED. **Do not attribute this line to CeeCee Lyles** — some secondary retellings online conflate the two flight attendants; the Commission's own footnote is unambiguous that it is Sandy Bradshaw's line.
- **CeeCee Lyles** (flight attendant, row 32) — called husband **Lorne Lyles**, a police officer, twice. First call (~9:47 a.m.) reached his answering machine; the canonical voicemail wording (per Lorne Lyles's account, Jere Longman's *Among the Heroes*, and the National September 11 Memorial & Museum) is quoted in full in Section 13. Second call (~9:58 a.m., minutes before impact) reached Lorne live; per his account they prayed together and she told him "We're getting ready to do it now." CONFIRMED — this is the canonical version; the shorter/differently-worded voicemail rendering in earlier drafts should not be used.
- **Others (broader "the whole cabin acted" beat — one line of acknowledgment is enough, do not build individual segments for all of these unless time allows):** Lauren Grandcolas (calm voicemail to husband Jack, ~9:39 a.m.); Linda Gronlund (message to her sister, ~9:46 a.m.); Marion Britton (call to a friend describing stabbed passengers, ~9:49 a.m.); Honor Elizabeth Wainio (call to stepmother Esther Heymann, ending with her having to go because the cockpit was being breached, ~9:53 a.m.); Edward Felt (911 call to a Westmoreland County, PA dispatcher from an onboard lavatory, ~9:58 a.m. — one of the more independently corroborated calls, being a government emergency-line log rather than family memory). CONFIRMED for existence/substance of the calls; see Section 13 for exact usable wording and Section 11 for the one disputed embellishment (Felt's alleged "explosion" detail).

**The vote and the assault:** passengers decided together and acted; the assault began at 9:57 a.m. CONFIRMED.

## 5. THE CVR TRANSCRIPT — what's public vs. not

**What is public:** a written transcript of the final portion of the cockpit voice recorder, prepared by the NTSB/FBI, was entered as a trial exhibit and read/played once, publicly, in federal court during the sentencing trial of Zacarias Moussaoui on April 12, 2006 (having also been played privately for victims' families beforehand). The 9/11 Commission Report's own Chapter 1 also quotes portions of the CVR directly, noting "the quotes are taken from our listening to the CVR, aided by an Arabic speaker."

**What is NOT public:** the actual audio recording has never been released for public broadcast or download. Its only public playing was the single 2006 courtroom session; it remains under court restriction. **The script must state on screen, explicitly, that only a transcript is being read/dramatized — never imply real cockpit audio is being played.**

**Usable, Commission/trial-sourced lines (CONFIRMED):**
- 9:57 a.m. — passenger assault begins.
- ~10:00:08 — Jarrah: "Is that it? Shall we finish it off?"
- ~10:00 — a hijacker (likely al-Ghamdi) replies: "No. Not yet. When they all come, we finish it off."
- ~10:00:26 — a passenger: "In the cockpit. If we don't we'll die!"
- ~10:00:42 — a passenger: "Roll it!"
- 10:01:00 — a hijacker: "Allah is the greatest! Allah is the greatest!" (repeated)
- 10:02:23 — a hijacker: "Pull it down! Pull it down!"
- Recording ends ~10:03:09–11 as the aircraft rolls and dives into the field.

**Too graphic / must NOT be scripted or dramatized, even though documented:**

## 6. THE NORAD/FAA RESPONSE

*(Research gap closed 2026-07-25 — verified directly against primary sources: the 9/11 Commission Report, Ch. 1, pp. 34 and 41–46; the Commission's Staff Statement No. 17, "Improvising a Homeland Defense" (June 17, 2004); and the Commission staff's own fuller 2004 investigative reconstruction, declassified by the National Archives and published with senior counsel John Farmer's annotations as "A New Type of War" (Rutgers Law Review, 2011). This supersedes the prior placeholder in full.)*

**Framing, up front:** everything below is the official investigation's own finding about its own investigation — a real, well-documented case of an institution giving an inaccurate public account of its response time, which the Commission's staff (using subpoenaed tapes and radar data) caught and corrected. It is NOT evidence of, and must never be scripted as implying, that anyone allowed the attacks to happen or manipulated the response to enable them. The Commission's own language for this is "the government's version of events" being wrong, not fraud-to-enable-attack. Keep this sharply distinct from the fringe shootdown theory in Section 8, which this section's facts help refute.

**The protocols going in, and the two morning scrambles before Flight 93 was even hijacked:**
- Pre-9/11 FAA/NORAD hijack protocol assumed a traditional hijacking (a discreet fighter escort, five miles behind, monitoring — not an intercept), multiple levels of Pentagon-level authorization, and time to work the chain of command. None of those assumptions held on 9/11. CONFIRMED (Commission Report Ch. 1; Staff Statement 17).
- **Otis Air National Guard Base, Cape Cod, MA** — two F-15s of the **102nd Fighter Wing / 101st Fighter Squadron, Massachusetts Air National Guard** (pilots Lt. Col. Timothy Duffy and Maj. Daniel Nash) were scrambled at 8:46 a.m. in response to American 11 (the first hijacking, reported to NEADS at 8:37:52 — nine minutes before it hit the North Tower, the most warning the military got of any of the four hijackings that morning). They were vectored to military airspace off Long Island, then to a Combat Air Patrol over Manhattan by 9:25 — they had no role in the response to Flight 93. CONFIRMED (Staff Statement 17; 9/11 Commission Report Ch. 1).

**The Langley scramble — exact unit, exact times, and what it was actually responding to (this is the Commission's single biggest "the public story was wrong" finding, and it is Flight-93-adjacent, not Flight-93-specific):**
- Three F-16s of the **119th Fighter Wing, North Dakota Air National Guard ("Happy Hooligans")** — a unit on alert-detachment duty at Langley Air Force Base, VA, not the host 1st Fighter Wing — were scrambled. Pilots: Capt. Dean Eckmann (lead), Maj. Brad Derrig, Capt. Craig Borgstrom (a third jet was added beyond the normal two-jet alert pair, armed with guns only, no missiles). CONFIRMED (Rutgers Law Review monograph, citing a Langley 119th Fighter Wing briefing, Oct. 6–7, 2003; multiple independent news accounts of the pilots by name).
- **Exact times:** NEADS Mission Crew Commander verbally ordered the scramble at 9:23 ("scramble Langley. Head them towards the Washington area"); the order was transmitted to Langley at 9:24; radar data show the fighters airborne at 9:30. CONFIRMED, verbatim from the Commission Report, Ch. 1, and Staff Statement 17.
- **What they were actually scrambled for — and this is the key finding:** NOT American 77, and NOT United 93. NEADS scrambled Langley because of a mistaken 9:21–9:24 report that American 11 — which had already crashed into the North Tower 38 minutes earlier — was "still airborne" and heading toward Washington, D.C. A NEADS log entry at 9:24 recording "American Airlines #N334AA hijacked" is American 11's tail number, not American 77's. This is documented across NEADS tapes, FAA-center tapes, and chat logs at NEADS, Continental Region HQ, and NORAD headquarters. CONFIRMED, verbatim from the Commission Report Ch. 1 (p. 34) and the Commission staff's 2011-declassified monograph.
- **Where they actually flew:** due to a miscommunication (a "generic" 60-mile due-east flight plan meant only to clear local airspace quickly, which the lead pilot and a local FAA controller mistook for superseding guidance), the Langley fighters headed east out over the Atlantic rather than north toward Baltimore/Washington as intended. They were redirected toward Washington only after a 9:36 report of an unidentified aircraft six miles from the White House; NEADS picked up a fading primary radar track over Washington at 9:38 — one minute after American 77 had already struck the Pentagon at 9:37:46, with the Langley fighters still roughly 150 miles away. CONFIRMED, verbatim.
- **Result for Flight 93 specifically:** the Langley F-16s did eventually reach a combat air patrol over Washington, D.C. — and were the only armed fighters anywhere near the capital during the window (roughly 10:13–10:23) when Flight 93 could theoretically have arrived had it not crashed. But (see below) they were never briefed on the actual threat, never had Flight 93's location, and never had shoot-down authority in time. CONFIRMED.

**Could any fighter have reached Flight 93? The Commission's own conclusion — quoted directly, not paraphrased:**
- NEADS first heard about United 93 at 10:07 a.m. — **four minutes after it had already crashed at 10:03:11.** The notice came from a Cleveland Center military liaison passing the plane's *last known* latitude/longitude; NEADS "was never able to locate United 93 on radar because it was already in the ground." Formal notification that it had crashed followed at 10:15. CONFIRMED, verbatim, Commission Report Ch. 1 / Staff Statement 17.
- The Commission's blunt summary line: **"Nor did the military have 47 minutes to respond to United 93, as would be implied by the account that it received notice of the flight's hijacking at 9:16. By the time the military learned about the flight, it had crashed."** (9/11 Commission Report, Ch. 1, p. 34 — the "9:16" figure referenced here is itself part of the discrepancy finding below.) CONFIRMED, verbatim.
- The Report's dedicated "**What If?**" section (Ch. 1, pp. 44–46) explicitly reopens the question — because NORAD's own prior claim to have been tracking and ready to intercept Flight 93 turned out to rest on the false 9:16/9:24 notification timeline — and concludes: *"NORAD officials have maintained consistently that had the passengers not caused United 93 to crash, the military would have prevented it from reaching Washington, D.C. That conclusion is based on a version of events that we now know is incorrect… Had it not crashed in Pennsylvania at 10:03, we estimate that United 93 could not have reached Washington any earlier than 10:13, and probably would have arrived before 10:23. There was only one set of fighters circling Washington during that time frame — the Langley F-16s… At that point in time, the Langley pilots did not know the threat they were facing, did not know where United 93 was located, and did not have shootdown authorization… Shootdown authority was first communicated to NEADS at 10:31 [i.e., 28 minutes after the actual crash]… NORAD officials have maintained that they would have intercepted and shot down United 93. We are not so sure."* CONFIRMED, verbatim, this is the Commission's own hedged-but-clear conclusion — script it as: in the real timeline, no intercept could have or did occur (NEADS didn't even know the plane existed until after it crashed); in the Commission's own best-case hypothetical replay, it judges intercept merely "unlikely," never certain. Do not upgrade "unlikely" (their word, for the hypothetical only) to "impossible" — the actual-historical-fact claim ("no fighter did or could reach it, because none knew where it was or had authority") is the one that is fully certain; the counterfactual hedge is the Commission's own, and should be represented as a hedge, not overstated.

**Vice President Cheney and the shootdown order — full chronology, and why it could not have affected Flight 93:**
- Cheney was evacuated to the White House shelter tunnel at 9:37 a.m. (after American 77 hit the Pentagon at 9:37:46) and reached the underground shelter conference room "shortly before 10:00, perhaps at 9:58" (the Commission's own best estimate — there is no documentary record of exactly when). CONFIRMED per Commission Report Ch. 1.
- The Vice President recalls calling the President shortly after arriving to discuss rules of engagement for the combat air patrol going up over Washington; both men recalled the President "signed off" on authorizing a shootdown if a hijacked plane would not divert. **The Commission found no documentary evidence of this specific call** — it relies on the recollections of the Vice President, the President, and Condoleezza Rice (who says she heard the Vice President's side of it) — and estimates it took place sometime before 10:10–10:15 a.m. CONFIRMED as an attributed recollection, not a documented record — script accordingly ("according to the Vice President's and President's own recollections," not as a hard fact with a timestamp).
- At 10:02 a.m. — one minute *before* Flight 93 actually crashed — the shelter began receiving Secret Service reports of an inbound aircraft presumed hijacked and heading toward Washington. This was Flight 93, but the Secret Service was tracking a *projected* path on a display, not an actual radar return, and did not yet know the plane was already down. Between roughly 10:10 and 10:18, a military aide twice reported the (already-crashed) aircraft as "80 miles out" and then "60 miles out"; Cheney authorized fighters to engage each time — reacting, per his chief of staff Scooter Libby, "in about the time it takes a batter to decide to swing." Cheney called the President at 10:18 for a two-minute confirmation call; presidential press secretary Ari Fleischer's contemporaneous notes record that at 10:20 the President said he had authorized a shootdown "if necessary." CONFIRMED, verbatim, Commission Report Ch. 1, pp. 41–42.
- **The order did not reach any fighter pilot until long after Flight 93 had already crashed.** NORAD Commanding General Larry Arnold broadcast the shoot-down clearance over a NORAD chat/instant-messaging log at **10:31 a.m. — 28 minutes after the 10:03:11 crash.** Even then, the NEADS commander did not pass the order to the Langley pilots "because he was unaware of its ramifications"; the only instruction the Langley pilots ever actually received was to "ID, type, and tail" unknown aircraft — never to engage. The Commission's own line: **"The Vice President was mistaken in his belief that shootdown authorization had been passed to the pilots flying at NORAD's direction."** CONFIRMED, verbatim, Commission Report Ch. 1, pp. 43–44.
- **Bottom line for the script:** every step of the Cheney shootdown-authorization chain — his conversations with the President, his two engagement authorizations, the confirmation call, and the order finally reaching NEADS at 10:31 — happened *after* Flight 93 had already crashed at 10:03:11. The order could not have affected Flight 93 even if it had been transmitted instantly and flawlessly, because the target it was meant to cover no longer existed in the air by the time the authorization process began in earnest. CONFIRMED.

**A second, separate track: the Andrews Air Force Base fighters — and the one specific false claim in this whole story (verifying the existing Section 8 detail):**
- A wholly separate set of fighters — F-16s of the **113th Wing, District of Columbia Air National Guard, 121st Fighter Squadron**, based at Andrews AFB, MD — launched under different, more permissive "weapons free" rules of engagement (decision to fire rests with the lead pilot), obtained via the Secret Service acting outside the NORAD chain of command entirely, at the request of General David Wherley (113th Wing commander). **The first Andrews fighter was airborne at 10:38 a.m. — roughly 35 minutes after Flight 93 crashed at 10:03:11.** Neither the President, the Vice President, NORAD headquarters, nor the NMCC knew during the morning that these fighters were even airborne. CONFIRMED, verbatim ("airborne at 10:38"), Commission Report Ch. 1, p. 44, footnote 235; independently corroborated (~10:38–10:39) by former Commission staff investigator Miles Kara's own later published timeline reconstruction of the 113th Wing's day.
- The genuine, well-documented story from this squadron is Lt. Col. Marc Sasseville and then-1st Lt. Heather Penney, who launched intending to ram their unarmed F-16s into Flight 93 if it wouldn't divert — a real "kamikaze mission" plan, confirmed by multiple independent interviews and the pilots' own numerous on-record accounts over 20+ years. They never found the aircraft and did not learn it had already crashed until sometime afterward. CONFIRMED — keep this account separate from the false claim below; it is not in dispute.
- **The false claim (verifies/updates the existing Section 8 reference):** a different pilot from the same squadron, **Maj. Billy Hutchison** (lead of "Bully" flight, the training sortie recalled that morning), was credited in Lynn Spencer's 2008 book *Touching History* with a detailed, dramatic account of personally locating Flight 93 on radar, requesting a vector and range from air traffic control, planning to fire training rounds into its engine and cockpit, and preparing to ram it with his own jet as a last resort. **Commission senior counsel John Farmer, joined by fellow staffers John Azzarello and Miles Kara, wrote in a 2008 *New York Times* op-ed ("Real Heroes, Fake Stories") that this specific account "isn't true"** — the Commission's own reconstructed timeline showed Hutchison's flight was not airborne until 10:38 a.m., roughly 35 minutes after Flight 93 had already crashed, making the described encounter physically impossible. When Commission staff confronted Hutchison directly with the discrepancy, he declined to explain it and left the meeting. CONFIRMED via Farmer's own published account (the *Times* op-ed and his book *The Ground Truth*, 2009) and independently corroborated by the Commission Report's own 10:38 Andrews-airborne timeline; **attribute this explicitly to Farmer's account** ("according to 9/11 Commission senior counsel John Farmer...") rather than presenting it as an anonymous or free-floating fact.

**The NORAD discrepancy finding — the institutional-accountability story itself, precisely sourced:**
- In May 2003 public testimony before the Commission, NORAD officials stated that NEADS received hijack notification of Flight 93 at 9:16 a.m., and of American 77 at 9:24 a.m., and that the Langley fighters were scrambled in response to one or both of those notifications. **All three statements were false**, per the Commission's own subpoenaed tapes and logs: Flight 93 was not even hijacked until 9:28 a.m. (the 9:16 entry actually referenced United 175's tail number); NEADS never received notice that American 77 was hijacked at all (only that it was "missing," at 9:34); and the Langley scramble was a response to the mistaken American-11-still-airborne report. CONFIRMED, verbatim, Commission Report Ch. 1, p. 34.
- The false "9:16" figure has a documented paper trail: NORAD's own draft public timeline, prepared internally on September 16, 2001, correctly listed no notification time ("N/A") for Flight 93. The 9:16 figure was reintroduced specifically in briefing materials prepared for NORAD officials' May 2003 congressional/Commission testimony. A subpoenaed e-mail from NEADS Battle Commander Col. Robert Marr to a former colleague, explaining the discrepancy, states: **"it is easiest to explain the simultaneous scramble order with the AA77 notification as the scramble being against AA77 – it takes a lot of time to explain to the public that you're scrambling fighters against a plane heading away from the possible target."** CONFIRMED, verbatim, Commission staff's declassified 2011 monograph "A New Type of War."
- The inaccurate account was repeated publicly for years afterward by named officials in official channels: then-Deputy Secretary of Defense Paul Wolfowitz told a television interviewer on September 15, 2001 that the Pentagon had been "following" Flight 93 and prepared to shoot it down (the same day, Air National Guard commander Gen. Paul Weaver told reporters, accurately, "We weren't even close"); the U.S. Air Force's own official history of 9/11, *Air War Over America*, quotes NEADS Commander Col. Robert Marr and Continental Region Commander Gen. Larry Arnold describing having tracked United 93's flight path and received "clearance to kill if need be" — the Commission's own tape and radar reconstruction shows NORAD never had Flight 93 on radar at all. CONFIRMED, verbatim, per the same 2011 staff monograph.
- The Commission Report's own summary, applied directly to this case: **"NORAD officials have maintained consistently that had the passengers not caused United 93 to crash, the military would have prevented it from reaching Washington, D.C. That conclusion is based on a version of events that we now know is incorrect."** CONFIRMED, verbatim, Ch. 1, p. 45.

**The "fog of war, not conspiracy" line — the Commission's own distinction, and the correct one for this script:**
- The Commission staff explicitly separated two different things: (1) the real-time floor-level personnel at NEADS and FAA facilities, who improvised in good faith under an unprecedented, simultaneous attack they had never trained for and deserve no discredit — **"We do not believe that understanding the truth about the events of that morning reflects discredit on the operational personnel from NEADS or FAA facilities… [they] thought outside the box"** — versus (2) the separate, later, higher-level public and testimonial narrative, which was inaccurate and, per the Marr e-mail above, reads as a deliberate choice of the simpler story over the messy true one, sustained across years of testimony and official publications. CONFIRMED, verbatim (staff monograph, "The Impact of Inaccurate Statements"; Staff Statement 17). **This is the correct on-screen framing: an institutional accountability failure in how the response was later described, not evidence anyone allowed the attacks or manipulated the response to enable them.**
- Farmer has since, in his own personal book and public statements — outside the Commission's own more measured official findings, and DISPUTED/opinion rather than a formal Commission conclusion — used considerably stronger language: he has said the tapes "told a radically different story from what had been told to us and the public for two years," and written in *The Ground Truth* that "at some level of government, at some point in time, a decision was made not to tell the truth about the national response to the attacks on the morning of 9/11." **Attribute this explicitly to Farmer's personal characterization if used at all** — the Commission's own official Report and staff statements never allege deliberate deception in those words; they document specific factual discrepancies and let the (already damning) documented e-mail trail speak for itself. Do not present Farmer's stronger personal framing as if it were the Commission's own institutional finding.

**Framing rule for the script (mirrors Section 8's discipline):** this section is about NORAD's *public account of its own response time* being wrong — not about the attacks themselves, and not about Flight 93 being intercepted, shot down, or allowed to proceed. Keep the Hutchison false-claim story and the NORAD-discrepancy story sharply separate from the fringe shootdown/no-plane theory in Section 8; they use overlapping facts (the same scramble times, the same Farmer investigation) but support opposite points — Section 8's fringe theory claims the military DID hit Flight 93; this section documents that the military never had the chance to, and that some of its own people initially, and inaccurately, claimed otherwise about their general capability that morning.

## 7. THE CRASH SITE AND INVESTIGATION

- Flight 93 crashed at approximately **10:03:11 a.m. EDT** in a reclaimed strip-mine field near **Shanksville, Somerset County, Pennsylvania**, roughly 20 minutes' flying time from Washington, D.C. The aircraft struck the ground inverted/nose-down at high speed (Commission/NTSB flight-path reconstruction places it in the 500s of mph; cite the reconstruction document rather than a specific single figure since minor variance exists across renderings). CONFIRMED.
- There was no survivable wreckage; no one on the ground was killed or injured — the only one of the four hijacked flights with zero ground casualties. CONFIRMED.
- **Identification of victims:** all 44 people aboard (40 non-hijacker victims + 4 hijackers) were identified through fingerprints, dental records, and DNA analysis, overseen by Somerset County Coroner Wallace Miller. The first roughly 16 victims were identified within two weeks via fingerprints/dental records; the remainder required DNA matching. Ziad Jarrah's remains were identified via a DNA sample submitted by his girlfriend in Germany; the other three hijackers' remains were identified by process of elimination. CONFIRMED. **Do not include any further forensic detail (piece counts, weight, percentage of body mass) — see Section 10, this is a required cut from the ethics review.**
- **Intended target:** the Commission's assessment — based on an Atta/bin al-Shibh exchange two days before the attacks, an Al Jazeera interview of Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and bin al-Shibh, and driver Salim Hamdan's interrogation — leans toward the White House as primary target with the Capitol as secondary/backup, but the Commission's own chapter language states only that Jarrah's objective was "to crash his airliner into symbols of the American Republic, the Capitol or the White House." **The Commission does not assert certainty between the two.** CONFIRMED as an open finding — script as "the Commission believed... but could not confirm which," never as a settled fact.
- Both houses of Congress were in session in the Capitol that morning; the Commission calculated Flight 93 accumulated roughly 88 minutes of combined delay (late departure + navigational confusion) versus the other hijacked flights, and that the Capitol/White House were evacuated roughly 28 minutes before Flight 93's earliest projected D.C. arrival (~10:13 a.m.) — meaning it is the passenger revolt, not evacuation timing, that the Commission credits with denying the hijackers their target, since the plane crashed at 10:03:11, short of Washington. CONFIRMED.

## 8. CONSPIRACY CLAIMS — REFUTED

*(The raw research contained four near-duplicate versions of the shootdown/no-plane claim across different research dimensions. Per the ethics review's consolidation recommendation, they are merged into the single segment below — this is the only version that should reach the script, run once, framed as closed.)*

**Claim (fringe, FOLKLORE-EXCLUDE):** Flight 93 was shot down by a U.S. military fighter jet or missile, or no real plane crashed at Shanksville at all — sometimes citing a "mystery white jet" seen near the crash site.

**What the official investigation found, refuting it (CONFIRMED, cite plainly and move on — do not give this proportional screen time to a real debate):**
- No missile-strike fragmentation or shrapnel signatures were found on any recovered debris.
- The debris field's spread is consistent with a high-speed, near-vertical impact breaking the aircraft apart on contact — not an external explosive warhead.
- CVR and flight-data-recorder data are fully consistent with a high-speed uncontrolled dive following the passenger assault, not an external strike.
- NORAD's own timeline (per the Commission) shows fighters were not scrambled in time to have reached the aircraft before its 10:03 a.m. crash. Commission counsel John Farmer demonstrated that one witness pilot's public claim of having intercepted/rammed Flight 93 was physically impossible, since his squadron was not airborne until 10:38 a.m. — 35 minutes *after* the crash; the pilot did not explain the false claim when confronted.
- The "white jet" some theorists cite was a civilian Dassault Falcon business jet that FAA air traffic control asked to descend and visually survey the wreckage *after* the crash — not a shootdown aircraft.
- All 44 people aboard were positively identified via DNA, fingerprint, and dental analysis (Section 7), physically refuting any "no plane/no bodies" version of the claim.

**Framing rule:** present strictly as "a claim existed → here is what investigators found that refutes it, with evidence." Never present as an open or balanced question; never let it run longer than roughly 30–45 seconds in the aftermath/investigation chapter.

**A second, narrower, non-fringe disagreement (DISPUTED, not folklore — handle differently):** whether passengers physically breached the cockpit door before the crash. The Commission's official conclusion, based on the CVR, is that the hijackers stayed at the controls throughout and deliberately crashed the plane when they judged the passengers were seconds from overcoming them. Some family members who have reviewed the CVR believe the passengers did breach the cockpit and may have injured or killed a hijacker. **Script this as an attributed disagreement in interpretation of the same real evidence** ("the Commission concluded X; some family members who have heard the tape believe Y") — not as a live open mystery and not lumped in with the shootdown folklore above.
