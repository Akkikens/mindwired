## 1. THE BOAT — K-141 KURSK

- CONFIRMED — K-141 Kursk was a Project 949A "Antey" (NATO: Oscar II) nuclear-powered cruise-missile submarine (SSGN) of the Russian Northern Fleet. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/oscar-submarine/
- CONFIRMED — Built at the Sevmash yard, Severodvinsk (near Arkhangelsk); laid down 1990, launched May 1994, commissioned 30 December 1994; entered the Northern Fleet ship list 1 March 1995, based at Vidyayevo. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://tass.com/society/1188845
- CONFIRMED — Named in 1993 for the Battle of Kursk, on the battle's 50th anniversary. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://tass.com/society/1188845
- CONFIRMED — Size: length 154.0 m (~505 ft — Wikipedia's disaster article rounds to 155 m, "about two jumbo jets"), beam 18.2 m, draft 9.0 m. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED (variants exist) — Displacement: 13,400 t surfaced / 16,400 t submerged (Wikipedia). Other reference sites give differing figures — cite the Wikipedia range on screen. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://www.naval-technology.com/projects/oscar-submarine/
- CONFIRMED — Performance: ~32 knots submerged, 16 knots surfaced; test depth estimated 300–500 m (some sources cite a 1,000 m design-depth claim — use "test depth ~300–500 m" as the safe figure and attribute the 1,000 m number if used). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — Standard complement: 44 officers, 68 enlisted. On 12 August 2000 there were 118 aboard: the crew plus 5 officers from 7th SSGN Division headquarters and 2 civilian design engineers aboard to observe a USET-80 torpedo (battery) test. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — Double-hull construction: thin steel outer hull (≈8–8.5 mm) coated in rubber (up to 80 mm) for quieting; inner pressure hull of ~50 mm steel; inner hull divided into nine watertight compartments. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- DISPUTED (number only) — Gap between the hulls: the disaster article describes a 1–2 m gap ("over two meters" in some retellings, designed to absorb torpedo hits); one summary of the K-141 article renders it as a 200 mm gap. Use "roughly two metres" with the disaster-article sourcing. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://vocal.media/history/the-sinking-of-russia-s-unsinkable-kursk-submarine
- CONFIRMED — The "unsinkable" reputation: designers described the class as unsinkable; it was claimed the boat "could withstand a direct hit from a torpedo," a reputation resting on the double hull and nine compartments. (Frame as "her designers called her unsinkable" — a claim, not a property.) https://www.rferl.org/a/kursk-russia-submarine-disaster/27183289.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — Armament: 24 × P-700 Granit (SS-N-19 "Shipwreck") cruise missiles; bow tubes 4 × 533 mm and 2 × 650 mm; ~24 torpedoes/tube-launched weapons. Purpose-built as a "carrier killer" against US carrier battle groups. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://nationalinterest.org/blog/buzz/how-russian-submarine-designed-kill-american-aircraft-18299
- CONFIRMED — Kursk was widely described as the pride of the Northern Fleet and had recently won a citation for excellence; its crew was recognized as among the best in the Northern Fleet. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://www.rferl.org/a/kursk-russia-submarine-disaster/27183289.html
- UNVERIFIED — "$2 billion" cost figures circulate (e.g., MegaProjects' "$2 Billion Disaster"); no official construction-cost record found in this sweep. Do not put a hard cost number on screen without better sourcing. https://megaprojects.channel/ships/kursk-submarine-disaster/
- CONFIRMED — Kursk was refitted in 1998 to carry HTP-fuelled torpedoes. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster

## 2. THE CAPTAIN AND THE 1999 MEDITERRANEAN DEPLOYMENT

- CONFIRMED — Commanding officer: Captain 1st Rank Gennady Petrovich Lyachin (deceased 12 Aug 2000; body recovered, identified March 2002). Posthumously awarded Hero of the Russian Federation by President Vladimir Putin [LIVING] "for courage and heroism, during performance of sailor's duties" (award 26 August 2000). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennady_Lyachin ; https://www.upi.com/Defense-News/2002/03/16/Kursk-captains-body-identified/13521016299240/
- CONFIRMED — In summer 1999 Kursk deployed to the Mediterranean to monitor the US Sixth Fleet during the Kosovo War — described in US Naval Institute Proceedings as simulating an attack on a US carrier battle group, "the first time in at least four years" a Russian submarine had done so. https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2003/june/kursks-loss-offers-lessons ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk
- ALLEGED (Russian accounts) — Russian-side accounts claim Kursk shadowed the carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt's group, carried out 5 simulated attacks on real targets, and that 72 crew members were put up for state awards, with Lyachin nominated for Hero of Russia on return. Attribute as "Russian naval accounts claim…" — the operational details are not independently verified. https://english.pravda.ru/russia/121899-kursk_nato/ ; https://knifesburg.ru/en/na-more/podlodka-kursk.html
- EXCLUDE — Pravda's framing that the deployment made Lyachin "a personal enemy of the Americans" and that the sub terrified NATO: state-media color, not a sourceable fact. https://english.pravda.ru/russia/121899-kursk_nato/
- CONFIRMED (reported, single main line of sourcing) — During the 1999 deployment the emergency rescue buoy was intentionally disabled by officers who feared its accidental release would betray the boat's position to the US fleet — and it remained inoperative in August 2000. Powerful origins→fall irony; attribute ("according to post-disaster reporting"). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — Despite the celebrated 1999 cruise, many of the crew had limited sea time by 2000 — a fleet-wide consequence of the funding drought. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141)

## 3. THE AUGUST 2000 EXERCISE ("SUMMER-X")

- CONFIRMED — The Northern Fleet exercise in the Barents Sea, August 2000, was the largest Russian naval exercise since the collapse of the Soviet Union — the first large-scale exercise in over a decade. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/russian-submarine-sinks
- DISPUTED (scale numbers) — Fleet size varies by source: "30 ships" (EBSCO/Encyclopedia.com summaries) vs. Wikipedia's fuller count of over 50 ships and submarines plus ~40 support vessels and ~80 aircraft. Safest on-screen line: "more than 30 warships and submarines." https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/russian-submarine-sinks ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — Kursk joined the exercise on 10 August 2000 and, unusually for an exercise, sailed with a full combat load of weapons. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — On the first day of the exercise Kursk successfully launched a Granit cruise missile fitted with a dummy warhead. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141)
- CONFIRMED — 12 August tasking: a practice (warhead-less) torpedo attack on the exercise's surface group led by the Kirov-class battlecruiser Pyotr Velikiy — flagship of Northern Fleet commander Admiral Vyacheslav Popov [LIVING]. At 08:51 Moscow time Kursk requested and received permission for the torpedo training launch; its firing window closed at 13:30, after which silence triggered concern. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED (boundary fact — hand off to "the fall" agent) — The weapon being prepared was a Type 65 "Kit" practice torpedo, 10.7 m long, ~5 tonnes, loaded in torpedo tube No. 4 (starboard); the first explosion came at ~11:28 local time (07:28 UTC). Two blasts were recorded, the second equal to several tonnes of TNT. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk_(K-141)
- CONFIRMED — Compartment positions at the moment of the accident: 7 men in the first (torpedo) compartment, 36 in the second (command post); 78 assigned to forward compartments 1–4 and 49 to aft compartments 5–9. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster

## 4. THE WEAPON — TYPE 65-76A AND HTP

- CONFIRMED — The Type 65 ("Kit," Russian for "whale") is a 650 mm heavyweight torpedo developed to kill US carrier battle groups; wake-homing; ranges around 50 km at 50 kt or up to 100 km at 30 kt; combat versions carry a ~450 kg-class warhead. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_65_torpedo ; https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/t-65-torpedo.htm
- CONFIRMED — Propulsion: a gas turbine burning kerosene oxidized by HTP (high-test peroxide — highly concentrated hydrogen peroxide). HTP gives huge power/range, but on contact with a catalyst (e.g., copper/brass) it decomposes, expanding ~5,000 times in volume into steam and oxygen in a violently exothermic, unstoppable reaction — "once decomposition begins, it cannot be stopped until the fuel is exhausted" (paraphrase of investigation findings). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_65_torpedo ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — The official Russian investigation (Prosecutor General Vladimir Ustinov's report, 2002) concluded HTP seeped through a faulty weld in the practice torpedo's casing, contacted catalytic metal, and the pressure burst the kerosene tank — an initial explosion equivalent to roughly 100–250 kg of TNT, registering ~2.2 on seismic scales. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED (as reported in the investigation record) — Defect lineage: the dummy torpedo came from a batch of 10 manufactured in 1990, of which six were rejected for faulty welding; welds on practice torpedoes got less scrutiny than on warshot weapons. The weapon was ~10 years old, with some components past service life. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- ALLEGED / UNCONFIRMED — That the torpedo had been dropped during transport before loading: raised in reporting, not confirmed across sources. Attribute if used. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED (as reported in investigation reporting) — The day before the exercise, leaking fuel was detected at the torpedo's rubber seals; junior officers reportedly took no action because of the exercise's importance, and the seals were not inspected before firing. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED (as reported) — The crew had never fired an HTP torpedo, had not been trained on HTP weapons (their last live firing, ~3 years earlier, was a simpler battery torpedo), the parent division never verified their qualification, and training-log signatures were reportedly faked. Attribute to the post-disaster investigation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — HMS Sidon precedent: on 16 June 1955, alongside HMS Maidstone in Portland Harbour, an experimental Mark 12 HTP torpedo suffered a "hot run" during loading — a burst oxidizer line sprayed HTP onto the weapon's copper innards — and the explosion sank Sidon with 13 dead. The Royal Navy subsequently abandoned HTP torpedo propulsion. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Sidon_(P259) ; https://www.dorset.live/news/history/explosion-queens-coronation-submarine-killed-7123575
- CONFIRMED — Most Western navies abandoned HTP torpedoes decades before 2000 because of the danger inherent in the design; Russia retained the Type 65 family (HTP's performance came cheap relative to alternatives). Voices inside the Russian defense establishment had opposed HTP torpedoes as volatile and dangerous — opposition noted in the Defense Ministry newspaper Krasnaya Zvezda. https://nationalinterest.org/blog/reboot/118-dead-hydrogen-peroxide-torpedo-sunk-russian-nuclear-submarine-209561 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — After the disaster Russia withdrew/mothballed the HTP-propelled Type 65-76 line (a 2010 RIA Novosti item covers an admiral urging their redeployment — proof they were pulled). https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/russia/2010/russia-100812-rianovosti01.htm

## 5. THE WORLD THAT BUILT IT — POST-SOVIET NAVAL DECAY (systemic-cause layer)

- CONFIRMED — After 1991 naval funding collapsed: between 1990 and 1995 the Russian fleet's ship count fell ~50% and naval aviation ~66%; ships were scrapped or laid up for lack of maintenance money. https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/mf-intro-r.htm ; https://naval-encyclopedia.com/modern/russian-navy.php
- CONFIRMED — Northern Fleet funding specifics (Bellona): in 1994 only 35% of allocated funds actually reached the Northern Fleet; in 1995 the fleet did not receive the 600 billion roubles budgeted; by 1998 personnel at Kola nuclear-submarine bases had gone unpaid for months. https://bellona.org/news/russian-human-rights-issues/nikitin-case/1998-10-naval-nuclear-waste-management-in-northwest-russia
- CONFIRMED — Over a hundred decommissioned nuclear submarines sat rotting at Arctic moorings through the 1990s; Russia retired more than half the Soviet submarine force as the budget imploded. https://www.nti.org/analysis/articles/nuclear-submarine-dismantlement/ ; https://www.armscontrol.org/act/1999-07/dismantling-russias-nuclear-subs
- CONFIRMED — Rescue capability had been gutted before August 2000: the navy's two India-class (Project 940 "Lenok") dedicated rescue-mother submarines were decommissioned in the 1990s (Pacific boat BS-486 in 1994; Northern Fleet boat BS-257 by 1999). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India-class_submarine ; https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/940.htm
- CONFIRMED — Two submarines fitted with advanced Poseidon-class deep-submergence rescue vehicles (rated to 693 m) had sat in a Saint Petersburg yard since 1994 "pending repairs" for lack of funds. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — The Northern Fleet's primary surface rescue ship, Mikhail Rudnitsky, was a ~20-year-old converted lumber carrier without stabilizers, limiting rescue work to calm seas. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://natsouth.livejournal.com/604.html
- CONFIRMED — The surviving Priz-class rescue submersibles (AS-34, AS-32) were in poor condition; during the actual rescue attempt crews cannibalized AS-32 for parts to keep AS-34 running, and AS-34 could not seal onto the escape hatch. (Boundary fact — the rescue itself is another agent's dimension; use here only as "the state of the kit in 2000.") https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_AS-34 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — Even routine kit was failing: a crane used for loading Kursk's weapons before the exercise was out of order, delaying loading. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED (framing, well-supported) — The narrative spine holds: the newest, proudest boat of a starving fleet — "fast, stealthy… the pride of the cash-starved post-Soviet sea force" — was sent to fire a decade-old bargain-fuel torpedo its crew had never handled, with the rescue force that could have saved survivors already scrapped or broken. https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/august-12/russian-sub-sinks-with-118-onboard ; https://www.npr.org/2003/01/13/917851/a-time-to-die-the-kursk-disaster

## 2. THE MISSED SIGNALS, THE SEARCH, THE FINDING

- CONFIRMED — Pyotr Velikiy's crew detected "a hydroacoustic signal characteristic of an underwater explosion" and felt their hull shudder at the time of the blasts; they reported it to fleet headquarters and the report was ignored/dismissed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED (variants on the alarm clock) — Kursk's expected torpedo-firing report window passed at 13:30 with no contact; the fleet did nothing decisive for hours. ## 3. THE OFFICIAL INVESTIGATION — USTINOV REPORT (2002)

- CONFIRMED — On 26 July 2002, Prosecutor-General Vladimir Ustinov [LIVING] announced (after briefing President Putin) that Kursk sank following the explosion of a torpedo caused by "emergency processes which took place inside the torpedo's oxidiser tank" — i.e., HTP leaking, with the government commission having announced the hydrogen-peroxide cause on the fourth torpedo tube's practice torpedo. https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/russia/2002/russia-020726-391ee734.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — Mechanism per the investigation: HTP seeped through a **faulty weld in the torpedo casing**, decomposed catalytically, and blew the torpedo — first blast — then fire detonated the combat warheads ~2 minutes later. The crew had no prior experience with, and had not been trained to handle or fire, HTP-powered torpedoes; 2000–2001 fleet inspections found corrosion and faulty rubber gaskets in other HTP torpedoes. https://www.cbsnews.com/news/sloppy-safety-procedures-sank-sub ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — The full criminal case file ran to **133 volumes and remains classified (top secret)**; the public got a **four-page summary**, published in Rossiyskaya Gazeta (August 2002). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://www.rferl.org/a/1100672.html
- CONFIRMED — The public summary described "stunning breaches of discipline, shoddy, obsolete and poorly maintained equipment" and "negligence, incompetence, and mismanagement," and said the rescue operation was unjustifiably delayed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/sloppy-safety-procedures-sank-sub
- CONFIRMED — The case was closed in 2002 with **no criminal charges**. Ustinov: no charges because the disaster was "a technical malfunction" and blame could not be placed on specific individuals; "Those who designed the torpedo couldn't foresee the possibility of its explosion." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- CONFIRMED — Instead of prosecutions: on/around 1 December 2001, Putin removed or demoted **14 senior Northern Fleet / Navy officers** — including Northern Fleet commander Admiral Vyacheslav Popov [LIVING] and chief of staff Vice-Admiral Mikhail Motsak [LIVING] — officially for "serious failures in the organisation of the military training activities of the fleet," officially NOT punishment for Kursk. https://edition.cnn.com/2001/WORLD/europe/12/01/kursk.putin/index.html ; https://www.voanews.com/a/a-13-a-2001-12-02-1-russian-66959742/378254.html ; https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/kursk-submarine-disaster

## 4. THE SALVAGE (2001)

- **CONFIRMED** — In May 2001 Russia contracted the Dutch consortium Mammoet–Smit (50:50 JV) to raise the hull. In ~3 months they designed, built and commissioned over 3,000 t of custom equipment. Method: the barge Giant 4 (24,000 t deadweight) anchored over the wreck with 26 computer-controlled hoists cabled through 26 holes cut into the pressure hull; a hydraulically driven suction-anchored saw wire cut off the mangled bow section, which was left on the seabed. Depth 108 m; lifted weight ~9,000 t — a world record for the heaviest object recovered from such depth. https://smit.com/projects/kursk ; https://www.mammoet.com/cases/kursk/ ; https://www.graphicnews.com/en/pages/12978/kursk-submarine-salvage
- **DISPUTED (dates — list variants)** — Lift/delivery dates: Wikipedia states the hull was raised Oct 3, 2001 (later Wikipedia text: raised "14 months after the accident"); GlobalSecurity/RFE-RL reporting has the sub raised Oct 8, 2001 and towed to Murmansk; SMIT's own case study gives delivery to the naval dry dock on Oct 22, 2001. The dry dock was at Roslyakovo, near Murmansk. Use: lifted in a single ~15-hour operation in early Oct 2001, in dock at Roslyakovo by late Oct. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/k-141-kursk.htm ; https://smit.com/projects/kursk ; https://www.mathworks.com/company/user_stories/international-salvage-team-brings-home-the-kursk-submarine-using-a-simulation-developed-in-simulink.html
- **CONFIRMED** — Reported salvage cost: ~US$65 million (Russian-government-funded). https://nationalinterest.org/blog/buzz/russian-nuclear-submarine-sank-some-crew-survived-couldnt-be-saved-207467 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster
- **CONFIRMED** — Body recovery: a Russian-Norwegian diving operation in Oct–Nov 2000 recovered the first 12 bodies (Lt-Capt Dmitry Kolesnikov's among the first, Oct 25, 2000 — his note is the sinking-dimension agents' beat); the raised hull yielded the rest — VOA reported 94 recovered from the hull by Feb 7, 2002. Final total: **115 of 118 recovered and buried in Russia; 3 never found.** https://www.voanews.com/a/a-13-a-2002-02-07-4-94-66276137/540162.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster ; https://iol.co.za/news/world/2000-10-27-kursk-tragedy-comes-to-life-with-last-note/
- **CONFIRMED** — The severed bow: some torpedo and torpedo-tube fragments were recovered from it for the investigation; the remainder was destroyed with explosives on the seabed in 2002. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_Kursk ; https://www.irishtimes.com/news/cutting-work-on-kursk-bow-finished-says-navy-1.396501
