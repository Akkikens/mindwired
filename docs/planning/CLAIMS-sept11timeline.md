### 🔴 THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RESOLUTION — the WTC-illness death toll

**The afternoon dimension's own research (`s11_dim_afternoon.md`, §5)
surfaced a claim that directly contradicts an already-baked-in correction:**
"deaths among responders/survivors from 9/11-related illness... have
surpassed roughly 9,400 — a figure that now exceeds the original 2,977
attack-day toll by more than three times over."

**`CLAIMS-groundzeroair.md` (Correction #1, verified by a direct read of the
CDC PDF) already EXCLUDES this exact comparison as an advocacy formulation.**
CDC reports **6,897 all-cause deaths** among WTC Health Program enrollees
(as of March 31, 2024) and states explicitly that it **"does not track cause
of death for deceased members."** There is no official WTC-illness death
toll — CDC does not certify illness as the cause of any of those 6,897
deaths — so there is no official figure that can be validly compared to the
2,977/2,753 attack-day tolls.

**Per the standing resolution rule for this file: the existing baked-in
correction wins unless the new research produces a primary document that
overturns it. The afternoon dimension's 2026 figure (sourced to secondary
CDC/WTC Health Program program-statistics reporting, not a direct primary
read that addresses cause-of-death tracking) does not do that — it restates
the same advocacy-style comparison groundzeroair already excluded, from a
different year's number. groundzeroair's ruling WINS.**

- **EXCLUDE, verbatim reason:** "More people have died from 9/11 illness than
  died on 9/11" (in any phrasing, any year's number, including "9,400" or
  "surpassed the attack-day toll") — CDC tracks all-cause deaths among
  enrollees, not illness-caused deaths, and states this explicitly. No
  official comparison figure exists. **Do not put this comparison in the
  title, thumbnail, script, or a Short, under any updated number.**
- **Approved phrasing instead (carried forward verbatim from
  groundzeroair):** "More than 57,000 people enrolled in the World Trade
  Center Health Program have had a cancer certified as linked to their
  exposure" (groundzeroair A3), or, if a later illness-death figure must be
  cited, state ONLY the raw enrollee-death count with its explicit "does not
  track cause of death" caveat attached in the same breath, and never framed
  as a comparison to the attack-day toll.
- **This is the template for every other new-dimension-vs-baked-in-correction
  conflict in this episode:** the baked-in correction wins by default; only a
  primary document that overturns it changes that; if it does, both are shown
  with the newer one dated and labeled. No other such conflict arose in this
  merge — every other new-dimension finding either supplements a gap none of
  the five files covered, or independently corroborates an existing
  correction (see below).

### DO NOT SAY THE RADIOS FAILED (repeater dispute — attributed, never adjudicated)

Commission investigators, after reviewing ~78 minutes of FDNY radio tape,
concluded the repeater and radios **were in fact working** — Chief Joseph
Pfeifer 🧍 told Commission staff his repeater attempt to reach Battalion
Chief Orio Palmer failed; investigators believed he had switched frequencies
rather than the system having failed outright; the Port Authority maintained
the system was in working order. **The dispute is unresolved and must be
presented attributed to each named party, never adjudicated by our own
narration.** The Commission's own Ch.9 account frames the failure as
**systemic** — a two-button, civilian-console-activated design, a failed
9:05 test, portable use nonetheless continuing on Channel 7 in the South
Tower — never as any individual's fault. **This episode assigns no personal
blame for the communications failure**, to Pfeifer, to the Port Authority
console operator, or to anyone else.

### Pentagon collapse timing — ~9:57, NOT 10:10

Two independent primary sources agree: the ASCE Pentagon Building
Performance Report states the partial collapse occurred "approximately 20
minutes after" the 9:37:46/9:38 impact; the 9/11 Commission Report (Ch.9,
sourced to the Arlington County AAR) states directly: **"At 9:55, the
incident commander ordered an evacuation of the Pentagon impact area because
a partial collapse was imminent; it occurred at 9:57, and no first responder
was injured."** No primary source supports a 10:10 collapse time. **10:15 is
a real, separately-sourced event** — the incident commander's order for a
**full evacuation of the command post itself**, prompted by an FBI warning
of another possible inbound hijacked aircraft — not the structural collapse.
Never conflate the two.

### UA175 impact time — 9:03:11, NOT 9:03:02

No NIST report or the Commission Report contains "9:03:02." The two figures
that actually exist in the primary record are **9:03:11** (NIST NCSTAR 1 and
the 9/11 Commission Report — matches `CLAIMS-wtccollapse.md`) and **9:02:59**
(NIST NCSTAR 1-7, a ten-second NIST-internal discrepancy — see next
correction). **Use 9:03:11 on screen.** Treat "9:03:02" as UNVERIFIED/likely
a conflation; do not use it.

### The NIST-internal 10-second discrepancies — use the Commission-matching times

NIST's own reports disagree with each other by ten seconds on both tower
impacts: NCSTAR 1 gives AA11 impact as **8:46:40** and UA175 impact as
**9:03:11** (both match the 9/11 Commission Report and `CLAIMS-wtccollapse.md`);
NCSTAR 1-7 (the evacuation report) gives **8:46:30** and **9:02:59** for the
same two events. **Use 8:46:40 and 9:03:11 on screen** — they match the
Commission and the already-locked wtccollapse fact pack. The ten-second gaps
are a NIST-internal inconsistency, not a scriptable dispute; do not dramatize
it unless the script specifically needs the caveat.

### The 9:42-vs-9:45 grounding-order dispute — and the Newsday gloss is blocked

Two men, two primary-sourced accounts, both real, neither smoothed:
- **9/11 Commission Report, verbatim:** at **9:42**, FAA Command Center
  national operations manager Ben Sliney 🧍 — his first day in the job —
  "ordered all FAA facilities to instruct all aircraft to land at the
  nearest airport." Chapter 1 does not mention Mineta in connection with
  this order at all.
- **Norman Mineta's own sworn Commission testimony** (23 May 2003): "at
  approximately **9:45** a.m. ... I gave the FAA the final order for all
  civil aircraft to land at the nearest airport as soon as possible."
- **The FAA's own institutional timeline (current, 2026):** "9:45 AM: At the
  direction of Transportation Secretary Norman Mineta, the FAA Command
  Center closed all U.S. airspace for the first time in history."
- **⛔ BLOCKED, explicitly:** the Newsday 2007 gloss claiming the Commission
  found Mineta gave the order "about an hour after Sliney did" — this
  sentence **appears nowhere in the Commission's actual text** and must
  never be repeated on screen or in narration.
- **Resolution carried forward from `CLAIMS-thegrounding.md`:** "two men gave
  a landing order within three minutes of each other. The Commission credits
  the operational one to the man on the floor; the Secretary's is the
  ratification from the bunker." Never say Mineta "took credit falsely."
  Show both rows in the master timeline (§3) rather than picking one.

## 5. THE SHOOTDOWN FINDING — stated precisely, with exact page citations

**State it exactly as the Commission states it. Do not compress it into "an
order was given and followed."**

The authorization chain, precisely: Cheney phoned Bush sometime before
10:10–10:15 a.m. to discuss rules of engagement; the Commission found **no
documentary evidence for this specific call** (CR1 p.40-41). Between
10:10–10:18, a military aide told Cheney an inbound aircraft (UA93, which
had already crashed) was 80, then 60 miles out; Cheney authorized fighters to
engage it each time (CR1 p.41). At 10:18, Cheney called Bush for a two-minute
confirmation call; at 10:20, per Ari Fleischer's contemporaneous notes, Bush
told him he had authorized a shootdown if necessary (CR1 p.41). Separately —
and this is the crux — **NORAD had no knowledge of United 93's hijacking at
all until after it had already crashed** (CR1 p.42): its air threat
conference representative said at 10:07 a.m. NORAD had "no indication of a
hijack heading to DC at this time." At 10:31, Gen. Larry Arnold instructed
staff to broadcast over NORAD's messaging system: **"Vice president has
cleared to us to intercept tracks of interest and shoot them down if they do
not respond"** — the first point the authorization is documented reaching
NORAD's operational chain at all (CR1 p.42).

**The Commission's precise, load-bearing finding — quote directly on screen,
never paraphrase into "the order was given and followed":**

> "In short, while leaders in Washington believed that the fighters above
> them had been instructed to 'take out' hostile aircraft, **the only orders
> actually conveyed to the pilots were to 'ID type and tail.'**"
> — 9/11 Commission Report, Ch.1, p.43 (CONFIRMED, exact quote)

> "**The Vice President was mistaken in his belief that shootdown
> authorization had been passed to the pilots flying at NORAD's
> direction.**"
> — 9/11 Commission Report, Ch.1, p.44 (CONFIRMED, exact quote)

**Why it broke down, named and attributed to each person's own Commission
testimony (treat all as living/identifiable per house rule):** NEADS
commander Col. Robert Marr 🧍 told the Commission he did not pass along the
order "because he was unaware of its ramifications." Mission commander Maj.
Kevin Nasypany 🧍 and senior weapons director Maj. Dawne Deskins 🧍 said they
did not pass the order to the Langley fighters "because they were unsure how
the pilots would, or should, proceed with this guidance." (CR1 p.43,
ALLEGED as self-reported testimony but CONFIRMED as the Commission's own
accepted account.)

**A separate, second chain had different rules entirely:** the 113th Wing,
DC Air National Guard, launched from Andrews starting 10:38 a.m. at the
Secret Service's request, operating "weapons free" — the shoot decision
resting with the lead pilot in the cockpit — a permissive rule NORAD
headquarters did not even know these aircraft were flying under (CR1 p.44).

**Bottom line, stated as the Commission states it:** by the time any version
of a shootdown order reached operational fighters, **United 93 had already
crashed in Pennsylvania at 10:03 a.m.** — 28+ minutes before authorization is
documented reaching NORAD's chain at all, and the engagement order itself
never reached the fighter pilots in any usable form that morning (CR1
pp.41-45, the "What If?" section explicitly walking back NORAD's own public
claim that it would have intercepted United 93, calling that claim's
underlying premise "incorrect").
