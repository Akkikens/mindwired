## THE SPINE

The hadal zone begins at 6,000 m. The animals there are the most isolated on the planet.
They are also full of our chemicals — at concentrations that, in one measure, match the
coastline, and in another exceed heavily industrialised regions. The episode is the
investigation behind those measurements, and it stops where the evidence stops.

---

## SOURCE 1 — Persistent organic pollutants (the headline)

**Jamieson AJ, Malkocs T, Piertney SB, Fujii T, Zhang Z. "Bioaccumulation of persistent
organic pollutants in the deepest ocean fauna." *Nature Ecology & Evolution*, 13 Feb
2017. DOI 10.1038/s41559-016-0051.**

Abstract, verified verbatim via Europe PMC:

> "The legacy and reach of anthropogenic influence is most clearly evidenced by its impact
> on the most remote and inaccessible habitats on Earth. Here we identify extraordinary
> levels of persistent organic pollutants in the endemic amphipod fauna from two of the
> deepest ocean trenches (>10,000 metres). Contaminant levels were considerably higher
> than documented for nearby regions of heavy industrialization, indicating
> bioaccumulation of anthropogenic contamination and inferring that these pollutants are
> pervasive across the world's oceans and to full ocean depth."

- The two trenches are the **Mariana** and the **Kermadec**.
- ⛔ **DO NOT USE the "50 times more contaminated than crabs from the Liaohe River"
  figure.** It is repeated everywhere, it is NOT in the abstract, the paper body is
  paywalled, and the Newcastle press release 404s. **Unsourced = unusable.** Say what the
  abstract says: "considerably higher than documented for nearby regions of heavy
  industrialization." That is quotable and it is enough.

## SOURCE 2 — Microplastics (the most quotable numbers, OPEN ACCESS)

**Jamieson AJ et al. "Microplastics and synthetic particles ingested by deep-sea amphipods
in six of the deepest marine ecosystems on Earth." *Royal Society Open Science*, 2019.
DOI 10.1098/rsos.180667. Open access — figures below are verbatim from the full text.**

- Six trenches around the Pacific Rim: **Japan, Izu-Bonin, Mariana, Kermadec, New Hebrides,
  Peru-Chile**. Depths **7,000 m to 10,890 m**.
- **"Over 72% of individuals examined (65 of 90) contained at least one microparticle."**
- Ingestion frequency by site ranged **50% to 100%** — lowest **New Hebrides (50%)**,
  highest **Mariana (100%)**.
- **1 to 8** microparticles per individual. Mean per trench from **0.9 ± 0.4** (New
  Hebrides) to **3.3 ± 0.7** (Mariana).
- **Fibres appeared in 84%** of amphipods; fragments in only 16%.
- Most prevalent single item: **blue fibres, 66%** — and **"all amphipods sampled from the
  Mariana Trench containing at least one of these."**
- Materials by FTIR: **nylon, polyethylene, polyamide, polyvinyl alcohol, PVC** (often
  with inorganic filler), semi-synthetics **rayon and lyocell**, and the natural fibre
  **ramie**.
- The comparison that IS the thesis: amphipods ingest at **"a similar frequency (72%) to
  crustaceans in coastal water habitats."**

## SOURCE 3 — Mercury

**Liu M, Xiao W, Zhang Q, Yuan S, Raymond PA, Chen J, Liu J, Tao S, Xu Y. "Substantial
accumulation of mercury in the deepest parts of the ocean and implications for the
environmental mercury cycle." *PNAS*, 2021. DOI 10.1073/pnas.2102629118.**

- Sediment cores from **four trench regions, 1,560 to 10,840 m**.
- Hg accumulating at **24 to 220 µg·m⁻²·y⁻¹** — **"greater than the global deep-sea
  average by a factor of up to 400."**
- **"most Hg in these trenches being derived from the surface ocean."**
- Vertical profiles rise from **pre-1900: 51 ± 14 ng·g⁻¹** to **post-1950: 81 ± 32 ng·g⁻¹**,
  an increase the paper says cannot be explained by organic-carbon delivery alone.

## SOURCE 4 — What actually lives down there

**Xu W et al. "Chromosome-level genome assembly of hadal snailfish reveals mechanisms of
deep-sea adaptation in vertebrates." *eLife*, 2023. DOI 10.7554/eLife.87198. Open access.**

- **Pseudoliparis swirei**, the hadal snailfish — **"the deepest vertebrate in the ocean,"**
  living at **6,000–8,000 m**.
- It **"may have entered and fully adapted to such extreme environments only in the last
  few million years,"** and spread into different Pacific trenches **within a million years**.
- Lost pigmentation, visual and skeletal-calcification genes. Uses **TMAO** against
  hydrostatic pressure. A **tandem duplication of a ferritin gene** raised its tolerance to
  reactive oxygen species.

**Chen Y, Barcan AS, Zhang J, Li J, He L, Wang Y. "In situ swimming behavior of the Mariana
snailfish Pseudoliparis swirei." *Scientific Reports*, 2026. DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-48409-2.
Open access.**

- First 3D quantification of its behaviour in the wild, from a baited lander at **nearly
  7,000 m**, 868 annotated instances.
- Routine speed **0.16–0.18 m/s** (~**0.71 body lengths per second**); short bursts to
  ~0.50 m/s.
- Estimated to **first detect bait odour at about 350 m** away.

## BINDING RULES FOR THE SCRIPT

1. **Never use the Liaohe "50x" figure.** See Source 1. If it cannot be sourced to a
   document in hand, it does not go in the episode.
2. **Do not make the descent video again.** mindwired's existing Mariana episode ("The
   Place on Earth Scarier Than Space") did 197 views with a depth-zone descent and a
   "scarier than space" mood. This is the investigation behind specific measurements.
3. **The plastic-bag beat is BURNED.** The old episode used Victor Vescovo's 2019 plastic
   bag in scene 2. Do not reuse it as the hook. The blue fibre replaces it and is better:
   it is invisible, it is in 100% of the Mariana animals, and it is in their bodies rather
   than on the seafloor.
4. **No doom, no lecture, no call to action.** This channel reports the record. The
   finding is quietly appalling on its own; editorialising weakens it and reads as
   activism rather than documentary.
5. **Distinguish the three contaminant stories** — POPs (2017), microplastics (2019),
   mercury (2021) are separate papers with separate methods. Do not blend their numbers.
6. **"Deepest vertebrate" not "deepest fish/animal."** The snailfish is the deepest
   *vertebrate*; amphipods are deeper and are invertebrates. Precision here is cheap.
7. **Say what is NOT known.** Why POPs concentrate so strongly in trenches is not settled;
   the funnel hypothesis (trenches accumulate sinking matter and it cannot leave) is a
   mechanism, not a proven cause. State it as such.
8. **72% is "over 72%," and it is 65 of 90 animals.** A small sample. Say the sample size
   out loud — it is the kind of honesty that earns the rest of the episode.
