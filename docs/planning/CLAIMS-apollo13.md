## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **Insulation material was TEFLON, not Kapton.** The Review Board's own case
   study: "wiring inside the oxygen tank had lost its Teflon insulation due to
   overheating during the de-tanking procedures at KSC." Never say Kapton —
   no primary source names it for this failure.
2. **The precursor alarm ~15 minutes before the explosion was on hydrogen tank
   1's low pressure**, not oxygen tank 1 — this is what triggered Capcom Jack
   Lousma's routine "stir up your cryo tanks" request.
3. **Swigert's real first words, in full: "Okay, Houston, I believe we've had
   a problem here."** Don't trim "I believe" — the whole point of the
   real-vs-movie beat is exactness. Lovell's repeat 15 seconds later: "Ah,
   Houston, we've had a problem. We've had a Main B Bus Undervolt." The
   movie's "Houston, we HAVE a problem" (present tense, no "had," no "here")
   is a 1995 screenplay invention — never present it as the real quote.
4. **"Failure is not an option" was never said during the 1970 crisis.** Gene
   Kranz has publicly confirmed it was written for the 1995 film and later
   used as his own memoir's title. His real real-time words to the room:
   "I have never lost an American in space, sure as hell aren't going to
   lose one now. This crew is coming home…" Use the real line if quoting him.
5. **"Look at that, mother" could not be verified as a real quote anywhere**
   (not in the transcript, not even in the movie). Use Lovell's actual,
   on-record line instead: "And there's one whole side of that spacecraft
   missing… Right by the high gain antenna, the whole panel is blown out,
   almost from the base to the engine." (GET 138:04:46–57).
6. **Apollo 20 was cancelled January 4, 1970 — three months BEFORE Apollo 13
   even launched**, to free a Saturn V for Skylab; it has zero connection to
   the accident. Only Apollo 18 and 19 were cancelled together, September 2,
   1970, in the post-accident/budget-pressure era. Never lump all three into
   one "cancelled because of the accident" line — that overclaims causation
   even for 18/19, and is flatly wrong for 20.
7. **Jim Lovell's cause of death IS now public** (a Lake County, IL coroner's
   finding released ~Oct 29, 2025): complications of a hip fracture
   sustained in a fall at home, with cardiovascular disease as a
   contributing condition. Either (a) omit a cause entirely — a legitimate,
   respectful editorial choice — or (b) if stated, attribute it explicitly to
   the coroner's finding. Never say "the cause was never disclosed" — that
   was only true for the first ~11 weeks after his death.
8. **Never conflate the two 2026 films called "Odyssey."** (a) Christopher
   Nolan's *The Odyssey* (Matt Damon, wide release July 17, 2026, the Homer
   epic) has nothing to do with Apollo 13. (b) The Apollo 13 documentary is
   Avi Belkin's *Odyssey* (Tribeca premiere June 7, 2026). Pull every date/
   score for the Apollo 13 film from a Belkin/Tribeca-specific source, never
   a generic "Odyssey (2026)" aggregator — real coverage is still actively
   mixing the two up.
9. **The exact phrase "passing the torch toward future Mars missions" in
   Lovell's posthumous Artemis II message is UNVERIFIED against the full
   transcript** — a corroborated source suggests the message said something
   else (about enjoying the view). The verified, safe-to-quote line is:
   "Welcome to my old neighborhood" (referencing his own Apollo 8 lunar
   orbit). Don't script the "passing the torch" phrasing as something Lovell
   actually said.
10. **The reentry communications blackout was ~6 to 6.5 minutes, not "12
    minutes."** Predicted blackout was 3:38; it ran roughly 2.5–3 minutes
    longer than predicted, which is exactly what made it alarming. Script the
    real overrun, which is dramatic enough on its own — don't inflate it.
11. **The CO2-scrubber "mailbox" fix's "sock" detail is confirmed only via
    secondary NASA-affiliated sources** (image captions), not a verbatim
    transcript quote — fine to use, just don't stage it as a direct quote
    from the tapes.

## 1. WHO'S WHO

- **Jim Lovell** — Commander. Second lunar flight (after Apollo 8), fourth
  spaceflight overall. **Died August 7, 2025**, at home in Lake Forest, IL,
  age 97 (see correction #7 for cause). CONFIRMED.
- **Jack Swigert** — Command Module Pilot. Originally backup CMP; became
  prime crew ~72 hours before launch when Ken Mattingly was pulled over
  measles exposure (see below). Elected to the U.S. House (Colorado's 6th)
  Nov 1982 while already diagnosed with bone cancer; died Dec 27, 1982, of
  respiratory failure from the cancer's progression — before being sworn in.
  Script as "elected but died before taking his seat," never "died in
  Congress." CONFIRMED.
- 🧍 **Fred Haise** — Lunar Module Pilot. **Alive, age 92 (b. Nov 14, 1933,
  Biloxi, MS), the sole surviving Apollo 13 crew member.** Every claim about
  him must be attributed and present-tense-cautious — no invented quotes, no
  mortality framing. Confirmed public appearances into 2026 (Yale Sept 2025,
  Discovery Science Center Feb 2026, Downtown Hays Science Festival May
  2026). His reported "I heard you're going to break our record" line (Apr
  2026, re: Artemis II) is attributed-to-outlet, not independently pinned to
  a primary recording — hedge it if used. CONFIRMED (status), UNVERIFIED
  (exact wording of the Artemis II remark).
- **Ken Mattingly** — Original prime Command Module Pilot, pulled from the
  flight ~72 hours before launch after backup LM pilot Charlie Duke came
  down with rubella and flight surgeons determined Mattingly (unlike Lovell/
  Haise/Duke, who were already immune) could incubate it mid-mission. Never
  developed measles. Later helped write the real Command Module power-up
  procedure from the ground. CONFIRMED — a real, citable irony.
- **Gene Kranz** — Flight Director. Plausibly still alive (b. 1933); apply
  the same attribute-don't-dramatize standard as Haise if quoted. CONFIRMED.
- **Ed Smylie** — Crew Systems Division chief; devised the CO2-scrubber
  "mailbox" fix with his ground team. CONFIRMED.
- **Sy Liebergot** — EECOM (electrical/environmental controller) on duty;
  first realized the fuel-cell loss ("I got a feeling we've lost two fuel
  cells. I hate to put it that way" — later called by Liebergot himself "the
  understatement of the manned space program"). CONFIRMED (quote sourced via
  IEEE Spectrum from Liebergot's own published account — MEDIUM confidence,
  spot-check against the raw voice loop before using as verbatim VO).
- **Edgar M. Cortright** — Director, NASA Langley Research Center; chaired
  the Apollo 13 Review Board (8 members incl. Neil Armstrong). CONFIRMED.

## 2. TIMELINE (all GET — ground elapsed time from launch, 13:13 EST Apr 11 1970)

- **Apr 11, 1970, 2:13pm EST** — Launch, Pad 39A, Kennedy Space Center, Saturn V.
- **46:40:02 GET** — First routine fan-stir of tank 2; oxygen quantity gauge
  spikes off-scale (early, unnoticed symptom of the internal short).
- **55:52:30 GET** — Master alarm, low pressure, **hydrogen tank 1** (correction #2).
- **55:52:58 GET** — Lousma: "We'd like you to stir up your cryo tanks" (routine).
- **55:53:20 GET** — Crew stirs oxygen tank 2's fans — the command that
  triggers the short in the damaged wiring.
- **55:54:53.555 GET** — Telemetry drops 1.8s; crew hears/feels a bang. Tank 2
  ruptures, blows off SM Bay 4's outer panel, damages tank 1's lines (which
  leaks out over the next ~2 hours) and the fuel-cell shelf.
- **55:55:20 GET** — Swigert: "Okay, Houston, I believe we've had a problem
  here." (correction #3)
- **55:57:45 GET** — Fuel cell 3 fails; AC bus fails within 2 seconds — total
  loss of Command/Service Module power imminent. Lunar Module Aquarius
  becomes the "lifeboat."
- **56:09:07 GET** — Lovell, looking out the hatch window: "…it looks to me,
  looking out the hatch, that we are venting something… out into space."
- **61:29:43 GET** — Lovell manually fires the LM descent engine (35s) to
  restore a free-return trajectory (the pre-accident path was NOT free-return
  and would have missed Earth by ~4,750 km).
- **79:27:39 GET** — The "PC+2" burn (4m24s) speeds return ~10 hours, shifts
  splashdown from Indian to South Pacific Ocean. Lovell flies it manually
  using Earth's terminator as a visual reference (guidance computer powered
  down to save power).
- **~89:05–094:00 GET** — CO2 scrubber "mailbox" built and installed (Ed
  Smylie's design; two CM lithium-hydroxide canisters, duct tape, plastic
  bags, a cardboard cue card, suit hoses).
- **~125–142 GET** — Command Module reactivation from scratch, an emergency
  checklist Mission Control wrote in ~3 days (normally a 3-month job);
  cabin near 45–50°F, heavily condensation-soaked.
- **138:04:46–57 GET** — Lovell sees the SM damage after jettison: "There's
  one whole side of that spacecraft missing… the whole panel is blown out."
  (correction #5)
- **142:40:46 GET** — Entry interface. Blackout runs ~6–6.5 min, ~2.5–3 min
  longer than the predicted 3:38 (correction #10) — the overrun itself is
  what alarmed Mission Control (feared a burned-through heat shield).
- **142:54:41–44 GET / Apr 17, 1970, 12:07pm CST** — Splashdown, South
  Pacific (21°39'S, 165°23'W), near USS Iwo Jima (LPH-2). All three crew
  recovered in good health.
- **June 15, 1970** — Apollo 13 Review Board report transmitted; central
  finding: the accident "was not the result of a chance malfunction in a
  statistical sense but, rather… the result of an unusual combination of
  mistakes coupled with a somewhat deficient and unforgiving design."

## 3. THE REAL TECHNICAL ROOT CAUSE

1. **Oct 1968** — Oxygen tank 2's shelf accidentally dropped ~2 inches during
   removal from an earlier Command/Service Module (a bolt was left in place,
   a lifting fixture broke). Passed post-drop testing; installed in SM-109
   (the module that flew Apollo 13). Board rated resulting-damage probability
   "low," but flagged it as a contributing factor.
2. **Mar 16–27, 1970** — During the Countdown Demonstration Test at KSC,
   oxygen tank 1 drained normally; **tank 2 would not drain past ~92%**
   through its normal vent line, repeatedly.
3. **The fatal fix** — Technicians used the tank's internal heaters (powered
   by 65V DC ground support equipment) plus fan-driven boiling, ~8 cumulative
   hours including one 1.5-hour continuous stretch, to force the oxygen out.
   The tank's thermostatic switch was rated for only 28V DC (the CSM's normal
   in-flight voltage) and had never been requalified for 65V. Post-flight
   testing proved it **welded shut at 65V** instead of opening at 80°F as
   designed — internal temperatures reached up to **1,000°F**.
4. **Teflon insulation on the fan-motor/heater wiring was destroyed** by that
   heat (correction #1) — invisible from outside the sealed, welded tank.
   Nobody knew. The pre-launch review focused on the (ultimately irrelevant)
   loose-fill-tube theory and never flagged the extended heater run.
5. **Apr 13, 1970** — The routine fan-stir command arcs the damaged wiring,
   igniting the oxygen atmosphere inside tank 2; it ruptures.

  WHO above; attribute every claim.
- **Jim Lovell died Aug 7, 2025** (see correction #7 for cause). Family
  statement (verified full text): "…he was Dad, Granddad, and the Leader of

  as of this research — don't claim one. See correction #8 for the
  Nolan-conflation trap.
- **Artemis II broke Apollo 13's 56-year distance record** on **April 6,

  resonant closer/coda material found in this research — recommend using it
