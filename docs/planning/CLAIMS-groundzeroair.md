## SECTION O — The 2001 assurances (the setup)

**O1. CONFIRMED.** EPA Administrator Christine Todd Whitman 🧍 publicly stated on
September 18, 2001 that "their air is safe to breathe and their water is safe to
drink." Text preserved at https://avalon.law.yale.edu/sept11/epa_002.asp
**VERBATIM NOTE (verification pass): the Avalon primary text reads "safe to BREATH"
— an apparent period typo in the transcribed release. If the quote is shown on
screen as a verbatim exhibit, reproduce it as it appears and do not silently
"correct" it. Spoken narration says "breathe."** The EPA newsroom archive mirror
403'd this session and was not independently read. *This is the sentence the whole episode hangs on.*

**O2. CONFIRMED — EPA's own Inspector General.** When EPA made that declaration,
it did not have the data to support it. OIG Report 2003-P-00012 (Aug 21, 2003)
lays out in its own Table 2-2 sampling timeline that PM2.5/PM10 sampling did not
begin until September 21 — *after* the "safe to breathe" statement — and that
data for PCBs, dioxin and PAHs was likewise incomplete.
Exhibit: `ex_sampling_1.png`, `ex_sampling_2.png`.
PDF: https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-12/documents/wtc_report_20030821.pdf

**O3. CONFIRMED — VERIFIED ON SCREEN BY ME, page 26 of the PDF (printed p.16).**
The OIG report reproduces Table 2-4, "Impact of CEQ Instruction on September 16
EPA Press Release." The report states in its own words: *"Every change that was
suggested by the CEQ contact was made. The CEQ official's suggested changes added
reassuring statements and deleted cautionary statements."* The table shows:
- **Deleted from the draft and not replaced:** "The concern raised by these
  samples would be for the workers at the cleanup site and for those workers who
  might be returning to their offices on or near Water Street on Monday,
  September 17, 2001."
- **Draft:** "Seven debris and dust samples taken Thursday, showed levels of
  asbestos ranging from 2.1 percent to 3.3 percent. EPA views a 1 percent level
  of asbestos as the definition for asbestos-containing material."
  **Issued:** "…ranging from 2.1 to 3.3 — slightly above the 1 percent trigger
  for defining asbestos material."
- **CEQ instruction, verbatim:** *"INSERT HENSHAW quote somewhere around here"* →
  produced the issued line "Our tests show that it is safe for New Yorkers to go
  back to work in New York's financial district," attributed to John L. Henshaw 🧍,
  Assistant Secretary of Labor for OSHA.
- The OIG also notes its analysis was limited because **CEQ officials declined to
  meet with it.**
Exhibit: `ex_ceqredline_1.png`. **This is the single best frame in the episode.**

**O3a. CONFIRMED — promoted to its own beat (ethics review finding 15).** When EPA's
own independent watchdog tried to establish how those edits happened, **CEQ officials
declined to meet with the investigators.** The OIG states this limitation itself, in
its own report. This is the OIG's own documented experience — zero defamation risk,
zero inference — and it is one of the cleanest, most damning facts in the file.
**Safe to state in the narrator's own voice.** It deserves its own scene, not a
subordinate clause.

**O3b. CONFIRMED — VERIFIED BY ME, page 25 of the PDF (new find, not in the fan-out).**
The report records an instruction from the White House Chief of Staff's office to
senior EPA officials that *"All statements to the media should be cleared through the
NSC [National Security Council] before they are released,"* and that per the EPA Chief
of Staff, one particular CEQ official was designated to work with EPA to ensure that
clearance was obtained. This establishes the **clearance structure** — institutional,
documented, and it explains the mechanism behind O3 without asserting anyone's motive.
Exhibit: extract page 25 as `ex_nscclearance_1.png` before scripting this beat.

**O4. CONFIRMED.** The OIG report records that the desire to reopen Wall Street
was a factor in the messaging. Exhibit: `ex_wallstreet_1.png`, `ex_safetobreathe_1.png`.
*Script note: state what the report says; do not extrapolate to motive beyond it.*

**O5. CONFIRMED (peer-reviewed / USGS).** The dust was pulverized concrete,
gypsum and glass fibers with trace-to-locally-high asbestos, and was strongly
alkaline — pH roughly 9 to 11.5 — plus lead, PAHs, dioxins and PCBs per EPA's own
sampling. Sources: Lioy et al. 2002 (Environmental Health Perspectives); USGS
Open-File Report 01-0429, https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2001/ofr-01-0429/
**Caveat: both primary sources were unreachable this session (USGS 403'd, EHP not

9.3-11.5) per secondary description, but this is PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, not a direct read.**
*This is why "caustic" is accurate and not editorializing.*
