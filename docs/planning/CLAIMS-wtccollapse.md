## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **NIST is explicit and unambiguous: no structural steel melted.** The
   mechanism is *strength loss from sustained heat*, not melting — steel at
   ~1,000°C (the fire temperatures NIST modeled) retains roughly 10% of its
   room-temperature strength, which is enough to explain the sagging/buckling
   NIST documents without any steel reaching its ~1,300–1,500°C melting range.
   Use exactly this framing: "the fires didn't melt the steel — they weakened
   it." Aluminum from the aircraft fuselage DID melt (~475–640°C) — that is
   the source of the molten material seen falling from the South Tower (see
   item 6). Never conflate the two metals.

2. **7 World Trade Center is a separate building, not a "third twin tower."**
   Completed 1987 — 14 years after the Twin Towers — on a different site
   footprint (north of the plaza), using a genuinely different structural
   system: an exterior moment-resisting frame (four perimeter moment frames,
   two-story belt trusses), not the Twin Towers' framed-tube system. State
   this disambiguation in the origins section before the collapse-mechanics
   chapters, or viewers will assume all three buildings failed the same way —
   they did not.

3. **The free-fall period is a REAL, NIST-CONFIRMED finding — not a truther
   fabrication, and not proof of demolition either. Precision required in
   both directions.** NIST's own final report (NCSTAR 1A, Nov 2008) documents
   that WTC 7's exterior north face descended in three measured stages, the
   middle of which — approximately 2.25 seconds, roughly eight stories —
   matched gravitational acceleration with no measurable structural
   resistance. State plainly: "NIST's own investigation measured and
   confirmed this." NIST's explanation is that the interior core columns
   beneath the observed section (including Column 79 and its neighbors) had
   already buckled and failed near-simultaneously across multiple floors, so
   the exterior facade encountered negligible resistance from below during
   that specific window — this is NOT the same claim as "the entire
   building's structural system failed simultaneously via synchronized
   demolition charges." Separately and honestly: NIST's own public position
   on this shifted over time — at its August 21, 2008 press briefing, lead

## CLAIMS BY ACT (chronological)

### Act I — Origins & construction (1966–2001)

- Port Authority of New York and New Jersey developed the WTC complex; ground
  broken 1966; first tenants moved into the still-unfinished North Tower Dec
  1970; formal dedication/ribbon-cutting April 4, 1973, Governor Nelson
  Rockefeller presiding. **CONFIRMED** (911memorial.org; Skyscraper Museum).
- Twin Towers: 110 stories each; North Tower 1,368 ft to roof (1,730 ft incl.
  later antenna), South Tower 1,362 ft; briefly the tallest buildings in the
  world (1972–73) until Chicago's Sears Tower in 1973 — frame as "among the
  tallest at the time," per correction #9. **CONFIRMED.**
- Architect: Minoru Yamasaki (Minoru Yamasaki Associates, with Emery Roth &
  Sons as associate architect). Structural engineering: Worthington, Skilling,
  Helle & Jackson; John Skilling, chief structural engineer; Leslie E.
  Robertson, lead project structural engineer, most closely credited
  historically with the towers' structural system. **CONFIRMED** (minor
  attribution-emphasis variance across sources — doesn't need resolving for
  this script).
- **The tube-frame structural system (the single most load-bearing
  engineering fact for this episode):** a perimeter "tube" of 236 closely
  spaced high-strength steel columns (59 per face above the lobby), tied at
  every floor by deep spandrel plates, carrying nearly all lateral (wind)
  load. A separate central core of 47–51 columns carried gravity loads only
  (elevators, stairwells, utility risers) — NOT designed to resist lateral
  wind load. Bridging the ~60-ft span between perimeter tube and core:
  lightweight, prefabricated steel bar-joist floor trusses under a thin
  (4-inch) lightweight concrete-on-corrugated-steel-deck floor slab. Chosen
  specifically to eliminate interior columns, freeing ~40,000 sq ft of
  column-free floor per level — a Port Authority requirement to maximize
  rentable office space — at the cost of using markedly less structural
  steel per square foot than conventional "heavy" skyscraper framing of the
  era. **CONFIRMED** (NIST NCSTAR 1 overview; MIT WTC materials/structures
  chapter). This is exactly what the fire/collapse-mechanics chapters need
  set up in advance.
- **7 World Trade Center is a separate building — see correction #2.** 47
  stories, completed 1987, north of the Twin Towers plaza, exterior
  moment-resisting frame (four perimeter moment frames + two-story belt
  trusses), not the framed-tube system. **CONFIRMED** (NIST NCSTAR 1; FEMA
  403 Ch. 5).
- **1993 WTC bombing (prior-attack context only — see correction #10).** Feb
  26, 1993: a truck bomb (~1,200 lbs urea-nitrate-fuel-oil-enhanced
  explosive) detonated on the B-2 underground parking level beneath the
  North Tower, killing 6, injuring roughly 1,000 (mostly smoke inhalation),
  ~$300M damage. The towers survived structurally, reopened, and operated 8
  more years. **CONFIRMED** (History.com; Britannica; 9/11 Memorial &
  Museum). Mechanism unrelated to the 2001 collapse — one sentence only, no
  causal implication.

### Act II — September 11, 2001: impact (brief, non-graphic, engineering-only)

- American Airlines Flight 11 struck the North Tower (WTC 1) at 8:46:40 a.m.
  between floors 93–99; United Airlines Flight 175 struck the South Tower
  (WTC 2) at 9:03:11 a.m. between floors 77–85. **CONFIRMED** (NIST NCSTAR
  1-2 series).
- Impact damage: in WTC 1, roughly half the exterior columns on the north
  face were severed or damaged (34 severed, 4 heavily damaged, 2 moderately
  damaged) and 3 core columns severed (4 more heavily damaged); in WTC 2, 29
  exterior columns severed (1 heavily damaged) and 5 core columns severed (4
  more heavily damaged). **CONFIRMED.**
- The impacts also stripped spray-applied fireproofing from steel over a
  wide area beyond the columns actually severed — trusses, connections, and
  surviving core columns were left with bare or thinly coated steel. NIST's
  own conclusion: the towers "would likely not have collapsed under the
  combined effects of aircraft impact damage and the extensive, multi-floor
  fires alone" — dislodged fireproofing is a named, necessary third factor,
  not an afterthought. **CONFIRMED** — use this exact three-factor framing
  (impact + fireproofing loss + fire), not just "impact and fire."

### Act III — Fire, weakening, and collapse: WTC 1 and WTC 2

- Jet fuel ignited fires but burned off within minutes; sustained fires were
  fed primarily by ordinary building contents (furniture, paper, carpet),
  spreading across ~40,000 sq ft on multiple floors per tower, reaching up
  to ~1,000°C in localized areas — comparable to other unsuppressed high-rise
  fires historically. **CONFIRMED.**
- No steel melted (correction #1) — strength loss, not melting; bare steel at
  ~1,000°C retains roughly 10% of its room-temperature strength.
  **CONFIRMED, exact phrasing to bake in.**
- With fireproofing gone, sagging composite floor trusses (still tied to the
  exterior wall via shear studs/diagonal struts) pulled the perimeter columns
  inward — the visually documented "inward bowing." NIST measured ~55 ± 6
  inches of inward bow on WTC 1's south face at floor 97, first visible about
  five minutes before collapse (~10:23 a.m.). **CONFIRMED.**
- Collapse-initiation event (bowed, overloaded perimeter columns buckling):
  WTC 2 at 9:59 a.m. (56 minutes after impact), WTC 1 at 10:28 a.m. (102
  minutes after impact). WTC 2 collapsed first despite a later, shorter fire
  — attributed to its impact geometry causing more direct core-column damage
  and more asymmetric fire distribution. **CONFIRMED.**
- **Scope note, important for accuracy:** NIST's structural analysis modeled
  *collapse initiation*, not a floor-by-floor simulation of the entire
  subsequent fall. For why the fall was total and unstoppable, NIST makes an
  energy argument instead: the undamaged structure below could have absorbed
  the sudden dynamic load of only about six additional floors, while 12–29
  floors of upper-building mass came down on it — "neither tower could have
  arrested the progression of collapse once collapse initiated" (NCSTAR 1,
  §6.14.4). **CONFIRMED, exact quote.**

### Act IV — WTC 7: damage, fire, and the Column 79 mechanism

- Not struck by either aircraft. Falling debris from the North Tower's
  collapse caused structural damage to WTC 7's lower floors and ignited
  fires on multiple floors; the same debris severed the New York City water
  main serving as both the primary and backup source for WTC 7's standpipe
  and sprinkler systems on floors 1–20 — no working fire suppression on the
  affected floors. **CONFIRMED.**
- Fires burned uncontrolled for roughly seven hours, concentrated on the
  building's east side (floors 7–9 and 11–13), before the building collapsed
  at 5:20:52 p.m. **CONFIRMED.**
- NIST's own framing: "The fires on multiple floors in WTC 7 were
  uncontrolled but otherwise similar to fires experienced in other tall
  buildings, [and] caused an extraordinary event" — the first known instance
  of fire alone causing the total collapse of a tall steel structure.
  **CONFIRMED, exact phrasing** — this is NIST's own description of why the
  case draws heightened scrutiny, not a script embellishment.
- **The mechanism, precisely (this is the technical core of the episode):**
  thermal expansion, not simple weakening. Long-span steel floor beams and
  girders in WTC 7's core (notably around floor 13) were heated unevenly and
  thermally expanded — critically, at temperatures "hundreds of degrees below
  those typically considered in current practice for fire-resistance
  ratings." A heated floor-13 girder spanning between Column 44 and Column
  79 expanded enough to lose its seated connection — it "walked off" its
  bearing seat rather than fracturing — leaving Column 79 laterally
  unsupported over roughly nine stories. The column buckled, triggering an
  upward-then-cascading progression of floor failures (first visible on
  video as the building's east mechanical penthouse dropping into the
  structure) that spread across the core and then the exterior frame.
  **CONFIRMED.**
- Dr. Shyam Sunder's own summary, verbatim: **"Heating of floor beams and
  girders caused a critical support column to fail, initiating a
  fire-induced progressive collapse that brought the building down."**
  **CONFIRMED — attribute to Sunder by name.**
- NIST states plainly: **"neither explosives nor fuel oil fires played a
  role in the collapse of WTC 7"** — corrects the "diesel generator fuel"
  misconception (correction #6). NIST separately rejects thermite-based
  demolition on practical grounds: it would require many thousands of pounds
  of material placed in direct contact with hundreds of structural members,
  undetected. **CONFIRMED.**

**Beat A — Free-fall speed + absence of blast evidence (~10–12s):**
- *Claim, stated plainly:* "WTC 7 fell essentially straight down, at the
  speed of gravity, for over two seconds — and if explosives brought it
  down, some ask, why wasn't a blast ever heard on tape?"
- *Rebuttal, NIST-sourced:* NIST's own final report confirms the measurement
  — roughly 2.25 seconds of free-fall-equivalent acceleration for the
  observed exterior section (state this as real, not a myth). NIST's
  explanation: the core columns beneath that section, including Column 79,
  had already buckled and failed near-simultaneously — not evidence the
  whole building failed via synchronized charges. On blast evidence
  specifically: per **NIST's WTC 7 Investigation FAQ, Question 33** (direct
  quote, citable on screen as "per NIST's own published FAQ"): a
  demolition-scale blast capable of failing the critical column "would have
  resulted in a sound level of 130 decibels (dB) to 140 dB at a distance of
  at least half a mile" — no such sound appears on any available video audio
  track or witness report.
- *Precision note (must be included, per correction #3):* one sentence
  acknowledging NIST initially called free fall "physically impossible" at
  its August 21, 2008 briefing before its November 2008 final report
  quantified it — state as fact, not as proof of anything beyond the
  sequence itself. Credit David Chandler individually as the primary
  publicly-documented challenger whose video analysis pushed for this
  correction (correction #3/Act VI) — do not pair his name equally with
  Steven Jones here.

**Beat B — Molten metal / thermite (~8–10s):**
- *Claim, stated plainly:* "Witnesses saw molten metal pouring from the
  towers, and a later study claimed to find thermite in the dust — signs,
  some say, of incendiary demolition charges."
- *Rebuttal, NIST-sourced:* the glowing material from the South Tower is
  attributed to molten aluminum from the aircraft (melts far below the
  fire's ~1,000°C), not molten steel. On thermite: the 2009 Harrit et al.
  paper (*The Open Chemical Physics Journal*, 9 co-authors including Steven
  Jones, lead author Niels Harrit) claiming thermitic residue was published
  in a journal whose own editor-in-chief later publicly distanced himself
  from it, and is not accepted by NIST or the broader structural-engineering
  community; NIST found no evidence supporting explosives/thermite use in
  the first place, and per **NIST's WTC 7 FAQ, Question 34**, calculates
  that "at least 100 lbs. of thermite would need to be placed around
  [each] column, ignited, and remain in contact" — implausible to place
  undetected on multiple columns.

**Beat C — The Silverstein "pull it" quote (~8–10s, DO NOT RESOLVE):**

**Beat D — The BBC's early report (~6–8s):**
