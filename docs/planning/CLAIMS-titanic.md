## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **"Unsinkable" was NOT an advertising slogan the public saw pre-voyage.**
   The trade journal *The Shipbuilder* (1911) and a White Star brochure called
   the watertight-compartment system "practically/virtually unsinkable" —
   qualified engineering language for an engineer readership. The unqualified
   "advertised as unsinkable" claim solidified in newspaper coverage AFTER the
   sinking. Script says "the trade press called her practically unsinkable,"
   never "White Star's ad campaign called her unsinkable."
2. **Titanic legally EXCEEDED the lifeboat minimum — the failure is
   regulatory, not corner-cutting.** British Board of Trade rules (Merchant
   Shipping Act 1894) scaled lifeboat count to gross TONNAGE, capped at the
   "over 10,000 tons" bracket — last revised when the biggest liner considered
   was ~13,000 tons. Titanic (46,328 tons) was regulated identically to a ship
   a third her size. Legal minimum was 16 boats; Titanic carried 20 (14
   standard + 2 cutters + 4 collapsibles) — MORE than required — with
   certified capacity for 1,178 of ~2,224 aboard (~53%). Never say "White Star
   cut costs on lifeboats"; say "the ship carried more boats than the outdated
   law required, and it still wasn't enough."
3. **Casualty toll is a disputed range — never state one hard number.**
   British Board of Trade figure ≈1,490; US Senate figure = 1,517; modern
   compiled lists run 1,496-1,517 depending on manifest reconciliation. Use
   "somewhere between 1,490 and 1,517" or "roughly 1,500," never a single
   precise figure as flat fact.
4. **Frederick Fleet's exact words: "Iceberg, right ahead."** No exclamation
   point, no embellishment — that's the verbatim US Senate/British inquiry
   testimony. Sixth Officer Moody's reply was "Thank you," not a dramatized
   line.
5. **The Mesaba ice warning (7:50-9:40pm range across sources) was never
   relayed to the bridge — it was left on the wireless-room table**, not
   ignored by the captain. Separately, wireless operator Jack Phillips did cut
   off the Californian's Cyril Evans ("Shut up, shut up, I am busy; I am
   working Cape Race") around 11:00pm — that exchange IS on the record under
   oath (Evans's sworn testimony). But the "paperweight" story — that Phillips
   pinned the Mesaba warning under a paperweight and later confessed this to
   Lightoller in a lifeboat — has ZERO documentary support. Treat as legend;
   EXCLUDE from the script as fact.
6. **The Californian incident stays DISPUTED, framed as "the inquiry found,"
   never asserted as flat guilt.** British Mersey Report: the Californian
   "could have pushed through the ice... and so have come to the assistance of
   the Titanic... [and] might have saved many if not all of the lives that
   were lost." US Senate: recommended "drastic action" against Captain Stanley
   Lord. Lord spent from 1958 until his 1962 death (and his son after him)
   trying to overturn this finding; Board of Trade petitions were rejected in
   1965 and 1968. 🧍 **Living researchers Dr. Paul Lee and journalist Senan
   Moloney actively argue a third "mystery ship" was between the two
   vessels** (also promoted in PBS's 2020 "Secrets of the Dead") — attribute
   any pro-Lord argument to them by name, never assert it as the settled
   answer, and never assert the anti-Lord inquiry findings as flat fact either
   — both sides are "the inquiry found X" / "researcher Y disputes it."
7. **The "women and children first" class-survival gap is CONFIRMED as an
   outcome (first-class women ~97% survived vs third-class overall ~25%), but
   the CAUSE was never formally investigated by either inquiry** (the British
   inquiry didn't interview third-class survivors and found no evidence of
   deliberate discrimination). The "gates were locked to keep them down"
   narrative is UNVERIFIED by the inquiries themselves — state the survival
   disparity as fact, do not assert deliberate barring as fact.
8. **Captain Edward Smith: the two inquiries diverge — present both,
   attributed.** British: not negligent (he followed the era's accepted
   practice of maintaining speed through ice) but that PRACTICE itself was
   condemned ("it is to be hoped that the last has been heard of this
   practice"). US Senate: harsher — his "indifference to danger was one of
   the direct and contributing causes of this unnecessary tragedy." Never
   pick one verdict as "the truth"; both are real, both are attributed to
   their inquiry.
9. **Bruce Ismay: never call him "a coward" as narrator fact.** He survived by
   boarding a collapsible after (per his own inquiry testimony) helping load
   others nearby; contemporary press (especially Hearst papers) branded him a
   coward for decades. Some historians have since taken a more sympathetic
   view (e.g. his granddaughter, the late medieval historian Pauline
   Matarasso, d.2023). Attribute the "coward" framing to contemporary press
   reaction, not settled historical judgment.
10. **No living Titanic survivors exist** — Millvina Dean, the last survivor,
    died 31 May 2009. Sensitivity gate for the core 1912 figures is
    substantially LOWER than recent aviation episodes (Concorde/AI171): all
    named principals (Ismay, Smith, Lord, Phillips, Bride, Fleet) are
    deceased. The only 🧍 living-person handling needed is for the ACTIVE
    Californian-defense researchers (correction #6) and safe, clearly-
    attributed quotes from Robert Ballard (wreck discoverer, alive) and James
    Cameron (director, alive — "as accurate as I could make it at the time,"
    safe if attributed to him).
11. **Southampton "departure day" footage commonly circulated online is
    misattributed Olympic film** (name blotted out on the original print) —
    no genuine surviving footage of Titanic's actual April 10 1912 departure
    exists. Never caption a Southampton-departure clip as Titanic; the only
    verified authentic Titanic motion-picture footage is seven shots from
    3 Feb 1912 (Belfast, being moved into the Thompson graving dock).
12. **The famous "iceberg photo"** (taken by a steward aboard the Prinz
    Adalbert the morning after) is only a speculative match to the actual
    berg — flag as unverified/disputed if used at all, don't caption it as
    confirmed.
13. **All Ballard/WHOI wreck-discovery imagery and every RMS Titanic Inc.
    salvage-expedition photo/video since 1987 is NOT public domain** — US
    federal courts (*Lindsay v. RMS Titanic*) confirmed RMST's exclusive
    salvor-in-possession rights over wreck-site photography. Avoid entirely,
    including Discovery/History Channel expedition documentaries.

## ACT 1 — THE SHIP AND THE FLAW (origins, the myth, the regulatory gap)

- **[CONFIRMED]** Olympic-class conceived 1907 when White Star chairman
  **J. Bruce Ismay** met Harland & Wolff's **Lord Pirrie** specifically to
  answer Cunard's new speed-record liners Lusitania/Mauretania — White Star's
  answer was size/luxury over speed, not a speed contest. White Star had been
  a wholly-owned subsidiary of J.P. Morgan's International Mercantile Marine
  Co. since 1902; Ismay was IMM president 1904-1913.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Keel laid 31 March 1909 (Harland & Wolff yard no. 401);
  launched 31 May 1911 (~10,000 spectators); ~26-month build; cost ~£1.5
  million; ~3,000 workers on Titanic directly (~15,000 at the yard's peak).
  **8 shipyard workers killed during construction** (28 serious + 218 minor
  recorded accidents).
- **[CONFIRMED]** Specs: length 882'9", gross tonnage 46,328; departed
  Southampton 10 April 1912 with ~2,224 aboard against a designed max capacity
  of 3,547 (840 staterooms).
- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #1]** "Practically unsinkable" — trade-press/
  brochure language, not a public ad slogan; the unqualified "unsinkable"
  claim is largely post-disaster media amplification.
- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #2]** The regulatory flaw: Board of Trade
  lifeboat rules scaled to tonnage, last revised for ~13,000-ton ships;
  Titanic (46,328 tons) legally required only 16 boats, carried 20, certified
  for 1,178 of ~2,224 aboard.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Seven documented ice warnings reached Titanic on 14 April
  1912: Caronia (9:00am, delivered to bridge), Baltic (~1:42pm, delivered to
  Capt. Smith, shown to Ismay), Amerika (position only), Californian
  (~5:00-7:30pm, delivered to bridge), Mesaba (~7:50-9:40pm across sources —
  explicit ice-field coordinates directly in Titanic's path, never relayed to
  the bridge, correction #5).
- **[CONFIRMED]** ~11:00pm: Californian wireless operator Cyril Evans warned
  "we are stopped and surrounded by ice"; Titanic's Jack Phillips (busy
  relaying passenger traffic via Cape Race) replied "Shut up, shut up, I am
  busy; I am working Cape Race." Evans shut his set down for the night
  shortly after — before the 11:40pm collision.
- 🧍 **[DISPUTED, see correction #9]** Ismay's later reputation as a "coward"
  is a press framing, not a settled historical verdict.

## ACT 2 — THE NIGHT (the collision, the sinking, the Californian)

- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #4]** Collision **11:40pm ship's time, 14
  April 1912**, speed ~22 knots, never reduced despite the day's warnings.
  Lookout **Frederick Fleet** struck the crow's-nest bell three times,
  telephoned the bridge; Sixth Officer Moody asked what he saw; Fleet:
  "Iceberg, right ahead." Ship sank **2:20am, 15 April 1912**.
- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #3]** Casualties: disputed range, ~1,490-1,517.
- 🧍 **[CONFIRMED — cold-open candidate]** ~1:42pm 14 April: Captain Smith
  personally handed Ismay the Baltic's ice warning at lunch; Ismay pocketed it,
  showed it to fellow first-class passengers Mrs. Emily Ryerson and Mrs.
  Marian Thayer that afternoon as a curiosity, then returned it to Smith
  before supper to be posted in the officers' chart room. Sourced to
  Ryerson's own deposition (Limitation of Liability hearings) plus Ismay's own
  inquiry testimony.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Captain **Stanley Lord** of the Californian was woken twice
  about unidentified rockets in the distance that night; testified he
  suggested they might be "company rockets" and, asked their color, testified
  "I do not know; I was asleep."
- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #7]** Survival by class: first-class women
  ~97% survived vs. third-class overall ~25% — a documented disparity; the
  British inquiry did not interview third-class survivors and found no
  evidence of deliberate discrimination in its own investigation.

## ACT 3 — THE VERDICT (the two inquiries, the legacy, what happened after)

- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #8]** The two official inquiries diverge on
  Captain Smith: British — not negligent, condemned the *practice* of holding
  speed through ice; US Senate — his "indifference to danger" was a direct
  contributing cause. Present both, attributed to their own inquiry.
- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #6]** The Californian finding: British Mersey
  Report concluded she could likely have reached survivors in time; US Senate
  recommended "drastic action" against Lord. Genuinely disputed since 1912;
  living researchers still argue a "mystery ship" theory (correction #6) —
  attribute, never assert.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **SOLAS (Safety of Life at Sea), the first convention,
  signed 20 January 1914** — 13 nations, London conference Nov 1913-Jan
  1914 — direct response to Titanic: lifeboats for everyone aboard (ending the
  tonnage-based formula), mandatory 24-hour radio watch (direct response to
  Californian's wireless operator being off-duty/asleep and missing the
  distress calls), and creation of the International Ice Patrol.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Ismay retired from public life, remained in charity work;
  diabetes led to a leg amputation in 1936; died of a stroke Oct 1937.
  Wireless operator **Jack Phillips died in the sinking**; **Harold Bride
  survived**, avoided celebrity, died 1956. Captain Lord died 1962, having
  spent his final years (via the Mercantile Marine Service Association)
  trying to clear his name; 1965 and 1968 Board of Trade petitions to reopen
  the finding were both rejected.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Wreck found **1 September 1985** by **Robert Ballard**
  (alive) during a then-secret US Navy mission, declassified 2008 — his own
  attributed quotes about the discovery are safe to use. Wreck protected under
  a 2001 UNESCO convention + 2003/2020 US-UK bilateral agreement; RMS Titanic
  Inc. holds salvor-in-possession rights (correction #13) and told courts in
  Dec 2023 it plans no new expedition.
- **[CONFIRMED — safe, one-line bridge]** OceanGate's Titan submersible
  imploded 18 June 2023 while diving TO the Titanic wreck, killing 5 — real
  connection, not a stretch, since this channel already has an OceanGate
  episode; use as the verbal-bridge "next video" tease, not as a scene.
- **[CONFIRMED, safe]** James Cameron's 1997 film drove the modern public's
  entire mental image of the disaster; Cameron (alive) has called it "as
  accurate as I could make it at the time" — useful for a brief "what the
  movie got right vs. what the real inquiries found" beat, a angle Oceanliner
  Designs and peers haven't run this way.
