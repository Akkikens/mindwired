## 1. Origins — the station that almost died (1984–1993)

- **CONFIRMED.** Jan 25 1984, Reagan SOTU: "Tonight, I am directing NASA to
  develop a permanently manned space station and to do it within a decade."
  Promised cost: **$8 billion**. (NASA History; Reagan Library.)
- **CONFIRMED.** Freedom burned **$11.2B by 1993 without launching
  anything** (CRS testimony, Marcia S. Smith, House Science Cmte, Apr 4
  2001, PDF on nasa.gov).
- **CONFIRMED — the best "it almost never existed" beat.** Jun 23 1993: the
  Roemer amendment to kill the station failed in the House by **ONE VOTE,
  216–215**. CRS counts **22 congressional termination votes** survived.
- **CONFIRMED.** Sept 1993: Clinton merges the redesign with Russia's
  planned Mir-2 → "International" Space Station. **Zvezda's hull was
  fabricated in the mid-1980s as the Mir-2 core block** (RussianSpaceWeb) —
  the station's backbone is Soviet hardware older than most viewers.
- **CONFIRMED.** GAO NSIAD-98-147 (May 1998): projected total US cost
  ~**$96B** — pre-launch, already 12× Reagan's number.

## 2. Assembly (1998–2011)

- **Zarya ("Dawn"/FGB)** — **CONFIRMED.** Launched Nov 20 1998, Proton-K,
  Baikonur Site 81. Built by Khrunichev (Moscow) under subcontract to
  Boeing, paid by NASA (~$220M) — **the first, Russian-built module is
  legally American-owned.** Constantly confused with Zvezda — don't.
- **Unity (Node 1) / STS-88** — **CONFIRMED.** Launched Dec 4 1998; Nancy
  Currie grappled Zarya Dec 6; Ross & Newman connected them over 3 EVAs
  (7h21m/7h02m/6h59m). **Dec 10 1998: Cabana and Krikalev entered side by
  side** — first entry. Cabana 🧍 (NASA 20th-anniv event, 2018): "I felt it

## 5. Physical stats (NASA Facts & Figures + flags)

**CONFIRMED (with CORRECTION #10/#16 flags):** mass 925,335 lb (419,725 kg);
pressurized volume 35,491 ft³ (1,005 m³), habitable 13,696 ft³ (388 m³) —
NASA's analogy "larger than a six-bedroom house" with 2 bathrooms, a gym, a
360° bay window; 8 miles of wire; >50 computers; ~1.5M lines of flight code
(US segment); orbit ~250 mi (~400–420 km), 51.6°, ~5 mi/s (~28,000 km/h),
~90 min/orbit, **16 sunrises/day**; crew of seven.

**WHY 51.6° — CONFIRMED:** Baikonur sits at ~46°N — you can't efficiently
launch below your latitude, and launching due east would drop spent stages
(and aborts) on **China**; the constrained azimuth gives 51.6° (same as
Salyut/Mir). Shuttle reached it from 28.5°N Florida at a payload penalty.
Bonus: the track overflies ~90% of Earth's population. (eol.jsc.nasa.gov
orbit tutorial; Newsweek.)

## 6. Life support — air & water

- **OGA — CONFIRMED:** electrolyzes recovered water; 5–20 lb (2.3–9 kg)
  O2/day, normal 12 lb (5.4 kg)/day; each astronaut uses ~2 lb (0.9 kg)
  O2/day (NASA ECLSS fact sheet FS-2008/174687main).
- **Elektron (Zvezda) — CONFIRMED, failure-prone:** pumps down Jan 1 2005
  (5 days troubleshooting); Sept 2006 overheat with mildly toxic potassium
  hydroxide leak; failed again Oct 2020. Backups: Progress O2 tanks + SFOG
  "candles" (lithium perchlorate — same device type that caught fire on Mir
  in 1997).
- **CDRA + Sabatier — CONFIRMED:** zeolite-bed CO2 scrubbing (Russian:
  Vozdukh); Sabatier/CRA operational since 2010 turns CO2 + waste H2 into
  water + vented methane — but **O2 recovery is limited to <50%** (reaction
  needs 4 H2 per CO2; electrolysis yields 2 — NTRS 20140012511). The air
  loop is only HALF-closed — say it.
- **CO2 human beat — CONFIRMED quotes, Scott Kelly 🧍, *Endurance* (2017):**
  "I can feel it. I can sense the levels with a high degree of accuracy
  based only on the symptoms I've come to know so well: headaches,
  congestion, burning eyes, irritability." And: "Our CO2 level could come
  down to a much more tolerable level with the flip of a switch in
  Houston… and yet we can't convince them to do this." Levels ~10× Earth's
  (CORRECTION #8).
- **Water — CONFIRMED:** WRS = UPA (vacuum-distills urine) + WPA (processes
  everything incl. humidity condensate — crew breath and sweat come back as
  drinking water). First recycled water drunk on orbit **May 20 2009**.
  **98% recovery since June 2023** (CORRECTION #9); NASA engineers: output
  is "cleaner than municipal tap water on Earth." First ground installation
  of licensed tech: **Iraq, 2006**.
- **The coffee line — CONFIRMED, multiply attributed:** Koichi Wakata 🧍
  (JAXA): "Here on board the ISS, we turn yesterday's coffee into
  tomorrow's coffee." NASA History Office (May 20 2022): "Yesterday's
  coffee is today's coffee." Jonny Kim 🧍 (Exp 73, 2025): "As the saying
  goes, yesterday's coffee is today's coffee."

## 7. Power & thermal

- **The 90-minute problem — CONFIRMED:** ~55 min sun / ~35 min eclipse per
  orbit — the station runs on batteries a third of every "day."
- **Battery swap — CONFIRMED:** 48 nickel-hydrogen → 24 lithium-ion via
  **14 spacewalks over 4 years (Jan 2017 → Feb 1 2021)**.
- **The Florida kicker — CONFIRMED, fully sourced:** with no HTV room left,
  NASA jettisoned **Exposed Pallet 9 — ~2.6 t of old NiH2 batteries — March
  2021**, the most massive object ever tossed from the ISS. Uncontrolled
  reentry **Mar 8 2024**; a **1.6-lb Inconel stanchion survived and punched
  through the roof of Alejandro Otero's house in Naples, Florida**. NASA
  confirmed the ID **Apr 15 2024** (nasa.gov blog); the family filed a
  claim against NASA.
- **Thermal — CONFIRMED:** two independent anhydrous-ammonia loops (Loop A
  on S1, Loop B on P1), ~35 kW each / ~70 kW total (NASA/Boeing ATCS
  overview). Ammonia = low freezing point (~−77°C) — and toxic.
- **Ammonia leak timeline — CONFIRMED:** P6/2B channel leaking ~5.2 lb/yr by
  mid-2012; Nov 1 2012 Williams+Hoshide reroute EVA; **May 9 2013 crew saw
  frozen ammonia flakes drifting away** — May 11 2013 emergency EVA
  (Cassidy+Marshburn, 5h30m) swapped the pump; leak stopped.
- **Jan 14 2015 ammonia false alarm — CONFIRMED:** 3:58 a.m. CST telemetry
  suggested ammonia entering the cabin water loop — crew masked up,
  **evacuated the entire US segment, sealed the hatch, sheltered in the
  Russian segment**. False alarm: erroneous Node 2 MDM computer reading.
  For hours the crew believed the thing thermal engineers fear most had
  happened.

- **Sleep — CONFIRMED:** phone-booth-sized quarters (~2 m × 1 m; 4 in
  Harmony + Nauka/Zvezda). The hook: **in microgravity your exhaled CO2

  with headaches (CSA; NTRS 20080013462).
- **Toilet — CONFIRMED:** UWMS, **$23M** development (CORRECTION #20),

  UPA → tomorrow's coffee.
- **Exercise — CONFIRMED:** ~2.5 hrs scheduled daily, 6 days/week (NASA
  OCHMO-TB-031). ARED (vacuum cylinders, up to 600 lbf, installed 2009),
  T2/COLBERT treadmill (bungee harness), CEVIS cycle. Bone: CORRECTION #14
  ranges; up to 30% lower-body muscle loss possible unprotected. Tracy
  Dyson 🧍 (Olympics.com 2024): "we get about an hour and 40 to two solid
  hours of exercise… resistive exercise… super important to maintain your
  bone density and muscle mass."
- **Food — CONFIRMED:** 200+ menu items; tortillas instead of bread (no
  crumbs; served since 1985); fresh fruit only with cargo arrivals; ~3.8 lb

  use).
- **Laundry: none — CONFIRMED:** clothes worn until dirty (underwear days,
  trousers weeks), bagged as trash, **burned up in a departing
  Progress/Cygnus**; crew of six ≈ 900 lb clothing/yr (~160 lb/person)

- **The smell of space — CONFIRMED (post-EVA airlock):** Don Pettit 🧍

## 11. Science — what it actually produced (+ the criticism)

- **Aggregate — CONFIRMED:** 4,000+ investigations, ~4,400 papers, 361 in
  2024 (CORRECTION #10); researchers from 108+ countries; ISS National Lab
  FY24: 100+ payloads, 51 peer-reviewed articles (record year); FY25: 76;
  cumulative NatLab pubs ~630. One experiment costs $250,000+ to fly (2014
  reporting).
- **⭐ THE DIFFERENTIATOR (nobody on YouTube has this) — CONFIRMED:**
  **Wang & Savin, npj Microgravity 11:34 (Jul 3 2025)** — first rigorous
  bibliometric test, building "Earth counterfactuals" (matched papers/
  patents by the SAME authors/inventors). 1,339 ISS experiments (2000–22);
  98 patents. **ISS papers +41% citations; controlling for researcher
  identity, public ISS papers +63%; private ISS papers: no significant
  difference. ISS patents +67% (public +82%; private: no meaningful
  advantage).** The value is real but concentrated in exactly the PUBLIC
  research the commercialization plan winds down. Include stated
  limitations (selection bias, no causation, incomplete recent patent
  data).
- **Twins Study — CONFIRMED, n=1 caveat mandatory:** Garrett-Bakelman et
  al., *Science* 364, eaau8650 (Apr 2019); Scott 🧍 in orbit Mar 2015–Mar
  2016, Mark 🧍 ground control. Telomeres lengthened in space then
  accelerated shortening on return; gene-expression changes 6× more in the
  second half-year; ~90% normalized after landing but some immune genes
  hyperactive 6 months later; carotid distension, ocular changes, cognitive
  decline postflight. Researchers stressed they cannot generalize; NatGeo:
  "the very epitome of a small sample size." Twins Study = roadmap, not
  conclusions — saying so earns credibility.
- **AMS-02 — CONFIRMED:** ~$2B, delivered STS-134 May 2011; positron
  fraction rises 0.05 @10 GeV → 0.15 @200–350 GeV; spectrum from 4.5M
  positrons up to 2 TeV, drop-off ~1 TeV; can't be explained by simple
  cosmic-ray collisions — but see CORRECTION #12 (no dark matter found).
  Samuel Ting 🧍: "None of the AMS results were predicted." Upgrade (2 new
  radiators + 10th tracker layer, ~+300% acceptance) targeted Feb 2026.
- **Cold Atom Lab — CONFIRMED, the cleanest "couldn't do this on Earth"
  story — lead with it over protein crystals:** first Bose-Einstein
  condensate in orbit (2018); Aug 13 2024 *Nature Communications*:
  matter-wave interference over the longest freefall ever in space (>0.1
  s), and the **first space quantum sensor via matter-wave interferometry —
  it measured the station's own vibrations with ultracold rubidium-87
  atoms.**
- **Colloids beat — CONFIRMED:** ACE-M-1 (Oct 2013–Mar 2014); **P&G used it
  to model shelf-life of Downy fabric conditioner, "a billion-dollar
  product sold worldwide"** — the "your laundry aisle contains ISS science"
  beat.
- **Bone countermeasure — CONFIRMED:** ARED + weekly alendronate together
  improve essentially all bone-physiology measures; ARED alone did not
  prevent hip trabecular decline — the drug closes the gap. Direct
  osteoporosis read-across.
- **Other verified (NASA 20-breakthroughs list):** FLEX cool flames (2.5×
  cooler than a candle, no visible flame); 250+ CubeSats deployed;
  ECOSTRESS/GEDI/OCO-3; NICER+MAXI black-hole binary MAXI J1820+070; Genes

- **LambdaVision — ALLEGED (company-sourced):** 200-layer bacteriorhodopsin

- **THE CRITICS (name + date every quote):** Steven Weinberg ⚰, Sept 2007

## 13. The international arrangement — a barter economy in orbit

- **Legal — CONFIRMED:** IGA signed **Jan 28 1998 by fifteen governments**
  (US, Russia, Japan, Canada + 11 ESA states). **Common misreport:** "15
  nations built it" implies 15 space programs — it's **five agencies,
  fifteen signatories**.
- **Barter principle — CONFIRMED (ESA's own words):** partners exchange
  goods/services "without a corresponding financial transaction, i.e.
  without an exchange of funds"; for ESA the point is explicit industrial
  policy — Europe's ISS money stays in European factories.
- **Utilization split — CONFIRMED:** non-Russian segment crew time/power:
  **NASA 76.6% · JAXA 12.8% · ESA 8.3% · CSA 2.3%** (ESA's 8.3% ≈ 13
  crew-hrs/week; Japan owes 12.8% of common ops costs). Russia runs its
  segment separately.
- **⭐ Best beat — CONFIRMED (ESA barter register, Aug 3 2000):** **the
  Cupola — the most photographed object in spaceflight — was not bought.
  Europe built it and traded it to NASA for shuttle cargo space** (transport
  of five external payloads + 68 kg extra Columbus launch mass). Other real
  deals: Oct 8 1997 — ESA gave **Nodes 2 and 3** (+freezers) for **the
  shuttle launch of Columbus** ("Nodes Barter"); Mar 18 1997 — glovebox +
  freezers for rack space + two shuttle flights for European astronauts;
  Aug 15 1997 — ESA arranged NASA's Super Guppy for 450 kg of upmass; Mar 1
  1996 — data management system for Zvezda ↔ ATV docking systems; Apr 17
  1997 — MPLM ECLSS ↔ Columbus primary structure (ASI).
- **The one cash exception — CONFIRMED:** Soyuz seats, ~$3.9B (§12) —
  replaced Jul 2022 by an actual barter: the no-funds-exchanged seat-swap.
- **Surviving 2022 — CONFIRMED:** Mar 2022, Rogozin 🧍 warned sanctions
  could disrupt reboosts and the **500-tonne** structure could "fall down
  into the sea or onto land" (ultimatum to NASA/ESA/CSA reported late Mar
  2022). **Jul 2022:** almost the moment the Kremlin announced Rogozin's
  removal, NASA and Roscosmos completed the seat-swap agreement. "The
  bluster left with Rogozin; the barter arrived." Borisov 🧍 told Putin
  "The decision to leave the station after 2024 has been made" — later
  walked back. Resolution timeline: CORRECTION #5. Scott Kelly 🧍 to TIME
  (Oct 2025): "We have no ability to kick Russia out of the club."

## 15. The ending — deorbit (2028–January 2031)

- **Timeline — CONFIRMED:** Jan 2022 ISS Transition Report → Point Nemo,
  ~2031 reentry. US/Japan/Canada/ESA committed through 2030; Russia per
  CORRECTION #5. Working end: **operate through 2030, brought down early
  2031** ("2030 vs 2031" in sources is ops-end vs splash — consistent, not
  contradictory).
- **Musk beat — CONFIRMED (X post, Feb 20 2025, via SpaceNews):** "The
  decision is up to the President, but my recommendation is as soon as
  possible. I recommend 2 years from now." NASA stood by 2030; ESA's
  Andreas Mogensen 🧍 publicly called Musk's related "stranded astronauts
  were political" claim a lie — angry exchange on record. "The man who owns
  the hearse wants the funeral early."
- **USDV — CONFIRMED:** contract Jun 26 2024 (CORRECTION #7); NASA will
  **own and operate** it — not a bought service. Vehicle (SpaceX Jul 17
  2024 briefing): Dragon derivative, trunk ~2× normal length, **46 Draco
  thrusters (16 attitude + 30 deorbit)** — some outlets say "30" (deorbit
  engines only; both right, be precise) — ~6× propellant, ≥3× power;
  final-burn thrust ~10,000 N firing 22–26 engines; delta-v ~**57 m/s
  (~127 mph)** against ~430 t. Delivery 2028, launch ~2029, docks 12–18
  months before the end. Ken Bowersox 🧍 (contract day): "…ensure a safe
  and responsible transition in low Earth orbit at the end of station
  operation."
- **The sequence — CONFIRMED (NASA Jun 2024 "ISS Deorbit Analysis Summary"
  white paper + Weigel briefing):** ~2028–29 boosting stops, drag pulls it
  down over 12–18 months → final crew launches just before USDV → crew
  stays through ~330 km, **last crew departs ~6 months before reentry at
  ~220 km** (lowest altitude crewed vehicles are rated for) — the last
  hatch closes and humanity's longest continuous off-planet presence ends →
  final week of shaping burns, then the ~57 m/s commit burn over the
  **South Pacific Oceanic Uninhabited Area (Point Nemo, ~2,688 km from
  land)** → aerodynamic breakup ~**84–72 km (nominal ~78 km)**: arrays and
  radiators tear off first, then modules shred → surviving fragments "from
  the size of a microwave oven to the size of an SUV" (NASA) in a footprint
  ~2,000 km long. The USDV dies with it. Weigel: "the vehicle's main
  purpose is to do the final series of burns that happen over the last
  week of the ISS's life as it's re-entering."
- **They had no choice — CONFIRMED (white paper):** graveyard boost
  infeasible (huge delta-v; penetrating-impact interval drops from ~51 yrs
  at operating altitude to <4 yrs near 800 km); disassembly impossible (no
  vehicle exists; industry showed no interest in the modules); uncontrolled
  decay = unacceptable casualty risk; no commercial taker.
- **Honest wrinkle — CONFIRMED:** ocean scientists/environmental-law
  experts raised concerns about sinking 250+ t of melted spacecraft at
  Point Nemo ("raises serious concerns for ocean health" — ocean-expert
  letters via Space.com/SpaceNews); NASA: toxics burn up, impact small.
  Report both. Mir came down there Mar 2001; the ISS will be by far the
  largest object ever deorbited.
- **End-of-station quote bank (all on record):** Andy Thomas 🧍: "It's a sad
  but probably inevitable milestone. To say we spent $100 billion over the
  last 25 years and we're going to dump it in the ocean is very sad…" Tom
  Jones 🧍 (dissent): "I would not like to see it dumped in the ocean" —
  boost it high to last "decades if not centuries." Bowersox 🧍: "I've
  never seen anything that's 100% in space." Robyn Gatens 🧍: "bittersweet
  to think about one day deorbiting it…" Wendy Whitman Cobb 🧍: "It hurts
  me to say it needs to come down… safety is going to trump sentimentality."
- **Closing-chapter Kelly quotes (Endurance, 2017):** "What is it worth to
  see two former bitter enemies transform weapons into transport for
  exploration…? …to me it's one of the things that makes this project worth
  the expense, even worth risking our lives." And: "Putting it into orbit —
  making it work and keeping it working — is the hardest thing that human
  beings have ever done…"
