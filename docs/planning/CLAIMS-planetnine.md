## 1. Origin & the 2016 evidence (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- Trigger: Chad Trujillo & Scott Sheppard's 2014 *Nature* paper on **2012
  VP113** noted 13 distant Kuiper Belt objects shared a strange orbital
  similarity and speculated an unseen planet might be responsible, without a
  working model. Batygin & Brown (Caltech) reanalyzed and found the real
  clustering was in **longitude of perihelion and orbital-plane
  orientation** — six of the most distant, best-determined KBOs (semi-major
  axis > 150 AU, including Sedna and 2012 VP113) all point in nearly the same
  direction and share a ~22°-tilted orbital plane. Calculated probability of
  this being random chance: **0.007% (~1-in-15,000)**.
- Predicted planet (2016 original): >10 Earth masses, avg. ~20× Neptune's
  distance, highly elongated orbit, 10,000-20,000 year period, likely a
  "mini-Neptune." **2021 refinement (Brown & Batygin, AJ 162(5)):** ~6.2
  Earth masses (+2.2/-1.3), perihelion ~300 AU (+85/-60), clustering
  significance 99.6%.
- **Real on-screen quotes (Caltech press release, Jan 20, 2016, verbatim):**
  Mike Brown: *"This would be a real ninth planet. There have only been two
  true planets discovered since ancient times, and this would be a third."*
  Konstantin Batygin: *"For the first time in over 150 years, there is solid
  evidence that the solar system's planetary census is incomplete."*
- **Mike Brown's real "Pluto killer" backstory** — genuinely great irony for
  the script: Brown co-led the team that discovered **Eris** (imaged Oct 21,
  2003 at Palomar; announced July 29, 2005), a body comparable in size to
  Pluto. That discovery directly forced the IAU's hand: on **August 24,
  2006** in Prague, the IAU created the "dwarf planet" category and demoted
  Pluto. Brown wrote a real memoir titled **"How I Killed Pluto and Why It
  Had It Coming"** (2010). The man who killed one planet is now hunting for
  a new one — media framing, not a direct Brown quote, but factually solid.

## 2. The decade-long search + the live 2026 status (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- Primary search tool: the **Subaru Telescope** (Mauna Kea), via the FOSSIL
  survey program. Also used over the decade: the Dark Energy Survey, Zwicky
  Transient Facility archive, and archival infrared data (IRAS/AKARI).
- **July 2025 — a real complication, not confirmation:** Subaru/FOSSIL
  discovered **"Ammonite" (2023 KQ14)**, only the 4th known sednoid, published
  in *Nature Astronomy*. Its orbit does NOT align with the other three
  sednoids and appears dynamically stable over time — actively weakening the
  original clustering argument. Real quote, lead researcher **Yukun Huang**
  (NAOJ): *"The fact that Ammonite's current orbit does not align with those
  of the other three sednoids lowers the likelihood of the Planet Nine
  hypothesis."*
- **The live current hook: Vera C. Rubin Observatory.** Its 10-year Legacy
  Survey of Space and Time (LSST) **officially began June 30, 2026**, after
  a "First Look" (June 2025) and an "Early Data Preview 2" release (**July
  27, 2026**) — weeks before this validation. Rubin is expected to catalog
  ~40,000 Kuiper Belt objects and is widely regarded as the decisive
  instrument. **Scott Sheppard (Carnegie): Rubin has "70-80% chance of
  finding it" if real.** **Real, current Batygin quote (The Debrief,
  2026-06-05): "We will get that new batch of discoveries basically starting
  this summer, starting summer twenty twenty-six. It will be a direct test
  of all lines of evidence."** No detection or exclusion has been announced
  as of this writing — the honest framing is "the real test just started,"
  not "the answer is imminent."
- **Other real 2025 developments, for texture:** the "Planet Y" paper (Siraj,
  Chyba, Tremaine/Princeton, Aug 2025) proposes a SEPARATE, smaller, closer
  hidden world (Mercury-to-Earth mass, 100-200 AU) based on different orbital
  evidence — explicitly distinct from Planet Nine, a good "it's not just one
  hunt" beat. A rogue-planet-capture paper (Izidoro/Kaib, Aug 2025) modeled a
  5-10% chance the solar system captured a free-floating planet rather than
  forming one in place.

## 3. The real skeptical case (CONFIRMED — present with full weight, never as a token mention)

- **Shankman et al. 2017** (OSSOS survey, AJ 154:50) and **Kevin Napier et
  al. 2021** (U. Michigan, *Planetary Science Journal*) argue the clustering
  is an **observational-bias artifact**: ETNOs are only detectable near
  perihelion, and ground surveys only look at certain patches of sky in
  certain seasons — that non-random "where/when you looked" pattern can fake
  an apparent alignment. Napier's bias-corrected analysis of 14 ETNOs found
  the real distribution statistically indistinguishable from random (17-94%
  confidence, i.e., no signal).
- **Brown & Batygin dispute this** (their own 2021 AJ paper) — both camps
  are still actively publishing; this is genuinely unresolved, not a settled
  score.
- **Competing explanations that need no hidden planet:** a self-gravitating
  disk of small icy bodies (Ann-Marie Madigan/CU Boulder) can mimic the
  clustering via collective gravity; a modified-gravity (MOND) explanation
  (Katherine Brown/Hamilton College & Harsh Mathur/Case Western, 2023).
- **Honest consensus read:** genuinely split, not resolved either way. Mike
  Brown (2024): *"I think it is very unlikely that P9 does not exist."*
  Samantha Lawler (skeptic, U. Regina): *"I don't believe that Planet Nine
  is one of them."* Pedro Bernardinelli (U. Washington, DES/Rubin survey
  scientist), the fairest single-line summary: *"The agnostic perspective
  now is that we do not have enough data either way."*
- A 2025 infrared-archive candidate (7-17 Earth masses, 500-700 AU) was
  publicly dismissed by Brown himself: *"Whether it is real or not, it is
  100 percent NOT Planet Nine"* — because its orbit doesn't match the
  clustering evidence. Good beat showing Brown's own rigor, not blind
  advocacy.
