## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **⭐ THE MOST-QUOTED LINE IN THIS STORY IS NOT A TRANSCRIPT.** Every retelling quotes
   *"We've got a bad fire — Let's get out — We're burning up."* The Board's own words about
   that second transmission: ***"The entire second transmission is garbled and is,
   therefore, subject to wide variation of interpretation… no definitive transcription is
   possible."*** The Board printed **three** candidate readings side by side. Bell Telephone
   Laboratories told them *"there will be nearly as many interpretations of what was said as
   there are qualified listeners."* **If we use it at all, it appears as the Board's three
   alternative readings, captioned as such — never as "the transcript."** This is the
   episode's thesis-level differentiator: the line everyone knows is an interpretation.
2. **"A failure of imagination" is NOT Frank Borman's.** It does not appear in his House
   (10 Apr) or Senate (11 Apr) 1967 testimony, in *Chariots for Apollo*, or in his 1999 oral
   history. It is a **line written for the actor playing Borman in HBO's *From the Earth to
   the Moon* (1998).** Do not attribute it to him. His documented equivalent, verbatim to
   the House: *"We are trying to tell you that we are confident in our management, and in
   our engineering and in ourselves. I think the question is really: Are you confident in
   us?"*
3. **No single ignition source was ever identified.** Board Finding 1, verbatim: *"No single
   ignition source of the fire was conclusively identified."* Never say "a spark from wire X
   started it." The Board's determination is a *most probable* region, not a cause.
4. **The inward hatch was not chosen because of Liberty Bell 7.** That link is encyclopedia
   framing. The documented rationale (Senate 956, and *Chariots*) is that an explosive or
   quick-opening hatch risked **accidental release in space**, which in a shirt-sleeve cabin
   meant certain death. NASA weighed one set of risks against another and wrote it down:
   *"these items had not been omitted through neglect, but because of decisions based on an
   attempt to balance one potential set of risks against another."*
5. **Hatch time: say "at least 90 seconds under normal conditions," citing the Senate.**
   Panel 3 gives 40 s (inside) and 70 s (outside) "under ideal conditions"; the Senate says
   "at least 90 seconds." Borman, asked if 90 was right: *"Approximately. It depends upon
   the training of the crew."* Do not present one clean number.
6. **Baron's report was 55 pages**, expanding toward ~500 when he died. The 169-page and
   275-page figures are encyclopedia numbers that conflict with the primary-anchored ones.
7. **The "phase-out of the incompetent" phrase could not be traced.** Do not use it.
8. **Kranz's "tough and competent" speech** exists only as he reconstructed it in his 2000
   memoir. No contemporaneous transcript. Attribute to the memoir or cut.
9. **Pad atmosphere after the redesign is DISPUTED** — *Chariots* says 16.2 psi, Wikipedia
   14.7. Say "roughly sea-level pressure, with nitrogen added."
10. **Grissom's "the conquest of space is worth the risk of life"** is cited by *Chariots* to
    "an interview several weeks before the fire" — **the original outlet was not traced.
    VERIFY BEFORE AIR or attribute as "he told an interviewer."**

## ACT 1 — A TEST NOBODY CALLED DANGEROUS

- **[CONFIRMED]** Formal name: *Space Vehicle Plugs-Out Integrated Test, OCP FO-K-0021-1,
  Spacecraft 012*. Purpose: demonstrate the vehicle in as near a flight configuration as
  practical, on simulated fuel-cell power with the umbilicals disconnected.
- **[CONFIRMED — the sentence the whole episode turns on]** *"This Plugs-Out Test had not
  been classified as hazardous because only those tests involving fueled vehicles,
  hypergolic propellants, cryogenic systems, high pressure tanks, live pyrotechnics or
  altitude chamber tests were routinely classified as hazardous."* No fuel, no pyrotechnics
  — therefore, by the rule as written, not hazardous.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Board **Finding 5**: *"Those organizations responsible for the planning,
  conduct and safety of this test failed to identify it as being hazardous."*
  **Determination 2:** ***"The test conditions were extremely hazardous."***
- **[CONFIRMED — nobody is named, and we will not invent one]** The Senate found it "had not
  been identified as hazardous by responsible officials" and "not recognized as being
  hazardous by either NASA or the contractor." **Borman, Senate, 11 April 1967:** *"I did
  not consider it as hazardous. I do not believe that anyone within the test organization or
  the program office considered it hazardous. And, this is the unfortunate trap through
  which we fell."*
- **[CONFIRMED — the Senate's explanation]** *"The committee can only conclude that NASA's
  long history of successes in testing and launching space vehicles with pure oxygen
  environments at 16.7 p.s.i. and lower pressures led to overconfidence and complacency."*
  And its preface: *"No single person bears all of the responsibility… It happened because
  many people made the mistake of failing to recognize a hazardous situation."*
- **[CONFIRMED — the detail almost nobody tells]** The emergency-egress practice was in the
  test **at the flight crew's own request**, added September 1966, because the *next* test —
  the fully fuelled Countdown Demonstration — *was* classified hazardous.
- **[CONFIRMED]** A **209-page revision** to the procedure was issued at **5:30 p.m. on 26
  January**, with four more pages at 10 a.m. on the 27th. Finding 7 says this prevented
  personnel becoming adequately familiar with it — **but did not contribute to the
  accident.** State both halves.

## ACT 2 — SIXTEEN POINT SEVEN

- **[CONFIRMED]** Finding 2: *"The test was conducted with a 16.7 pounds per square inch
  absolute, 100-percent oxygen atmosphere."* Flight pressure was to be **5 psia**.
- **[CONFIRMED — the engineering reason, and it matters]** The cabin was run ~2 psi **above**
  outside air so the **plug-type inner hatch sealed itself against internal pressure**
  (Borman, Senate) and so leaks could be detected (Faget, *Chariots*). The same pressure that
  sealed the hatch is what made it impossible to open.
- **[CONFIRMED]** The Board did **not** blame oxygen alone. Finding 9: a pure-O2 system
  *"presents severe fire hazards **if** the amount and location of combustibles… are not
  restricted and controlled."* On a diluent gas: it *"would undoubtedly reduce the risk of
  fire to a significant degree. At the same time it would introduce other operational
  problems and risks."*
- **[CONFIRMED]** In a 16.5 psia oxygen atmosphere, *"sparks blown from arcs can ignite
  combustible material several inches from the arc."*
- **[CONFIRMED — the complacency root]** CM 012 had already been pressurised with pure oxygen
  above 14.7 psia **four times, for 6 hours 15 minutes total** — about two and a half times
  longer than on 27 January. It had always been fine before.
- **[SECONDARY — verify]** Habitable volume 210 cu ft. Not in the Board text.

## ACT 3 — THE HATCH

- **[CONFIRMED]** Three hatches: the **Boost Protective Cover hatch** (outermost, "in place
  but not fully latched" because wire bundles for the test had distorted the cover), the
  **ablative hatch**, and the **inner pressure-vessel hatch** — the first one the crew would
  have to open.
- **[CONFIRMED — verbatim]** *"With a slightly higher pressure inside the Command Module than
  outside, opening the inner hatch is impossible because of the resulting force on the
  hatch."* Finding 4: *"Due to internal pressure, the Command Module inner hatch could not be
  opened prior to rupture."* **Determination: "The crew was never capable of effecting
  emergency egress."**
- **[CONFIRMED]** Timing: see correction #5.
- **[CONFIRMED]** The design choice was deliberate and written down: a quick-opening hatch
  was considered and rejected because accidental release in space *"was considered to impose
  a risk in excess of the benefits."* *Chariots*: North American **had been told not to**
  design an explosive hatch, because an accidentally blown hatch meant "an instant vacuum and
  certain death for a crewman not wearing his pressure suit."
- **[CONFIRMED]** An outward-opening Block II hatch **was already in design before the fire**
  — for spacewalk convenience, not for escape.
- **[ALLEGED — testimony, attribute by name]** Borman to the House: *"If they had had a hatch
  that opened outward and opened in 2 seconds there is no question in my mind that they would
  have escaped… It is my firm opinion that the crew would have escaped with a hatch that
  opened in 8 seconds."* Van Dolah, Senate: with a 2-3 second hatch *"the crew could have
  escaped with only minor injuries at most."* **These are opinions of witnesses, not Board
  findings.**

## ACT 4 — TEN POINT THREE SECONDS

All times GMT (EST = GMT−5), 27 January 1967.

- **[CONFIRMED]** 18:00 Grissom enters, then Chaffee, then White · 18:20 hold, a "sour" odour
  in the suit loop (the Board found it **not related to the fire**) · 19:45 hatches installed,
  oxygen purge begins · ~22:40 hold for a **live-microphone communications fault** · 23:20
  a T-10 hold begins.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **23:30:55 — a momentary power failure.** The Board's probable ignition
  moment.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **23:31:04.7 — the first transmission.** Board: *"an exclamatory remark.
  This transmission is not clear. Listeners believe this initial remark was 'Hey' or 'Fire'
  but this is not certain."* Laboratory analysis supports Grissom.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **23:31:06.2** — *"…Got a fire in the cockpit,"* preceded by an unclear
  word, "I've" or "We've." *"Many listeners believed this transmission was made by the
  Pilot… However, no firm conclusion can be drawn."*
- **[CONFIRMED]** **6.8 seconds of silence.** Then **23:31:16.8–23:31:21.8**, the second
  transmission — the famous one, which **cannot be transcribed** (correction #1). It ends,
  in the Board's phrase, *"with a cry of pain."* **⛔ Do not dramatise or voice that.**
- **[CONFIRMED]** Total crew voice: **10.3 seconds.**
- **[CONFIRMED]** ~23:31:19 the cabin ruptures. **23:31:22.4 — all voice and data end**,
  three seconds later. Van Dolah: the fire *"probably lasted only about 25 seconds."*
- **[CONFIRMED]** Medical: consciousness lost 15–30 seconds after the first suit failed;
  cause of death *"asphyxia due to inhalation of toxic gases due to fire,"* with thermal
  burns contributory. **Nothing further on screen.**
- **[CONFIRMED]** Rescue: pad crew fled the flash, returned with extinguishers, and began
  working on the hatches about a minute after the first report; **five men** took turns in a
  White Room where visibility was "virtually zero"; **all three hatches were open at about
  23:36 — roughly five minutes.** Named on the House record: D. O. Babbitt, J. D. Gleaves,
  L. D. Reece (North American), H. H. Rogers (NASA QC). Rep. Daddario put on the record that
  they *"acted with great courage."* **Name them — they are the forgotten people in this
  story.**

## ACT 5 — WHAT THE BOARD ACTUALLY FOUND

- **[CONFIRMED — Finding 1, verbatim and complete]** *"There was a momentary power failure at
  23:30:55 GMT. Evidence of several arcs was found in the post-fire investigation. No single
  ignition source of the fire was conclusively identified. DETERMINATION: The most probable
  initiator was an electrical arc in the sector between −Y and +Z spacecraft axes… near the
  floor in the lower forward section of the left-hand equipment bay where Environmental
  Control System instrumentation power wiring leads into the area between the Environmental
  Control Unit and the oxygen panel. **No evidence was discovered that suggested sabotage.**"*
- **[CONFIRMED — the honest core]** On the most likely spot, under the lithium hydroxide
  access door, where power cable insulation was vulnerable to abrasion: *"**If this cable
  were the cause, it cannot be proven since both the power cable and the inside edge of the
  door were completely destroyed.**"* Eliminated: electrostatic discharge, spontaneous
  combustion, mechanical heat, impact, resonance heating.
- **[CONFIRMED]** A **wrench socket** was found in the spacecraft (Enclosure 27).
- **[CONFIRMED — the Board's own summary, and the line to close the act on]** It listed the
  conditions rather than a cause — sealed cabin, oxygen atmosphere, extensive combustibles,
  vulnerable wiring, vulnerable coolant plumbing, inadequate escape, inadequate rescue — and
  then said: ***"in its devotion to the many difficult problems of space travel, the Apollo
  team failed to give adequate attention to certain mundane but equally vital questions of
  crew safety."***
- **[SECONDARY — do not upgrade]** The "glycol leak → electrolysis" chain is encyclopedia
  synthesis. The Board says only that coolant leakage "has been a chronic problem" and the
  coolant "is both corrosive and combustible."
- **[CONFIRMED]** The Board: established by Deputy Administrator **Robert Seamans** the night
  of the fire, convened 28 January at KSC under **Dr. Floyd Thompson** of Langley, **21 task
  panels**, **11 findings**, report dated **5 April 1967**, every member concurring. **Frank
  Borman** was its only astronaut. Nearly **3,000 pages**. Borman to the House: *"Perhaps you
  haven't had time to read the 3,000 pages."*

## ACT 6 — CONGRESS, AND THOMAS BARON

- **[CONFIRMED]** Senate: Committee on Aeronautical and Space Sciences under **Clinton
  Anderson**, hearings February 1967–January 1968, report 30 January 1968. House:
  Subcommittee on NASA Oversight under **Olin Teague**, hearings April–May 1967.
- **[CONFIRMED — the Phillips report]** A December 1965 review of North American. Phillips'
  cover letter to NAA's president, 19 December 1965: *"I am definitely not satisfied with the
  progress and outlook of either program."* Senator **Mondale** raised its existence on 27
  February 1967; Administrator **Webb** replied that he knew of *"no unusual General Phillips
  report."* The Senate's finding: the task-force findings *"had no effect on the accident,
  did not lead to the accident, and were not related to the accident"* — **but** the
  committee *"believes it should have been informed."* Mondale's additional views name
  *"evasiveness… lack of candor… patronizing attitude toward Congress."* **State both: not
  causal, and not disclosed.**
- **[CONFIRMED — Baron, documented only]** Thomas Baron, a North American quality inspector
  at KSC from September 1965 to November 1966, compiled a **55-page** report of quality
  complaints, leaked it to the press, and was **discharged on 5 January 1967**. North
  American rebutted it point by point, *"although some company officials later testified
  before Congress that about half of the charges were well-grounded."* He was expanding it
  toward ~500 pages when the fire happened. He **testified on 21 April 1967**. Under
  questioning he said an NAA technician "knew exactly what caused the fire" — a man he had met
  by chance in a drugstore — and admitted he took that to a newspaperman rather than to NASA
  or the Board. That technician testified Baron *"gets all his information from anonymous
  phone calls."*
- **[CONFIRMED]** **Baron, his wife and his stepdaughter died on 27 April 1967 — six days
  after he testified — when a train struck their car** near Mims, Florida. NASA's own history
  gives it one word of comment: *"Ironically."*
- **⛔ STOP THERE.** No official document alleges foul play; there is no inquest finding in
  the record. **State the documented facts, give the one-word comment, and move on. Do not
  platform, do not wink, do not "leave it to the viewer."** This is the beat that decides
  whether this is a documentary or a conspiracy channel.

## ACT 7 — WHAT CHANGED

- **[CONFIRMED — NASA's own list to the Senate]** All pure-oxygen tests redefined as
  hazardous · test-procedure responsibility redefined · an independent **Office of Flight
  Safety** · emergency training and pad fire/rescue equipment · all crewed flights moved to
  **Block II** · combustibles *"severely restricted and controlled"* — which NASA called
  *"more significant than any other improvement resulting from the accident"* · a new
  quick-opening hatch · emergency oxygen · launch facilities modified for rapid egress.
- **[SECONDARY — NASA histories]** The **unified hatch**: one outward-opening hatch replacing
  two, openable in about five seconds (*Chariots*) or three (*Moonport*), and **openable
  regardless of internal pressure**. Materials: over 3,000 tests on more than 500 materials;
  nylon replaced with Beta fibre and Teflon. Plumbing: soldered aluminium oxygen lines
  replaced with brazed stainless steel. Atmosphere on the pad became **60% oxygen / 40%
  nitrogen** (recommended 4 March 1968) at roughly sea-level pressure; flight stayed pure
  oxygen at 5 psi.
- **[CONFIRMED — the only primary-anchored forms of the "it made Apollo 11 possible" claim]**
  The Senate, January 1968: *"It is the committee's hope that the remainder of the program
  will be carried out with greater understanding and dedication than if there had been no
  accident."* Borman, 1999 oral history, asked whether the changes let NASA recover fast:
  *"Absolutely. Just take a look at the difference in recovery time between the Apollo and
  the Shuttle."* **Anything stronger must be attributed to a named person or cut.**
- **[CONFIRMED — the humane closing fact]** The widows asked that **"Apollo 1"** be reserved
  for the flight that never flew. NASA made it official on **24 April 1967**.

## SENSITIVITY / ETHICS GATE

- **⛔ Never play the fire audio.** It is the sound of three men dying; its provenance was
  never established (no NASA-hosted copy exists — every circulating copy is a re-upload); all
  three men have living children. **The Board's own transcript on screen is more powerful and
  costs nothing morally.**
- **The stronger alternative, and it is available and public domain:** NASA's audio archive
  holds the crew's **press conference of 16 December 1966** (tape 530-AAG, 58 min) — Grissom,
  White and Chaffee talking about the mission six weeks before. **Confident voices, then a
  typed transcript and silence.** Use that.
- **⛔ Do not dramatise "a cry of pain."** Do not voice any crew line.
- The Board's evidentiary detail on the crew's positions and remains is in the record and is
  **gratuitous on screen**. Use only Finding 4's conclusion and the one-sentence
  reconstruction: Grissom left his couch, White reached for the hatch as trained, Chaffee
  kept communications as trained.
- The widows' 1972 settlement is factual; weigh whether it belongs at all.

  Mondale and Baron are deceased. The crew's families are living.
