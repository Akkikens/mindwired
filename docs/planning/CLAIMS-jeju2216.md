## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (bake in — do NOT revert)

1. **179 dead of 181 aboard** (175 passengers + 6 crew). **2 survivors**, both
   flight attendants, from the **rear jump seats**. 173 South Korean and 2 Thai
   passengers.
2. **Deadliest aviation disaster on South Korean soil**, and the deadliest
   involving a South Korean airline since 1997 (Korean Air 801, Guam). Be precise
   with this framing — check before using any "deadliest ever" phrasing.
3. Aircraft: **Boeing 737-8AS (737-800)**, registration **HL8088**, built **2009**
   (15 years old). Engines **CFM International CFM56-7B26**. It is NOT a 737 MAX —
   this is a common error in circulation and the distinction matters.
4. **The bird was the Baikal teal**, a migratory duck — identified from blood and
   feathers found in **both** engines. The **right** engine took severe damage.
5. **The aircraft landed gear-up, on its belly, in the opposite direction.** It
   touched down roughly **1,200 m** along the runway after a go-around from runway
   01 and a second approach to **runway 19**.
6. **It did not "hit a wall" in the ordinary sense.** It struck a **concrete
   structure inside an earthen berm supporting the ILS localizer antenna array**,
   roughly **250 m beyond the runway end**, about **4 m** high including the
   antenna. Describing it as a plain wall is imprecise; the point is that it was a
   *navigation aid mounting* that was supposed to be breakable.

## THE BLACK BOXES — the spine of this episode

**CONFIRMED (South Korean Transport Ministry, announced 11 Jan 2025):** both the
**CVR and the FDR stopped recording approximately four minutes before impact.**

- **CONFIRMED:** the CVR was recovered intact; a transcript was produced 4 Jan 2025.
  The FDR was damaged with a severed connector and was sent to the **NTSB in
  Washington** for read-out (6 Jan 2025).
- **ALLEGED / leading theory, NOT confirmed:** simultaneous loss of both recorders
  points to a **total loss of electrical power**. Attribute this — Sim Jai-dong, a
  former Transport Ministry investigator, is the named source in wire coverage. Do
  not state a cause for the recorder failure as fact.
- **SYSTEMIC ANGLE (verify before scripting):** reporting (Japan Times, 15 Jan 2025)
  frames this as a **decades-long gap in recorder power rules** — older aircraft are
  not required to carry an independent backup power supply that would keep recorders
  alive through a total electrical failure. ⚠ UNVERIFIED against the primary
  regulation; confirm the applicable rule and the aircraft's actual fit before any
  line asserts it.

**Why this is the episode's spine:** the channel is called Black Box Breakdown, and
in this accident the black boxes went dark for exactly the window that mattered.
Everything the investigation concluded about those last four minutes was inferred
from something other than the recorders.

## THE EMBANKMENT — the systemic case

- **CONFIRMED:** the localizer antenna array was mounted on a **concrete structure
  within an earthen berm**, ~250 m past the runway end, ~4 m tall overall. A
  **concrete slab was added in 2023**.
- **CONFIRMED (Anti-Corruption and Civil Rights Commission, Dec 2025):** the
  embankment **violated frangibility safety requirements**.
- **CONFIRMED (state audit, reported Mar 2026):** **14 non-compliant localizer
  installations at 8 airports** — including Muan, Jeju and Gimhae — were wrongly
  approved and carried certified safety inspections for **up to 22 years** despite
  failing frangibility requirements.
- **REPORTED:** the concrete mounting was chosen as a **cost-saving measure**
  because the terrain sloped and levelling it would have required major earthworks.
  ⚠ Attribute to the state report; do not assert motive beyond what it says.
- **THE RULE:** international standards require navigation aids sited near runways
  to be **frangible** — built to break away on impact. **Exhibit of choice: FAA AC
  150/5300-13 and/or ICAO Annex 14.** The FAA document is a US federal work and
  therefore unambiguously public domain, which makes it the safe on-screen exhibit
  regardless of how the ARAIB licence question resolves.

## THE SURVIVABILITY FINDING — the hook

- **CONFIRMED (government-commissioned computer simulation, revealed 8 Jan 2026 by
  SBS and the New York Times):** **all aboard would have survived** had the
  localizer and its supports been absent or built from frangible materials.
- **CONFIRMED:** the report had been **withheld despite requests from the
  families** before it was revealed by the press.
- ⚠ Phrase carefully. The finding is that they **could have** survived under the
  simulation's assumptions — that is strong enough on its own. Do not upgrade it to
  a flat "everyone survived the landing," which overstates the record.

## THE CONTESTED FINDING (handle with the AI171 protocol)

- **19 Jul 2025 — interim findings presented to families:** the crew **shut down
  the left engine**, which was relatively undamaged, **instead of the damaged right
  engine**.
- **DISPUTED by:** the victims' families and the pilots' union, who accused ARAIB
  of **ignoring other causes, specifically the airport barrier**.
- **CONFIRMED consequence:** the **scheduled release was withdrawn** following the
  protests, and ARAIB agreed to widen the inquiry beyond pilot error to
  infrastructure and systemic factors.
- **CONFIRMED:** 2 Dec 2025 — interim report delayed; ICAO rules require an interim
  update when a final report is not completed within a year.
- **CONFIRMED:** 22 Dec 2025 — South Korea's parliament passed a bill authorising an
  **independent probe**.
- **STATUS:** as of Aug 2026 the final report is **still unpublished and overdue**
  (it had been expected around June 2026).

**Scripting rule:** present the engine finding and the embankment finding with equal
weight, both attributed, neither asserted. The honest frame is that an interim
finding blamed the crew, the families forced it open, and a separate state finding
says the structure is what killed everyone.
