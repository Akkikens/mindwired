## 1. THE COLD WAR CONTEXT

- **Sputnik (Oct 1957) triggered US anti-ballistic-missile research** into
  high-altitude nuclear effects. CONFIRMED.
- **Operation Argus (Aug-Sep 1958)**, South Atlantic, USS Norton Sound —
  tested physicist Nicholas Christofilos's trapped-electron/ABM-shielding
  theory. Real precedent for Starfish Prime. CONFIRMED.
- **1958-1961 US-USSR-UK test moratorium** ended when the USSR resumed
  testing (commonly dated ~September 1961), capped by the October 30, 1961
  Tsar Bomba — the largest nuclear device ever detonated. The US resumed
  testing with Operation Dominic, of which Fishbowl (the high-altitude
  series) was a part. CONFIRMED.

## 2. THE DEVICE AND THE TEST

- **Delivery**: Thor DSV2E booster (serial 195), launched from Johnston
  Island Launch Emplacement 1 (LE-1). CONFIRMED.
- **Warhead**: W49 (Los Alamos design, ~1,665 lb), yield ~1.4 megatons.
  CONFIRMED.
- **Burst altitude**: 400 km (~248 miles) above the Pacific near Johnston
  Island. CONFIRMED.
- **Date/time**: July 9, 1962, 09:00:09 UTC — which was still **July 8,
  late evening, Johnston Island local time**, but **11:00:09 PM, July 9,
  Honolulu time** (Hawaii is on the other side of the date line from
  Johnston relative to UTC rollover — script must state the date carefully
  to avoid an apparent contradiction; "July 9" is correct for the
  Hawaii-observer framing this episode leads with). CONFIRMED.

## 3. THE REAL PHYSICS

- **EMP mechanism**: prompt gamma rays Compton-scatter atmospheric electrons
  (~20-40 km altitude), which spiral along Earth's magnetic field lines,
  radiating a synchronized electromagnetic pulse over a vast area. CONFIRMED
  (arXiv:1611.03390, "Anthropogenic Space Weather").
- **The Hawaii EMP hit ~1,445 km (898 miles) away, arriving in well under a
  second** — the geomagnetic disturbance was "effectively instantaneous"
  with the more intense pulse arriving roughly 2 seconds after detonation.
  CONFIRMED (Vittitoe 1989, Sandia SAND88-3341 — the authoritative forensic
  reconstruction).
- **Real documented Hawaii effects**: ~300 streetlights out via fuse-
  tripping (see correction #1), burglar alarms triggered, a Kauai telephone
  microwave relay link damaged/phone service cut. CONFIRMED.
- **Satellites damaged/destroyed by the artificial radiation belt** (source:
  Hess 1963, JGR 68(3), the foundational peer-reviewed paper):
  - **TRAAC** — failed within days/weeks. CONFIRMED.
  - **Transit 4B** — solar array degraded, failed within months. CONFIRMED.
  - **Ariel 1** (UK's first satellite) — solar cells degraded, failed by
    ~1962-63; most-cited non-US casualty. CONFIRMED.
  - **Telstar 1** — see correction #3 (slow death, not instant).
  - **Injun I** — degraded per Hess's listing. CONFIRMED.
  - Roughly a third of LEO satellites then in orbit were affected within
    months — a small absolute number (very few satellites existed in 1962)
    but a real, striking fraction. CONFIRMED.
- **The artificial radiation belt persisted for years** — bulk of injected
  electrons decayed within days/weeks, but a smaller population at low
  L-shells persisted much longer; some Starfish electrons were still
  detectable ~5 years later (1968), with some analyses extending toward a
  decade at the most stable shells. Script as "several years, by some
  estimates up to a decade" — not an exact number. CONFIRMED.
- **Visual spectacle**: from Honolulu (~1,445 km away), a sudden bright
  flash then an expanding artificial aurora — mottled red/pink giving way
  to white-yellow streaks, visible several minutes. From Kwajalein
  (~2,600 km away): "brilliant white flash" → "expanding green ball" →
  "white fingers" and concentric ring structures, persisting up to 90
  minutes as the aurora drifted along field lines. From Fiji and New
  Zealand (~2,000+ miles), the flash itself was visible. CONFIRMED, sourced
  to contemporary 1962 "Quick Look" technical reports.
- **Contemporary real quote (Hilo Tribune-Herald, 1962)**: "It looked as
  though the heavens had belched forth a new sun that flared briefly, but
  long enough to set the sky on fire." CONFIRMED, contemporaneous published
  account, widely cited — HIGH confidence, safe to quote directly.
