# CLAIMS — Milwaukee, 1991 (the Dahmer accountability record)
**Criminal Record episode 2.** Frame: **institutional failure**, told through the
documents. This is NOT a recap of the killings, and the distinction is both
editorial and commercial — a recounting of the murders would ship age-restricted
and demonetized, and it would be the tenth thousandth version of that video.

Nobody needs another telling of what Dahmer did. The record of how he was
*allowed to continue* has barely been touched, and it exists in filings anyone
can read.

## Hard rules (stricter than Idaho's)
- **No description of the killings, the dismemberment, the apartment contents,
  or any remains.** Not one line. The convictions establish what happened; the
  episode is about the paperwork around them.
- No crime-scene imagery, no victim photographs, no photographs of the
  apartment. No AI depiction of anyone.
- Victims are named only where the record requires it and their treatment is the
  point — principally **Konerak Sinthasomphone** (14) and his older brother
  **Somsack Sinthasomphone** (13 at the time of the 1988 offence).
- Dahmer's own psychology is out of scope. No "inside the mind of" framing, no
  interview clips, no photographs of him beyond a court document if one appears.
- The officers are named because a published appellate opinion names them and
  they were public employees acting in that capacity. Their conduct is described
  exactly as the record describes it — no invented motive, no speculation about
  what they thought.
- Do not assert racism or homophobia as established fact. The record supports
  that these criticisms were widely made and that the city faced a
  discrimination claim; state it that way.

## Primary documents (both in hand, both public records)
1. **John A. Balcerzak v. Board of Fire and Police Commissioners of the City of
   Milwaukee**, Wisconsin Court of Appeals, No. 98-2889 — 19 pages, fetched
   2026-08-03 from wicourts.gov:
   https://www.wicourts.gov/ca/opinions/98/pdf/98-2889.pdf
   Local: `public/shorts/dahmer/exhibits_src/balcerzak_opinion.pdf` + OCR text.
   Contains, in the court's own words: that the officers "released
   Sinthasomphone, who was a minor, into Dahmer's care"; that "Dahmer murdered
   Sinthasomphone shortly thereafter"; the chief's discharge of both officers;
   the Fire and Police Commission upholding the discharges; and the vacating of
   those discharges with reinstatement.
2. **Estate of Sinthasomphone v. City of Milwaukee**, 785 F. Supp. 1343 (E.D.
   Wis. 1992) — the federal civil-rights suit brought after Konerak's death.
   Full text public via Justia:
   https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/785/1343/2144838/
   *(fetch the text for exhibit pages before scripting the civil-case chapter)*

## The chronology (all from the record / contemporaneous reporting)
- **1988** — Dahmer is arrested for drugging and sexually assaulting a
  13-year-old boy, **Somsack Sinthasomphone**. He is convicted and **placed on
  probation**.
- **May 27, 1991** — while still on that probation, officers responding to a
  citizen complaint encounter Dahmer with **Konerak Sinthasomphone**, 14,
  Somsack's younger brother. Three women had found the boy in the street.
  Per the appellate opinion, after a brief investigation the officers released
  the boy — a minor — into Dahmer's care. He was killed shortly afterward.
- **July 1991** — Dahmer is arrested. Once the release of Konerak becomes
  public, the then-Chief of Police discharges the officers involved for
  violating department rules during that investigation.
- The **Fire and Police Commission upholds the discharges**.
- On appeal, the discharges are **vacated**; the officers are **reinstated**,
  with **back pay reported at about $55,000 each**. Reinstatement drew
  significant public criticism, including the argument that the investigation
  was cursory because Konerak was not white and was assumed to be in a
  relationship with Dahmer. *(Attribute as criticism, not finding.)*
- **Feb 15, 1992** — a jury rejects Dahmer's insanity plea after roughly five
  hours and finds him guilty on **15 counts of murder**.
- **Feb 17, 1992** — **15 consecutive life terms**.
- **Nov 28, 1994** — Dahmer is killed by another inmate in prison. One line.
  No detail.
- **1995** — Balcerzak is later elected president of the Milwaukee Police
  Association. **2017** — he retires from the department. *(Both reported;
  verify each independently before it goes on screen.)*

## The episode's actual thesis
A man on probation for assaulting a 13-year-old was handed that boy's
14-year-old brother by uniformed officers, and the paperwork that followed
reinstated them with back pay. Every step of that sentence is in a filing. The
question the episode ends on is not "why did he do it" — it's **what the record
shows a system will forgive.**

## Not established / do not claim
- That the officers knew what Dahmer was. The record does not support it.
- Any characterisation of the officers' inner motives.
- That reinstatement was legally wrong — the appellate court's reasoning is a
  procedural one under Wis. Stat. § 62.50, and the episode should explain that
  rather than editorialise it. Show the statute; let it land on its own.
- Numbers of victims beyond the 15 counts of conviction.
