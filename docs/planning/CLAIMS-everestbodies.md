## ACT 1 — The mountain, the death toll, why bodies stay

- **[CONFIRMED]** Most of Everest's dead lie above ~8,000m in the "death zone," where
  cold (routinely −40°C), dry air and UV exposure mummify rather than decompose
  bodies. Source: Smithsonian.
- **[CONFIRMED, estimate not count — correction #1]** "Over 200" bodies remain on the
  mountain (2015 BBC Future estimate, repeated by Smithsonian/CBS/CNN/Outside); total
  recorded deaths since 1922 exceed 300 (Himalayan Database), ~130+ Nepali
  workers/Sherpas. Source: CBC Documentaries, Smithsonian.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Commercial guided Everest climbing traces to **Dick Bass** (Utah
  ski-resort owner, no serious mountaineering background), summiting April 30, 1985,
  guided by David Breashears, completing his "Seven Summits" quest — his 1986 book is
  widely credited with launching the commercial-guiding boom. Source: Seattle
  Times/Wikipedia.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **Mountain Madness** founded by Scott Fischer & Wes Krause, 1984,
  Seattle. **Adventure Consultants** founded by Rob Hall & Gary Ball, 1991, New
  Zealand. By 1996 Rob Hall charged clients **$65,000/head** (highest rate of any
  operator) — Krakauer's *Into Thin Air* reporting.
- **[CONFIRMED — recovery physiology/cost]** Death-zone recovery requires 6-10
  climbers physically dragging/lowering a body over days (no mechanical extraction
  possible); routine helicopter ceiling is ~Camp 2 (~6,400m) — the record-highest
  helicopter rescue ever performed was ~7,800m, May 2013, under exceptional conditions,
  not repeatable on demand. Recovery cost clusters at **$40,000-80,000 per body**
  (2024 Nepal Army operation: 5 bodies + 11 tonnes waste, >£480,000/~$600,000 total,
  ~$75-80K/body per Major Aditya Karki). **[DISPUTED]** the popular claim that a
  frozen body's weight "roughly doubles" is an unverified dramatization — the
  defensible claim is simply that 150-200+ lbs of dead weight is nearly unmoveable at
  8,000m+, not that freezing itself doubles mass.
- **[CONFIRMED — currency]** Nepal raised the Everest permit fee to $15,000/climber
  (spring), effective Sept 1, 2025 — officials explicitly tied the hike to
  body-removal/cleanup costs. Correction #6 applies to the separate pending insurance
  bill. Source: Kathmandu Post, Alan Arnette's blog.

## ACT 2 — May 10-11, 1996: two disasters, one storm

- **[CONFIRMED]** South side ("Into Thin Air"): Rob Hall's Adventure Consultants +
  Scott Fischer's Mountain Madness summit the same day; a blizzard hits during descent
  (~5:30pm May 10). **Named dead:** Rob Hall (35, NZ, remains on mountain near South
  Summit — widow explicitly asked he be left there 🧍); Andy Harris (31, NZ guide,
  never found); Doug Hansen (46, US postal worker/client, never found); Scott Fischer
  (40, US, remains on mountain, found by Boukreev); Yasuko Namba (47, Japan, recovered
  in 1997 via a mission funded by husband Kenichi Namba 🧍, returned to Japan). Source:
  Wikipedia (Hall/Harris/Hansen/Fischer/Namba pages), corroborating Krakauer.
- **[DISPUTED — correction #9]** No formal inquiry exists. Krakauer's *Into Thin Air*
  criticizes guide Anatoli Boukreev for descending ahead of clients without oxygen;
  Boukreev/DeWalt's *The Climb* disputes this; Boukreev was separately awarded the
  1997 David A. Sowles Memorial Award for solo-rescuing three clients that night, and
  died in an avalanche on Annapurna, Dec 25 1997. Present both accounts, don't pick a
  side.
- **[CONFIRMED]** North side (ITBP): a 6-man Indo-Tibetan Border Police team (leader
  Commandant Mohinder Singh) attempts the first Indian ascent via the Northeast Ridge.
  Three turn back; **Subedar Tsewang Samanla** (team leader, summited 1984), **Lance
  Naik Dorje Morup** (48), and **Head Constable Tsewang Paljor** (28, from Sakti
  village, Ladakh) continue and radio a summit claim ~17:30-18:00 Beijing time, May 10
  (a Chinese summit certificate for Morup records 17:30). **[DISPUTED]** Krakauer
  argues they were still ~150m short of the true summit when they called in. Samanla
  reportedly spent extra time on summit religious ceremonies, a detail cited as a
  factor in the late, storm-caught descent. All three die overnight/into May 11 —
  found alive but severely frostbitten by a Japanese team on May 11 per multiple
  secondary sources (not independently primary-verified this session). Source:
  Wikipedia (1996 ITBP expedition page), GearJunkie, cross-corroborated.

## ACT 3 — "Green Boots": 30 years, the wrong name, and the 2026 reveal

- **[CONFIRMED]** A body in a limestone alcove at ~8,500m on the Northeast Ridge,
  nicknamed "Green Boots" for its bright green Koflach boots, was first filmed May
  1996 by British filmmaker Matt Dickinson (*Summit Fever* documentary), described
  then only as "from India." For ~30 years it was **widely but not universally**
  attributed to Tsewang Paljor.
- **[CONFIRMED — the actually-correct minority account, now vindicated]** A 1997
  article by P.M. Das (deputy leader of the 1996 ITBP expedition) in the *Himalayan
  Journal* described Morup struggling with frostbitten hands between the First and
  Second Steps, and a second search party finding his body near a boulder close to
  Camp 6 with intact clothing/rucksack — placing his death in the late afternoon of
  May 11. This account named Morup, not Paljor, all along.
- **[CONFIRMED]** In 2014, a Chinese expedition (China Tibet Mountaineering
  Association, which manages the north side) moved the body to a less visible spot —
  why recent climbers report seeing it less prominently than in older accounts.
- **[CONFIRMED, mechanism CONTESTED — correction #2]** June 2026: ITBP floats a formal
  government tender for a "Phase II retrieval operation" to recover "Dorje Morup
  (Green Boots)," bids due June 29 2026, target extraction window June-Sept 2026
  (monsoon season), delivery to Delhi targeted by October 2026. ITBP spokesperson
  named in coverage: Kamlesh Kamal 🧍. Use correction #2's required phrasing for how
  the identification claim is presented on screen.
- **🧍 [CONFIRMED, attributed]** Dorje Morup's wife, **Konchok Yangskit** (~75, Ladakh),
  gave her first public interview (BBC, republished Aug 10 2026): *"On some days,
  before I went to sleep, I would think that there'd be a knock on the door tonight...
  I will take my last breath only after seeing him."* Their son **Punchok Dorjai** 🧍
  plans a funeral near Martyrs' Park, Leh, and wants Morup and his two teammates
  formally recognized as martyrs.
- **🧍 [UNVERIFIED, weak source — do not quote directly]** A claim that Tsewang
  Paljor's own family finds the "Green Boots" nickname dehumanizing traces only to a
  fan wiki, not primary journalism. If used at all, hedge heavily ("reports
  suggest...").
- **[CONFIRMED — human-scale, pre-climb]** Morup, 48, ITBP Lance Naik, had already
  climbed Stok Kangri (Ladakh's highest peak) before Everest. Per wire reporting
  (journalist paraphrase of the widow's decades-later recollection, not a verbatim
  1996 transcript), he told her he'd come back "a hero" and asked her to meet him at
  the airport.
- **[EXCLUDE]** Do not invent a specific city, lab, or scientist name for the DNA
  process — none is documented anywhere. Do not invent a specific home village for
  Morup beyond "Ladakh" — no source confirms one.

## ACT 4 — Mallory & Irvine: the still-open 1924 mystery

- **[CONFIRMED]** George Mallory (37-38) and Andrew "Sandy" Irvine (22), British 1924
  expedition, last seen climbing toward the summit on the Northeast Ridge, June 8,
  1924, then vanish. Whether they summited 29 years before Hillary/Norgay's confirmed
  1953 ascent remains the central open question.
- **[CONFIRMED]** May 1, 1999: the Mallory and Irvine Research Expedition finds
  Mallory's body at ~8,160m. Personal effects helped confirm identity; **no camera**
  was found on him. No evidence he reached the summit — remains unresolved.
- **[CONFIRMED — recent, strong hook]** Sept/Oct 2024: a National Geographic-affiliated
  team (Jimmy Chin, Erich Roepke, Mark Fisher) finds what is *believed* to be Irvine's
  remains (boot, partial foot, sock embroidered "A.C. Irvine") on the Central Rongbuk
  Glacier — the first possible physical evidence of his fate since 1924. Identity was
  described as "believed," not 100% forensically confirmed at time of reporting. The
  Kodak Vest Pocket camera he was believed to carry was **not** found with these
  remains and remains missing — the one artifact that could theoretically prove a 1924
  summit. 🧍 **Julie Summers**, Irvine's great-niece/biographer, was the first family
  member informed and the family volunteered DNA samples.

## ACT 5 — Two named individual tragedies (legacy/ethics beats)

- **[CONFIRMED — correction #3]** Hannelore Schmatz (German, 4th woman ever to summit,
  first German), died on descent Oct 2, 1979, ~100m above Camp IV, from
  exhaustion/exposure. Body remained visible for years, held roughly in place by her
  rope. **October 1984:** a Nepalese police recovery attempt kills two more people —
  police inspector Yogendra Bahadur Thapa (36) and Sherpa guide Ang Dorje (35) both
  fell to their deaths trying to retrieve her remains. Use correction #3's wind/cut-
  rope account, never "avalanche."
- **[CONFIRMED]** David Sharp (British, 34) dies solo/unguided in the same alcove area
  ("Green Boots' Cave"), May 15, 2006, after ~30-40 climbers pass him over roughly two
  days (exact count varies by source — treat as approximate). Some climbers (Sherpa
  guide Phurba Tashi, climber Maxime Chaya, Sherpa Dorjee) did attempt to help — gave
  oxygen, tried to move him — before continuing; complicates a flat "everyone just
  walked past" framing. 🧍 **Mark Inglis** (double amputee, summited same push) later
  said in interviews his team radioed expedition leader Russell Brice and was told
  Sharp was "effectively dead" — see correction #8, this is ALLEGED/partially
  retracted, not settled fact. Sir Edmund Hillary (d.2008, so historical record) 
  publicly condemned the climbers who passed Sharp: *"On my expedition, there was no
  way that you would have left a man under a rock to die... Human life is far more
  important than just getting to the top of a mountain."* Reignited the "climber's
  code" debate (correction #7 — debate, not a rule change). One real statistic: 
  expeditions with a death on a commercial trip completed their summit push 80.6% of
  the time afterward vs. 37.8% for non-commercial expeditions.

## ACT 6 — 2026: still happening (the closing loop back to the cold open)

- **[CONFIRMED — correction #4]** Spring 2026 was a record season (1,008 summits) that
  still killed five people: Lhakpa Dendi Sherpa (52, fatal fall near Gorakshep before
  even reaching the mountain proper), Bijay Ghimire Bishwakarma (35, first Dalit
  Everest summiter, died in the Khumbu Icefall of suspected cardiac arrest/altitude
  sickness during an acclimatization rotation, posthumously honored by the Nepal
  Mountaineering Association), Phura Gyaljen Sherpa (20-21, fell into a Lhotse Face
  crevasse), Arun Kumar Tiwari (53, India, died near the Hillary Step after summiting),
  Sandeep Are (46, India/US, snow blindness after removing goggles for summit photos,
  died of exhaustion/cerebral edema despite rescue attempts).
- **[CONFIRMED — correction #5]** Following those deaths, Nepal's Army + local
  authorities + expedition operators prepared a cleanup campaign (reported ~July 28,
  2026) TARGETING at least five bodies from Everest/Nuptse plus debris. No completion
  report exists as of this research. Safe cumulative stat: since organized Army
  cleanup campaigns began in 2019, ~119 tonnes of waste + 14 bodies + several
  skeletons recovered in total.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Nepal's first five-year Everest cleanup plan rolled out late 2025;
  new tightened rules (a 7,000m Nepali-peak prerequisite, climbers responsible for
  their own rubbish) are reported but per Alan Arnette's live March 2026 season update,
  were **NOT yet in effect for the 2026 season** — only the fee/waste rules were live.
  The body-recovery insurance bill is separately still pending (correction #6).
