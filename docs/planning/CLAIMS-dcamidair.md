## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **The Black Hawk's ADS-B Out was off LEGALLY.** The ATC factual report
   states PAT25 "was not transmitting ADS-B data during the accident flight
   and was operating under an existing Department of Defense (DOD) exemption
   that permitted this." Script it as a *policy* failure, never as a
   violation, a cover-up, or "they turned it off to hide." Both aircraft were
   still tracked on radar.
2. **This is NOT a "pilot error" episode — but the NTSB did name the
   helicopter crew as one cause among several, and the script must say so.**
   The verbatim probable cause (§6 below) leads with the FAA's route placement
   and the system's overreliance on visual separation, and *also* names "the
   lack of effective pilot-applied visual separation by the helicopter crew"
   as causal, alongside the tower team's degraded performance and the Army's
   altimeter failure. Script all of it, in the NTSB's own order. Do **not**
   reduce the accident to crew error (wrong, and the reverse of what the
   report emphasises), and do **not** sanitise the crew finding out of it
   either (dishonest). A traveller-forum post titled "Pilot error the cause"
   surfaced in research — not a source, do not use.
3. **67 dead = 64 aboard the airplane + 3 aboard the helicopter.** Do not
   write "67 passengers." The airplane's 64 includes crew.
4. **Altitudes shown to the controller are rounded to 100-foot increments.**
   Primary source, ATC factual report: "All altitudes, regardless of source,
   are rounded to the nearest 100-foot increment for display to ATC." Never
   script the controller as able to see a precise 278 vs 200 foot difference.
5. **"Pass behind the CRJ" was not simply ignored — the words were stepped
   on.** The CVR transcript shows the transmission was interrupted by a
   0.8-second mic key from PAT25 itself. Script the mechanism, never
   "the crew disregarded the instruction."
6. **Route 4 was not "closed after the crash" in one clean act.** The FAA
   first eliminated the Hains Point–Wilson Bridge portion after the NTSB's
   urgent recommendations; the June 12, 2025 chart update eliminated route 4
   altogether. Two steps, both documented.
7. **Callsigns:** the airplane is **JIA5342** (PSA Airlines, operating as
   American Eagle 5342); the helicopter is **PAT25** (Priority Air Transport
   25, US Army UH-60L). "American Airlines Flight 5342" is the common press
   name and is acceptable in narration, but the docket name is JIA5342.

## 2. The route — the systemic spine — CONFIRMED (primary document)

All of the following is quoted or closely paraphrased from the **ATC Group
Chairman's Factual Report §2.5.1**, and is the strongest material in the
episode because it is the FAA describing its own chart:

- Helicopter routes on the Baltimore-Washington Helicopter Route Chart **"had
  no lateral boundaries"** and were meant to follow linear features such as
  highways or shorelines. [CONFIRMED]
- **"The lines drawn on the chart depicting a helicopter route are not scaled
  and have no associated measurements."** [CONFIRMED — exact quote]
- The routes **"had mostly been developed decades ago, with minimal changes
  over the years, mostly involving altitude changes."** [CONFIRMED]
- **There had been no changes to this chart between 2014 and June 2021**, when
  route 1's altitude near the South Capitol Street Bridge was changed from 200
  to 300 feet. [CONFIRMED]
- Route 4's segment ran between Hains Point and the Wilson Bridge, directly
  under the approach corridor. Max altitude on that segment: **200 feet**.
  [CONFIRMED — chart; corroborated by NTSB]
- **After the accident:** NTSB issued urgent safety recommendations
  **A-25-001 and A-25-002**; the FAA eliminated the Hains Point–Wilson Bridge
  portion of route 4, and per the **chart update effective June 12, 2025,
  route 4 was eliminated altogether.** [CONFIRMED — primary]
- The FAA also published a Special Notice in the DCA Chart Supplement
  (effective **April 17, 2025**) creating three **"Cautionary Areas."**
  [CONFIRMED — primary]

**Narrative line this supports (safe to assert):** a helicopter route with no
width, drawn decades ago, unscaled and unmeasured, ran underneath a runway
approach — and four months after it killed 67 people, the FAA deleted it from
the chart.

## 3. Surveillance and the altitude problem

- **PAT25 was not transmitting ADS-B, under a permitted DoD exemption.** Both
  aircraft were tracked by radar; STARS fused radar + ADS-B for the
  controller's display. [CONFIRMED — primary, ATC factual §2.0]
- **Controller display altitudes are rounded to the nearest 100 feet.**
  [CONFIRMED — primary, exact quote in §2.0 NOTE]
- **The collision occurred at 278 ft msl, about 2,500 ft from the runway 33
  threshold** — on a route segment limited to 200 ft. PAT25's recorded
  altitude at 2047:59 was 278 ft. [CONFIRMED — **primary**, AIR-26-02, stated
  in four separate places]
- **The barometric altimeter error, verified by NTSB flight test** (AIR-26-02
  §1, Army/Sikorsky developmental flight-test data). Use these numbers, not
  the "130 feet" figure that circulated in press coverage:
  - All three test helicopters showed **barometric altimeter values LOWER than
    true altitude** over the Potomac.
  - **In a hover, the barometric altimeter can read 80 ft low** from main-rotor
    downwash position error.
  - By **30 kts** the error decreases to about **35 ft low**; by **120 kts**
    downwash no longer affects it; above 120 kts it reads **high**.
  - **The accident helicopter was ESSS-equipped**, which added further position
    error — **lowering the reading by 50 ft** at speeds over 30 kts, versus the
    35 ft otherwise.
  [ALL CONFIRMED — primary]
- **The Army did not ensure pilots were adequately informed about allowable
  barometric altimeter error tolerances; as a result the crew was flying above
  the published maximum route altitude.** [CONFIRMED — primary, verbatim
  finding, AIR-26-02 p. xxii]

## 3b. THE CAUSAL THREAD — the spine of the episode (CONFIRMED, primary)

AIR-26-02, p. xxii, verbatim — this is the most important paragraph in the
whole investigation and the episode should be built around it:

> "We found that the helicopter crew did not effectively apply visual
> separation. **Due to degraded radio reception, the crew did not receive
> information about the airplane's circling approach to a different runway,
> which reinforced an incorrect expectation that the airplane did not pose a
> conflict.** That expectation shaped the crew's visual scan and led the
> instructor pilot to report 'traffic in sight' and request visual separation
> **without positively identifying the airplane.**"

**What this means, and what the script must land:** the crew almost certainly
was not looking at Flight 5342 when they said they had it in sight. They never
heard that the jet had been switched to a *circling approach to runway 33* —
so they were expecting it somewhere else entirely. Then the one transmission
that would have corrected them — "pass behind the CRJ" — was cut out by their
own 0.8-second mic key. Meanwhile their altimeter was reading low, putting
them 78 feet higher than the route allowed, on a charted line that had no
width, watched on a display that rounds every altitude to the nearest hundred
feet. [ALL CONFIRMED — primary]
- The UH-60L cockpit had **both** a barometric and a radar altimeter; the
  Helicopter Ops factual report documents the instrument layout and that at
  least one Army standardization pilot said he did not use radar-altimeter
  bugs in that context. [CONFIRMED — primary, HELO-A §figures 12/37/38]

## 4. The final ninety seconds — CONFIRMED (primary, verbatim)

Certified FAA facility timeline (UTC), from the FAA Aircraft Accident Package:

- **0145Z** — PAT25 reports at the Memorial Bridge; LC acknowledges.
- **0146Z** — LC issues PAT25 traffic: a CRJ at 1,200 feet circling to runway
  33, just off the Wilson Bridge. **PAT25 reports traffic in sight and
  requests visual separation; LC approves.**
- **0147Z** — LC asks PAT25 if the CRJ traffic is in sight and to pass behind
  the CRJ. PAT25 reports traffic in sight and requests visual separation.
  **"Instructions to pass behind the CRJ were not read back."** LC states

  helicopter off the approach end of runway 33 on the **Crash Phone.**
- **0149Z** — Ground Control advises MWAA Fire Command of a collision in the

## 5. The finding nobody could explain away — CONFIRMED

**Verbatim, AIR-26-02 §1.14 "Flight Safety Data" — use these exact numbers:**
The FAA's ASIAS program reported that between **October 2021 and December
2024** there were **15,214 occurrences** between commercial airplanes and
helicopters near DCA with **lateral separation under 1 nm and vertical
separation under 400 ft** — and **85 occurrences** with **lateral separation
under 1,500 ft and vertical separation under 200 ft.** [CONFIRMED — primary]

The **85** is the number to lead with: 85 times in a little over three years,
an airliner and a helicopter came within 1,500 feet and 200 feet of each other
at this one airport, and it was recorded.

Follow-on finding, same section: inquiries revealed **"the FAA lacked a
standardized approach to defining and tracking data that might be used to
assess midair collision risk"** — which is why the NTSB had to form its own
Safety Data group. [CONFIRMED — primary, verbatim]
- The NTSB has recommended **ADS-B In 17 times since 2006.** Equipment cost
  cited at roughly **$1,500–$3,000**; aircraft with ADS-B In showed a **53%**
  lower accident rate and **89%** lower fatal accident rate. [CONFIRMED as
  presented at the Board Meeting — SECONDARY]

## 6. Probable cause and aftermath — CONFIRMED (NTSB, Jan 27 2026)

**PROBABLE CAUSE — VERBATIM** from *Aviation Investigation Report AIR-26-02*,
§3.3, p. 299 (PDF on disk: `NTSB_AIR2602_FinalReport.pdf`, downloaded from
https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/AIR2602.pdf).
**This is the exhibit page — cite AIR-26-02 §3.3, never a paraphrase:**

> "The NTSB determines that the probable cause of this accident was the
> Federal Aviation Administration's (FAA) placement of a helicopter route in
> close proximity to a runway approach path; their failure to regularly review
> and evaluate helicopter routes and available data, and their failure to act
> on recommendations to mitigate the risk of a midair collision near Ronald
> Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA); as well as the air traffic
> system's overreliance on visual separation in order to promote efficient
> traffic flow without consideration for the limitations of the see-and-avoid
> concept. Also causal was the lack of effective pilot-applied visual
> separation by the helicopter crew, which resulted in a midair collision.
> Additional causal factors were the tower team's loss of situation awareness
> and degraded performance due to the high workload of the combined
> helicopter and local control positions and the absence of a risk assessment
> process to identify and mitigate real-time operational risk factors, which
> resulted in misprioritization of duties, inadequate traffic advisories, and
> the lack of safety alerts to both flight crews. Also causal was the Army's
> failure to ensure pilots were aware of the effects of error tolerances on
> barometric altimeters in their helicopters, which resulted in the crew
> flying above the maximum published helicopter route altitude."

**Contributing factors (verbatim, same page):**
- "the limitations of the traffic awareness and collision alerting systems on
  both aircraft, which precluded effective alerting of the impending collision
  to the flight crews;"
- "an unsustainable airport arrival rate, increasing traffic volume with a
  changing fleet mix, and airline scheduling practices at DCA, which regularly
  strained the DCA air traffic control tower workforce and degraded safety
  over time;"
- "the Army's lack of a fully implemented safety management system, which
  should have identified and addressed hazards associated with altitude
  exceedances on the Washington, DC, helicopter routes;"
- "the FAA's failure across multiple organizations to implement previous NTSB
  recommendations, including Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast In,
  and to follow and fully integrate its established safety management
  system…"
- "the absence of effective data sharing and analysis among the FAA, aircraft
  operators, and other relevant organizations."

[ALL CONFIRMED — primary, AIR-26-02 §3.3. 74 findings, **50 recommendations**
(A-25-001 through A-26-057).] **Note:** a second report number, **AIR-25-01**,

- **FAA response:** closed the relevant segment of Route 4 (2025) and
