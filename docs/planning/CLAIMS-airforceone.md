## 1. NEUTRAL SUMMARY

On the morning of September 11, 2001, President George W. Bush 🧍 was at the Emma E. Booker Elementary School in Sarasota, Florida, for an education event. He learned of the first World Trade Center crash before entering a classroom, and at 9:05 a.m. his chief of staff, Andrew Card 🧍, whispered to him that a second plane had hit and that America was under attack. Bush stayed in the classroom another five to seven minutes, made a brief statement from the school, and departed by motorcade at 9:35. Air Force One took off at about 9:54–9:55 a.m. with no fixed destination; the pilot's stated objective was to get "as fast and as high as possible" and decide where to go afterward. The aircraft had no fighter escort for its first roughly 95 minutes in the air; Texas Air National Guard F-16s joined it only minutes before it landed at Barksdale Air Force Base, Louisiana, at about 11:45.

Meanwhile, in the White House shelter, Vice President Dick Cheney 🧍 twice authorized fighters to engage an inbound aircraft — believed to be United 93 — between roughly 10:10 and 10:18, saying he did so on the basis of an earlier call with the President. The Commission found no documentary record of that earlier call. Deputy Chief of Staff Joshua Bolten 🧍 suggested Cheney call the President to confirm; a two-minute call was logged at 10:18, and Ari Fleischer's 🧍 notes aboard Air Force One record at 10:20 that the President said he had authorized a shootdown if necessary. None of this reached the fighters in time: United 93 had already crashed at 10:03:11, NORAD's Northeast Air Defense Sector (NEADS) did not learn the flight existed until 10:07, and shootdown authority first reached NEADS at 10:31 — 28 minutes after the crash. Even then, it was never passed to the pilots.

Around 10:30 a report of a threat to "Angel" — the Air Force One call sign — spread through the shelter and the aircraft. The Commission concluded it "originated in a misunderstanding by a watch officer in the White House Situation Room" and was "unfounded," though it noted the Situation Room's director disputes that account. Air Force One went on to Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska (arriving 2:50 p.m.), from where Bush held a secure video conference, before returning to Andrews Air Force Base in the evening and addressing the nation from the White House at 8:30 p.m.

## 3. BUSH'S MORNING — SARASOTA

- **Location:** Emma E. Booker Elementary School, Sarasota, Florida; the President "was to read to a class and talk about education." CONFIRMED, p. 35.
- **First crash notification:** Rove, outside the classroom, describes "a small, twin-engine plane." Bush's reaction: pilot error. CONFIRMED, p. 35. Then Rice at 8:55: "that's all we know right now, Mr. President." CONFIRMED, p. 35.
- **The Card line — exact wording and timing.** The Commission's text (p. 38): *"The President was seated in a classroom when, at 9:05, Andrew Card whispered to him: 'A second plane hit the second tower. America is under attack.'"* The 9:05 comes from the President's Daily Diary; the wording comes from Card's own Aug. 7, 2002 interview with AP's Ron Fournier (n. 203). CONFIRMED. **Use exactly these eleven words.** Common variants ("a second plane has hit the second tower," "we're under attack") are paraphrases — do not use.
- **Why he stayed:** "The President told us his instinct was to project calm, not to have the country see an excited reaction at a moment of crisis... He saw their phones and pagers start to ring." CONFIRMED, p. 38. Attribute as the President's own explanation to the Commission.
- **How long he stayed:** "another five to seven minutes, while the children continued reading," then holding room "shortly before 9:15." CONFIRMED, p. 38–39. The popular "seven minutes" (from *Fahrenheit 9/11*) is the top of the Commission's range — say "five to seven minutes" or "roughly seven minutes," not "seven minutes of inaction."
- **Information vacuum:** Between 9:15 and 9:30 "No one in the traveling party had any information during this time that other aircraft were hijacked or missing... no one with the President was in contact with the Pentagon... The only decision made during this time was to return to Washington." CONFIRMED, p. 39.
- **Secret Service posture:** "anxious to move the President to a safer location, but did not think it imperative for him to run out the door." CONFIRMED, p. 39.
- **The 9:45 call (Fleischer's notes):** "Sounds like we have a minor war going on here, I heard about the Pentagon. We're at war . . . somebody's going to pay." CONFIRMED, p. 39, n. 206. **The Report records no shootdown content in this call.** Fleischer's notes also record at 9:45 "be sure to set first lady + daughter protected" (2016 release; cross-checked with the Report's "asked the Secret Service about the safety of his family," p. 39). CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed).

## 4. THE DEPARTURE, THE CLIMB, AND THE MISSING ESCORT

**Destination undecided; climb first.** "Card, the lead Secret Service agent, the President's military aide, and the pilot were conferring on a possible destination... The President strongly wanted to return to Washington and only grudgingly agreed to go elsewhere... Air Force One departed at about 9:54 without any fixed destination. The objective was to get up in the air—as fast and as high as possible—and then decide where to go." CONFIRMED, p. 40 (source for the objective: lead agent Edward Marinzel, n. 207). Staff Statement 17 adds: "All witnesses agreed that the President strongly wanted to return to Washington." CONFIRMED.

**The pilot:** Col. Mark Tillman 🧍, USAF, presidential aircraft commander. CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed — he has given on-record interviews for 20+ years; not named in the Commission text).

**The figure at the end of the runway / the runway reversal.** This is **not in the Commission Report.** It rests on Tillman's own repeated accounts and Rove's account to Garrett Graff (*The Only Plane in the Sky*, 2019): as Air Force One taxied, the Secret Service reported a person at the end of the runway who appeared to be holding a long gun; Tillman took off at full power toward/over that end rather than taxi slowly past; the person turned out to be a man with his children holding a **video camera**. Rove to Graff: "[Col. Tillman] stood that thing on its tail—just nose up, tail down, like we were on a roller coaster." CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed to Tillman and Rove — script as "according to the pilot, Col. Mark Tillman"). **Two details to handle carefully:** (a) whether Tillman literally *reversed runway direction* vs. simply aimed the departure over the spot — accounts differ in wording; the safe form is "took off at full power over the spot where the man was standing rather than taxi past him." (b) The identity/motive of the man has never been publicly established — say "later reported to have been a father with a video camera," not "confirmed."

**The steep climb.** Tillman (2011, Fox/CBS interviews): "The plane climbed out rather steeply, so that there was no way they could shoot at us." CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed). Sarasota tower controller Pierson issued "a standard straight-out climb from runway three-two." CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed — 2026 local interviews).

**Altitude.** **NEEDS SOURCE for "45,000 feet."** USAF radar data cited by The Aviationist show Air Force One crossing the Florida Panhandle westbound at about FL390 (39,000 ft); the plan was "to climb above any airliner in the 30K feet range." The VC-25's certified ceiling is 45,000 ft; the 45,000 figure repeated online is not traced to a primary record. Say "climbed to around 39,000–40,000 feet, above airline traffic," or omit the number.

**"Fighter escorts for Air Force One" requested at 9:59** via the air threat conference — the request was made *after* takeoff. CONFIRMED, p. 38.

**The "Holy cow" exchange — exact wording and provenance.**
> Herndon Center: Air Force One . . . still does not have a fighter escort
> FAA HQ: They still do not!? Holy Cow!
> FAA HQ: Is the NMCC aware of this?

Time: **10:02 EDT.** Source: transcribed FAA phone line x4530 (Herndon Command Center ↔ FAA Headquarters), Commission file 148-911-03011356A-1-s2, as published by former Commission staff investigator Miles Kara ("9-11: Air Force One; Flight to Barksdale, an uncertain decision," oredigger61.org). CONFIRMED (secondary, from a named Commission staffer citing the archival file). **Two corrections to the brief:** (1) this is an **FAA** line (Herndon/HQ), **not a NORAD/NEADS tape**, and it is **not in the April 9, 2008 NORAD/USNORTHCOM FOIA release**; (2) the speakers are FAA Command Center and FAA HQ personnel — not the Secret Service, not the White House. Script it as "on an FAA phone line preserved in the Commission's files."

**How long without escort, and who finally showed up.** Air Force One flew unescorted from ~9:54 until F-16s of the **147th Fighter Wing, Texas Air National Guard (Ellington Field, Houston)** joined at **11:28 EDT (radar) / 11:29 (pool-report sighting)** — about five minutes before the Barksdale landing. That is roughly **94–95 minutes** airborne without escort. CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed to Kara's radar reconstruction; the Commission text does not give the escort join time). The Houston Center call "Air Force One, you have fast movers your 7 o'clock" is reported by The Aviationist — **NEEDS SOURCE (audio)**; do not present as a verified recording.

**Important context (so the escort story isn't overstated):** the Langley F-16s were over Washington from about 10:00 and NORAD ordered all air sovereignty aircraft to battle stations at 9:49 (p. 38) — the shortfall was in *getting fighters to the President's aircraft*, not in the nation having no fighters up. CONFIRMED.

## 5. THE SHOOTDOWN ORDER — the heart of the episode

**The chain of command, stated by the Commission (p. 43):** *"In most cases, the chain of command authorizing the use of force runs from the president to the secretary of defense and from the secretary to the combatant commander."* The Vice President is not in it. **The Commission also found (p. 43): "The President apparently spoke to Secretary Rumsfeld for the first time that morning shortly after 10:00. No one can recall the content of this conversation, but it was a brief call in which the subject of shootdown authority was not discussed."** CONFIRMED. Script: "The Vice President is not in the military chain of command. The one person who is — the Secretary of Defense — spoke to the President shortly after ten, and shootdown authority didn't come up."

**Cheney's claim of prior authorization — and the Commission's exact hedge (pp. 40–41):**
> "The Vice President stated that he called the President to discuss the rules of engagement for the CAP. He recalled feeling that it did no good to establish the CAP unless the pilots had instructions on whether they were authorized to shoot if the plane would not divert. He said the President signed off on that concept. The President said he remembered such a conversation, and that it reminded him of when he had been an interceptor pilot. The President emphasized to us that he had authorized the shootdown of hijacked aircraft."
>
> "Rice... remembered hearing him inform the President, 'Sir, the CAPs are up. Sir, they're going to want to know what to do.' Then she recalled hearing him say, 'Yes sir.' She believed this conversation occurred a few minutes, perhaps five, after they entered the conference room."
>
> **"We believe this call would have taken place sometime before 10:10 to 10:15. Among the sources that reflect other important events of that morning, there is no documentary evidence for this call, but the relevant sources are incomplete. Others nearby who were taking notes, such as the Vice President's chief of staff, Scooter Libby, who sat next to him, and Mrs. Cheney, did not note a call between the President and Vice President immediately after the Vice President entered the conference room."**

CONFIRMED, verbatim. **This is the exact hedged language. Use it as written.** The Commission does *not* say Cheney lied, does *not* say the call didn't happen, and does *not* say it did. It says: no record, incomplete records, two note-takers sitting next to him didn't note it. The Commission relied on "(1) phone logs of the White House switchboard; (2) notes of Lewis Libby, Mrs. Cheney, and Ari Fleischer; (3) the tape (and then transcript) of the air threat conference call; and (4) Secret Service and White House Situation Room logs, as well as four separate White House Military Office logs" (n. 216). CONFIRMED.

**The two engage authorizations (p. 41):** "At some time between 10:10 and 10:15, a military aide told the Vice President and others that the aircraft was 80 miles out. Vice President Cheney was asked for authority to engage the aircraft. His reaction was described by Scooter Libby as quick and decisive, 'in about the time it takes a batter to decide to swing.' The Vice President authorized fighter aircraft to engage the inbound plane. He told us he based this authorization on his earlier conversation with the President. The military aide returned a few minutes later, probably between 10:12 and 10:18, and said the aircraft was 60 miles out. He again asked for authorization to engage. The Vice President again said yes." CONFIRMED, verbatim. **Note: the aircraft being tracked was United 93 — which had crashed at 10:03. The Secret Service "was relying on projections and was not aware the plane was already down in Pennsylvania" (p. 41).** The "80 miles"/"60 miles" were projections of a plane that no longer existed.

**Bolten (p. 41):** "Bolten watched the exchanges and, after what he called 'a quiet moment,' suggested that the Vice President get in touch with the President and confirm the engage order. Bolten told us he wanted to make sure the President was told that the Vice President had executed the order. He said he had not heard any prior discussion on the subject with the President." CONFIRMED, verbatim. **Libby's contemporaneous note (n. 220): "10:15–18: Aircraft 60 miles out, confirmed as hijack—engage? VP: Yes. JB [Joshua Bolten]: Get President and confirm engage order".** CONFIRMED, verbatim — this is a primary-document quote and the strongest single exhibit in the episode.

**The 10:18 call and Fleischer's 10:20 note (p. 41):** "The Vice President was logged calling the President at 10:18 for a two-minute conversation that obtained the confirmation. On Air Force One, the President's press secretary was taking notes; Ari Fleischer recorded that at 10:20, the President told him that he had authorized a shootdown of aircraft if necessary." Endnote 221: **"Fleischer's 10:20 note is the first mention of shootdown authority."** CONFIRMED, verbatim.

> **⚠ Exact-wording caveat on the Fleischer note.** The Commission *paraphrases* the note ("authorized a shootdown of aircraft if necessary"). When Fleischer released the six handwritten pages in September 2016 (to Reuters' Steve Holland and Yahoo News' Olivier Knox), Knox transcribed the 10:20 line as **"authorized shoot down if reason"** — a plausible handwriting reading of a hurried note. Fleischer's own later tweets render it "Authorized shoot down if necessary." **Do not put "authorized shot down if necessary" in quotation marks as the note's text.** Safe forms: "Fleischer's note at 10:20 records the President saying he had authorized a shootdown if necessary" (Commission paraphrase, attributed), or show the handwritten page itself if a licensed image is available.

**Two separate confirmation channels — don't merge them.** The 10:18–10:20 Cheney↔Bush call (shelter/AF1) and the 10:25 "confirmation" received by Richard Clarke's White House *video teleconference* (p. 37: "Clarke reported that they were asking the President for authority to shoot down aircraft. Confirmation of that authority came at 10:25, but the commands were already being conveyed in more direct contacts with the Pentagon") are different rooms and different times. CONFIRMED.

**The 10:39 Cheney–Rumsfeld exchange (p. 43) — quote in full or not at all:**
> Vice President: There's been at least three instances here where we've had reports of aircraft approaching Washington—a couple were confirmed hijack. And, pursuant to the President's instructions I gave authorization for them to be taken out. Hello?
> SecDef: Yes, I understand. Who did you give that direction to?
> Vice President: It was passed from here through the [operations] center at the White House, from the [shelter].
> SecDef: OK, let me ask the question here. Has that directive been transmitted to the aircraft?
> Vice President: Yes, it has.
> SecDef: So we've got a couple of aircraft up there that have those instructions at this present time?
> Vice President: That is correct. And it's my understanding they've already taken a couple of aircraft out.
> SecDef: We can't confirm that. We're told that one aircraft is down but we do not have a pilot report that did it.

CONFIRMED, verbatim (DOD transcript, Air Threat Conference Call, n. 233). **Landmine:** "they've already taken a couple of aircraft out" is Cheney's *mistaken understanding at 10:39*, not a fact — the Commission's next line is: "The Vice President was mistaken in his belief that shootdown authorization had been passed to the pilots flying at NORAD's direction" (p. 44). Never present the 10:39 line without the correction.

**Cheney's public statement five days later (Meet the Press, Sept. 16, 2001, White House transcript):** "the president made the decision, on my recommendation as well, wholeheartedly concurred in the decision he made, that if the plane would not divert, if they wouldn't pay any attention to instructions to move away from the city, as a last resort, our pilots were authorized to take them out." CONFIRMED (White House archive transcript). Cheney in his 2011 memoir *In My Time* recalled the moment word came of a plane down south of Johnstown: "Had it been forced down? Had it been shot down by one of our pilots following the authorization I'd conveyed?" CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed). Use both only as attributed recollection.

- NEADS "was never able to locate United 93 on radar because it was already in the ground" (SS17). Formal crash notification at 10:15 (SS17). CONFIRMED.
- **"It is not clear how the shootdown order was communicated within NORAD. ## 8. "ANGEL IS NEXT" — the threat that wasn't

**What the Commission actually says (Ch. 10 p. 325 + n. 1) — quote exactly:**
> "One of these alarms was of a reported threat against Air Force One itself, a threat eventually run down to a misunderstood communication in the hectic White House Situation Room that morning."
>
> n. 1: "Sometime around 10:30, after the decision had already been made not to return to Washington, a reported threat to 'Angel'—the code word for Air Force One—was widely disseminated in the Presidential Emergency Operations Center (PEOC) and aboard Air Force One. Notes from the morning indicate that Vice President Cheney informed President Bush in a phone conversation shortly after 10:30 that an anonymous threat had been phoned into the White House that was viewed as credible. At about the same time, news of the threat was conveyed on the air threat conference call. The Secret Service's Intelligence Division tracked down the origin of this threat and, during the day, determined that it had originated in a misunderstanding by a watch officer in the White House Situation Room. **The director of the White House Situation Room that day disputes this account.** But the Intelligence Division had the primary job of running down the story, and we found their witnesses on this point to be credible. During the afternoon of September 11 the leadership of the Secret Service was satisfied that the reported threat to 'Angel' was unfounded."

CONFIRMED, verbatim.

**⛔ Four things the script MUST get right here:**
1. **The Commission never prints the words "Angel is next."** It says "a reported threat to 'Angel'." The three-word phrase comes from participants' accounts — Fleischer's notes (10:32, "Angel is next," 2016 release), Tillman ("we were told Angel was next," CBS 2011), Bush's and Cheney's later tellings. CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed). Fine to use — but attribute it to Fleischer's notes/Tillman, not to the Commission.
2. **The Commission does NOT say "those words were never said."** It says the threat "originated in a misunderstanding by a watch officer in the White House Situation Room" and was "unfounded," *and* records that the Situation Room director disputes that. The best participant explanation of the mechanism is Fleischer's (Miller Center oral history): someone in the Situation Room received a report that "Air Force One" was or could be next; when it was passed to the PEOC the speaker substituted the code word "Angel"; because the code word was classified, hearers inferred insider knowledge. CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed to Fleischer). **Script the debunk as: "The Secret Service traced it to a misunderstanding inside the White House Situation Room — someone relaying a garbled report used the code word themselves. By that afternoon the Secret Service considered the threat unfounded. The Commission accepted that finding, while noting the Situation Room's director disputes it."**
3. **The timing matters:** the threat came "after the decision had already been made not to return to Washington" (n. 1) — it did **not** cause the diversion. Scripts that say "a threat to Air Force One forced the President away from Washington" invert the Commission's sequence. CONFIRMED.
4. **The public claim and its retraction is itself part of the record:** Fleischer, Sept. 12, 2001 briefing: "There was real and credible information that came in to the White House, and that is the reason why the White House, Air Force One, took the actions that it took." Asked when: "On the flight from Sarasota to the first location." CONFIRMED (White House transcript). The Commission: "Fleischer told us he cited the information in good faith... they had decided to let people know about the threat, all of them believing it was true. According to Fleischer, only weeks later did he learn—from press reports—that the threat was unfounded. We have not found any evidence that contradicts his account." CONFIRMED, n. 1. The retraction was first reported by Mike Allen, *Washington Post*, Sept. 27, 2001 ("White House Drops Claim of Threat to Bush"). CONFIRMED (secondary). William Safire's Sept. 13, 2001 *NYT* column "Inside the Bunker" reported the threat as "Air Force One is next" with code words showing insider knowledge, sourced to a "high White House official" and confirmed by Rove — this is the origin of the "inside job" flavor. CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed).

**Operational consequence aboard the aircraft:** Tillman posted security at the cockpit stairs/door because the code-word use raised fear of an insider. CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed to Tillman and Fleischer).

**"Angel" as a non-public call sign:** described by the Commission as "the code word for Air Force One" (n. 1) and by Tillman as "the classified call sign of Air Force One." CONFIRMED. (It has since been retired/changed — say "the call sign at the time.")

## 9. THE "MILITARY SECRETLY SHOT DOWN UNITED 93" THEORY — claim, then evidence

**Framing rule (non-negotiable):** state the claim plainly and fairly, test each plank against a cited record, let the record settle it. **Never** use "cover-up," "what they don't want you to know," "questions remain," or "we may never know." The Commission's *own* findings — that NORAD's original story was false and that the shootdown order came too late to be executed — are the strongest evidence *against* the theory, and the script should say so directly.

### 9a. THE CLAIM as proponents state it
Proponents argue United 93 was destroyed by a U.S. fighter or missile before 10:03 and that the government concealed it. They point to:
1. **Debris scatter "over several miles"** — paper, mail and light material found at **Indian Lake** (a lake community roughly 1.5 miles from the crater — NPS gives 1.5; some accounts 2–2.5 miles) and burned paper at **New Baltimore, ~8 miles away**; they argue a plane that hit the ground intact cannot scatter debris that far. CONFIRMED that these finds occurred (NPS: "Debris was recovered from Indian Lake, 1.5 miles away, and from the lawns of nearby homes and farmer's fields."). CONFIRMED that the "eight miles" figure appears in contemporary press and proponent literature — **NEEDS SOURCE for a primary (FBI/NTSB) statement of the exact New Baltimore distance.**
2. **Eyewitnesses describing a second aircraft** — chiefly Susan McElwain 🧍, who described a small white jet, "no markings," low and fast, which she says was "definitely military" and not a Falcon 20; others reported a white/light-grey jet nearby before and after the crash. CONFIRMED that these accounts exist and have been repeated on record for 20+ years.
3. **Official remarks that sound like admissions:** Rumsfeld, Dec. 24, 2004, to troops in Baghdad: "the people who attacked the United States in New York, shot down the plane over Pennsylvania and attacked the Pentagon" — the Pentagon said he misspoke (CNN, Dec. 27, 2004). CONFIRMED (secondary). Cheney's 10:39 "it's my understanding they've already taken a couple of aircraft out" (p. 43) and his Meet the Press statement that pilots "were authorized to take them out." CONFIRMED (primary). Deputy SecDef Wolfowitz, Sept. 15, 2001, saying the Pentagon had been "following" Flight 93 and was prepared to shoot it down. CONFIRMED (per Commission staff monograph; see CLAIMS-flight93 §6).
4. **The engine section found "some distance" from the crater** and the general severity of fragmentation. CONFIRMED that an engine fan section was recovered away from the main crater (FBI account to the Commission of a "bounce"; the exact distance is variously reported — **NEEDS SOURCE for the number**).

### 9b. THE EVIDENCE to test it against
1. **All 44 aboard were positively identified** — by fingerprints, dental records and DNA — under Somerset County Coroner Wallace Miller 🧍. The first ~16 within two weeks by fingerprints/dental, the rest by DNA; Jarrah via a DNA sample from his girlfriend in Germany; the other three hijackers by elimination. NPS: "Enough remains were recovered to identify everyone who was on board the plane using dental records, fingerprints, or DNA analysis." CONFIRMED (NPS; Pittsburgh Post-Gazette Oct. 2001 court record on death certificates; cross-checked with CLAIMS-flight93 §7). *(Per the flight93 ethics rules: state the identification fact; never the remains statistics.)*
2. **The cockpit voice recorder was recovered and its content is in the Commission's text** — quotes "taken from our listening to the CVR, aided by an Arabic speaker." The recording documents the hijackers deciding to "pull it down," the aircraft rolling, and ending at impact at 10:03:11 — a deliberate crash under hijacker control while passengers assaulted the cockpit. No external strike, warning, or anomaly appears. CONFIRMED (Commission Ch. 1 pp. 13–14; Moussaoui trial exhibit; CLAIMS-flight93 §5).
3. **NEADS did not know United 93 existed until 10:07 — four minutes after it crashed.** "NORAD did not even know the plane was hijacked until after it had crashed" (p. 44). The 9:16 hijack-notification time NORAD gave in May 2003 testimony "was incorrect. There was no hijack to report at 9:16" (SS17). "By the time the military learned about the flight, it had crashed" (SS17; p. 34). CONFIRMED, verbatim. **This is the single strongest refutation, and it comes from the Commission's subpoenaed tapes and radar, not from NORAD's word.** You cannot shoot down an aircraft you do not know is there.
4. **Shootdown authority reached NEADS at 10:31 — 28 minutes after the 10:03:11 crash — and even then was never passed to the pilots** ("the only orders actually conveyed to the pilots were to 'ID type and tail,'" p. 43). "At 10:10, the pilots over Washington were emphatically told, 'negative clearance to shoot'" (p. 45). CONFIRMED, verbatim. An order that arrives 28 minutes after the event cannot have caused the event.
5. **Who was actually airborne and with what.** The only armed fighters near Washington in the window were the three Langley F-16s (over D.C. ~10:00) — "Two of the three Langley fighters were fully armed (i.e., with missiles and guns); the third fighter carried only guns" (n. 238) — and they "did not know the threat they were facing, did not know where United 93 was located, and did not have shootdown authorization" (p. 45). The Andrews jets (113th Wing) were first airborne at 10:38 — 35 minutes after the crash. Lt. Col. Marc Sasseville 🧍 and Lt. Heather Penney 🧍 have said repeatedly, on the record since 2011 (Washington Post, Sept. 2011; Penney's own site; American Legion 2026), that there was **no time to load live missiles** — their jets carried only the minimal ball ammunition kept aboard for trim — and that their plan was to **ram** the airliner: Sasseville the cockpit, Penney the tail. CONFIRMED (secondary, attributed to the pilots; the Commission confirms the 10:38 first-airborne time and the "weapons free" ROE, p. 44). Both Otis F-15s were over Manhattan. No other armed interceptor was anywhere near western Pennsylvania at 10:03. CONFIRMED.
6. **The debris.** The FBI's explanation, given to the Commission and repeated in the public record, is that the light material (paper, mail, insulation, fabric) was lofted by the fireball/thermal plume and carried downwind — to Indian Lake and, for the lightest paper, New Baltimore — and that the engine fan section "bounced" from the impact. CONFIRMED that this is the FBI's stated explanation (via Commission staff files and contemporaneous FBI briefings reported at the time); **NEEDS SOURCE for a single citable FBI document — if not found, attribute as "the FBI's explanation, as reported at the time and to the Commission."** Proponents' counter (Altoona winds ~10 mph, direction) is a genuine point of contention on *mechanism*, not on *cause*: heavy structure, engines and human remains were all in or immediately around the crater; only light debris travelled. CONFIRMED (NPS: "Most of the pieces of wreckage were the size of a notebook or smaller. The largest pieces recovered were parts of the plane's two engines and a piece of fuselage." Crater excavated to 85 × 85 ft, 27–40 ft deep). A missile strike at altitude would distribute heavy structure along a track measured in miles; it did not.
7. **The white jet.** FAA controllers asked a civilian Dassault Falcon 20 business jet, inbound to Johnstown, to descend and visually locate the crash site *after* impact. CONFIRMED (Commission-era FAA account; CLAIMS-flight93 §8). McElwain's insistence that what she saw was not a Falcon is her sincere recollection; it is not evidence of a weapon release — no weapon, no fragmentation signature, and no pilot report exists (Rumsfeld at 10:39: "we do not have a pilot report that did it," p. 43).
8. **The "admissions."** Rumsfeld's 2004 sentence was retracted as a misstatement within three days; Cheney's 10:39 "taken a couple of aircraft out" is his *belief*, which the Commission states in the next paragraph was *mistaken* (p. 44); Wolfowitz's Sept. 15 claim was part of the *inaccurate* NORAD-side narrative the Commission later demolished — the same day, ANG commander Gen. Paul Weaver said "We weren't even close." The pattern in every case is officials *believing or claiming more capability than existed*, never a pilot, a radar track, or a weapon.

### 9c. The Commission's hedge — and which way it cuts
> "NORAD officials have maintained that they would have intercepted and shot down United 93. We are not so sure. We are sure that the nation owes a debt to the passengers of United 93. Their actions saved the lives of countless others, and may have saved either the Capitol or the White House from destruction." (p. 45) CONFIRMED, verbatim.

**This cuts *against* the theory.** The Commission is doubting NORAD's claim that it *could* have intercepted United 93 in a hypothetical where the passengers had not acted. It is the opposite of saying the military *did*. Scripts that lift "We are not so sure" out of context to imply doubt about *what happened* are misquoting. Put the full sentence on screen with its subject.

### 9d. On-screen verdict wording (use as-is)
"The claim is that a fighter destroyed United 93 before it hit the ground. The record shows: the air defense sector didn't know the plane existed until four minutes after it crashed; the authority to shoot arrived twenty-eight minutes after it crashed and was never given to a pilot; the only fighters that could have reached it were briefed to identify, not engage; the cockpit recorder ends with the hijackers rolling the aircraft into the ground; and every person aboard was identified from the crater. The claim doesn't survive contact with the government's own tapes — tapes that the same government had, at first, described wrongly."

## 12. SENSITIVITY / EDITORIAL RULES (non-negotiable)

> **The pilots' actual orders:** "while leaders in Washington believed that the fighters above them had been instructed to 'take out' hostile aircraft, the only orders actually conveyed to the pilots were to 'ID type and tail.'" — p. 43.

> **The correction:** "The Vice President was mistaken in his belief that shootdown authorization had been passed to the pilots flying at NORAD's direction." — p. 44.

> **Chain of command:** "In most cases, the chain of command authorizing the use of force runs from the president to the secretary of defense and from the secretary to the combatant commander. The President apparently spoke to Secretary Rumsfeld for the first time that morning shortly after 10:00. No one can recall the content of this conversation, but it was a brief call in which the subject of shootdown authority was not discussed." — p. 43.

> **What If:** "NORAD officials have maintained consistently that had the passengers not caused United 93 to crash, the military would have prevented it from reaching Washington, D.C. That conclusion is based on a version of events that we now know is incorrect. The Langley fighters were not scrambled in response to United 93; NORAD did not have 47 minutes to intercept the flight; NORAD did not even know the plane was hijacked until after it had crashed." — p. 44.

> **What If, close:** "NORAD officials have maintained that they would have intercepted and shot down United 93. We are not so sure. We are sure that the nation owes a debt to the passengers of United 93." — p. 45.

> **Angel:** "a threat eventually run down to a misunderstood communication in the hectic White House Situation Room that morning." — Ch. 10 p. 325. / "...determined that it had originated in a misunderstanding by a watch officer in the White House Situation Room. The director of the White House Situation Room that day disputes this account... During the afternoon of September 11 the leadership of the Secret Service was satisfied that the reported threat to 'Angel' was unfounded." — Ch. 10 n. 1.

> **Fleischer, Sept. 12, 2001:** "There was real and credible information that came in to the White House, and that is the reason why the White House, Air Force One, took the actions that it took, in accordance with all existing plans." — White House transcript.

> **Cheney, Meet the Press, Sept. 16, 2001:** "if the plane would not divert, if they wouldn't pay any attention to instructions to move away from the city, as a last resort, our pilots were authorized to take them out." — White House transcript.

> **Tillman (CBS, Sept. 10, 2011):** "Eventually, at one point, we were told Angel was next, the classified call sign of Air Force One. (We) didn't know how they were gonna (try to) do it." — attribute to Tillman.

> **Rove on the climb (to Garrett Graff):** "[Col. Tillman] stood that thing on its tail—just nose up, tail down, like we were on a roller coaster." — *The Only Plane in the Sky* (2019); attribute to Rove.

> **The 10:02 NEADS reflection:** "This is a new type of war." — p. 46; n. 241.
