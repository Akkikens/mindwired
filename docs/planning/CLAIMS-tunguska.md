June 30, 1908 atmospheric explosion over the Podkamennaya Tunguska River,
Siberia, which flattened ~2,150 km² of forest with no crater and no recovered
meteorite ever found. Labels:
**CONFIRMED** (sourced, verified) / **DISPUTED** (legitimate sources disagree)
/ **ALLEGED** (attributed to a named party, not settled) / **UNVERIFIED**
(repeated widely but not independently confirmed against a primary source) /
**EXCLUDE** (do not use).

## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **"80 million trees flattened" is the traditional popular figure, NOT a
   settled precise count — DISPUTED.** NASA, Britannica, and Scientific
   American all repeat "80 million," but more recent tree-ring/forestry
   re-analysis (cited in Wikipedia's "forestfalls" discussion and a 2021
   arXiv paper) argues the true number may be overstated by roughly a factor
   of four. **Use:** "the blast flattened forest across roughly 830 square
   miles — the traditional estimate puts the toll at 80 million trees, though
   newer tree-ring analysis suggests the real count may be considerably
   lower." Do not state 80 million as a bare, unhedged fact.
2. **Human casualties are UNVERIFIED, not a confirmed toll.** The commonly
   cited "up to three deaths" traces to oral testimony gathered at an Evenki
   council in **1926 — eighteen years after the event** — with no forensic or
   documentary corroboration. **Use:** "there are unconfirmed reports that as
   many as three Evenki herders may have died," never "three people died."
3. **The eyewitness's name is "S. Semenov" ONLY — do not invent "Sergei."**
   Every primary-chain source (Krinov 1966; Vasiliev et al. 1981; Wikipedia)
   uses the initial only. "Sergei Semenov" and "a farmer having breakfast"
   are later blog/SEO embellishments (vocal.media, historicalblindness.com
   and similar) not present in the primary translation — **EXCLUDE the full
   first name and the "farmer" occupation claim**; he is documented only as
   present at the Vanavara trading post.
4. **The distance Semenov was thrown is a translation discrepancy, not a
   contradiction — don't stack both numbers as if independently confirmed.**
   Commonly quoted English retellings say "twenty feet" (~6 m); Wikipedia's
   own paraphrase says "a few metres." This is almost certainly a translation
   variance from the Russian original (likely "several sazhens"). **Use:**
   "thrown several meters off the porch" or similar single hedged phrasing —
   do not present "twenty feet" as a precise, independently-verified figure.
5. **Avoid false precision on the exact time — "07:17:11 local time" is a
   scientific back-calculation, not a clock reading at the scene.** That
   figure (Wikipedia) is derived from seismograph/barograph traces at
   Irkutsk and other stations combined with witness recollection of
   sun/clock position — not a synchronized timestamp anyone recorded on site.
   Sources range 7:14–7:17 AM. **Use:** "at approximately 7:15 AM local time"
   or "around 7:14–7:17 AM" — never claim second-level precision.
6. **"Magnitude 5.0 on the Richter scale" is an approximate modern
   comparison, not a historical measurement — the Richter scale wasn't
   invented until 1935, 27 years after the event.** Some seismic stations
   registered ground tremors described as *comparable to* a magnitude-5
   quake by later analysis. **Use:** "seismographs across Eurasia recorded
   ground tremors — by later analysis, comparable to a magnitude-5
   earthquake" — flag as an approximate retrospective comparison, not an
   original reading.
7. **The Evenki "curse" / shaman-summoned-thunder-god explanation is
   cultural cosmology, NOT a competing causal theory — frame it as such and
   only as such.** Ethnographer Innokenty Suslov's 1926 material records
   that some Evenki explained the blast as the shaman Magankan summoning the
   thunder-spirit Agdy to annihilate a rival clan (the Shanyagir, whose camp
   bore the brunt of the blast) amid an inter-clan feud. This is a real,
   documented belief the witnesses held — it must be presented on-screen
   strictly as "how the people who experienced it explained it in their own
   belief system," in the same register as noting a culture's flood myth,
   **never** intercut with or presented alongside the scientific theories
   (asteroid/comet/black hole/antimatter) as if it were another rival
   hypothesis for what physically happened.
8. **"Andrey Onkoul lost 250 reindeer" is an UNVERIFIED oral-report figure —
   no independent count exists.** **Use:** "a herder reportedly lost his
   entire reindeer herd without a trace" rather than citing "250" as a fact.
9. **The recovered micro-spherules (1950s–60s Soviet expeditions) are
   DISPUTED/partially confirmed, not conclusive proof of an extraterrestrial
   body.** Microscopic silicate/magnetite spheres with elevated nickel-to-iron
   ratios were recovered from soil and tree resin and are "considered
   consistent with" cosmic material — but their conclusive attribution to
   the Tunguska body specifically, versus ordinary background micrometeorite
   fallout, carries real scientific caveat. **Use:** "consistent with,"
   never "proof of."
10. **Lake Cheko is a genuinely DISPUTED open scientific question — not a
    solved mystery, and not fringe either.** Real, currently-active geologist
    Luca Gasperini (University of Bologna) proposed in peer-reviewed 2007–2012
    papers that this small lake ~8 km from the epicenter could be an impact
    crater from a surviving fragment. Russian sediment-coring research (2017)
    found material only ~280 years old at the deepest sampled layer, which —
    if representative — would make the lake predate 1908 and undercut the
    hypothesis. **Attribute Gasperini's hypothesis to him by name** as an
    ALLEGED/contested proposal; present the counter-evidence with equal
    weight. Do not resolve it either way on-screen.
11. **The black hole / antimatter / alien-spacecraft explanations are real
    historical claims by named people, but have ZERO current scientific
    standing — state that plainly, don't leave them as open questions.**
    - Mini black hole (1973): physicists Jackson & Ryan proposed a
      ~10²³ kg primordial black hole passed through Earth near Tunguska.
      REFUTED — no matching exit-shockwave signature was ever found on the
      opposite side of the globe (which the hypothesis itself predicted), and
      a 1974 *Nature* rebuttal (Beasley & Tinsley) plus Hawking's later
      black-hole-evaporation work argue an object that small couldn't have
      survived to reach Earth intact.
    - Antimatter (1965): physicists Cowan, Atluri & Libby suggested
      matter-antimatter annihilation. REFUTED — fails to explain the mineral/
      dust debris found at the site (annihilation would leave essentially
      none), and there's no evidence of natural antimatter clumps in the
      solar neighborhood.
    - Alien spacecraft/UFO: traces to a **1946 Soviet SCIENCE-FICTION story**
      by engineer Alexander Kazantsev (not a scientific claim at all),
      internationally popularized by Thomas Atkins & John Baxter's 1976 book
      *The Fire Came By*. No physical evidence has ever been produced. Present
      as a fictional/popular-culture origin story, attributed by name, then
      dismissed — never as a live theory.
12. **Object size and energy yield are RANGES across the literature, not
    single numbers — don't round to one figure.** Diameter: commonly cited
    ~50–60 m, full literature range ~50–80 m. Energy: commonly cited
    10–15 megatons TNT, full published range ~3–50 megatons depending on
    assumed burst altitude/composition. **Use ranges, hedge with "roughly"/
    "estimated."**
13. **Asteroid-vs-comet origin is DISPUTED among specialists, not settled.**
    A widely-cited 2001 statistical study (Farinella, Foschini et al.) puts
    the odds at roughly 83% asteroidal / 17% cometary — a real, quotable,
    published estimate, but the underlying question remains open, not closed.
14. **Chelyabinsk (2013) energy comparison is an order-of-magnitude range,
    not a precise figure — commonly cited 400–500 kilotons, but estimates
    vary meaningfully across papers.** Don't state a single exact kiloton
    number as settled.
15. **The Richter-scale-era instrument network point is a real, useful
    "proof this really happened at this scale" beat — keep it CONFIRMED and
    separate from the eyewitness accounts.** Seismographs (Irkutsk, Tashkent,
    Tbilisi, Jena, and others) and barographs (Ditcham Park House, England,
    ~5,600 km away, operated by Capt. C. J. P. Cave; also Germany, Denmark,
    Croatia, Batavia/Indonesia, Washington D.C.) independently registered the
    blast within hours — this is hard instrumental evidence, distinct from
    and more reliable than any individual's memory.
16. **Living, currently-publishing scientists appear in this fact base by
    real name — attribute their claims/quotes precisely to them, never
    blend into "scientists say."** David Morrison (b. 1940, NASA Ames,
    NEO-risk-assessment pioneer, still active) is directly quotable via
    NASA's own site. Mark Boslough (Sandia National Laboratories emeritus/
    University of New Mexico) personally visited the Tunguska site in 2008
    and co-authored a 2025 *Scientific Reports* paper still using Tunguska
    physics to correct an unrelated claim (Tall el-Hammam, Jordan) — that
    2025 paper is itself a correction of someone else's retracted claim, so
    attribute it carefully as Boslough's position, not a settled consensus
    about the other site. Natalia Artemieva (Planetary Science Institute) is
    a co-author on the standard Tunguska-Chelyabinsk review paper. Luca
    Gasperini (University of Bologna) is the named proponent of the disputed
    Lake Cheko hypothesis (see #10) — his claim must read as HIS attributed
    hypothesis, not fact.

## Act 1 — Origins & the world in 1908 (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- Location: Podkamennaya ("Stony") Tunguska River basin, ~60°54′11″N
  101°54′35″E, then Yeniseysk Governorate of the Russian Empire, now
  Evenkiysky District, Krasnoyarsk Krai, Siberia. The Podkamennaya Tunguska
  is a 1,160-mile tributary of the Yenisei.
- The nearest settlement, Vanavara, was a tiny Evenki-hunter trading post
  (not a town) ~65 km from the eventual epicenter.
- The region: permafrost taiga, no roads or railways, boggy/mosquito-ridden
  in its brief summer, snow-covered most of the year. Even in 1927, the
  Trans-Siberian Railway (built in stages 1904–1916) only got Kulik as far
  as Taishet, ~350 miles from the blast zone, before overland/river travel.
- The Evenki (historically "Tungus," giving the region its name) were
  nomadic reindeer-herding hunter-fisher clans across the Siberian taiga,
  organized by clan, with an animist/shamanic religion — the word "shaman"
  itself derives from Evenki.
- Scientific context: the idea that rocks fall from space was itself only
  ~100 years settled science by 1908. Ernst Chladni proposed it in 1794
  against Newton/Aristotle-derived resistance; the L'Aigle meteorite shower
  (France, 1803), investigated for the French Academy by Jean-Baptiste Biot,
  forced acceptance. Giuseppe Piazzi found the first asteroid (Ceres, 1801);
  Max Wolf's photographic astrometry (from 1891) sharply accelerated asteroid
  discovery, so several hundred asteroids were cataloged by 1908 (exact 1908
  tally **UNVERIFIED** — no single authoritative count found).
- What did NOT exist in 1908: any concept of an atmospheric "airburst" impact
  — an object detonating in the sky, devastating ground below, with no crater
  and no fragments. Illustrating how unsettled even ordinary cratering science
  still was: Daniel Barringer's 1905 paper arguing Arizona's Meteor Crater
  was impact-formed was directly contested for years by USGS chief geologist
  Grove Karl Gilbert, who argued (partly via the 1883 Krakatoa eruption) it
  was a volcanic steam explosion. **Framing note:** the observation that
  "airburst physics didn't exist yet, so nobody had a framework for a
  craterless blast" is a reasonable synthesis from these confirmed facts, not
  a single sourced quote — flag as inference if used verbatim on screen.
- Leonid Kulik (1883–1942), Russian/Soviet mineralogist, is the central
  scientist of the investigation. He served in WWI, was briefly imprisoned
  for revolutionary activity, then resumed science in Tomsk/Leningrad.

## Act 2 — The morning of June 30, 1908 (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- Approximately 7:14–7:17 AM local time (17 June, Old Style calendar), a
  massive airburst occurred over the Podkamennaya Tunguska basin. Treat the
  exact minute as **UNVERIFIED**; "approximately 7:15 AM" is safely CONFIRMED
  across NASA/Britannica/Wikipedia (see Correction #5 — avoid false
  second-level precision).
- Best-documented single eyewitness: **S. Semenov**, at the Vanavara trading
  post ~65 km south of the epicenter (see Cold Open section below and
  Correction #3–4 on name/distance hedging). Testimony recorded by Kulik's
  expedition in **1930 — 22 years after the event** — standard citation
  Vasiliev et al. (1981), also documented in Krinov's *Giant Meteorites*
  (1966).
- Evenki eyewitness **Chuchan**, of the Shanyagir clan, ~30 km from the
  epicenter — testimony recorded by ethnographer I. M. Suslov in 1926,
  describing falling trees, burning branches, a light "like a second sun,"
  repeated knock-downs from successive blast waves. **CONFIRMED as
  documented testimony**; precise sensory detail from an 18-year-old memory
  is inherently **UNVERIFIED**.
- Sequencing pattern (CONFIRMED, corroborated across multiple independent
  accounts): blinding light/heat arrived almost instantly, the shockwave
  arrived seconds to roughly a minute later and physically threw witnesses
  down, windows shattered as far as Vanavara. Cited by scientists as key
  evidence of a distant, high-altitude explosion rather than a ground impact.
- Instrumental corroboration (CONFIRMED, see Correction #15): seismographs at
  Irkutsk, Tashkent, Tbilisi, Jena and barographs across Eurasia — including
  Capt. C. J. P. Cave's station at Ditcham Park House, Hampshire (~5,600 km
  away) — registered the blast within hours; some accounts describe the
  pressure pulse circling the globe. Abnormally bright "white nights" (bright
  enough to read newsprint or golf at 2 a.m. in Scotland) were widely
  reported across Europe/Asia for days afterward.
- Definitive contemporary scientific analysis: **Whipple, F.J.W. (1930),
  "The Great Siberian Meteor and the Waves, Seismic and Aerial, Which It
  Produced," Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society, vol. 56,
  pp. 287–304.** Old enough to likely be PD; whether a scanned chart image is
  freely available online is **UNVERIFIED** — worth a direct search at
  production time (RMetS archive / HathiTrust / archive.org).
- Andrey Onkoul (Evenki herder) reportedly lost his reindeer herd without a
  trace; other Evenki lost dogs, reindeer, supply-cache platforms (see
  Correction #8 on the specific "250" figure — UNVERIFIED).
- Evenki cosmological explanation (shaman Magankan / thunder-spirit Agdy /
  inter-clan feud against the Shanyagir clan) — see Correction #7. Frame as
  cultural belief only, never as a rival causal theory.
- Human casualties: commonly cited "up to three deaths" — see Correction #2,
  **DISPUTED/UNVERIFIED**, no forensic corroboration, sourced only to 1926
  oral testimony.

## Act 3 — Scale of the blast (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- Forest flattened across roughly 2,150 km² (830 sq mi) in a radial,
  butterfly/wing-shaped pattern extending ~15–35 km (9–22 mi) from the
  epicenter.
- "80 million trees" — see Correction #1, **DISPUTED**, standard popular
  figure but possibly overstated by a factor of ~4 per newer tree-ring
  analysis.
- Energy release: commonly cited 10–15 megatons TNT (≈1,000× Hiroshima, on
  the order of the 1980 Mount St. Helens eruption); full published range
  ~3–50 megatons depending on model assumptions (Correction #12) — cite as a
  range.
- Seismic effect at some stations described as comparable to a magnitude-5
  earthquake — see Correction #6, this is a retrospective approximate
  comparison, NOT an original Richter reading (scale didn't exist until
  1935).

## Act 4 — The 19-year gap and Kulik's expeditions (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- No scientist reached the site until 1927 — 19 years after the event.
  Attributed by every source to two compounding factors: (1) extreme physical
  remoteness (no roads/rail, taiga/permafrost/bog, narrow seasonal travel
  window); (2) collapse of orderly Russian state science across WWI
  (1914–18), the Russian Revolution (1917), and Russian Civil War (1918–22) —
  years that directly overlapped Kulik's prime working career.
- Ethnographer **Innokenty Suslov** — a Soviet official (Chairman of the
  Krasnoyarsk Committee for Assistance to Northern Peoples) — visited the
  region in **1926**, a full year before Kulik, to organize the first Soviet
  tribal councils among the Evenki, and it was Suslov's 1926 material that
  first alerted Kulik and the Soviet Academy of Sciences to the story's scale.
  This is a genuinely useful "who got there first" beat, distinct from Kulik.
- Kulik first attempted an expedition in 1921, failed to physically reach
  the zone, and succeeded only in **1927**, guided in the final stretch by
  local Evenki hunters.
- On arrival: an ~8 km inner zone of standing, branch-stripped, scorched
  trees ("like a forest of telephone poles") surrounded by radially-flattened
  forest pointing away from a central point — a pattern consistent with a
  shockwave/thermal pulse from directly overhead. **CONFIRMED, critical
  finding:** no crater, no recovered meteorite fragments. Several
  bowl-shaped bogs Kulik initially suspected as craters were later ruled out
  (one contained tree stumps predating 1908).
- Kulik led further expeditions in 1928, 1929–30, and 1938 (aerial
  photography of the fell-zone) — never found a crater or a definitive
  meteorite body.
- Subsequent Soviet expeditions (1950s–60s, associated with Kirill
  Florensky) recovered microscopic silicate/magnetite spheres from soil and
  tree resin with chemical signatures (elevated nickel-to-iron ratios)
  considered consistent with extraterrestrial material — see Correction #9,
  **DISPUTED/partially confirmed**, not conclusive proof.

## Act 5 — Modern scientific consensus and open disputes

- **CONFIRMED consensus** (NASA, Britannica, peer-reviewed modeling incl.
  Boslough & Crawford, post-Chelyabinsk comparative studies): the event was
  an airburst — atmospheric disintegration/explosion of a small stony
  asteroid (minority of models: icy comet fragment), ~50–60 m diameter (lit.
  range 50–80 m — Correction #12), entering at a shallow angle at ~27 km/s
  from the east-southeast, detonating ~5–10 km (3–6 mi) altitude. Because it
  was destroyed in the atmosphere and never reached the ground intact, no
  crater and no large fragments were ever found.
- **DISPUTED:** asteroid-vs-comet origin — a 2001 Farinella/Foschini et al.
  statistical study puts odds at ~83% asteroidal/17% cometary, a real
  published estimate, but the question remains genuinely open among
  specialists (Correction #13).
- **DISPUTED (legitimate, not fringe):** Lake Cheko as a possible secondary
  impact crater from a surviving fragment — Luca Gasperini's 2007–2012
  papers vs. 2017 Russian sediment-coring evidence suggesting the lake
  predates 1908 (Correction #10). Present as open scientific disagreement.
- **REFUTED/no current scientific standing, but real historical claims by
  named people — attribute then dismiss (Correction #11):** mini black hole
  (Jackson & Ryan, 1973, *Nature*; rebutted by Beasley & Tinsley, 1974);
  antimatter (Cowan, Atluri & Libby, 1965, *Nature*); alien spacecraft (1946
  Kazantsev science fiction, popularized by Atkins & Baxter's 1976 *The Fire
  Came By*).

## Act 6 — Legacy: why Tunguska still matters (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- Tunguska is routinely cited by NASA/planetary science as the largest
  cosmic impact witnessed in recorded human history and functions as the
  benchmark "worst realistic case" in near-Earth-object risk modeling.
- **David Morrison** (NASA Ames, b. 1940, still active), quoted directly on
  NASA's own site: Tunguska "is the largest cosmic impact witnessed by
  modern humans... It also is characteristic of the sort of impact we are
  likely to have to protect against in the future." **Living person — quote
  attributed by name, sourced directly to NASA's own page, safe to use
  verbatim with attribution.**
- NASA's Planetary Defense Coordination Office (PDCO) was formally
  established **January 7, 2016**, explicitly to detect/characterize
  hazardous NEOs — its mission line traces directly back to Tunguska-class
  airbursts as the reference threat scale; PDCO later sponsored the DART
  kinetic-impactor demonstration mission.
- In January 2018, PDCO sponsored a ~50-person workshop at NASA Ames
  applying post-Chelyabinsk modeling tools to the 1908 event, producing eight
  peer-reviewed papers in the July 2019 *Icarus* vol. 327 special section
  (see Primary Exhibit Document above).
- **The Chelyabinsk link (Feb 15, 2013):** the largest airburst since 1908,
  commonly cited at 400–500 kilotons (order-of-magnitude range, not a precise
  figure — Correction #14), extensively dashcam/security-camera documented —
  giving scientists real measured data to validate decades of Tunguska-based
  airburst models. Definitive paper: Olga Popova et al., *Science* 2013.
  Review: Natalia Artemieva & Valery Shuvalov, "From Tunguska to Chelyabinsk
  via Jupiter" — also notes Shoemaker-Levy 9's 1994 Jupiter impact as the
  earlier bridge that made airburst modeling credible at all.
- **Mark Boslough** (Sandia National Labs emeritus/UNM, living, currently
  publishing) personally visited the Tunguska site in 2008 for a documentary
  expedition and co-authored a 2025 *Scientific Reports* paper using precise
  Tunguska physical evidence to correct a since-retracted claim about an
  unrelated site (Tall el-Hammam, Jordan) — cite carefully per Correction #16.
- Forward-looking legacy: Tunguska-scale impacts (city-flattening,
  effectively no-warning-possible at current detection rates) are the
  working threat model behind PDCO, DART, and ongoing NEO-survey efforts,
  including the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's LSST survey (expected to sharply
  increase detection of Tunguska/Chelyabinsk-sized objects).
