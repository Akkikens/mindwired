### C1. THE SEAL — the episode's best engineering image

- **[CONFIRMED]** — Leak rate **under 10% per year** of atmosphere exchanged with
  outside air, confirmed by **two independent methods**: inflating the lungs to a
  ~125 Pa test overpressure and watching them deflate over weeks; and spiking the
  atmosphere with **sulfur hexafluoride, helium and krypton** and measuring
  dilution over months.
- **[CONFIRMED — verbatim from Dempster, and the single best documentary line in
  the fact base]** — "The testing indicated that in approximately **80 km of
  caulked glazing joints and 16 km of welded seams**, the aggregate cross section
  of pinholes and construction flaws is approximately equivalent to **a single
  hole about 19 mm diameter**."
  - **Narration-safe:** "Fifty miles of sealed glass joints. Ten miles of welded
    seam. And every flaw in all of it added up to the equivalent of one
    three-quarter-inch hole."
- **[CONFIRMED — why the tightness is the whole reason there is a story]** — the
  oxygen decline averaged **~140 ppm per day** over the first 16 months.
  Dempster's simulations show it **would have been completely masked by a leak
  rate as low as 1% per day.** An ordinary greenhouse would never have caught it.
  **This is the wedge: the building was tight enough to notice its own
  suffocation.**
- **[UNVERIFIED — do NOT use the superlative]** — "tightest-sealed structure ever
  built" has **no primary source**. Wikipedia's "largest closed ecological system
  ever created" is cited only to a popular reference book. **Use the 19 mm-hole
  image instead of a superlative**, or say "one of the tightest."

### C2. THE LUNGS — why thumbnail B's subject exists

- **[CONFIRMED]** — Two expandable variable-volume chambers (**West Lung** and
  **South Lung**), in domes connected by underground tunnels. Purpose per
  Dempster: to absorb expansion and contraction of the internal atmosphere **as
  temperature and humidity varied**, and to absorb **regional barometric pressure
  changes**, without creating a pressure differential. Result: internal/external
  differentials held under **±8 Pa** — about one-thousandth of a psi.
- **UPI's 1991 layman framing, ideal for narration:** "As the air heats and
  expands in the sealed Biosphere, two giant dome-enclosed 'lung' pouches inflate.
  **Otherwise the heat and pressure would burst the glass walls.**"
- **[CONFIRMED]** 🧍 — Frame and glazing designed and made by **Peter Jon Pearce**

### F1. THE MECHANISM — the payoff, and the thing no farm channel explains

- **[CONFIRMED — Eos abstract, verbatim in substance]** — "the O₂ loss is caused
  by **microbial respiration of the excessive amount of organic matter
  incorporated into the experiment's soils**, and furthermore, that **the
  respired CO₂ is reacting with the structure's concrete to form calcium
  carbonate.**"
- **[CONFIRMED — the chemistry, exactly]** — two reactions in sequence:
  1. Respiration: `CH₂O + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O` — one mole CO₂ per mole O₂; gas volume
     invariant.
  2. Carbonation of uncured concrete: `CaO + CO₂ → CaCO₃`.
- **[CONFIRMED — Dempster's own framing, and this IS the episode's turn]** —
  "The obvious explanation of loss to respiration was not evident because **the
  implied increase of carbon dioxide did not occur**, and furthermore, **the
  atmospheric volume of Biosphere 2 was shrinking.**"
  - **Narration-safe:** "The mystery was never that the oxygen was disappearing.
    It was that the carbon dioxide wasn't appearing to match it. The building's
    own concrete was eating the evidence."
- **[CONFIRMED — the isotope method]** — Severinghaus et al. analysed ¹²C/¹³C in
  atmosphere, biomass, scrubber product and concrete. Concrete carbonate δ¹³C
  averaged **−16.8 ± 0.4‰**, consistent with soil-respired CO₂ rather than the
  original cement carbon. Inner concrete surfaces held roughly **ten times** the
  calcium carbonate of outer surfaces.
- **[CONFIRMED, two published magnitudes — cite the range]** — Severinghaus et
  al.: **in excess of 750 kmol** CO₂ sequestered. Dempster 1999: "on the order of
  **600 kmol, or 26 t**, distributed over about **15,800 m²** of exposed
  concrete." **Say "roughly six to seven hundred and fifty kilomoles — on the
  order of twenty-five tonnes — locked into about fifteen thousand square metres
  of bare concrete."**
- **[CONFIRMED — why photosynthesis could not keep up]** — soils were
  deliberately over-loaded with organic matter for fertility, so respiration
  outran photosynthesis. Compounded by **two years of abnormally high cloudiness,
  possibly ENSO-related.** Net CO₂ uptake occurred only when outside sunlight
  reached ≥25 mol m⁻² day⁻¹.

### F2. CO₂, the scrubber, and the failed furnace

- **[CONFIRMED]** — CO₂ held deliberately below **5,000 ppm** to protect ocean
  pH. Daily highs **2,800 ppm first winter, 4,200 ppm second winter**; diurnal
  swing ~600 ppm; residence time **3-4 days**.
  - **De-sensationalizing comparison worth keeping (Nelson et al. 2015):** Space
    Shuttle ran 5,000-10,000 ppm; ISS under 2,000-9,000 ppm.
- **[CONFIRMED — what the scrubber actually was, and the part nobody tells]** — a
  NaOH wet scrubber (`2NaOH + CO₂ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O`, then lime to precipitate
  storable solid). **An electric furnace was installed to reverse the reaction
  and close the loop in summer — but its heating elements failed and the cycle
  was never completed during the two years.**
  - **The failed furnace is the honest version of the scrubber story: the system
    was designed to be reversible and closed; the reversal broke.**
- **[CONFIRMED — the scrubber's share was small]** — it removed **~98 kmol /
  4.3 t** of CO₂ across the two fall-winter periods (Dempster 1999); Allen &
  Nelson put it at "about 1.6% of atmospheric carbon dioxide," which "left
  unaccounted the bulk of the oxygen loss."
- **[DISPUTED — N₂O]** — rise of **~40 ppm per year** (Allen & Nelson 1999) vs
  **34 ppm per month** (Nelson et al. 2003). **Prefer the 1999 figure and flag.**
  Cause: soil denitrification with no stratosphere and no UV photolysis — the
  glass eliminated >99% of incident UV.
  - **⚠ [UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE] the "79 ppm" figure and the brain-damage /
    vitamin-B₁₂ claim.** Appears in secondary summaries; not sourced to a
    peer-reviewed Biosphere 2 paper.
- **[CONFIRMED — cheap, concrete, good]** — the same glass caused a **vitamin D
  deficiency** requiring supplements. B₁₂ was also low (near-vegan diet).

## G. HUNGER — and the conflict of interest

- **[CONFIRMED]** — Over two years the crew averaged **2,200 kcal, 73 g protein,
  32 g fat** per person per day from a **2,000 m² cropping area** supplying about
  **80% of nutritional needs**. **Agriculture and food processing consumed ~45%
  of crew time.** (Silverstone & Nelson, *Adv. Space Res.* 1995)
- **[CONFIRMED — the first six months were the hungry ones]** — Walford et al.,
  **PNAS 1992, 89:11533**: first ~6 months averaged **1,780 kcal/day**. "After the
  first six months, the calorie and fat intake was increased slightly by adding
  more peanuts to the diet."
- **[CONFIRMED — weight loss, two published figures]** — First 6 months (PNAS
  1992): men **74 → 62 kg**, women **61 → 54 kg**; BP 109/74 → 89/58; cholesterol
  191 → 123 mg/dl. Full two years (Walford et al., *J Gerontol* 2002;57(6):B211):
  **"a marked and sustained weight loss of 17 ± 5%, mostly in the first 8
  months"**; crew "remained in excellent health."
  - **Wikipedia's "16%" is close but not the published figure. Use "about
    seventeen percent."**
- **[CONFIRMED — the details that sell the hunger]** — broad mites and root-knot
  nematode cut yields; standout crops rice, sweet potato, beet, banana, papaya;
  African pygmy goats the most productive animals; **coffee was rationed to one
  cup per person every few weeks.**
- **[CONFIRMED as structural fact / DISPUTED as framing — handle carefully]** ⚰🧍
  **Roy Walford was simultaneously the crew's sole physician AND the world's
  leading advocate-researcher of caloric restriction for life extension.** He
  founded the Calorie Restriction Society, had personally followed a CR diet for
  years, and **the crew's daily allotments were planned using Walford's own
  diet-planning computer program** (stated flatly in Silverstone & Nelson 1995).
  He published the crew as research subjects (PNAS 1992, J Gerontol 2002) — the
  mission's food shortfall became his dataset.
  - **His own words are the fairest way to present the charge, and he offered
    them unprompted:** "I think if there had been any other nutritionist or
    physician, they would have freaked out and said, 'We're starving,' but I knew
    we were actually on a program of health enhancement."
  - **Balancing fact [CONFIRMED]:** the 2002 paper's own conclusion is that all
    eight remained in excellent health and high physical/mental activity.
  - **Narration-safe framing:** "The experiment's biggest failure and the
    doctor's life's work happened to point in the same direction — and he was the
    only one qualified to call it."

## H. THE DIE-OFF

### M5. THE FINGERTIP — use it at the mid-video turn, NOT the cold open

- **[CONFIRMED]** 🧍 — Poynter, 29, farm manager, severed the tip of the **middle
  finger of her left hand** in a rice-hulling machine ~12 days in. Walford
  reattached it inside; **the reattachment failed**; she exited for surgery and
  returned the same day, still losing the fingertip.
- **[DISPUTED — dates]** — *Discover* says "twelve days into the mission" (= 8
  Oct); Seattle Times says injury **9 October**, exit **11 October**, "returned
  within hours"; a *Deseret News* piece **published 11 October** quotes an SBV
  spokesman saying she would "probably" need to leave — supporting the 11 Oct
  exit. **Best construction: injured on or about 9 October 1991; out through the
  airlock on 11 October for a few hours.**
