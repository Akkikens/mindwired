## 1. Origins — the three caldera-forming eruptions (CONFIRMED)

- **Huckleberry Ridge eruption, ~2.1 million years ago** (precise: 2.0773±0.0034
  Ma) — created the Island Park Caldera. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone)
- **Mesa Falls eruption, ~1.3 million years ago** (1.3001±0.0006 Ma) — the smallest
  of the three — created the Henry's Fork Caldera. [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/calderas.htm)
- **Lava Creek eruption, 640,000 years ago** (precise: 631.3±4.3 ka) — created the
  modern Yellowstone Caldera visitors walk across today; deposit volume >1,000 km³
  (240 mi³). [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/age-lava-creek-supereruption-and-magma-chamber-assembly-yellowstone-based-40ar39ar-and) · [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/calderas.htm)
- Modern caldera: **53 × 28 miles (85 × 45 km)**, largest in North America, magma
  "in some places only a few miles underground." [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/yellowstone) · [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/calderas.htm)

## 2. The hydrothermal system + Old Faithful (CONFIRMED)

- **10,000+ hydrothermal features, 500+ active geysers** — roughly half of all
  geysers on Earth, the largest concentration anywhere. [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/hydrothermal-systems.htm) · [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/news/just-how-many-thermal-features-are-there-yellowstone)
- A USGS/NPS drill hole at Norris Geyser Basin recorded **459°F (237°C) at 1,087
  feet down** — direct measured evidence of the heat driving the system.
- **Old Faithful:** named Sept 18, 1870 by the Washburn–Langford–Doane Expedition
  (Nathaniel P. Langford's journal). Erupts every ~90 min (range 50-127 min), 1.5-5
  min duration, expels 3,700-8,400 gallons per eruption, 106-184 ft high. Not the
  largest or most regular geyser — just the most frequent among the "big" ones,
  which is why it became famous. [NPS](https://home.nps.gov/articles/old-faithful-geyser.htm)

## 3. The real 2026 event — Biscuit Basin, June 13 (CONFIRMED, primary USGS source)

- **5:09:45–5:10:15 a.m. MDT, Saturday, June 13, 2026** — a hydrothermal explosion
  a few tens of meters north of Black Diamond Pool, Biscuit Basin. [USGS video record](https://www.usgs.gov/media/videos/video-small-hydrothermal-explosion-biscuit-basin-june-13-2026)
- Detected by a remote research/monitoring camera (installed May 2025) plus
  seismic + infrasound instruments — **no person witnessed it**; the basin has
  been closed to the public since the larger July 23, 2024 explosion at the same
  spot. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/yvo/news/oops-it-did-it-again-another-small-hydrothermal-explosion-biscuit-basin)
- **Three new vent groups**: a northern vent ~18.5 m (61 ft) long, water ~90°C; a
  middle group of ~5 small vents, ~85°C; a southern fracture ~15 m long, ~89°C.
  Ejected rock traveled only a few meters — explosive energy assessed as LOWER
  than the 2024 event. **No injuries.**
- The Firehole River ran milky-grey with sediment for 5-6 km downstream — a park
  interpretive staff member (unnamed) noticed this later that morning, but did not
  witness the explosion itself.
- **Yellowstone NP geologists visited the site June 14, 2026** (the day after) and
  documented the three vents. Named team: Jefferson Hungerford (Park Geologist),
  Kiernan Folz-Donahue, Brandon Duktig, Liam Rogers, Margery Price.
- **June 14-16, 2026**: a collapse near the middle vent group formed a new
  circular boiling pool (~6.5 × 5.3 m). **June 18, 2026**: that pool had several
  few-minute spouting episodes throwing boiling water 6-9 m (20-30 ft) high — this
  clip is MORE visually dramatic than the explosion footage itself.
- **New monitoring camera installed July 22, 2026**, viewing both Black Diamond
  Pool (2024 site) and the June 13 vent area.
- 🧍 **Michael Poland** (Scientist-in-Charge, Yellowstone Volcano Observatory) —
  real, on-record quotes: *"These are not uncommon events in Yellowstone. They
  probably happen a few times per year somewhere in the park, often completely
  unobserved."* Also: *"[a] smallish hydrothermal explosion, but still
  impressive,"* and *"Usually, it is unobserved and happens in the backcountry.
  But every now and then, we get one in a front-country area that we can
  observe."* On the milky river: *"The river stayed like that just for a few
  hours."* [Discover Magazine](https://www.discovermagazine.com/recent-hydrothermal-explosion-at-yellowstone-national-park-later-a-new-thermal-pool-appears-49284)
- 🧍 **Jamie Farrell** (Chief Seismologist, YVO / Univ. of Utah) — named
  co-contributor on the official USGS writeup, no individual quote found; do not
  invent one.

## 4. The "mantle wind" study, 2026 (CONFIRMED, secondary-sourced mechanism)

- Study first-released online **April 9, 2026**; formally issued in *Science* Vol.
  392, Issue 6794 (June 27, 2026) — ScienceDaily's June 22, 2026 write-up covers
  this issue. Led by the **Institute of Geology and Geophysics, Chinese Academy
  of Sciences (IGGCAS)**.
- 🧍 **Liu Lijun** (corresponding/lead author) and **Zebin Cao** — confirmed named
  across CAS's own release and phys.org. (Bo Wan/Ling Chen/Craig Lundstrom appear
  only in one secondary summary — treat as plausible-but-not-independently-
  confirmed if named on screen.)
- **Old theory ("mantle plume"):** Yellowstone sits above a deep plume of hot rock
  rising vertically from the core-mantle boundary, ~2,900 km down.
- **New finding ("mantle wind"):** a slow, HORIZONTAL, eastward-flowing current of
  hot rock in the shallow asthenosphere — generated by the long-buried, still-
  sinking remnants of the old Farallon tectonic plate — sweeps hot material
  sideways underneath Yellowstone. In plain language: instead of a "hose" of heat
  rising straight up from Earth's core, Yellowstone sits in the path of a slow
  underground "current" of warm rock moving sideways. This pushes against thicker
  crust to the east and buoyant crust to the west, stretching/tearing a channel
  that lets magma rise and pool in a broad, shallow "mush" zone rather than one
  deep conduit.
- I did not access the original *Science* paper (paywalled) — mechanism details
  come from CAS's release + phys.org + ScienceDaily, not the primary text.

## 6. The 1959 Hebgen Lake earthquake (CONFIRMED, DISPUTED magnitude — see corrections)

- **August 17, 1959, 11:37 p.m. MST**, epicenter ~15 mi north of West Yellowstone,
  Montana, just outside the park's western boundary. Preceded by a ~M6.3 foreshock,
  followed by 350+ aftershocks.
- **The Madison Canyon landslide**: ~38-50 million m³ of rock/mud/debris sheared
  off the canyon's south wall at up to ~160 km/h (100 mph), crossed the canyon
  floor, ran up the opposite wall, and dammed the Madison River — forming Quake
  Lake.
- **28 people died.** ~19 remain entombed under the slide, never recovered. Rock
  Creek Campground (site of most deaths) now sits under ~100 ft of Quake Lake.
- Highway 287 dropped into the lake in sections / was cut by fault scarps up to
  ~20 ft high. Hebgen Dam did NOT fail (seiche waves overtopped it, causing panic,
  but only minimal structural damage). Damage: ~$11M in 1959 dollars (~$121M in
  2025 dollars).
- **Inside the park**: the shaking triggered 289 hot springs to erupt as geysers
  in following days, 160 with no prior record of activity — real, dramatic,
  honest proof of how connected the region's plumbing is, WITHOUT claiming the
  quake was volcanic (see correction #7).
- 🧍 **Anita Painter Thon** — was 12, camping with her family for her birthday;
  her mother was killed, her father critically injured. Has since authored three
  books (*Shaken in the Night*, *The Twenty Eight*, *Princess: A Dog's Tale*) and
  spoke publicly at the 2019 60th-anniversary commemoration. Age ~79 today;
  current living status UNVERIFIED — attribute any quote to her published work,
  never assert she is alive today.
