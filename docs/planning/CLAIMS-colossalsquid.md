## ACT 1 — THE CENTURY OF CORPSES

**C1.1 CONFIRMED.** Described 1925 by **Guy Coburn Robson** (1888-1945, deceased),
British Museum (Natural History), from **two arm crowns from two animals**, taken from a
sperm whale's stomach in the winter of **1924-25**.

**C1.2 DISPUTED — the location.** Wikipedia's specimen catalogue (citing Robson 1925:272
and Lipiński et al. 2000:107) says **50 miles north of Smith Island, South Shetlands**.
Te Papa's centenary page says "near the Shetland Islands." **Te Papa's own collections
page says "at the Falkland Islands."** INFERRED reconciliation (label as inference, not
fact): in 1924-25 the South Shetlands were administered as the **Falkland Islands
Dependencies**, which would compress to "Falklands" in a careless source.

**C1.3 CONFIRMED / PARTLY VERIFIED.** Named for **Hamilton**. Almost certainly **James
Erik Hamilton (1891-1957)**, Falkland Islands government naturalist from 1921, resident
on **Deception Island** during whaling seasons, who with A. G. Bennett shipped cetacean
material to the NHM. The Falklands biography of Hamilton **does not mention the squid**;
the identification is strongly circumstantial. **Do not assert it as documented.**

**C1.4 EXCLUDE.** "Norwegian whalers." No authoritative source. Drop.

**C1.5 CONFIRMED — the counting problem, and it is a feature not a bug.**
Answers to "how many have we found" that are all true and all different:
- **8 adults** ever reported, **6 from sperm whale stomachs** (Te Papa)
- **12 complete specimens**, 6 of them juvenile/subadult, plus **55 beak-only records**
  (McClain et al. 2015 — who add: *"we found the least information for Mesonychoteuthis
  hamiltoni"* of all the marine megafauna they audited)
- **~155** net-caught specimens, location only (Rosa et al. 2017)
- **188** geographical positions (Xavier et al. 1999)
- **29** numbered catalogue records 1925-2025
**On-screen formulation:** "Depending on what you count — bodies, arm crowns, or beaks —
somewhere between eight and a dozen complete or adult animals. Against roughly a hundred
and fifty netted juveniles, most of them a few centimetres long, and hundreds of beaks
pulled out of things that ate them."

**C1.6 CONFIRMED.** Of the large specimens, **essentially all are female.** One mature
male appears once, among partial remains at the Smithsonian (Voss 1980:394). **Mature
males are essentially unknown.**

**C1.7 CONFIRMED.** 33 juveniles netted 1962-1985 at **3-146 mm** mantle length. Smallest
whole animal on record: a **23 mm paralarva**. Nearly every large animal ever landed came
off a **longline** or out of a **predator's stomach** — not a net.

## ACT 2 — WHAT THE BODIES SAID

**C2.1 CONFIRMED — the 2007 Te Papa animal.** Caught **early Feb 2007** (announced 22 Feb),
Ross Sea, by the NZ longliner ***San Aspiring*** (Sanford Ltd), attacking an **Antarctic
toothfish** already hooked at **1,500 m**. Rode the line up. Nine crew, ~two hours to
land it. First mature/adult specimen recovered intact; heaviest cephalopod ever
documented. Gifted to Te Papa, frozen ~14 months, thawed and examined **April 2008** by
**Steve O'Shea 🧍**, **Kat Bolstad 🧍**, **Tsunemi Kubodera 🧍**. On permanent display;
~9 million visits since 2008.

**C2.2 CONFIRMED — the shrinking squid (a whole beat).** Announced at sea at **8-10 m**.
Measured after thawing: **4.2 m**. Same animal. Te Papa's explanation: the tentacles
shortened and shrank post-mortem; commercial squid tissue can shrink up to **22%** with
dehydration, and this one spent 14 months frozen. **The headline squid and the specimen
in the case are six metres apart, and both numbers are honest.**

**C2.3 CONFIRMED — weight. DISPUTED figure, give the range.** **495 kg** thawed/frozen
(the figure Rosa et al. 2017 and Jereb & Roper propagate as the species maximum);
**~470 kg** is Te Papa's own estimate for the animal in life. Use **470-495 kg**, say
which is which.

**C2.4 CONFIRMED — length is a junk metric for squid.** The 2003 animal was **longer**
(5.4 m) and weighed only **300 kg**; the 2007 animal was shorter (4.2 m) at 495 kg. Te
Papa: these are *"incredibly plastic animals."* **Mantle length is the scientific
currency. Max ML: 2.5 m.**

**C2.5 CONFIRMED — the beak is why we have any size numbers at all.** Beaks are chitinous
and indigestible; they survive in predators' stomachs when everything else dissolves.
Colossal squid has the **largest beak of any living cephalopod**, lower rostral length
roughly **twice** the giant squid's. LRL scales to mantle length and mass — which is how
nearly every size estimate for this species is generated: **from a black parrot-beak in a
whale's gut, not from a body.**
- 2003 specimen LRL **37-38 mm**; 2007 specimen LRL **41 / 42.5 mm** (Te Papa's own two
  pages disagree — DISPUTED, note it); largest known from a whale stomach: **49 mm**.
- **ALLEGED (Te Papa, on beak allometry after Clarke / Cherel 🧍 / Xavier 🧍):** a 49 mm
  beak *"could be up to a massive 600 or 700 kilograms"* — immediately conceding *"we
  can't say for sure what size colossal squid a 49 millimetre beak length represents."*
- **CONFIRMED CORRECTIVE:** the 49 mm beak does NOT imply a 12-14 m animal. The 2007
  squid carried a 42.5 mm beak at 2.5 m ML. Craig McClain 🧍 concludes it *"seems likely…
  belonged to an individual under a 3 metre ML."*

**C2.6 CONFIRMED — the whale connection.** *M. hamiltoni* beaks are **14% by number** of
beaks in Antarctic sperm whale stomachs but, because of the animal's size, an estimated
**77% of the biomass** — rising to **23% by number at South Georgia**. Clarke 1980,
*Discovery Reports* 37:324. **Caveat on screen:** reconstructed from beak size, not
weighed; from commercial-whaling-era Antarctic samples; not a global figure. See the
corrections table — neither research agent read Clarke's primary.
**The line that earns the act break:** the science of this animal is a by-product of the
industrial slaughter of sperm whales.

**C2.7 CONFIRMED — 17 predator species** eat it: penguins, seabirds, fish, marine mammals.
Chief: **sperm whales** (Clarke 1980) and **sleeper sharks** (Cherel & Duhamel 2004 —
**61.1%** of 36 Kerguelen sleeper shark stomachs held colossal squid remains in the
specimen record's summary, **66%** in the biology dossier's reading of the same study;
DISPUTED, report one and attribute). A 2004 Kerguelen study pulled **89 beaks representing
at least 49 individual squid out of 22 sharks** — one paper outnumbering every intact
animal ever recovered.

**C2.8 CONFIRMED — albatross detail.** Wandering, black-browed and grey-headed albatrosses
carry colossal squid remains, but **>60% had potentially been scavenged** (Alvito et al.
2015). They are eating dead ones off the surface, not killing them.

**C2.9 ALLEGED / HANDLE WITH CARE — sperm whale scars.** Do **not** assert that scars on
sperm whales are colossal squid marks. Circular sucker scars are routinely attributed to
*Architeuthis* and *Kondakovia* too, and cannot be assigned to species from the scar
alone. **Use the published, species-matched evidence instead:** beak-shaped bites and
**club-hook scratches matching *M. hamiltoni*** on captured Patagonian toothfish
(Remeslo, Yukhov, Bolstad 🧍 & Laptikhovsky 🧍 2015, 2019).

**C2.10 CONFIRMED — 2014 specimen and the live thaw.** *San Aspiring* again, Ross Sea,
1,200-1,800 m, on a toothfish line; recovered entire but **missing its tentacles**;
female, ~350 kg, ~3.5 m. **Eggs found in the mantle.** Dissected **16 September 2014** at
Te Papa, led by **Kat Bolstad 🧍**, with AUT postgraduate **Aaron Boyd Evans 🧍**.
Livestreamed ~3.5 hours; Te Papa reports **over 800,000 live viewers from 180+ countries**
(a later Te Papa page says 900,000+; Fox reported 142k — DISPUTED, pick one and attribute).
**DISPUTED — capture date:** Te Papa says January 2014, the catalogue says mid-2014. The
**dissection date is solid.**
**Through-line for the edit:** Evans counted arm suckers at this table in 2014, and
eleven years later his arm-hook test confirmed the 2025 footage. Same man, both ends.

**C2.11 CONFIRMED — 2015, the smallest intact one.** NIWA, Ross Sea, Antarctic Ecosystems
Voyage (29 Jan - 11 Mar 2015). Entire, good condition, **~12 cm** juvenile. The
hook-and-sucker signature was **already visible on the arms**. Examined at Te Papa by
Bolstad 🧍 and Evans 🧍.

## ACT 3 — THE ANIMAL ITSELF

**C3.1 CONFIRMED — heaviest, not longest.** 495 kg vs *Architeuthis*'s ~220 kg (95% under
250 kg); giant squid verified to **12 m** long vs colossal's 4.2-6 m. Rosa et al. title it
*"the world's largest invertebrate"* and mean **heaviest**. NHM's own framing: the giant
squid *"is not actually the largest… that position of honour is reserved for the colossal
squid."* **Script line: "Longest squid: the giant. Heaviest: the colossal."**
⚠ NHM pages returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher — **verify any NHM quote manually** before it
goes on screen. Same for Nilsson et al. 2012 full text (403; figures taken from the
authors' own 2013 reply, read in full).

**C3.2 — THE EYE.** See corrections table for the numbers and the mechanism.
- **CONFIRMED:** largest eye of any **living** animal, ~3x the diameter of any other
  extant animal's; the largest fish eyes reach only 9-10 cm. Lens 80-90 mm, spherical.
- **CONFIRMED:** examined by **Dan-Eric Nilsson 🧍** and **Eric Warrant 🧍** (Lund) on the
  Te Papa material, by underwater camera and endoscope — **one intact eye, one damaged.**
- **DISPUTED (the whole hypothesis):** Nilsson 2012 vs Schmitz 2013 x2. Attribute both.
- **CONFIRMED (the physics, uncontested):** below ~600 m there is no daylight; the only
  large luminous objects are animals **triggering plankton bioluminescence as they move**.
  A diving sperm whale lights a diffuse glow along its own body.
- **CONFIRMED (the modelled number):** at 800 m in clear water a whale-scale target is
  detectable at **~120 m** — nearly twice the range for anything else visible.
- **CONFIRMED (the pinch, and the best line in the section):** sperm whale echolocation
  range **exceeds** 120 m. The squid does not win. Rosa et al.: the squid *"may not
  necessarily register the predators before being detected themselves by the whale's
  sonar… may, however, be visually warned in sufficient time to attempt an escape."*
- **CONFIRMED, underused:** each eye carries **two photophores**. Function is
  **ALLEGED, three competing hypotheses** (counter-illumination / deception / headlight
  or rangefinder). Te Papa states "headlight" as fact; the peer-reviewed review lists it
  as one of three guesses. **Use as hypothesis.**
- ⚠ **Liu et al. 2017** (optic lobe undersized relative to eye — a detection-and-flee eye,
  not a hunting eye) is on ***Architeuthis*, NOT the colossal squid.** Do not misattribute.

**C3.3 — THE HOOKS (the thumbnail promise; get the anatomy exactly right).**
- **CONFIRMED:** the **only hooked squid in Cranchiidae** (~20 species) — a genuine outlier
  in a family of small transparent glass squid. *Mesonycho-teuthis* = "middle-claw squid."
- **TENTACLES (2, ~2.1 m) — the strike weapon.** Club carries **22-25 hooks in two rows**
  on the manus, flanked by small marginal suckers. Each hook on a short stalk in a
  flattened depression, **single main claw**, and it **rotates — 360°, by Te Papa's blog
  up to 720°**.
- **ARMS (8, 0.85-1.15 m) — the holding weapon.** Arm hooks are **larger, FIXED, and do
  NOT rotate**, in a **double row down the middle of each arm**, most of them
  **three-pointed** (main claw plus two basal cusps). **These are the diagnostic ones.**
- **THE DIAGNOSTIC, verbatim from Bolstad 🧍:** *"the colossal squid is the only squid that
  we know of that has suckers near the beak, then hooks midway along the arm, and then
  suckers again toward the arm tips."*
- **CONFIRMED contrast:** *Architeuthis* has **no hooks at all** — it grips with toothed
  sucker rings. Those rings make the famous ring-scars on whales. Colossal squid leave
  **hook scratches**. Different mark, different animal.
- **UNVERIFIED / EXPLICITLY UNKNOWN — put this on screen:** Te Papa states it is *"not
  known whether the squid actively controls each hook individually, or whether the hooks
  swivel passively once latched onto the prey."* **Nobody has ever watched it happen.**

**C3.4 — METABOLISM.** **Rosa 🧍 & Seibel 🧍 2010**: routine metabolic rate 0.036 µmol O₂
h⁻¹ g⁻¹ at 1.5 °C; **45.1 kcal/day**; **~30 g of prey per day**. Their conclusion,
verbatim: it *"is not a voracious predator capable of high-speed predator-prey
interactions. It is, rather, an ambush or sit-and-float predator that uses the hooks on
its arms and tentacles to ensnare prey that unwittingly approach."*
⚠ **CRITICAL CAVEAT, must be spoken:** **nobody has ever measured a colossal squid's oxygen
consumption.** This is a **phylogenetic inference** — modelled from other cranchiids and
scaled by body size. Best available; not a measurement.
- Nat Geo's framing (ALLEGED): a 5 kg toothfish sustains a 500 kg adult ~200 days. The
  arithmetic gives ~167. **Say "months, not days."**
- **CONFIRMED pushback, and the film's thesis:** Rosa et al. 2017 refuse the "sluggish
  drifter" press framing of their own result — *"does an organism need to be an active
  pursuit predator to be fearsome? For instance, white sharks, leopards, crocodiles,
  large monkfish and pythons are all ambush predators (many with low energy demands), and
  it is their capability to hide and strike without warning that makes them inherently
  aggressive."*
- **CONFIRMED:** stable isotopes (**Cherel 🧍 & Hobson 🧍 2005**) place it among the **top
  predators of the Southern Ocean.** Slow, cold, cheap to run — and apex.

**C3.5 — BUOYANCY.** **CONFIRMED for Cranchiidae:** an enormous **coelom** filled with
ammonium chloride solution, near-isosmotic with seawater but lighter, giving near-neutral
buoyancy (Denton, Gilpin-Brown & Shaw 1969). Cranchiids store it in a **dedicated
chamber**; every other ammoniacal squid family disperses it through muscle. The chamber
lets the animal **float and respire without ever contracting the mantle — breathe without
swimming.**
⚠ **DISPUTED for this species:** Rosa et al. flag the colossal squid as a cranchiid
outlier — *"much more muscle density"* than its transparent relatives. **No published
ammonium measurement exists for an adult *M. hamiltoni*.** Say: it belongs to a family
that floats on ammonium and keeps the chamber; whether a 495 kg muscular adult is
actually neutrally buoyant **has not been measured**.
**Bonus, CONFIRMED:** ammonium is also why the tissue dissolves fast in whale stomach
acid — leaving beaks and hooks. Which is exactly why our size record is a record of beaks.

**C3.6 — DEPTH AND RANGE. CONFIRMED.** Circumpolar Southern Ocean endemic, Antarctic
continent to the Sub-Antarctic Front. **Ontogenetic descent:** juveniles under 40 mm ML
live **surface to 500 m**; late juveniles descend to ~**2,000 m**. Adults are meso- and
bathypelagic. Females caught shallower than expected — **INFERRED**, possibly a spawning
migration.

**C3.7 — WHAT IS STILL UNKNOWN (this is the closing act, not a footnote).**
- **Adult behaviour: UNOBSERVED.** Te Papa: *"No one has observed live colossal squid
  swimming."* Every behavioural claim in this film is inference from anatomy, corpses,
  and stomach contents. **Say that out loud.**
- **Lifespan: 2 to 5+ years, all estimated, none validated.** Te Papa says maturity in
  1-3 years; a 2024 Coimbra MSc (Farnan) puts max age at **5.2 years** from beak
  increments **assumed daily — an assumption never validated for this species**. The
  standard statolith-ring method **cannot be used**: too little material to validate ring
  periodicity (Rosa et al. 2017). ⚠ Farnan full text unobtainable — second-hand.
- **Reproduction: largely unknown.** *"Records of egg masses and hatchlings are essentially
  non-existent"* (Rosa et al. 2017). **No hectocotylus** — the modified arm most squid mate
  with. Te Papa calls the proposed mechanism *"educated guessing."*
- **Fecundity: DISPUTED by ~500x.** Jereb & Roper (FAO): up to **4.2 million oocytes**.
  Te Papa: **6,000-8,000 eggs**. Probably lifetime ovary count vs ripe eggs at one moment
  — **say so rather than picking one.**
- **Population: unknown.** IUCN Least Concern reflects **absence of known threat, not
  knowledge.** See corrections table on the 90-million-tonne assumption.
- Also unknown: whether the hooks are actively controlled; what the eye photophores are
  for; whether an adult is neutrally buoyant; growth curve; swimming speed; hunting posture.

## ACT 4 — 9 MARCH 2025

**C4.1 CONFIRMED.** ROV **SuBastian**, from R/V ***Falkor (too)***, filmed a **~30 cm
juvenile** at **600 m** near the **South Sandwich Islands**, on **9 March 2025**.
Announced **15 April 2025**. Expedition: **"GoSouth"**, an Ocean Census flagship,
**20 Feb - 26 Mar 2025** (35 days). Chief scientist **Dr Michelle Taylor 🧍** (Essex).
Partners: Schmidt Ocean Institute · Nippon Foundation-Nekton Ocean Census · GoSouth
(Plymouth + GEOMAR + British Antarctic Survey) · GSGSSI.
⚠ **DISPUTED depth:** Smithsonian says ">3,200 feet" (~975 m). **Use 600 m** — both
primary releases say so; Smithsonian appears to be in error.

**C4.2 CONFIRMED — and it is a better story than "scientists found it".** The animal was
**first flagged by a member of the public watching the ROV livestream.** The ROV was on
its way down to do other work. Taylor 🧍: *"We filmed it because it was beautiful and
unusual, and then we kind of descended back all the way down to the seafloor to do the
exploration that the rest of that dive was focused on."*

**C4.3 CONFIRMED — the verification, and the five weeks of silence.** The team **withheld
the footage** while independent experts confirmed the species (PBS). Five weeks between
filming and announcement. Verifiers: **Dr Kat Bolstad 🧍** (AUT) and **Dr Aaron Evans 🧍**
(independent Cranchiidae specialist), consulted independently. The test was **hooks in the
middle of the eight arms** — see C3.3.

**C4.4 — THE COLD OPEN (recommended).** Southern Argentina, **second week of March 2025**
(⚠ exact date UNVERIFIED; she says "the day that we got back on shore" for a clip
collected "the day before"). Bolstad 🧍 and Linley 🧍 have just come off a five-week
Antarctic voyage running a purpose-built camera rig to film an **adult** colossal squid.
They failed. Gear packed, goodbyes said. Verbatim, Science Friday, 18 Apr 2025:
- *"There was a moment. It was a very specific moment."*
- *"We got off the ship. We had packed up all our gear. We had said goodbye to everybody.
  We went and checked into a weird Airbnb in Southern Argentina, and then we got this
  footage."*
- *"And then we got the high-res version, and I could see the key features. And I was
  like, yeah, they've got it."*
Then **Evans 🧍**: *"I started hyperventilating."* (ScienceAlert; Nat Geo carries *"We got
very excited, and we started hyperventilating."* — **two renderings of one reaction; pick
one, caption its source, do not blend**.) Then the reveal: 30 centimetres. A baby.
⚠ **UNVERIFIED:** whether any photograph exists of Bolstad and Linley at that moment. Build
from her audio, the ROV clip, and stills.

**C4.5 CONFIRMED — what the footage shows.** Near-transparent juvenile; **iridescent
eyes**; visible internal organs; **rust-red/brown chromatophores** on the transparent body
(Bolstad 🧍, ABC) — implying it can switch between transparent and opaque. First look at
**undamaged** morphology: every previous specimen was netted, hauled, or digested.
**Steve O'Shea 🧍** (independent commentator, not on the expedition): *"It is lovely to see
this species being filmed by scientists in a non-invasive way, alive rather than dead on a
trawler deck or mangled in a net."*
Evans 🧍: *"We could think of this maybe as a 'teenager' squid… it's still got a lot of
growing to do."* And: *"This is not the giant animal we'd expect the colossal squid to be."*

**C4.6 CONFIRMED — what it does NOT show.** No adult. No feeding, hunting, predation or
mating. Nothing on lifespan, growth rate or adult behaviour. The 30 cm figure is the
team's **estimate**, not an independent measurement. Bolstad 🧍: *"We actually don't know
very much about what they eat themselves… we don't know the lifespan."*

**C4.7 ⚠ HONESTY GATE — CONFIRMED ABSENCE.** There is **no peer-reviewed paper** on the
2025 observation. It was an institutional press release plus two named independent
verifiers. **Never call it "published research."** Call it a confirmed observation,
independently verified.

**C4.8 CONFIRMED — Bolstad's framing, and the film's closing register.**
- *"It's exciting to see the first in situ footage of a juvenile colossal and humbling to
  think that they have no idea that humans exist."*
- *"We get to introduce the live colossal squid to the world as this beautiful, little,
  delicate animal"* — without *"some of that monster hype."*
- *"One of the things I love about this footage is how delicate this animal appears to be.
  It looks like a glass sculpture."*

**C4.9 — THE THESIS LINE (why the adult stays hidden). CONFIRMED, attributed.**
Bolstad 🧍: *"They know we are there long before we know they are there."* Longer form:
*"Much of our scientific and filming gear is noisy and bright, so squid will be aware of
our equipment long before we know they're there — and they will stay well away."*
This is a **methodological** answer, not a mystical one — and it closes the loop with the
eye (C3.2).

## ACT 5 — THE HUNT, STILL OPEN

**C5.1 DISPUTED / UNRESOLVED — the rival footage.** **6 January 2023**, ~176 m, ~680 miles
south of Argentina: **Kolossal**, founded by **Matt Mulrennan 🧍**, filmed a ~4-5 inch
glass squid and called it *"potentially, the first colossal squid ever filmed in its
natural environment."* Experts split three ways: juvenile colossal, adult *Galiteuthis
glacialis*, or an undescribed *Taonius*. **Dr William Reid 🧍** (Newcastle) among the
sceptics. **Still unresolved at Dec 2025**, with **Aaron Evans 🧍** as a peer reviewer
declining to confirm: *"The squid seen here could belong to different life stages of
either of those taxa"* — while calling it *"an exciting example of wild cranchiid
behavior."*
**This is the spine of the verification act:** the same expert whose arm-hook test
confirmed 2025 is the expert who will not confirm 2023. It also explains the five weeks
of silence. ⚠ The journal that ran the Kolossal review could not be identified.

**C5.2 CONFIRMED — no adult, still.** No second confirmed colossal squid observation and
**no adult filmed** as of 1 Sept 2026. The specimen catalogue still ends at record #29 —
the 2025 juvenile. **The episode's title is literally true on the day it ships.**

**C5.3 CONFIRMED — where the hunt goes next.** The Schmidt team is **building and testing
new camera systems** aimed specifically at filming an adult (PBS). ***Falkor (too)* has no
Southern Ocean expedition in 2026** — the published 2026 programme is entirely Atlantic.
⚠ **Press-reported, NOT institutional:** ABC reported Schmidt would not return to
Antarctica **until 2028**. No SOI document confirms this. Attribute to the reporting.
⚠ **UNVERIFIED:** Kolossal's 2025-26 activity. Last solid reporting is Hakai, May 2024.

**C5.4 CONFIRMED — the arc closes itself.** Rosa et al.'s 2017 review ends its "future
directions" by recommending exactly this: *"Baited cameras and remotely operated vehicles
could be deployed in the search of live footage, as has happened recently for other deep
sea 'giant' squids."* Eight years later an ROV did it — by accident, on the way to
somewhere else, spotted by a stranger on the internet.

**C5.5 CONFIRMED — the scale of the gap it closed.** Before 2025 the colossal squid was
the **only known extant species of truly giant (>50 kg) cephalopod never filmed in its
natural habitat.** *Architeuthis*: photographed 2004, filmed 2012. *Taningia danae*: 2005.
*Onykia robusta*: by 1993. *Kondakovia longimana*: 2017.

**C5.6 — CLOSING BUTTON.** Mulrennan 🧍: *"The hunt continues."*
