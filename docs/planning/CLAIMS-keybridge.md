## ACT 1 ADDENDUM — the historical precedent (Sunshine Skyway)

- **[CONFIRMED]** May 9, 1980, ~7:30 a.m.: the 19,734-ton phosphate freighter
  Summit Venture, blinded by a sudden squall that knocked out its radar,
  struck a support column of the old Sunshine Skyway Bridge in Tampa Bay,
  Florida. About 1,200 feet of bridge collapsed. Sources: FOX 13 Tampa Bay; WTSP; Tampa Bay Times.
- **[CONFIRMED]** 35 people died — 26 of them aboard a Greyhound bus that
  plunged with six vehicles roughly 150 feet into the bay. Victims ranged from
  an infant to a 92-year-old. One man survived after his pickup truck bounced
  off the ship's bow instead of falling. The current Skyway bridge opened in
  1987 with new safety features.
- **[CONFIRMED]** This is the real, verified disaster that led the industry to
  adopt AASHTO's 1991 vessel-collision-risk standard — the Key Bridge, built
  in 1977, predated it and was never required to be retested.

## ACT 1 — THE RISE (the bridge, the ship, the crew)

**The bridge:**
- **[CONFIRMED]** 1.6-mile steel continuous-truss bridge over the Patapsco River
  estuary, carrying I-695 (Baltimore Beltway); main truss 2,644 ft with a
  1,200-ft main span flanked by two 722-ft back spans. Built because the Baltimore
  Harbor Tunnel had hit capacity by the early 1960s; a second tunnel priced higher
  than a 4-lane bridge, so the bridge became the Beltway's final link. Prime
  engineer: **J. E. Greiner Company** of Baltimore. Construction began 1972;
  **opened March 23, 1977** (75¢ opening-day toll). Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Scott_Key_Bridge_(Baltimore); cnsmaryland.org/2024/03/26/the-history-of-baltimores-key-bridge; washingtonpost.com 2024-03-26.
- **[DISPUTED]** Construction cost: $110M pre-construction estimate vs. $141M
  reported final cost (~$743M in 2024 dollars) — present both, don't pick one.
- **[CONFIRMED]** The bridge was the **only** Baltimore Harbor crossing legally
  open to hazmat vehicles (both tunnels ban them) — its loss was an immediate
  logistics crisis, not just a detour. Carried 12.4M+ vehicles in 2023, generating
  ~$56M in tolls (~8% of MDTA's total toll revenue). Sources: cnsmaryland.org; thebanner.com/community/transportation/baltimore-bridge-mdta-toll-revenue.
- **[CONFIRMED]** 🧍 **"Fracture-critical" design** — no structural redundancy;
  losing one key support could bring down the whole structure. Its pier
  protection (concrete "dolphins" fendered with timber) was built to 1970s
  criteria; a 1980 vessel strike on Pier 17 was stopped by the fendering, which
  was then rebuilt to the SAME original criteria rather than upgraded — the
  origin-story irony that the near-miss became "proof" the system worked.
  **[CONFIRMED, NTSB]** The bridge predates AASHTO's 1991 post-Sunshine-Skyway
  ship-strike vulnerability standard and was never required to be retroactively
  assessed; NTSB later calculated its collapse risk at **~30× the modern
  acceptable threshold**, and found the Maryland Transportation Authority never
  conducted the recommended assessment. NTSB has since recommended vulnerability
  assessments for **68 other US bridges across 19 states** (incl. Golden Gate,
  Chesapeake Bay, Verrazzano-Narrows, Greater New Orleans) — most aging bridges
  over navigable water were never assessed at all. Sources: npr.org/2024/04/04/1242605876; ntsb.gov/news/press-releases/Pages/nr20250320.aspx; cbsnews.com (68-bridge list).

## ACT 2 — THE DISASTER (NTSB final report, MIR-25-40)

- **[CONFIRMED — verbatim NTSB probable cause, report p.202]:** *"...the probable
  cause of the contact of the containership Dali with the Francis Scott Key
  Bridge was a loss of electrical power (blackout), due to a loose signal wire
  connection to a terminal block stemming from the improper installation of
  wire-label banding, resulting in the vessel's loss of propulsion and steering
  close to the bridge... Contributing to the collapse... and the loss of life was
  the lack of countermeasures to reduce the bridge's vulnerability to collapse due
  to impact by ocean-going vessels... Also contributing to the loss of life was
  the lack of effective and immediate communications to notify the highway
  workers to evacuate the bridge."* Use as the single most quotable line in the
  episode — cold-open reveal or an exact-quote chapter card.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Technical root cause: signal wire **"Wire 1"** in the ship's
  high-voltage switchboard disconnected from a WAGO terminal block because its
  heat-shrunk wire-label banding covered the ferrule's full insulated collar,
  blocking full insertion into the spring-clamp gate — left vulnerable to
  vibrating loose. When it did, breaker HR1 opened unexpectedly, cutting power to
  steering-gear pumps, the fuel-flushing pump, and main-engine cooling pumps.
- **[CONFIRMED — correction #4]** TWO separate blackouts: **Blackout 1** (0125:00,
  the Wire-1 fault) — steering pumps 1–3 and main engine lost; LV power restored
  0125:58. **Blackout 2** (0127:04, a SEPARATE cause) — generators 3 & 4
  underperformed from insufficient fuel pressure because the vessel was drawing
  generator fuel through a **flushing pump not designed for that continuous
  duty**, disconnecting both generators and blacking out the whole vessel again;
  power restored 0127:36 — 93 seconds before impact.
- **[CONFIRMED — full VDR timeline, NTSB Appendix C, correction #5 supersedes the
  preliminary report's rounder numbers]:** 0125:00 first blackout (0.6 mi / 3
  ship-lengths out) · 0125:08 main engine shuts down · 0125:58 LV power restored ·
  0125:59 senior pilot phones the Maryland pilots' dispatcher requesting the
  bridge be closed (correction #2 — THIS call, not the later broadcast, triggers
  the traffic stop) · 0126:13 pilot orders 20° port rudder · 0126:39 pilots call
  for tug assist (never arrives in time) · 0126:44 dispatcher calls MDTA command
  center · 0126:46 dispatcher relays the closure request · 0127:02 senior pilot
  orders **"port anchor"** (correction #3 — a good-faith measure NTSB says
  couldn't have worked) · 0127:04 second blackout (HV + LV both down) · 0127:11
  pilot orders a sécurité call · 0127:25 sécurité call broadcast (correction #2 —
  NOT a mayday) · 0127:36 power restored again · 0127:46 bow thruster ordered full
  to port (not yet running; later found ineffective above 5 kn anyway) · 0128:10
  bow exits the channel, begins grounding in mud · 0128:21 MDTA police stop all
  vehicle traffic · 0128:22 both bridge ends closed · 0128:59 anchor chain sound
  recorded (~10 sec before impact) · **0129:09 — Dali strikes Pier 17 (IMPACT)** ·
  0129:15–0129:28 Spans 18, then Pier 17, then Spans 20/21/22 collapse in sequence
  (one span carried 4 vehicles, one carried 2, one carried the bridge inspector's
  vehicle). **Total elapsed time, first power loss to impact: 4 minutes 9
  seconds.** Use NTSB's own "Time Until Impact" countdown format for an on-screen
  ticking-clock sequence.
- **[CONFIRMED — the pivotal "lives saved" beat, attribute correctly per
  correction #2]:** The senior pilot's 0125:59 phone call to the shoreside
  dispatcher — NOT the anchor, NOT the sécurité broadcast — is what NTSB credits
  with clearing vehicle traffic. MDTA police (already stationed at both bridge
  ends that night for an unrelated project) stopped all vehicle traffic at
  0128:21, **48 seconds before impact**. NTSB's exact words: *"the quick actions
  of the pilots, the pilots' shoreside dispatcher, and the MDTA, to stop bridge
  traffic prevented a greater loss of life."*
- **[CONFIRMED — the tragic asymmetry, act 2→3 turn]:** That same fast response
  never reached the 7-person road crew + inspector already on the span — NTSB
  found they were never notified, and states they "may have had sufficient time"
  to reach a surviving section had they been warned as fast as police were.
  Listed explicitly as a contributing cause of the six deaths alongside the
  bridge's structural vulnerability.
- **[CONFIRMED]** 🧍 **Real, sourced maintenance-channel exchange** (the cleanest
  ethical pivot into the tragedy, per the human-thread scout): a worker on the
  bridge's own maintenance radio asked what was happening; another replied,
  *"They're just holding traffic because a ship lost its steering, that's all."*
  The bridge collapsed **less than 30 seconds later** — no evacuation order ever
  followed on that channel. Real, sourced (NBC/PBS), no invented dialogue, no
  named victim. Sources: pbs.org/newshour/nation/construction-workers-had-little-warning.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Casualties (NTSB Table 1): 6 fatal (all highway workers), 1
  serious (a highway worker), 1 minor (a Dali crew member); the 2 Maryland pilots
  aboard were uninjured; zero fatalities aboard the ship. Damage: Dali damage
  exceeded **$18 million**; bridge replacement estimated **$4.3–5.2 billion**,
  reopening not expected until **late 2030**. Formerly carried 34,000+
  vehicles/day (~10% trucks), now permanently detoured.
- **[CONFIRMED]** NTSB's earlier urgent report (Mar 18 2025, reproduced as
  Appendix E, MIR-25-10) is the source of the 30×-risk and 68-bridge findings
  above — good "this could happen again" closing/verbal-bridge material.
