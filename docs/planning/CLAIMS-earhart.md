## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **It is USCGC Itasca, a Coast Guard cutter — NOT "USS Itasca."** "USS"
   denotes a US Navy ship; Itasca was a Coast Guard vessel. Get this exactly
   right in every caption/lower-third — a Black Box Breakdown-caliber episode
   should not make this rookie error.
2. **The final transmission's signal was LOUD AND CLEAR (S5, maximum
   strength), not fading.** Do not write a cold open where Earhart's voice
   "grows fainter" or the signal "weakens" as she disappears — that is
   backwards. Chief Radioman Leo Bellarts described the opposite: the
   transmission was so strong he ran to the bridge expecting to see her
   plane. The dread in this scene comes from the escalating URGENCY of her
   words arriving at full volume, not a dying signal. Use: "her voice came in
   loud and clear — and it was the words, not the signal, that told him
   something was wrong."
3. **The famous full-quote final transmission is a cleaned-up reconstruction
   of a terser/more garbled raw handwritten log entry.** TIGHAR's own
   analysis says the now-standard "WE ARE ON THE LINE 157 337..." wording is
   the smoothed/typed version; treat minor wording variance across sources
   (archives.gov vs. TIGHAR vs. secondary write-ups) as normal transcription
   variance, not a factual dispute — but don't present the quote as a
   word-perfect verbatim transcript beyond what the archival log supports.
4. **Itasca's own commanding officer inserted details into the OFFICIAL
   report that are NOT in the original rough log** (e.g., "cloudy and
   overcast" per TIGHAR's Log Jam analysis) — a genuine, source-documented
   instance of the official record being embellished after the fact by Cdr.
   Warner K. Thompson. Good "even the official record has holes" beat; do
   not present the Itasca's written reports as a flawless primary source.
5. **The 157/337 line's geometric intersection with Gardner Island
   (Nikumaroro) is a CONFIRMED navigational/mathematical fact. That Earhart
   and Noonan actually flew there and landed is NOT a confirmed fact — it is
   TIGHAR's theory ("the Nikumaroro hypothesis"), attributed to TIGHAR/Ric
   Gillespie by name, never stated as settled history.** Same applies to
   every TIGHAR-recovered artifact (aluminum panel, shoe fragments, cosmetic
   jar, mirror compact, jackknife, liquor bottle, sextant box) — all
   "consistent with" claims, none proven to be Earhart's or Noonan's.
6. **Lt. John Lambrecht's "signs of recent habitation... no answering wave"
   line from the USS Colorado's July 1937 overflight of Gardner Island is a
   real, verbatim quote from his official Navy report.** Its evidentiary
   significance for Earhart's fate is entirely theoretical (TIGHAR's
   inference) — the quote itself is CONFIRMED, the "this means they were
   alive down there" reading of it is ALLEGED/theory, keep the two visually
   and verbally separated on screen.
7. **The July 1937 Navy/Coast Guard search conclusion and the January 5, 1939
   legal death declaration are TWO SEPARATE EVENTS 18 months apart — do not
   conflate them.** The search (ended July 19, 1937) concluded "lost at sea,
   presumed crashed, no wreckage found" and remains the US government's
   standing position. The Jan 5, 1939 ruling was a Los Angeles County
   Superior Court in-absentia probate/estate declaration requested by George
   Putnam, unusual for being granted well before the normal seven-year
   waiting period. Script must present these as distinct beats, not one
   event restated twice.
8. **The 2018 Jantz "bones" study is a probabilistic statistical argument,
   NOT a scientific identification.** Richard Jantz's Fordisc re-analysis
   found the 1940 Nikumaroro bones (long since lost — no DNA retest is
   physically possible) "more consistent with Earhart than with 99 percent
   of individuals in a large reference sample." This is DISPUTED — a 2015
   Cross & Wright paper defended the original 1940 examiner Dr. Hoodless's
   "stocky, middle-aged male" finding as methodologically sound. Present as
   competing statistical arguments over 80-year-old measurements of
   since-lost bones, not as a resolved identification.
9. **The 2019 Nat Geo "Expedition Amelia" DNA testing result is INCONCLUSIVE
   (samples too degraded/small), not a definitive "no match."** Some
   secondary summaries loosely paraphrase this as a negative result — the
   more accurate framing per History News Network/USF is that the test
   could not produce a definitive answer either way. Do not say "DNA proved
   it wasn't her" — say the DNA testing was inconclusive.
10. **The History Channel's 2017 "Japanese capture" photo is DEBUNKED — do
    not use, and do not present the broader Japanese-custody theory as
    credible.** The photo the show presented as evidence of Earhart/Noonan
    in Japanese custody at Jaluit Atoll was found by blogger Kota Yamano
    published in a 1935 Japanese travelogue book — two years BEFORE the
    1937 disappearance. This specific claim is conclusively false. The
    broader Japanese-capture theory (Goerner, Loomis) has no credible
    evidence at all; mention only with explicit attribution if at all, per
    house style, and lead with the debunked-photo fact if the theory comes
    up.
11. **Deep Sea Vision's January 2024 "sonar image of the Electra" was
    revisited in November 2024 and identified as a natural rock formation —
    DEBUNKED, not an open lead.** Do not present this as unresolved evidence;
    it was widely reported, then retracted by its own team.
12. **The "Taraia Object" expedition has NOT departed and has produced NO
    findings as of this research (Aug 2, 2026).** It was postponed twice and

## ACT I — Origins: fame, Purdue, the Electra, Noonan (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- **Pre-1937 career (CONFIRMED, high confidence, minimal hedging needed):**
  June 17–18, 1928 — first woman to cross the Atlantic by air, as a
  passenger aboard the Fokker F.VII "Friendship" (pilot Wilmer Stultz,
  mechanic Louis Gordon), Trepassey, Newfoundland → Burry Port, Wales. May
  20–21, 1932 — first woman (2nd person after Lindbergh) to fly solo
  nonstop across the Atlantic, red Lockheed Vega, Harbor Grace, Newfoundland
  → near Londonderry, Northern Ireland; earned the Distinguished Flying
  Cross. Jan 11–12, 1935 — first person to fly solo across the Pacific,
  Honolulu → Oakland, 2,408 miles, a route that had killed 10 prior
  aviators attempting it. Sources: PBS American Experience; ameliaearhart.com;
  Smithsonian Pioneers of Flight.
- **Purdue connection (CONFIRMED per Purdue's own archives):** 1935, Purdue
  president Edward Elliott recruits Earhart as a visiting faculty member —
  documented title "Counselor on Careers for Women" (held 1935–1937; some
  secondary sources loosely render this "adviser to the Department of
  Aeronautics" — use the documented title). April 1936, Purdue trustee David
  Ross helps establish "The Amelia Earhart Fund for Aeronautical Research."
  Total fund ~$80,000 — see DATA CORRECTION #14. The Electra 10E was publicly
  framed as a "flying laboratory" carrying genuinely new equipment
  (radio-telephone gear, updated navigation instruments, wing de-icing);
  DISPUTED/UNVERIFIED how much of an actual ongoing research program (vs.
  publicity framing) this represented — sources describe stated intent, not
  an executed program. Earhart took delivery from Lockheed's Burbank plant
  July 24, 1936. Sources: archives.lib.purdue.edu; stories.purdue.edu.
- **The aircraft (CONFIRMED specs):** Lockheed Model 10E Electra Special,
  registration NR16020 ("R" = Restricted category, modified configuration).
  All-metal twin-engine low-wing monoplane, retractable gear; length 38 ft 7
  in, wingspan 55 ft, empty weight 7,265 lb; two Pratt & Whitney Wasp S3H1
  radials (550 hp continuous/600 hp takeoff each); ten interior fuel tanks,
  1,151-gallon total capacity (stock Electra 10 configuration far exceeded
  for world-flight range). Sources: This Day in Aviation; the Stratus
  Project.
- **Fred Noonan — career (CONFIRMED):** born April 4, 1893, Cook County,
  Illinois; years as a merchant seaman before aviation; by the early 1930s a
  leading figure in Pan American Airways' navigation section, one of the
  era's top oceanic air navigators, credited with navigating Pan Am's
  pioneering trans-Pacific survey/Clipper flights (San Francisco–Honolulu),
  which built his reputation as Earhart's preferred navigator over other
  candidates. Married Mary Beatrice "Bee" Martinelli (Oakland hairdresser)
  March 27, 1937 — roughly three months before he disappeared. Sources:
  history.com; archives.lib.purdue.edu (Noonan papers); Smithsonian Time and
  Navigation exhibit.
- **Fred Noonan — Pan Am departure and drinking allegations: DISPUTED/
  ALLEGED. See DATA CORRECTION #15 — do not state a single settled reason.**
- **First world-flight attempt (CONFIRMED):** Earhart, Noonan, navigator
  Harry Manning, and technical advisor/pilot Paul Mantz depart Oakland March
  17, 1937; Oakland → Honolulu; crash on takeoff from Luke Field, Ford Island,
  March 20, 1937 (ground-loop, reported cause a blown tire/uneven power
  application). Luke Field Crash Report archived at TIGHAR. Electra returns
  to Lockheed Burbank for repair; route reversed to eastbound (partly for
  seasonal winds).
- **Second world-flight attempt / route to Lae (CONFIRMED, cross-verified
  TIGHAR + Purdue archives):** Noonan now sole navigator (Manning, Mantz do
  not continue). Departs Oakland May 21, 1937: Oakland → Burbank → Tucson →
  New Orleans → Miami (arrives May 23) for final overhaul. June 1, 1937 —
  "official" world-flight departure, Miami → San Juan, Puerto Rico. Full
  route: San Juan (Jun 2) → Caripito, Venezuela (Jun 3) → Paramaribo,
  Suriname (Jun 4) → Fortaleza, Brazil (Jun 6) → Natal, Brazil (Jun 7) →
  Saint-Louis, Senegal (Jun 8, South Atlantic crossing) → Dakar, Senegal
  (Jun 10) → Gao, Mali (Jun 11) → N'Djamena/Fort-Lamy, Chad (Jun 12) → El
  Fasher, Sudan (Jun 13) → Khartoum, Sudan (Jun 13) → Massawa, Eritrea (Jun
  14) → Assab, Eritrea (Jun 15) → Karachi (Jun 17) → Calcutta (Jun 18) →
  Akyab/Sittwe, Burma (Jun 19) → Rangoon (Jun 20) → Bangkok (Jun 20) →
  Singapore (Jun 21) → Bandoeng, Java (Jun 24) → Soerabaja, Java (Jun 25,
  back to Bandoeng Jun 25/27 for engine repairs) → Koepang, Timor (Jun 28) →
  Port Darwin, Australia (Jun 29) → Lae, New Guinea (Jun 29). By Lae: ~22,000
  of a planned ~29,000-mile equatorial circumnavigation covered, with the
  hardest leg — Lae → Howland Island, 2,556 miles — still ahead. Sources:
  tighar.org World Flight Route Map; archives.lib.purdue.edu/repositories/2/
  archival_objects/10338; Purdue Archives blog "Remembering Amelia Earhart's
  Round-the-World Flight: The 80th Anniversary"; Flashback Miami.

## ACT II — The disaster: Lae takeoff to the last transmission (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- **Lae takeoff, July 2, 1937 (CONFIRMED, HIGH confidence):** ~10:00 a.m.
  local, a day later than planned (July 1 departure scrubbed by wrong-
  direction wind/threatening cloud). Aircraft carried ~1,100 gallons of
  gasoline (some sources cite 1,150 gal / ~15,000 lb all-up weight against a
  16,500 lb rated max — MEDIUM confidence on the exact weight, HIGH
  confidence on the ~1,100-gallon fuel load). Runway: ~3,000-foot grass
  strip ending at a bluff into the shark-populated Huon Gulf. Guinea Airways
  ground staff recalled the heavily loaded Electra "could barely lift on
  takeoff," using "every inch of runway and then some." Last known
  photograph of the aircraft taken by Guinea Airways employee Alan Board.
  Distinct detail: Noonan had been unable to set his marine chronometers
  against a radio time signal on July 1 due to radio trouble — the celestial
  navigation for the entire 2,556-mile overwater leg began with an unresolved
  timing error already baked in. Sources: thisdayinaviation.com/2-july-1937.
- **The radio log — the spine of this act (CONFIRMED, primary source, see
  Primary Exhibit above).** Chief Radioman Leo G. Bellarts on watch aboard
  USCGC Itasca off Howland Island. Chronological KHAQQ transmissions (Howland/
  ship local time): ~06:14-15 ("about two hundred miles out," requesting a
  bearing); ~06:45-46 (repeats, "about 100 miles out"); **07:42** — "we must
  be on you but cannot see you, but gas is running low, been unable to reach
  you by radio, we are flying at 1,000 feet" (S5, max signal strength);
  ~07:58 — "we are circling but cannot hear you" (log's original word later
  corrected from "drifting" to "circling"); 08:00-03 — "we recd ur sigs but
  unable to get a minimum, please take bearing on us and answer 3105 wid
  voice"; **08:43 — the final, last-confirmed transmission ever received:**
  "WE ARE ON THE LINE 157 337 WL REPT MSG WE WILL REPEAT THIS MESSAGE ON 6210
  KCS, WAIT, LISTENING ON 6210 KCS. WE ARE RUNNING NORTH AND SOUTH." After
  this — silence, no further transmission ever logged. See DATA CORRECTIONS
  #2–4 for signal-strength and record-embellishment nuance.
- **Bellarts's own recollection (MEDIUM-HIGH confidence — 1973 interview
  with researcher Elgen Long, via wingsoverkansas.com, corroborated by
  TIGHAR's Bellarts bio page; decades-later recollection, not a primary
  transcript, but consistent across independent write-ups):** "She was so
  loud that I ran up to the bridge expecting to see her coming in for a
  landing." Also: "The last time we heard her voice it was so loud and clear
  that you could hear her outside the radio shack... it sounded as if she
  would have broken out in a scream if she hadn't stopped talking," and "She
  was just about ready to break into tears and go into hysterics."
- **157/337 — the navigational meaning (CONFIRMED math; ALLEGED
  significance — see DATA CORRECTION #5).** These are magnetic/compass
  headings for a sun-line navigation technique (Noonan's method) describing
  a line of position running roughly NW-SE, passing near Howland Island and
  — per later celestial-navigation analysis — also near Gardner Island
  (Nikumaroro), ~350 nautical miles SE of Howland. The geometric intersection
  is confirmed; that they flew the line to Gardner and landed is TIGHAR's
  theory, not an official finding.

## ACT III — The search and the official record (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- **Search mobilization (CONFIRMED, well-documented, Navy/USCG sources):**
  When Itasca received no further contact it got underway; the Navy
  mobilized one of the largest air-sea search operations in history at the
  time.
- **USS Colorado (BB-45)** — in Hawaii on an ROTC midshipman cruise, diverted;
  launched three Vought O3U-3 scout floatplanes. Roughly July 7–12 (TIGHAR's
  granular log dates the actual overflights July 8–11): searched Winslow
  Reef (~5,000 sq mi, reef not found), McKean Island (uninhabited "except
  for myriads of birds"), **Gardner Island** — lead pilot Lt. John O.
  Lambrecht's official report: "signs of recent habitation were clearly
  visible but repeated circling and zooming failed to elicit any answering
  wave... it was finally taken for granted that none was there" (CONFIRMED
  as written; significance ALLEGED — see DATA CORRECTION #6) — plus Hull,
  Sydney, Phoenix, Enderbury, Birnie, Canton Islands, all negative.
- **USS Lexington** — 62 embarked aircraft, searched 100,000+ sq mi NW/W of
  Howland, July 13–19, 1937, launching waves of 40–62 planes in grid patterns
  two miles apart. Escorts USS Swan and Itasca simultaneously searched the
  Gilbert Islands. Every mission log records some variant of "nothing was
  sighted."
- **Search called off July 19, 1937 (CONFIRMED):** ~16 days, ~9 vessels, ~66
  aircraft, ~4,000 personnel, cost widely cited as over $4 million (~$88M
  today), covering an area "roughly the size of Texas" — no oil slick,
  wreckage, or debris ever found. US Navy's official contemporaneous
  conclusion: the Electra ran out of fuel, crashed, and sank — "lost at sea,
  presumed crashed, no wreckage recovered" — the US government's standing
  official position to this day; no US agency has ever revised this finding.
- **Legal death declaration — a SEPARATE event, Jan 5, 1939 (CONFIRMED, see
  DATA CORRECTION #7):** LA County Superior Court grants an in-absentia death
  declaration at George Putnam's request, for estate/probate purposes —
  unusually fast, the judge himself called it extraordinary given the normal
  seven-year wait.

## ACT IV — Aftermath, theories, and the 2025–2026 news cycle

- **The two "conventional" theories (CONFIRMED framing, UNVERIFIED as
  fact):** the dominant "crashed-and-sank" theory (ran out of fuel, went
  down at sea) remains UNVERIFIED — no confirmed wreckage ever recovered.
  Deep Sea Vision's Jan 2024 sonar image — DEBUNKED as a rock formation, see
  DATA CORRECTION #11.
- **TIGHAR's Nikumaroro castaway theory (ALLEGED — attributed to Ric
  Gillespie/TIGHAR by name, see DATA CORRECTION #5 and #16):** ~12
  expeditions since 1988; artifacts recovered (aircraft aluminum fragment,
  woman's shoe — a 1930s "Blucher-style oxford" sole plus separate heel — a
  broken cosmetic jar consistent with the era's Dr. C.H. Berry's Freckle
  Ointment, a mirror compact, a jackknife, a Benedictine liquor bottle, a
  sextant box marked 1542/3500 since lost) — DISPUTED/UNVERIFIED,
  "consistent with," never "proven to be Earhart's."
- **The 1940 bones (CONFIRMED historical record; modern re-analysis
  DISPUTED, see DATA CORRECTION #8):** Colonial officer Gerald Gallagher
  found 13 bones near a campfire site on Nikumaroro in 1940. Dr. D.W.
  Hoodless (Fiji, 1940–41) concluded "stocky, middle-aged male" — against
  Earhart. TIGHAR rediscovered Hoodless's report/measurements in the late
  1990s; 1998 TIGHAR-affiliated forensic anthropologists contested his
  methodology at the American Anthropological Association. 2018: Richard
  Jantz's Fordisc re-analysis of Hoodless's original seven measurements —
  "more consistent with Earhart than with 99 percent" of a reference sample
  (peer-reviewed, *Forensic Anthropology*, but probabilistic, not
  identification). DISPUTED by a 2015 Cross & Wright paper defending
  Hoodless's original finding. The bones themselves have been lost since the
  1940s — no new physical/DNA test is possible; the debate is permanently
  confined to statistical argument over old measurements.
- **"Expedition Amelia" (2019) and DNA testing (UNVERIFIED/INCONCLUSIVE, see
  DATA CORRECTION #9):** National Geographic's two-hour special, aired Oct
  20, 2019, feat. Robert Ballard, Fredrik Hiebert, Thomas F. King, and
  forensic anthropologist Erin Kimmerle (USF, living — attribute by name).
  Kimmerle located/examined the 13 bones at the Te Umwanibong Museum, Tarawa,
  Kiribati; reconstructed a skull assessed as consistent with an adult female
  matching some of Earhart's known features (incl. a sinus condition); sent
  samples for DNA comparison against Earhart relatives — results
  INCONCLUSIVE (samples too degraded/small), NOT a negative match. Ballard's
  underwater search around Nikumaroro that expedition found nothing
  conclusive.
- **Japanese-capture theory (FRINGE/DISPUTED-DEBUNKED, see DATA CORRECTION
  #10):** circulating since the 1960s (Fred Goerner, Vincent Loomis), no
  credible physical/documentary evidence. History Channel's July 2017
  "Amelia Earhart: The Lost Evidence" presented a National Archives photo as
  showing Earhart/Noonan in Japanese custody at Jaluit Atoll; within about a
  week, blogger Kota Yamano (living — attribute by name) found the identical
  photo in a 1935 Japanese travelogue book, two years before the
  disappearance — conclusively debunking that specific claim. No subsequent
  record has substantiated the broader theory.
- **2025–2026 declassification (CONFIRMED release, UNVERIFIED significance —
  see DATA CORRECTION #17):** Sept 26, 2025, Trump administration directs
  declassification of all Earhart-related government records; DNI Tulsi
  Gabbard and National Archives begin rolling release at
  archives.gov/ameliaearhart — 4,624 pages (Sept 2025), 3,715 pages (Nov 25,
  2025), 1,810 pages (Dec 17, 2025), 6 pages (Jan 9, 2026). Contents: final
  radio transmissions, weather/aircraft-condition data, search-location
  records, internal Navy investigations, period ephemera. NewsNation's
  "Reality Check" (host Ross Coulthart, living — attribute by name) framed
  documents as challenging the "tunnel vision" of the official search — real
  journalism on a real release, but UNVERIFIED/overstated to call this a
  resolution; no smoking-gun finding reported.
- **The Taraia Object (UNVERIFIED/PENDING, see DATA CORRECTION #12):**
  lagoon-floor anomaly spotted via Apple Maps satellite imagery in 2020 by
  retired Navy veteran Mike Ashmore (living); fresh attention via CNN (Oct
  28, 2025); Archaeological Legacy Institute (director Richard Pettigrew,
  living) partnered with Purdue University; expedition postponed twice, now
  rescheduled for "the first week of October" 2026 per Purdue's own
  newsroom — as of Aug 2, 2026, NOT departed, NO findings. Ric Gillespie
  (TIGHAR) himself is skeptical, believes it's likely a downed coconut tree.
- **The 2026 "solved" video (UNVERIFIED CLICKBAIT, see DATA CORRECTION
  #13):** "Top History" channel's "Amelia Earhart Mystery Finally Solved in
  2026 And It Isn't Good" (Jan 1, 2026, ~429K views) appears to repackage
  the pre-existing Nikumaroro-castaway narrative riding the real 2025 news
  wave. No primary source corroborates an actual solve. The case remains
  officially unresolved as of August 2026.

## ARCHIVAL FOOTAGE PLAN

**Newsreel footage (video) — strongest visual asset class for this
episode.** Universal Newsreel (1929–1967) donated its full library to NARA
(Record Group 200); never renewed copyright on the bulk of it, so the whole
corpus is public domain — digitized/searchable at catalog.archives.gov and
mirrored on Archive.org's "Universal Newsreel Collection." High confidence
Earhart material exists here for: her 1932 solo Atlantic return, the 1935
Honolulu–Oakland solo Pacific crossing, and — most useful for this episode —
1937 world-flight coverage (Oakland departure March 1937; Luke Field/Honolulu
ground-loop crash aftermath, likely caught by press photographers/newsreel
cameras; the Miami send-off June 1, 1937, well documented as filmed and
crowd-drawing).
- **Deprioritize/flag as licensed, not free:** Fox Movietone (Univ. of South
  Carolina Newsfilm Library) and British Pathé (YouTube-hosted but licenses
  commercial reuse through its own paid store) — usable only if a specific
  clip is separately confirmed PD.
- **Verify before scripting as "footage":** whether actual 16mm motion film
  survives of the July 2, 1937 final Lae takeoff, vs. only the famous Alan
  Board still photograph. Medium confidence only — check directly.

**Photographs:**
- Purdue's "George Palmer Putnam Collection of Amelia Earhart Papers"
  (Purdue Archives and Special Collections/Karnes Archives) — substantially
  digitized; hangar/construction photos of the Electra, world-flight
  planning documents, personal photos. Check per-image licensing — Purdue's
  digital collection often states "permission required" rather than CC0.
- Wikimedia Commons (Category:Amelia Earhart, Category:Lockheed Electra 10E,
  Category:Fred Noonan) — solid body of PD-tagged press photography
  (portraits, Electra ground shots, Earhart-with-Noonan photos, press
  conferences), mostly PD via 1930s non-renewal or US-government origin.
- Library of Congress Prints & Photographs Division (search "Amelia
  Earhart") — unrenewed 1930s press-agency photos (Harris & Ewing, National
  Photo Company, NY World-Telegram morgue), tagged "no known restrictions" —
  high-confidence PD.
- Smithsonian Open Access (airandspace.si.edu / si.edu/openaccess) — check
  for CC0 images of NASM's Earhart-related objects (flight gear, scale
  Electra model, memorabilia) — medium-high confidence given the 2020
  open-access CC0 initiative.

**USS Colorado / USS Lexington / USCGC Itasca search — strong PD photo/
document source.** All official US government vessels/operations. US Coast
Guard Historian's Office (history.uscg.mil) maintains a dedicated Earhart/
Itasca page with photos and the radio log transcripts — a genuinely
load-bearing exhibit for this episode, the same role FAA/NTSB transcripts
play in other Black Box episodes. NARA holds official Navy/Coast Guard search
records (RG 26 Coast Guard; RG 24/38/80 Navy) — high confidence these include
photos of Colorado's scout planes and Lexington's air group over the Phoenix
Islands, medium confidence on digitization/searchability without an
in-person NARA pull.

**Explicit no-footage beats — dossier/reconstruction candidates:**
- The moment of disappearance itself (after the final 08:43 transmission,
  the plane running out of fuel somewhere over open ocean) — zero real
  footage by definition, no camera was there. Clean `dossier: true`
  candidate: case-file reconstruction of the Itasca radio log excerpts,
  dead-reckoning position estimates, and the 157/337 sun-line, not any
  attempted dramatization.
- The specific search-plane sweeps over the empty ocean — no scene-specific
  footage exists (generic 1937 Navy carrier/catapult-launch footage exists
  but isn't literally "the search for Earhart" and must not be mislabeled as
  such). Use real generic Navy-search-era footage only if honestly framed as
  illustrative, or fall back to dossier treatment.

**TIGHAR/modern Nikumaroro material — flag as NOT free.** TIGHAR's decades
of Niku expeditions (video/photo of shoe fragments, sextant box, sonar
imagery) is TIGHAR's own copyrighted material, typically licensed to
documentary producers for a fee — do not treat as fetchable. Nat Geo's
"Expedition Amelia" (2019) and History Channel's "The Lost Evidence" (2017)
are fully copyrighted, off-limits.
- **Alternative modern-footage lead:** NOAA Coral Reef Conservation Program
  research expeditions to the Phoenix Islands Protected Area (2000s–2010s,
  includes Nikumaroro/Gardner Island itself); NOAA Photo Library
  (photolib.noaa.gov) is a federal PD source — medium confidence for real
  aerial/underwater footage of the actual island (modern-era, not archival,
  but genuinely the real place) — worth a direct search before assuming no
  site footage exists.

**Footage-fetch search terms to use directly:** "Universal Newsreel Amelia
Earhart 1937," "Amelia Earhart Electra Oakland departure," "Amelia Earhart
Miami takeoff June 1937," "USS Itasca 1937" / "USCGC Itasca 1937," "USS
Colorado Earhart search," "USS Lexington Earhart search 1937"; Wikimedia
Commons: "Lockheed Electra 10E," "Amelia Earhart Purdue," "Fred Noonan."
Verify Purdue's and Smithsonian's per-image license terms individually
rather than assuming blanket PD from the institution name alone.

## COLD OPEN

**Recommended: the USCGC Itasca radio shack, 0843 local time, July 2,
1937.** Stronger than the Lae takeoff alternative — a named eyewitness with a
verbatim, vividly physical recollection, exact clock times tied to a primary
archival log, and a concrete action (a man running to the bridge) staged
almost verbatim. Slots directly into the channel's existing `RadioScene`
treatment (waveform + transcript beat) already used for JAL123/Helios522/
US1549-style episodes.

Beat: Aboard USCGC Itasca, stationed off Howland Island, Chief Radioman Leo
G. Bellarts is on watch. A tightening sequence of transmissions from
Earhart's Electra (call sign KHAQQ) has been coming in all morning. At 07:42
local: "we must be on you but cannot see u but gas is running low been
unable to reach you by radio we are flying at a 1000 feet" — signal at
maximum strength. At 08:43 local, the final transmission ever received: "we
are on the line 157-337... will repeat this message... we are running north
and south." Then — nothing. Bellarts, decades later: **"She was so loud that
I ran up to the bridge expecting to see her coming in for a landing."**

**Critical accuracy note for the script (do not violate DATA CORRECTION
#2):** the signal was loud and clear (S5) at the very end — the dread comes
from her words growing more urgent while arriving at full volume, and from a
man bolting for the deck expecting to see her plane appear, not from a
weakening/fading signal. Do not write "her voice grew fainter" — it is
factually backwards from the sourced material.

**Alternative/flashback beat (weaker character material, still HIGH
confidence): Lae, New Guinea, ~10:00 a.m. local, July 2, 1937.** Earhart and
Noonan take off in the heavily loaded Electra (NR16020, ~1,100 gallons of
fuel) from a ~3,000-foot grass strip ending at a bluff into the Huon Gulf;
ground staff recall it "could barely lift on takeoff," using "every inch of
runway and then some." Distinct underused detail: Noonan had been unable to
set his marine chronometers against a radio time signal the day before due
to radio trouble — the celestial navigation for the entire 2,556-mile
overwater leg began with an unresolved timing error already baked in before
the wheels left the ground. Alan Board's photograph is the last known image
of the aircraft. Usable as a "six hours earlier" flashback immediately after
the Itasca hook, not as the primary cold open.
