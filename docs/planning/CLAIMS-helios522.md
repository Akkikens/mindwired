## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **The cold-open engineer's name is a media attribution, NOT in the
   official report.** "Alan/Allan Irwin" appears constantly in journalism
   and documentaries but does not appear anywhere in the 198-page AAIASB
   report itself, which refers to him only as "Ground Engineer number one."
   The Greek CRIMINAL proceedings DO name an "Alan Irwin" as a defendant
   (chief engineer) — so the name is real and applies to a real named
   defendant, just not sourced to the AAIASB technical report. Script may
   use the name (it's the real defendant's name from the criminal case,
   confirmed via Greek court reporting) but attribute it to the court case,
   not the AAIASB report, when citing on screen.
2. **The captain/first-officer names are likewise media attributions, not
   AAIASB report text.** "Hans-Jürgen Merten" (captain) and "Pambos
   Charalambous" (first officer) are the names used across journalism and
   are consistent with the bios the report DOES give (age, nationality,
   licensing, flight hours) — treat names as reliable via convergent media
   sourcing, but bios/hours/history are the CONFIRMED (report-sourced) part.
3. **The prior-flight cabin-crew report was "hard bangs" and a "freezing"
   door seal — NOT "a whooshing sound and cold air."** The popular
   paraphrase is embellishment. Use the report's actual language.
4. **The pressurization mode switch was NOT a formal dereliction of duty.**
   AAIASB finding 3.1.3.9: leaving the selector off AUTO "was not a formal
   omission," though "it would have been prudent" to reset it. The Board
   ALSO faulted Boeing's ambiguous maintenance-manual procedure (no explicit
   checklist step confirming AUTO) and the flight crew's three separate
   missed chances to catch it (preflight, after-start, after-takeoff
   checks). This is a DISTRIBUTED finding, not one person's fault — script
   must not simplify to "the engineer forgot a switch."
5. **The "second person in the cockpit" (a girlfriend, per some
   documentaries) is DISPUTED/EXCLUDE.** The official report found only one
   male in the cockpit (Andreas Prodromou) with no DNA evidence for a second
   occupant. A 2006 documentary's claim of a second attendant (Haris
   Charalambous) via DNA contradicts the official report — do not use.
6. **Andreas Prodromou held a real UK Commercial Pilot License (JAR CPL
   A/IR), not merely a private license.** Some secondary sources (a
   commonly-cited blog) understate this. He was not 737-type-rated, but he
   was a real, licensed commercial pilot — state this accurately, it
   actually strengthens the "closest thing to a chance" framing honestly.
7. **The "passenger farewell text message" is a CONFIRMED HOAX — EXCLUDE
   ENTIRELY.** Widely circulated ("we're freezing, goodbye") but the
   supposed sender was not on the manifest, and Greek police arrested the
   person who spread it for disseminating false information (source:
   FlightGlobal). Do not use, reference, or imply this ever happened.
8. **The famous "F-16 intercepting the 737" image (Commons file
   Helios522.png) is a CGI/3D illustration, explicitly labeled as such by
   Commons — NOT a real photograph.** Many documentaries (including at
   least one Mayday/Air Crash Investigation-style show) present it as if
   real. If used at all, it must carry an "illustration" label — never
   presented as real footage/photography.
9. **All times below are LOCAL (EEST, UTC+3)** unless stated otherwise —
   two research passes independently sourced the same events in EEST and in
   UTC (a clean 3-hour offset explains the apparent discrepancy; not a fact
   dispute). Use EEST consistently in the script for audience clarity; note
   in narration only if citing a raw CVR/report timestamp directly.
10. **Two SEPARATE criminal prosecutions, different countries, different
    outcomes — do not conflate them.** Cyprus: all four defendants
    (chairman Andreas Drakos, CEO Demetris Pantazis, ops manager Giorgos
    Kikidis, chief pilot Ianko/Yanko Stoimenov) were ACQUITTED Dec 2011; an
    appeal ordered a retrial, but it was dropped under double-jeopardy rules
    because the same men were concurrently on trial in Greece. Greece:
    Pantazis, Kikidis, and Stoimenov were CONVICTED of manslaughter
    (~2012–13, exact date disputed between sources), 10-year sentences

12. **A claimed EASA regulation (EASA recommendation 2006-44 mandating

## Origins / background (CONFIRMED unless noted)

- Helios Airways established 1999 per the AAIASB report itself (Wikipedia
  says 1998 — DISPUTED, use 1999 when citing the official report). First
  independent, privately-owned Cypriot airline; low-cost model, MD's own
  quoted words: aimed to "fly at probably half the price." Owner acquired
  the carrier in 2004 specifically to feed his own tour operator, Libra
  Holidays.
- Fleet at the time of the crash: 1× 737-300, 2× 737-800, 1 leased A319.
- Safety record: repeated UK CAA audits 2003–2005 found no in-house quality
  system, outdated manuals, incomplete training files, understaffed
  management; the airline nearly lost its Air Operator Certificate in Nov
  2004.
- The accident aircraft, registration 5B-DBY: 737-300, built 1998, ~17,900
  flight hours/16,085 cycles at the time of the accident.
  - 16 Dec 2004: this same aircraft suffered a rapid decompression on a
    prior flight (Warsaw–Larnaca) — a hole in the aft-door seal, 3
    passengers hospitalized. The cause was never conclusively determined
    (electrical fault in the outflow valve OR inadvertent door opening —
    DISPUTED even in the earlier Cypriot inquiry).
  - 9 June – 14 Aug 2005: nine separate "Equipment Cooling System" technical
    log write-ups in the two months before the fatal flight.

- The ground engineer (male, 44, UK-licensed freelance contractor via a UK
  agency; "Ground Engineer number one" in the report, named "Alan Irwin" in

  pressurization leak check.

  leaks or abnormal noises." The pressurization mode selector is left in
  MANUAL, not reset to AUTO.

## The flight — timeline (CONFIRMED, EEST local time)

- 09:07 — takeoff, Larnaca, bound for Athens.
- 09:12:38 — cabin altitude warning horn sounds at 12,040 ft. The crew
  misidentifies it: this horn is tonally IDENTICAL to the ground-only
  takeoff-configuration warning horn (report p.133).
- 09:14:11 — Capt. Merten radios Helios operations: reports "the takeoff
  configuration warning on" and "cooling equipment normal and alternate off
  line" (report p.16) — describing symptoms of the real problem
  (depressurization) but naming the wrong system entirely. The ground
  engineer, on the same call, asks whether the pressurization panel is set
  to AUTO. Merten — already becoming hypoxic — does not engage with the
  question and asks about circuit breakers instead. This is the last
  transmission from the flight (report p.137).
- ~09:20 — last contact, aircraft climbing through ~28,900 ft.
- 09:23:32 — aircraft levels at FL340 (~34,000 ft) on autopilot, continuing
  its programmed route.
- 09:37 — enters Athens FIR with no contact; begins circling.
- 10:12–10:50 — nineteen failed ATC contact attempts.
- 10:40 — aircraft enters a holding pattern at the KEA VOR (its programmed
  fallback).
- 11:05 — two F-16s of the Hellenic Air Force 111th Combat Wing scramble
  from Nea Anchialos Air Base. (Pilots not named in the report — UNVERIFIED/
  not publicly disclosed.)
- 11:23–11:24 — F-16s intercept on the aircraft's 6th holding loop. They
  see: the first officer slumped motionless, the captain's seat empty,
  passenger oxygen masks dangling, no signs of struggle or terrorism.
- 11:49 — flight attendant Andreas Prodromou (25, holder of a real UK
  Commercial Pilot License, not 737-type-rated) enters the cockpit on
  portable oxygen, sits in the captain's seat, briefly waves at the F-16s.
- 11:49:50 — left engine flames out from fuel exhaustion; the aircraft
  exits its holding pattern and begins descending.
- 11:54 — CVR records five mayday calls from Prodromou — never heard by
  Athens ATC, because the radio was still tuned to the Larnaca frequency.
- 11:59:47 — right engine flames out.
- 12:03:32 — crash into hills near Grammatiko, ~40km/25mi from Athens.
- 121 aboard (115 passengers + 6 crew: 107 Cypriot incl. crew, 13 Greek incl.
  crew, 1 German — Capt. Merten). All 121 killed. Zero survivors. 118 bodies
  recovered.

## Probable cause (AAIASB report p.171 causal chain — CONFIRMED)

Direct causal chain, in order: (1) crew never recognized the pressurization
mode selector was left in MANUAL; (2) crew never identified the true nature
of the problem; (3) crew incapacitation from hypoxia (except Prodromou); (4)
fuel exhaustion; (5) impact with the ground.

Distributed contributing factors: the ground engineer left the selector on
MANUAL after a leak check and didn't restore AUTO; the incoming crew missed
the wrong setting on three separate checks (preflight, after-start,
after-takeoff); the identical horn tone for the cabin-altitude and
takeoff-configuration warnings directly caused the fatal misdiagnosis;
Boeing's maintenance manual had no explicit checklist step confirming AUTO
after a leak check.

**Important script-structure note:** the AAIASB report itself is narrowly
TECHNICAL — it does not assign corporate blame. The corporate-negligence
verdict (against named executives) came from separate Greek/Cypriot criminal
courts, not from AAIASB. Keep these two distinct in the episode's structure,
the same way JAL123/737 MAX distinguish "what the accident report found" vs.
"what the courts later decided."

## Aftermath (see DATA CORRECTIONS #10–14 above for the load-bearing details)

- Helios resumed flying days after the crash; rebranded as "Ajet" (styled
  αjet) in March 2006 to distance itself; ceased operations entirely by
  Oct/Nov 2006 — roughly 14 months after the crash, ~7 months after the
  rebrand.
- FAA Airworthiness Directive 2011-03-14 (compliance deadline March 2014)
  required 737-100 through -500 aircraft to be retrofitted with distinct
  cockpit warning lights separating the two previously-identical horn
  alerts — CONFIRMED via FAA's own Lessons Learned page for this accident.
- A permanent monument and small Greek Orthodox church stand at the crash
  site in Grammatiko, bearing all 121 victims' names and photos; an annual
  memorial service is held there. Paralimni, Cyprus (home to many victims/
  crew) holds its own annual commemoration.
