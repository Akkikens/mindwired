## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

1. **US Airways was NOT "mid-merger" with America West by January 2009 in
   the corporate sense — that concluded in 2005-2007.** The America West
   Holdings/US Airways Group corporate merger closed Sept 27, 2005; the FAA
   merged their operating certificates Sept 25, 2007; the "America West"
   brand was fully retired Nov 4, 2007. The real, dramatically useful
   tension still live in Jan 2009 is the **unresolved pilot-seniority
   fight**: pilots rejected the 2007 arbitration ("Nicolau Award") and
   formed a breakaway union (USAPA) in 2007-08; a joint seniority list still
   wasn't ratified. Both Sullenberger and Skiles were legacy "East"
   (pre-America-West) pilots, not directly part of that fight. Don't call
   the airline itself "still merging" — the correct hook, if used at all, is
   the labor dispute, not the corporate structure.
2. **Skiles was the MORE senior pilot by career, not the junior one — but
   brand-new to this exact aircraft.** Skiles had been a US Airways
   **Captain** (including on the Fokker 100) until he was downgraded back to
   First Officer in the post-9/11 industry contraction ("I lost my captain
   bid, as did many, in the industry contraction," his own words). He
   received his A320 type rating just **2 weeks** before the accident (Dec
   31, 2008) and had only **37 hours** in the type — Flight 1549 was his
   very first trip as pilot flying in an A320, and the first time he and
   Sullenberger had ever been paired together. Sullenberger had 19,663 total
   hours (4,765 on the A320); Skiles had 15,643 total hours but almost none
   on this airframe. A genuinely strong dramatic beat — script it precisely,
   don't flatten Skiles into "the new guy."
3. **A mechanically UNRELATED right-engine compressor stall occurred 2 days
   before the accident (Jan 13, 2009, LGA→CLT) — real, eerie, but not
   causal.** Maintenance replaced a T25 sensor and borescoped the engine
   clean; no further faults were logged. NTSB's sole probable cause was bird
   ingestion. If mentioned, frame explicitly as coincidence — never imply
   foreshadowing or a causal link.
4. **The popular "~208 seconds" figure is Sullenberger's own popularized
   number (from his Senate testimony and talks, e.g. "208 Seconds: A
   Lifetime of Lessons"), not literal NTSB report language.** The report's
   own CVR timestamps span ~213 seconds between the "birds" callout
   (1527:10) and CVR end (1530:43.7). Both figures are close and both are
   fine to use, but attribute "208 seconds" to Sullenberger's own accounting
   if scripted as a direct figure, rather than presenting it as an NTSB
   finding.
5. **Ditching conditions were colder than assumed: ~19°F air (-7°C), ~41°F
   water (5°C)** — not ~24°F. Touchdown was on the Hudson roughly 8.5 miles
   from LaGuardia; the NTSB report gives no street-level location (only
   "Weehawken, New Jersey" as the jurisdictional accident site). "Near 48th
   Street" is a commonly-cited approximation only — secondary sources vary
   between roughly W.46th and W.50th. Don't over-specify street precision on
   screen.
6. **NO passenger or crew member ever sued US Airways or Airbus over the
   ditching itself** — confirmed by on-record 2009 AND 2014 quotes from
   aviation-disaster attorney Justin Green (Kreindler & Kreindler) calling
   the total absence of litigation "amazing." **Do NOT use the widely
   repeated claim that passenger Dave Sanderson "filed the first lawsuit"
   against US Airways** — it traces only to an uncited SEO-aggregator page,
   contradicts the reliable reporting above, and is inconsistent with
   Sanderson's well-documented public persona as a motivational
   speaker/Sullenberger admirer. Treat as false; do not repeat it.
7. **Passenger Denise Lockie's 2014 lawsuit was against her EMPLOYER
   (Staples), not the airline** — a wrongful-termination/PTSD claim after
   medical leave, outcome unconfirmed. She is a strong CURRENT (Jan 2026)
   on-record voice: she partnered with the flight attendants' union (APFA)
   to publicly oppose reduced cabin-crew staffing on American's 787-9s,
   explicitly invoking her own 1549 experience and naming Welsh, Dent, and
   Dail as "instrumental." Good closing/epilogue material if a present-day
   beat is wanted.
8. **Sullenberger's ICAO ambassadorship ran only ~5 months (Feb–Jul 2022),
   not 2021–2024** as might be assumed from his Dec 2021 Senate
   confirmation. Use the verified Feb–Jul 2022 dates.
9. **HIGH-SENSITIVITY, VERY RECENT LIVING-PERSON UPDATE — flag for Akshay's
   own editorial call, do not include reflexively:** On July 14, 2026 (~2.5
   weeks before this research), Sullenberger, now 75, publicly disclosed via
   his own website statement — corroborated by CNN, NBC, and ABC News — an
   early-stage Alzheimer's diagnosis (received ~August 2025). He described
   mild symptoms and said he is going public partly to give other families
   courage to do the same. This is real, current, and safe to state as fact
   if used — but it has **nothing to do with the 2009 event** and risks
   reading as an exploitative "sad update" tacked onto a triumphant story if

   respectful present-day epilogue beat, framed exactly as dignified as his
   own statement — never as a twist, teaser, or thumbnail hook.
10. **The specific "12-inch angle iron" detail (the object that injured
    flight attendant Doreen Welsh) is UNVERIFIED** — found only in
    lower-tier secondary summaries. The underlying fact (a serious leg
    laceration from cabin-floor/structural debris, requiring surgery) IS
    confirmed directly from the NTSB's own injury table. Don't script the
    specific measurement as fact.
11. **Real archival video of the actual event EXISTS and is public domain —

## THE REAL STORY (facts for the script, organized by act)

### Act 1 — The airline, the aircraft, the crew
- **[CONFIRMED primary]** N106US: Airbus A320-214, MSN 1044, manufactured
  June 15, 1999 (Toulouse), delivered to US Airways and placed in Part 121
  service August 3, 1999. Two CFM International CFM56-5B4/P engines. At
  accident time: 25,241 total flight hours, 16,299 cycles. Last heavy
  inspection Dec 6, 2008; all recent checks clean apart from the unrelated
  Jan 13 right-engine stall (correction #3).
- **[CONFIRMED primary]** Captain Chesley "Sully" Sullenberger III, 57 (b.
  Jan 23, 1951). US Air Force Academy grad, F-4 Phantom II fighter pilot
  1973-80 (Luke AFB training; squadrons at RAF Lakenheath and Nellis AFB),
  Academy cadet glider instructor. Hired by Pacific Southwest Airlines Feb
  25, 1980 (PSA → USAir → US Airways) — a ~29-year airline career by 2009.
  Founded aviation-safety consultancy Safety Reliability Methods, Inc. in
  2007, running alongside his flying career. 19,663 total hours, 4,765 on
  the A320. No accident/enforcement history on record; reported excellent
  health.
- **[CONFIRMED primary]** First Officer Jeffrey Bruce Skiles, 49 (b. Nov 18,
  1959, Madison, WI). Both parents were pilots; flying from age 16. Hired by
  US Airways April 7, 1986 after regional carrier Midstate Airlines. Former
  Captain (incl. Fokker 100), downgraded to FO in the post-9/11 contraction
  (correction #2). A320 type rating Dec 31, 2008 — 37 hours in type, first
  trip ever paired with Sullenberger.
- **[CONFIRMED primary]** Per the CVR, Skiles was pilot flying at takeoff;
  after the bird strike Sullenberger took the controls ("my aircraft,"
  15:27:23) while Skiles ran the QRH dual-engine-failure checklist —
  investigators later found the sequence remarkably fast and difficult to
  replicate in simulation.
- **[CONFIRMED, NTSB anonymizes/secondary maps names]** Three flight
  attendants, combined ~95 years' experience (cross-validates against NTSB's
  own anonymized "Flight Attendant A/B/C" ages/training dates: 27+39+29=95).
  **Doreen Welsh** — most senior (training since 1970), working alone at the
  rear; Flight 1549 was her last-ever flight before retiring; sustained a
  serious leg laceration from cabin-floor debris (correction #10), continued
  directing passengers to the over-wing exits before evacuating last.
  **Sheila Dail** and **Donna Dent** (lead) worked the front near door 1L.
  All three testified before the House Aviation Subcommittee Feb 24, 2009.

### Act 2 — January 15, 2009: the flight (all times EST, CVR/FDR-sourced)
- **1524:54** — Cleared for takeoff, LaGuardia Runway 4.
- **1527:10.4** — Captain says "birds" on the CVR. FDR: bird encounter at
  2,818 ft, ~4.5 miles north-northwest of LGA, ~2 minutes after departure.
  Canada geese, later DNA/feather-confirmed as migratory birds specifically
  (well-corroborated secondary sourcing). Both engines' fan/core speeds
  decelerate immediately.
- **1527:33** — Mayday call: *"mayday mayday mayday...this is...Cactus
  fifteen thirty nine hit birds, we've lost thrust in both engines, we're
  turning back towards LaGuardia"* (Sullenberger misstates the flight number
  under stress — NTSB footnotes the correction to 1549). Direct CVR quote of
  a living person — use verbatim, never paraphrase as new dialogue.
- **1528:05-1528:46** — LGA departure control (controller Patrick Harten)
  offers Runway 13, then 31, then 4 — Sullenberger answers "unable" each
  time.
- **~1529:00-1529:21** — Sullenberger raises the Hudson himself ("we may end
  up in the Hudson"), then asks about Teterboro; Harten clears him toward
  TEB Runway 1.
- **1529:21-1529:36** — Sullenberger: "we can't do it" → "we're gonna be in
  the Hudson" — the ditching decision, committed.
- **1530:43.7** — CVR ends: "we're gonna brace." Touchdown ~1530-1531, on
  the Hudson ~8.5 miles from LGA (accident site listed as Weehawken, NJ).
- **[CONFIRMED primary, injury table]** All 155 aboard (150 passengers incl.
  a lap-held child, 5 crew) evacuated via forward/overwing exits onto wings
  and slide-rafts. Zero fatalities. NTSB Table 1: 5 serious injuries (1
  cabin crew + 4 passengers), 95 minor, 55 uninjured.
- **[CONFIRMED primary, cause]** *"the ingestion of large birds into each
  engine, which resulted in an almost total loss of thrust in both engines
  and the subsequent ditching on the Hudson River."*

### Act 3 — The rescue (minutes, not hours)
- **[CONFIRMED, multi-source corroborated]** NY Waterway ferry *Thomas
  Jefferson* (Capt. Vincent Lombardi), already under way from West 39th
  Street, reached the plane roughly 3-4 minutes after touchdown — first
  vessel on scene, pulled ~56 survivors off the wings via a Jason's Cradle.
  14 NY Waterway ferries responded in total; NY Waterway vessels rescued
  ~143 of the 155 aboard, with USCG and FDNY rescuing the rest.
  Lombardi's own quotes: *"because we don't know what kind of scene we're
  pulling up to"* (to his crew, before arrival); *"I saw people walking out
  from the plane, and it kind of looked like they were walking on
  water"*; *"I approached slow because I didn't want to carry a wake in and
  knock anybody [in] the water."*
- **[CONFIRMED]** US Coast Guard Vessel Traffic Service cameras captured the
  entire event on video in real time (see FOOTAGE/EXHIBIT PLAN) — this is
  the strongest available real-footage asset for the episode.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Cabin quotes: passenger 3F Ricardo Valeriano recalled the
  captain's PA as three words, "brace for impact," delivered "in a very calm
  yet direct voice"; passenger Gerry McNamara: *"The stewardesses were
  yelling in unison 'Brace! Brace! Brace!' It was a violent hit — the water
  flew up over my window — but we bobbed up and were all amazed that we
  remained intact."* (Note: this is the MOST reproduced beat in existing
  1549 media — including the film "Sully" — use briefly, don't build around
  it.)

### Act 4 — Aftermath, reforms, and status today (2026)
- **[CONFIRMED]** No lawsuit against US Airways/Airbus (correction #6). US
  Airways gave passengers $5,000 checks for immediate needs plus a ticket
  refund, and separately offered $10,000 per passenger in exchange for
  agreeing not to sue.
- **[CONFIRMED]** NTSB's final report carried 34 safety recommendations:
  bird-ingestion engine-certification testing at low airspeed/reduced power
  (FAA finalized a strengthened rule in 2023 — 14 years later, matching the
  actual 1549 scenario of birds ingested during climb rather than takeoff);
  dual-engine-failure/ditching simulator-training requirements; life-vest/
  flotation-equipment rule changes; expanded wildlife-hazard-management
  requirements for Part 139 airports. NYC-area goose culling followed
  (~1,235 removed from 17 locations in 2009; ~70,000 cumulative by 2017).
- **[CONFIRMED]** Sullenberger retired from US Airways/American, March 3,
  2010, after 30 years. Nominated by President Biden (June 2021), confirmed
  by the Senate (Dec 2, 2021), served as US Ambassador to ICAO Feb-Jul 2022
  only (correction #8). CBS News aviation/safety expert since 2011; authored
  *Highest Duty* (2009) and *Making a Difference* (2012). See correction #9
  for his July 2026 Alzheimer's disclosure — editorial call, handle with care
  if used at all.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Skiles retired from American Airlines Nov 17, 2024 (final
  flight: AA87, London Heathrow–Chicago O'Hare) after serving as VP of the
  Coalition of Airline Pilots Associations (~28,000 pilots), contributing to
  the First Officer Qualification rule and the Airline Safety Act of 2010,
  and publishing 100+ safety articles since 2011. Still giving public talks
  in 2026 (e.g. EAA Chapter 22, Rockford IL, April 2026).
- **[CONFIRMED, lower-tier source on some details]** Doreen Welsh has spoken
  candidly about severe, ongoing PTSD (panic around water, concentration
  difficulty, periodic depression) since the crash. Sheila Dail retired from
  American Jan 15, 2019 (the 10th anniversary), described lasting sleep
  difficulty. Donna Dent's exact retirement date is UNVERIFIED, but she was
  named and praised as "instrumental" by Denise Lockie as recently as
  January 2026 — implying she's alive and remembered fondly.
- **[CONFIRMED]** ATC Patrick Harten's LinkedIn currently reads "Retired and
  enjoying life" (exact date unconfirmed) — see COLD OPEN PICK for his full
  account.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Passengers Dave Sanderson (motivational speaker, says his
  talks have raised $14.8M+ for the Red Cross — his own claimed figure, not
  independently audited), Michael Leonard (became a keynote speaker/
  entrepreneur), and Brad Wentzell (says the crash gave him "a greater sense
  of purpose") have built public post-crash careers around the event.
  Survivors/crew held a well-covered 15th-anniversary reunion in NYC
  (Jan 12-15, 2024) tied to CNN's *The Whole Story with Anderson Cooper*.
- **[CONFIRMED]** The recovered aircraft has been on public display at the
  Sullenberger Aviation Museum (formerly Carolinas Aviation Museum),
  Charlotte, NC, since June 10, 2011.

## COLD OPEN PICK

**Air traffic controller Patrick Harten, New York TRACON (N90), 1515
Stewart Avenue, Westbury, NY — 3:12 p.m., January 15, 2009.** Not
Sullenberger's cockpit, not the geese, not the ferry rescue, not a
passenger — every existing 1549 documentary and the film "Sully" already
open there. Harten took the LaGuardia departure radar position at 3:12 p.m.
after a 90-minute bike ride to work; fifteen minutes later, a mayday call
came in. His own sworn words, from his Feb 24, 2009 congressional
testimony: *"I asked him to repeat himself, even though I heard him just
fine. I simply could not wrap my mind around those words. People don't
survive landings on the Hudson River; I thought it was his own death
sentence,"* and: *"I believed, at that moment, I was going to be the last
person to talk to anyone on that plane alive."* Unable to reach his wife by
phone, he texted her: *"Had a crash. Not OK. Can't talk now."* He spent
30-45 minutes believing everyone aboard had died before a colleague told him
"They survived" — he didn't believe it until he turned on a TV.

**Production bonus:** the actual FAA-released, public-domain radio recording
of this exact call is already sitting in this repo
(`public/shorts/_evidence/us1549/LGA_AWE1549_1-15-09_(LC_2010z-2025z).mp3`) —
the cold open can run on literal real audio, not narration over stock,
which is both a stronger hook and consistent with this channel's
real-audio-first and honesty rules.

**Reserve for later beats:** the ferry-rescue thread (Lombardi's quotes) —
strong material but an action/rescue POV without the cold open's needed
"outcome still unknown" irony, better as the mid-episode rescue chapter; the
