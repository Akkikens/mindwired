## ⚠ DATA CORRECTIONS (baked in — do NOT revert)

   The alien-megastructure hypothesis is scientifically **excluded**: a 2018

   ordinary dust, NOT a solid opaque object (a planet, asteroid swarm, or
   megastructure would dim all wavelengths equally). Correct framing: "the
   alien hypothesis is dead; the real ongoing mystery is which flavor of

## THE REAL STORY (facts for the script, organized by segment)

### Segment 1 (cold open) — The Wow! signal discovery, 1977
- **[CONFIRMED, Ehman's own first-person account]** August 15, 1977, 22:16
  EDT (03:16 UTC Aug 16): Big Ear radio telescope (Ohio State University
  Radio Observatory, on Ohio Wesleyan University land near Delaware, Ohio)
  receives a signal, fully unattended — "No one was at the telescope at that
  time. The receiver and computer were doing their jobs unattended."
- **[CONFIRMED]** Big Ear recorded data onto continuous line-printer paper
  via an IBM 1130 computer. Gene Mikesell, the observatory's mechanical
  technician, would stop/reset/restart the computer every 3-4 days and
  deliver stacked printouts to Ehman's home. Ehman was, at the time, an
  UNPAID VOLUNTEER (Big Ear's NSF funding had been cut in 1972; paid staff
  including Ehman had lost their jobs and found other employment, but kept
  reviewing data voluntarily).
- **[CONFIRMED, verbatim]** "A few days after the August 15, 1977 detection
  (probably on August 19), I began my routine review of the computer
  printout... A few pages into the computer printout I was astonished to see
  the string of numbers and characters '6EQUJ5' in channel 2 of the
  printout. I immediately recognized this as the pattern we would expect to
  see from a narrowband radio source of small angular diameter in the sky.
  In the red pen I was using I immediately highlighted those six characters
  and wrote the notation 'Wow!' in the left margin."
- **[CONFIRMED]** After finishing his review (checking for recurrence on
  later days — none), Ehman called colleagues John Kraus and Bob Dixon, who
  were "astonished too." Kraus coined the phrase "Wow! signal."
- **[CONFIRMED]** Intensity code "6EQUJ5": each character is a 10-second-
  averaged intensity in standard deviations above background noise (1-9,
  then A=10 upward); "U" ≈ 30-31 standard deviations — an exceptionally
  strong reading. Frequency: 1420.4556 ± 0.005 MHz (Ehman's 1998 refined
  value), very close to the hydrogen line (1420.4058 MHz) — a frequency of
  special SETI interest since hydrogen is the universe's most abundant
  element. Duration: the full 72-second observation window, with a smooth
  rise/peak/fall matching exactly what a fixed celestial point source would
  produce as Big Ear's stationary meridian-transit beam swept past it (NOT
  the abrupt on/off you'd expect from typical terrestrial interference).
- **[CONFIRMED]** Direction: constellation Sagittarius. Because the signal
  appeared in only one of Big Ear's two feed horns, there are two possible
  right-ascension solutions (a known ambiguity in the original data).

### Segment 2 — Tabby's Star (KIC 8462852 / Boyajian's Star)
- **[CONFIRMED]** Flagged by Planet Hunters citizen-science volunteers
  (Zooniverse, visually inspecting Kepler light curves) — formally credited
  as paper co-authors (K.J. Jek, M.R. Omohundro, H.M. Schwengeler, D.M.
  LaCourse, among others), not just acknowledged. Lead author Tabetha S.
  Boyajian; paper posted arXiv Sept 2015, published MNRAS April 2016,
  "Planet Hunters IX/X. KIC 8462852 – Where's the Flux?"
- **[CONFIRMED]** Dimming events: non-periodic, asymmetric, deepest reaching
  ~22% brightness loss (Feb 28, 2013), other major dips ~15% (March 2011)
  and ~8% (April 2013). A normal planet transit (even Jupiter-sized) blocks
  only ~1% of a Sun-like star's light — a 22% dip would need an occulting
  object ~70% the star's width, physically implausible for any solid
  planet. Combined with non-periodicity, this ruled out an ordinary
  transiting-planet explanation.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Jason Wright et al.'s 2016 paper explored whether the
  light curve's qualitative features were consistent with a partially-built
  Dyson swarm, calling it "the most promising stellar SETI target
  discovered to date" — framed as a falsifiable, testable, last-resort
  hypothesis, not a claim of detection (see correction #2).
- **[CONFIRMED]** Other real natural hypotheses in the peer-reviewed
  literature: a swarm/family of disintegrating exocomets (the long-favored
  candidate, given no infrared excess was found — arguing against a thick
  opaque dust cloud); a circumstellar dust disk/ring; the star having
  consumed a planet ~10,000 years ago with lingering debris; a disrupted
  exomoon/exo-Trojan debris.
- **[CONFIRMED — the 2018 result that changed everything, see correction
  #1]** Multi-wavelength photometry (Boyajian et al. 2018, Las Cumbres
  Observatory campaign) found wavelength-dependent dimming — the signature
  of ordinary dust, ruling out any opaque solid object including a
  megastructure. Boyajian: "Dust is most likely the reason why the star's
  light appears to dim and brighten." Wright: "This latest research rules
  out alien megastructures, but it raises the plausibility of other
  phenomena."
- **[CONFIRMED]** A 2019 Spitzer/Swift/AstroLAB IRIS study found evidence
  for a longer-term dust cloud with a ~700-day orbital period contributing
  to the star's slower secular dimming trend.
- **[REPORTED, very recent — July 2026]** A new paper (Madurga-Favieres et
  al., MNRAS, using TESS + radial-velocity data) reports TENTATIVE (2.3σ,
  NOT confirmed) evidence of a giant planetary-mass companion (~9.4 Jupiter
  masses, ~2.35 AU orbit) that could gravitationally stir comet/planetesimal
  debris into the inner system — the authors themselves caution more
  observations are needed to confirm this isn't a false positive.

### Segment 3 (the strangest, closing reveal) — 'Oumuamua (1I/2017 U1)
- **[CONFIRMED]** October 19, 2017: Robert Weryk, a postdoc at the
  University of Hawaii's Institute for Astronomy, spots a faint fast-moving
  streak in Pan-STARRS1 survey data (Haleakalā Observatory, Maui) — part of
  NASA's routine near-Earth-object survey. Weryk: "It's just something that
  happened to run into us, and we were very lucky to have been operating
  the telescope that night and looking in that direction." "We've never
  seen anything like this before." By ~Oct 25-30, orbital eccentricity
  calculated at ~1.19-1.20 (any value >1 = unbound hyperbolic = definitively
  interstellar, not solar-system-bound) — reclassified within weeks: comet →
  asteroid → first-ever interstellar object designation 1I/2017 U1. Name
  "'Oumuamua" = Hawaiian for "scout"/"messenger from afar arriving first."
  It had already passed perihelion (closest solar approach) before
  discovery — found ~40 days after, already outbound.
- **[CONFIRMED, precise numbers]** Shape: brightness varied by a factor of
  ~10 (amplitude estimates vary ~1.5-2.6 mag depending on rotation model) on
  a 7.3-hour tumbling rotation — implying an elongation ratio of at least
  ~5:1 (some models up to ~10:1, exact ratio model-dependent/disputed among
  papers). No known solar-system asteroid/comet exceeds ~3:1 — a genuine,
  quantified outlier. Dark red color, similar to organic-rich outer-solar-
  system bodies.
- **[CONFIRMED, precise numbers]** Non-gravitational acceleration: Micheli
  et al. 2018 (Nature) found the outbound trajectory could NOT be fit by
  gravity alone — required a radial acceleration term of (4.92 ± 0.16) ×
  10⁻⁶ m/s² at 1 AU, at high statistical significance.
- **[CONFIRMED, precise numbers]** The puzzle: this is the kind of push seen
  in comets from outgassing — but deep imaging and Spitzer Space Telescope
  non-detections found NO coma, no dust tail, no gas emission, despite
  passing within ~0.26 AU of the Sun. Quantified dust-production upper
  limits (≤~2×10⁻⁴ to 2×10⁻³ kg/s) sit two to three orders of magnitude
  below typical near-Sun active comets. Spectroscopic searches found no CO,
  CO2, CN, or other typical cometary gas lines. This mismatch — a measured
  comet-like kick with zero comet-like visible activity — is the real,
  peer-reviewed puzzle.
- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #3]** Bialy & Loeb 2018 (ApJL): proposed
  solar radiation pressure could explain the acceleration IF 'Oumuamua had
  an extreme area-to-mass ratio (thickness ~0.3-0.9mm) — explicitly hedged
  between (a) an unknown natural ISM/protoplanetary-disk process, or (b)
  artificial lightsail debris. Loeb's 2021 popular book *Extraterrestrial*
  argues far more confidently for the artificial interpretation than the
  hedged paper did. Loeb founded the Galileo Project (Harvard, 2021)
  partly motivated by 'Oumuamua.
- **[CONFIRMED, see correction #4]** "The Natural History of 'Oumuamua"
  (Nature Astronomy, July 2019, 14 leading experts) concluded observations
  are consistent with a purely natural origin in every case. Since then,
  competing NATURAL hypotheses (not alien) remain actively debated and
  unresolved: molecular hydrogen ice (Seligman & Laughlin 2020, later
  disputed by Loeb himself co-authoring a rebuttal); nitrogen-ice fragment
  from an "exo-Pluto" (Jackson & Desch 2021, itself later challenged);
  radiolytic H2 from water ice via cosmic rays (Bergner & Seligman 2023,
  Nature, also disputed in a same-year Nature rebuttal). No settled final
  answer on the MECHANISM exists — but no peer-reviewed consensus supports
  the artificial-origin hypothesis; it remains essentially exclusive to Loeb.
- **[CONFIRMED — good closing contrast beat]** Two more interstellar
  objects have since been found — 2I/Borisov (2019) and 3I/ATLAS (2025) —
  both showing ordinary, unambiguous cometary activity (visible tails/
  outgassing). This sharpens why 'Oumuamua specifically stands out as the
  anomalous case among all three known interstellar visitors.
