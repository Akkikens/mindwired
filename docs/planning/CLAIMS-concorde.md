## ACT 1 — THE RISE (origins, the machine, the golden age)

- **[CONFIRMED]** Anglo-French **treaty signed 29 November 1962** — a government-to-government treaty (not a commercial contract), deliberately structured so cancellation carried heavy penalties, which is why it survived repeated attempts to kill it. Signed under Macmillan's UK govt (Julian Amery, Minister of Aviation, for Britain 🧍 deceased). Manufacturers: **BAC (British Aircraft Corporation)** + **Sud Aviation** (→ Aérospatiale 1970); ~350 French + 250 British firms. Sources: heritageconcorde.com/1962-concorde-treaty; corporate.airfrance.com; baesystems.com/heritage/bac-concorde.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Name **"Concorde"** chosen/announced by **Charles de Gaulle** (d.1970). Britain first spelled it "Concord"; **Tony Benn** (d.2014), UK Technology Minister, restored the French "e" in Dec 1967 ("Excellence, England, Europe, Entente"). Use as anecdote, not official acronym. Sources: designmuseum.org/design/concorde; aerotime.aero.
- **[CONFIRMED]** US competitor **Boeing 2707** (swing-wing, ~Mach 2.7, up to ~277 seats) — **Congress cut funding in 1971**, no prototype flew; ~$1B US federal money spent. (See correction #5 — no "one vote.") Sources: globalsecurity.org; simpleflying.com/boeing-2707-cancelled.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Soviet **Tupolev Tu-144** ("Konkordski") beat Concorde to the air — first flight **31 Dec 1968**, first supersonic June 1969; Tupolev bureau under **Alexei Tupolev** (d.2001). **[DISPUTED cause]** Tu-144S broke up at the **Paris Air Show, 3 June 1973** over Goussainville — 6 crew + 8 on ground killed; cause never established. Damaged SST confidence, fed Concorde order cancellations. Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Paris_Air_Show_Tupolev_Tu-144_crash.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Prototype **001 (F-WTSS) first flew 2 March 1969, Toulouse, pilot André Turcat** (d.2016); ~28 min, gear/nose down. Prototype **002 (G-BSST) first flew 9 April 1969, Filton→Fairford, pilot Brian Trubshaw** (d.2001). Rollout of 001 at Toulouse **11 Dec 1967**. Sources: thisdayinaviation.com; airbus.com; baesystems.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** First **Mach 1: 1 Oct 1969**; first **Mach 2: 4 Nov 1970** (001). Sources: thisdayinaviation.com; simpleflying.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Program cost **~£1.3 billion** vs original **~£70M** estimate (correction #1). Sources: baesystems.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Commercial service began **simultaneously 21 January 1976** — BA London Heathrow→Bahrain (BA300, capts Norman Todd & Brian Calvert, deceased); Air France Paris→Rio via Dakar. Sources: airwaysmag.com; heritageconcorde.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** US landing-rights fight: **Port Authority of NY & NJ banned Concorde from JFK (11 Mar 1976)** over noise; BA/AF sued; federal court found the ban discriminatory (1977); **US Supreme Court let Concorde into JFK, Oct 1977**. Interim service to **Washington Dulles from May 1976**. Scheduled **JFK service from Nov 1977** — the signature, most profitable route. Sources: airwaysmag.com; simpleflying.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** The machine: **ogival (ogee) slender delta wing**; **four Rolls-Royce/Snecma Olympus 593 turbojets with reheat (afterburner)**, ~38,000 lb thrust each with reheat — the only civil airliner in sustained service using afterburners; the **droop ("droop-snoot") nose** for takeoff/landing visibility. Cruise **just over Mach 2 (~1,350 mph) at ~60,000 ft**; **London–New York in ~3.5 hours**. Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce/Snecma_Olympus_593; heritageconcorde.com; baesystems.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **Sonic-boom-over-land ban** confined profitable supersonic cruise to transatlantic ocean routes — the core reason the network never expanded. Aluminium airframe **heated to ~100–127 °C and physically stretched several inches (~15–25 cm)** in cruise (correction #4). Sources: simpleflying.com; aerospaceweb.org.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **20 airframes built** (2 prototypes + 2 pre-production + 16 production); **14 entered airline service (7 BA, 7 Air France)**. Only **BA and Air France** ever operated it commercially. Sources: baesystems.com; simpleflying.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Why no other airline flew it: ~70+ options from ~16 airlines (Pan Am, TWA, American, Continental, United, etc.) **evaporated after the 1973 oil crisis** + overland sonic-boom bans + the Tu-144 Paris crash; Pan Am's 1973 cancellation triggered the cascade; BA/AF took theirs up only because their governments backed the buys. Sources: simpleflying.com/concorde-orders; heritageconcorde.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **Fastest transatlantic crossing by any commercial airliner: 2 h 52 min 59 s, 7 February 1996** (JFK→LHR, BA G-BOAD; capt Leslie Scott). Guinness-recognized. Sources: guinnessworldrecords.com; aerospacebristol.org.
- **[CONFIRMED / soften]** Luxury era: celebrity/royalty/executive clientele, champagne service; fare **~$10,000+ round trip** in final years (correction #3). Named-passenger claims must be individually sourced. Source: britishairways.com heritage.
- **KEY PEOPLE (all deceased):** Sir George Edwards (d.2003, head of BAC); Sir Archibald Russell (d.1995, BAC/Bristol chief designer); Pierre Satre (d.1980, Sud Aviation technical director); Lucien Servanty (d.1973, Sud lead engineer); André Turcat (d.2016); Brian Trubshaw (d.2001). Binational design leadership (correction #9).

## ACT 2 — THE CRASH (Air France 4590, 25 July 2000)

- **[CONFIRMED]** **AF4590**, non-scheduled charter **CDG → New York JFK**, 25 July 2000; passengers mostly **German tourists** joining a cruise. Aircraft **F-BTSC**. Departed **runway 26R**. Crashed ~90 s after tyre burst into the **Hôtelissimo hotel, Gonesse** (La Patte d'Oie). Sources: BEA report; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_France_Flight_4590; FAA F-BTSC.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **Death toll = 113:** 100 passengers + 9 crew (3 cockpit + 6 cabin) aboard + 4 on the ground. No survivors aboard.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Crew (deceased — treat with dignity, attribute actions to BEA): **Captain Christian Marty** (54, also first to windsurf the Atlantic); **First Officer Jean Marcot** (50); **Flight Engineer Gilles Jardinaud** (58). Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Marty; airlive.net.
- **[CONFIRMED per BEA — causal chain, correction #7]:**
  1. A **titanium wear strip** fell onto 26R from a **Continental Airlines DC-10** that departed shortly before (engine-3 thrust-reverser cowl door; per BEA, not fitted per approved procedure — attribute to BEA).
  2. Concorde's **tyre No. 2 (left main gear) ran over it and burst**.
  3. A ~**4.5 kg** chunk of tyre debris struck the wing underside at **fuel tank 5**.
  4. The impact **did NOT puncture** the tank — it created a pressure shockwave (hydrodynamic ram) that **ruptured the tank from the inside**.
  5. Leaking fuel **ignited** — BEA: **probable** ignition from an **electrical arc in the gear-bay wiring**.
  6. **Engines 1 and 2 lost thrust**; engine 2 shut down. Aircraft was **past V1** (committed). **Gear would not retract**; drag stayed high; it could not climb, lost speed, **stalled**, crashed ~90 s later.
  Sources: BEA report; en.wikipedia.org; FAA Lessons Learned.
- **[DISPUTED — contributing factors]** Aircraft reportedly **~810 kg over MTOW**, near aft CG, with ~**8-kt tailwind**; a **missing landing-gear bogie spacer** (Air France maintenance) is widely reported as possibly contributing — contested; BEA's official chain centers on tyre/tank. Continental alleged the fire may have started before the strip — **not the BEA conclusion**; present as litigation claims. Sources: en.wikipedia.org; key.aero; Bloomberg.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Known prior vulnerability: earlier Concorde tyre-burst events (e.g. 1979 Washington). Post-crash fixes: **Kevlar tank liners, Michelin NZG tyres, hardened wiring**. Sources: Smithsonian; FAA.
- **[CONFIRMED] 🧍 HIGH SENSITIVITY — Continental / John Taylor (correction #8):** French criminal trial opened Feb 2010 (Pontoise); **Dec 2010 court convicted Continental + mechanic John Taylor of involuntary manslaughter** (Taylor: 15-month suspended sentence + €2,000 personal fine; Continental: €200,000 fine + ~€1M to Air France). **29 Nov 2012, Versailles appeals court overturned the criminal convictions** — held Taylor could not have foreseen the catastrophe and his error did not amount to criminal liability; **Continental retained ~70% civil liability**. On screen: "convicted, then cleared on appeal"; never assert Taylor caused the deaths. Sources: NBC News; JURIST; Bloomberg; CSMonitor.
- **[CONFIRMED]** BEA **final report published 16 January 2002** (French civil-aviation accident authority; public record → exhibit). English PDF URL above.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Certificates of Airworthiness of the whole in-service fleet **withdrawn 16 August 2000** by UK CAA + French DGAC — Concorde grounded. Sources: FAA; concordesst.com.

## ACT 3 — THE FALL (return, economics, the grounding decision, legacy)

- **[CONFIRMED]** Return-to-service modifications (per CAA/DGAC directives): **Kevlar-rubber liners** in the vulnerable tanks (EADS Toulouse, >150 pieces/aircraft, ~£17,000 each); **Michelin NZG (Near Zero Growth) burst-resistant tyres**; **reinforced/armoured wheel-well wiring**. Programme cost **~£17M** (+ ~£14M BA cabin refit) (correction #2). Sources: heritageconcorde.com; flightglobal.com; Smithsonian.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **First post-crash commercial flight 7 November 2001** — both BA and AF flew New York — **less than two months after 9/11**. Sources: cbsnews.com; concordesst.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **9/11 crushed premium transatlantic demand** — Concorde's core market (London/Paris–NY business travellers) collapsed and didn't recover; the relaunch landed into a depressed market. **[CONFIRMED/approx]** Concorde burned ~4× the fuel per passenger of a 747; **[CONFIRMED per AF]** Air France cited a ~72% rise in maintenance costs. Sources: simpleflying.com; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde.
- **[CONFIRMED — proximate cause]** **Airbus** (which inherited type-support obligations of Aérospatiale/BAC via EADS/Airbus) announced it would **end maintenance support and spares** — without manufacturer support the aircraft couldn't economically stay airworthy, forcing the airlines' hands. Sources: airwaysmag.com; simpleflying.com.
- **[CONFIRMED / DISPUTED profit figures]** The **programme never recovered its costs**; in operation **BA ran Concorde profitably** in the boom years (BA-claimed totals → treat as company claims), while **Air France's was rarely profitable**, flown largely "for national pride." Sources: etonomics.com; simpleflying.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **10 April 2003:** BA and Air France simultaneously announced retirement. BA CEO **Rod Eddington** 🧍 framed it as a commercial decision (falling revenue vs rising maintenance) — verify exact quote wording before on-screen use. Air France (chairman **Jean-Cyril Spinetta** 🧍) exited first. Sources: heritageconcorde.com; airwaysmag.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **Air France last commercial flight 31 May 2003** (F-BTSD, AF001, JFK→CDG). AF farewell flights: F-BTSD→Le Bourget 14 Jun; **F-BVFC flew AF's very last Concorde flight 27 Jun 2003** (CDG→Toulouse). Sources: concordesst.com; airdatanews.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **BA last commercial flights 24 October 2003** — three Concordes landed at Heathrow within minutes (from Edinburgh, a Bay-of-Biscay loop, and the last scheduled supersonic passenger flight BA002 from JFK, capt **Mike Bannister** 🧍). **[CONFIRMED]** The **very last flight of any Concorde: 26 November 2003, G-BOAF (last built) Heathrow→Filton**, capt **Les Brodie** 🧍. Sources: history.com; airwaysmag.com; migflug.com.
- **[CONFIRMED]** **~18 airframes survive** in museums (F-BTSC lost in the 2000 crash). Verified locations (spot-verify tail↔museum before naming): Intrepid Museum NYC (G-BOAD); Smithsonian Udvar-Hazy (F-BVFA); Museum of Flight Seattle (G-BOAG); Aerospace Bristol/Filton (G-BOAF); Brooklands (G-BBDG); National Museum of Flight East Fortune (G-BOAA); Musée de l'Air Le Bourget (F-BTSD + prototype F-WTSS); Manchester Runway Visitor Park (G-BOAC); Barbados (G-BOAE). Sources: smithsonianmag.com; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Concorde_aircraft.
- **[CONFIRMED]** Why nothing replaced it: extreme fuel burn / poor per-passenger economics, the overland sonic-boom ban, high maintenance, small premium market. **[CONFIRMED milestones / UNVERIFIED timeline]** Boom Supersonic (CEO **Blake Scholl** 🧍) is building the Overture; its **XB-1** demonstrator first flew 22 Mar 2024 and **broke the sound barrier 28 Jan 2025** (first civil supersonic flight since Concorde). Overture EIS ~2029–2030+ is a projection. Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_XB-1; boomsupersonic.com.
