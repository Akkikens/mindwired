A NASA astronaut lost a glove in orbit — and it never came down. This is what really happens to the gear astronauts lose in space.

When something slips away on a spacewalk, it doesn't fall and it doesn't rust. At 17,500 mph, a lost glove — or a $100,000 tool bag — becomes its own satellite. Then atomic oxygen and ultraviolet radiation slowly tear it apart, turning it into a high-velocity projectile with the energy of a bullet.
