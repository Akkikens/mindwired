# CTR Engine — packaging that actually earns the click

## The one truth this skill exists for
On a young channel YouTube shows each video to a small test audience (~a few hundred
impressions). **Click-through rate on that test decides whether the video escapes to
a bigger audience or dies.** Production quality does NOT rescue a low CTR. So packaging
(title + thumbnail) is not the last step — it is the product. Target CTR: **6%+ is
alive, 8-12% is good, 12%+ can go viral.** Below 4% the video is effectively dead
regardless of how good it is.

## Process (run every time)

### 1. Find the ONE curiosity gap
Every winning title/thumbnail pair creates a question the viewer *must* click to answer,
without giving away the answer. Write the gap in one sentence first, e.g.:
- "There's a hole in the universe and it shouldn't exist" (not "The Boötes Void explained")
- "This switch almost stranded them on the Moon" (not "Apollo 11 documentary")
Bad packaging *describes*. Good packaging *withholds*.

### 2. Generate 10 titles, then cut to 3
Rules for each title:
- **≤ 55 characters** (mobile truncates ~48-55). Front-load the hook word.
- Lead with the emotional/curiosity beat, not the topic keyword, UNLESS the keyword
  itself is the draw (e.g. "Apollo 11", "Black Hole").
- Use ONE of these proven frames per title, vary across the 3:
  - **Contradiction:** "There's a Hole in the Universe" / "The Star That Shouldn't Exist"
  - **Stakes/threat:** "The Object That Could End the Universe"
  - **Number + payoff:** "8 Places in Space That Terrify Scientists"
  - **Forbidden/secret:** "The Space Theory Scientists Discuss Only at Night"
  - **Personal/second-person:** "What You'd See Falling Into a Black Hole"
- NO clickbait you can't pay off. NO ALL-CAPS words (reads as spam in 2027). NO "..."
- Score each 1-10 on: curiosity gap, scroll-stopping first 3 words, payoff honesty,
  mobile length. Keep the top 3 for A/B testing.

### 3. Thumbnail: 3 concepts, built for 170px
The thumbnail is seen at phone size first. Non-negotiable checks:
- **The subject must read at 170px wide.** One dominant shape/idea. Do the squint test.
- **≤ 4 words of text, or zero.** Modern (2027) = minimal editorial, NOT stacked
  MrBeast text. See THUMBNAILS.md → Workflow C.
- **Contrast:** the focal point must pop against a dark background (Mindwired is dark).
- **Curiosity, not summary:** show the intriguing *thing*, not a caption of the topic.
- The title and thumbnail must NOT say the same words — they should combine into the
  gap (title asks, thumbnail teases, neither fully answers).
- Emit a ready-to-paste GPT image prompt for each concept using THUMBNAILS.md Workflow C.

### 4. Deliver
- 3 titles (ranked, with the score and which frame each uses)
- 3 thumbnail concepts (with GPT prompts)
- The single recommended title+thumbnail pairing and WHY it's the strongest gap
- A one-line prediction of what could make it fail (honesty check)

## Testing discipline (the long game)
- Use YouTube's built-in **Test & Compare** (3 thumbnails) on every upload once eligible.
- After 48h, read CTR in Studio. <4% = repackage and consider re-uploading as a new video.
  6%+ = leave it, the topic works. Log the winner's *pattern* (which frame, which visual)
  in memory so the next video reuses what worked.
- One repackage beats ten new videos. The channel's growth is a CTR-learning loop.

## What this skill will NOT do
Promise virality, invent fake numbers, or approve packaging that overpromises what the
video delivers (that tanks retention and long-term trust). If the topic itself has no
proven demand, say so and point to the icahn-validate skill first —
no packaging saves a topic nobody searches for.
